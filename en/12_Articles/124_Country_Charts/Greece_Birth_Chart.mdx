The pages split. AstrologyKing uses the 1974 Campion chart, and says it came from someone else. Oxford Astrologer uses 25 March 1821, with no time and no place. Horoscopes of Europe shows 1822 as its main chart, and lists the 1974 date under important dates, citing world-of-wisdom.com. A commenter, Thomas Gazis, argues for 1 January 1822 in the old calendar.

The 1830 chart needs a plain warning. It rests on a single anonymous comment, and the research saw it only in a search summary, not in the page text. Its time, 20:28 in London, is labelled rectified. The research does not say how the time was chosen. We computed it and report its score below, but we do not treat it as a source-backed candidate.

Three traps matter here.

**The calendar.** Greece used the Julian calendar until 1923, so dates in 1821 and 1822 are ambiguous unless a source states the calendar. The 1822 sources do state it. The 25 March 1821 page does not. If it is Julian, the date is 6 April 1821 in the Gregorian calendar. We kept the date as the source gives it, and the chart has no time anyway.

**The time zone.** Athens used EET, +02:00, in July 1974 without summer time. Earlier dates would use local mean time.

**The place.** AstrologyKing does not name the place. Athens is taken from the Horoscopes of Europe entry.

## Testing the chart against history

We tested each timed chart against dated events. The rules were fixed before the charts were cast. An event scores 2 points if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 point if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies one of the houses for that kind of event, or is the named planet. The houses are:

- war: 6, 7, 8, or Mars
- economic crisis: 6, 8, 12, or Saturn
- constitutional change: 1, 9, 10

Only events dated to the day are scored, and only events after the chart date count. The lead chart dates from 24 July 1974, so almost the whole history of modern Greece falls before it. Three scored events remain. Their sources are in the research file.

A time that was chosen with known events in mind would score well on those events, and we cannot rule that out. We therefore do not take the score as support for the 1830 chart, and we do not drop the 1974 chart because of its own. The honest conclusion is that this test cannot decide between them.
