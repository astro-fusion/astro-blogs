# Iraq Birth Chart: Sagittarius Rising, 1921 or 1932?

Astrologers disagree about when Iraq was born. Two of the sources we could use take the independence of 3 October 1932 and two take the founding of the kingdom on 23 August 1921, a split that cuts across Vedic and Western practice. We lead with 1932, 13:30 in Baghdad, because it is the date of full independence. In the sidereal zodiac (Lahiri, whole-sign) that chart has Sagittarius rising and the Moon in Scorpio, in Vishakha. Confidence is low, and our test does not separate the two charts.

The 1921 chart has the better pedigree: it is a recorded time, rated A by Rodden, for the day Faisal I was proclaimed king under the British Mandate. The 1932 chart marks sovereign independence, when the Anglo-Iraqi treaty took effect and the League of Nations admitted Iraq. We chose 1932 as the lead for that reason only.

**The time trap.** The 1932 time is not a Baghdad event. Campion's 13:30 is the League vote, which took place at 11:30 in Geneva, moved to Baghdad. The chain is 11:30 in Geneva, 10:30 UT, 13:30 in Baghdad at +03:00. The offset is not stated on the page, so it is inferred. A chart cast at 11:30 in Baghdad would be wrong.

## What the chart says about the country

This is a reading in the classical manner, worded as tradition. The chart is one of two rivals and its time is inferred.

Sagittarius rising is read as a state with a long horizon and a strong sense of law and belief. The lagna lord is Jupiter. It sits in the 9th house in Leo, the house of fortune, law, religion and long journeys, in its own trine. Venus and Ketu share the house. The 9th house with three bodies in it is a house that is full: tradition reads that as a country in which law, faith and inheritance carry great weight and are hard to separate.

The 10th house is Virgo, and the Sun and Mercury are both in it. The 10th is the house of government. Mercury, its lord, is in its own sign with the Sun, which is read as an administrative, document-driven state and a strong executive. Saturn is in the 2nd house in Capricorn, its own sign. The 2nd is the house of wealth, family and speech, and a strong Saturn there is read as resources held for a long time and carefully.

The Moon is in the 12th house in Scorpio, in Vishakha, at 1.8 degrees. The 12th is the house of loss, hidden things and foreign lands. The Moon is read as the public mood, and in the 12th it is read as a mood that is withdrawn or burdened. Mars is in Cancer in the 8th house, the house of upheaval and what is concealed. Mars is in its sign of debilitation there. Tradition reads that pair, a 12th house Moon and an 8th house Mars, as a chart in which the public mood and the use of force are both under strain.

None of this is evidence. The two rival charts give different readings, and the page does not take sides on the history.

## Testing the chart against history

We tested each chart against dated events. The rules were fixed before the charts were cast. An event scores 2 points if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 point if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies one of the houses for that kind of event, or is the named planet. The houses are:

- war: 6, 7, 8, or Mars
- leader's death: 8, 10
- constitutional change: 1, 9, 10
- territorial change: 4, 7, 12

Only events dated to the day are scored. The maximum is 20 points for ten events. The facts follow the research file and are worded as it words them. The table shows the periods for c1 and the points for c1 and c2.

## What this chart cannot tell you

- The date is disputed. The sources are split between 1921 and 1932, and no test here resolves it.
- The 1932 time is a Geneva vote time shown as 13:30 in Baghdad. It is inferred, and the +03:00 offset is not stated on any page.
- Both Vedic sources give a date with no time. The 1932 Vedic source starts its dasha on the date alone.
- Astro-Databank could not be read.
- A country that went from mandate to kingdom, republic and later governments has more than one beginning. A chart for one date reads one of them.
- The scores come from ten events and broad rules. A chart that fits the past does not predict the future.
