# Taiwan Birth Chart: Pisces Rising on a Noon Convention

There is no single agreed birth chart for Taiwan. Astrologers who chart Taiwan generally use charts for the Republic of China (ROC), most often its establishment in Nanjing on 1 January 1912. Cast at noon in Nanjing, that gives a Pisces ascendant at 19.7 degrees and the Moon in Aries in Krittika. Others use the date the ROC Constitution took effect, 25 December 1947, cast in Taipei. This page describes charts that astrologers use. It takes no position on political status. We rate the confidence as low, because the date is shared but the time is not.

All three astrologer pages agree on 1 January 1912. That is the consensus on the date, and the whole of it. The time is a three-way split: midnight, noon, and none. The date commemorates Sun Yat-sen's inauguration as provisional president and the proclamation of the Republic of China in Nanjing (see [the 1911 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_Revolution)). Reports of the hour of the inauguration conflict. Search summaries give 11 pm, midnight and evening, and we could not confirm any of them. A late-evening time would also change the date in UT.

The pages apply the chart differently. Adams applies the 1912 chart to China as a whole. AstrologyKing and Rath apply it to Taiwan directly. For 1947, the Constitution of the ROC took effect on 25 December 1947 (ratified 25 December 1946; see [the Constitution page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_China)). Taipei was not the seat of government in 1947, so a Taipei place for that date is a choice by the author. We found no astrologer chart for the events of 1949.

A note on Rath's chart. His page says Mars is in the lagna, but the page text does not show the date, time or place that produce it. None of the timed charts we computed has Mars in the ascendant sign: Mars is in Taurus on both 1912 charts (with Pisces and Virgo rising) and in Leo on the 1947 chart (with Virgo rising). So we could not reproduce his chart, and we did not compute it.

There are time-zone traps. China had no uniform standard time in 1912. We use China Coast Time, +08:00. Local mean time at Nanjing would be +07:55. A five-minute difference shifts the chart slightly. We did not compute that variant. No source states an offset.

## What the chart says about the country

This reading rests on a noon convention, so treat it as a description of one candidate chart. Pisces rises, so Jupiter is the lagna lord. Jupiter is in Scorpio in the 9th house, in Anuradha at 11.9 degrees, and it is close to Venus at 3.2 degrees. The 9th is read as the house of law, belief and long-held ideas. A lagna lord in a trine is classically a supportive placement. Jupiter also rules Sagittarius, the 10th house, so the lord of the 1st and 10th sits in the 9th. Readers link this combination with an identity bound up with public role and principle.

The Sun and Mercury are in Sagittarius in the 10th, the house of government and standing, in Jupiter's own sign. The Sun, at 16.8 degrees in Purva Ashadha, rules the 6th. Mercury, retrograde at 2.8 degrees in Mula, rules the 4th and 7th. Tradition reads planets in the 10th as the public face of the state, and a retrograde Mercury there as an administration that revisits and revises.

The Moon, the significator of the people, is in Aries in the 2nd, in Krittika at 29.2 degrees, the last degree of the sign. The Moon rules the 5th. It shares the 2nd with Saturn (20.9 degrees in Bharani, retrograde) and Rahu (4.5 degrees in Ashwini). The 2nd is the house of wealth and speech. The Moon's sign depends on the hour, because it sits within one degree of changing sign. Saturn there is classically read as caution about resources. Saturn also rules the 11th and 12th houses.

Mars is in Taurus in the 3rd, the house of effort and communication, in Krittika at 1.7 degrees. It rules the 2nd and 9th. Ketu is in the 8th in Libra. We state all of this as what the tradition says about the placements. It is not a claim that the chart caused or foretold anything.

Twelve hours moves the 1912 ascendant from Pisces to Virgo. The Moon is in Aries on all three charts, but at 29.2 degrees in Krittika for noon and 22.1 degrees in Bharani for midnight. That shifts the Vimshottari dates. The two 1912 charts therefore have different dasha clocks, and they score differently against the same events.

## Testing the chart against history

We tested each timed candidate against events dated to the day. The rules were fixed before the charts were cast. An event scores 2 if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies one of the houses linked to that kind of event (counted from the Vedic ascendant), or is the named planet:

- War: houses 6, 7, 8, or Mars.
- Economic crisis: houses 6, 8, 12, or Saturn.
- Leader death or assassination: houses 8, 10.
- Constitutional change: houses 1, 9, 10.
- Territorial change: houses 4, 7, 12.
- Natural disaster: houses 4, 8, 12.

The noon chart leads chance by 3.1 points, the largest lead of the three. The midnight chart is at chance, and the 1947 chart is 0.4 above it. Some cautions apply. Nine events is a small sample. Five of the nine are "constitutional change", the kind with the widest set of qualifying houses. The same date scores 15 at noon and 11 at midnight, which shows how much the result depends on the hour. Noon is a convention, so the lead may reflect luck in the time chosen. We read this as: the noon chart is the best of three and is modestly encouraging, but it is not proof. We found no evidence that any of the charts is the "right" one.

## What this chart cannot tell you

There is no recorded time for any of these charts. The ascendant, every house and every dasha date depend on a convention: noon, midnight, or none. Reports of the actual hour of the 1912 inauguration conflict and are known to us only from search summaries.

The choice of moment is also open. Astrologers chart the ROC from 1912 and from 1947, and use the chart for China as a whole or for Taiwan. There is no agreed founding moment for Taiwan, and a chart for a state tells you about the moment cast, not about its status. This page takes no position on political questions, and no chart here is a statement about them.

Finally, the evidence is limited. Nine events cannot separate charts. The one that leads does so on a noon convention, and the same date at midnight scores at chance.
