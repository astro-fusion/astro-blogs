That is the whole list. The research opened Astrotheme and nothing else: a second chart-site address returned an error, a world-map astrology site has no Kuwait page, and several web searches returned only Astrotheme. No Vedic source was found, so the sidereal chart here is the Western chart recast with the Lahiri ayanamsa.

The date follows Wikipedia, whose [Kuwait page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait) gives 19 June 1961 as independence from the UK, the end of the 1899 protectorate agreement with Britain. Kuwait does not mark that day today. Its national day is 25 February, the anniversary of the coronation of Sheikh Abdullah. The same page says the national day was first celebrated on 19 June and later moved because of the summer heat. So there are two possible dates, and the research chose the independence date because that is the one Astrotheme uses.

Astrotheme shows the time as 12:00 AM. A founding act with a time on the clock would not usually fall at exactly midnight, and no source says anything was done at that hour. That is why we call it a placeholder.

**The time-zone question.** The time-zone database treats Kuwait as a link to Riyadh, which is UTC+3 from 14 March 1947. We used +03:00, so 00:00 on 19 June is 21:00 UTC on 18 June. The database comments say that local time across the Arabian Peninsula was not standardised until about 1968. The offset is likely but not certain. Astrotheme's own figures agree with the engine on the tropical positions: its listed ascendant of 29 degrees 46 minutes of Pisces matches the engine's 29.8, and its Sun at 27 degrees 21 minutes of Gemini matches 27.4.
