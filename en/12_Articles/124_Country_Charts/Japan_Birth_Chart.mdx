# Japan Birth Chart: Capricorn Rising, and a Chart That Scores Below Chance

The birth chart of Japan is most often cast for the day the post-war constitution came into force: 3 May 1947, midnight, Tokyo. In the sidereal zodiac (Lahiri) that gives Capricorn rising, with Sun in Aries and Moon in Virgo. Confidence is low. No candidate has a recorded time, no group of astrologers has a clear majority, and in our test this chart scores below chance.

Of five astrologer pages read, two use the midnight 1947 chart, one uses it at noon, one uses the Diet vote and two use 1952. Campion is the common upstream source for both the 1947 and 1952 charts. There is no clear majority and no recorded time, which is why confidence is low. The qhdc.org page says most Japanese astrologers regard c3 as the national chart; that is the page's own claim and we could not confirm it. It also says European and American astrologers usually read Japan as Libra, which its author disputes.

The traps are about clocks. Midnight is the start of the legal day, not a moment. Adams uses local mean time for 1947 although Japan had used standard time (+09:00) since 1888, so her noon chart is cast at +09:19. The 1952 chart from Campion is listed in GMT, and 22:30 is our conversion to Japan time. GaneshaSpeaks gives no basis for 23:24 and no zone, so +09:00 is assumed. The research found no astrologer source for the Meiji Constitution, so it is not a candidate. Japan had occasional daylight time in 1948 to 1951, but not on any of these dates, according to the time-zone database.

## What the chart says about the country

Capricorn rising is read as a nation that is disciplined, hierarchical and long-lived. The lagna lord, Saturn, sits in Cancer in the 7th house, in Pushya. The 7th is the house of partners and relations with others, so the tradition reads a country whose identity is defined through its dealings with outside powers. That sits closely with a post-war chart written under an occupation and a treaty alliance. The description is a classical reading, not evidence.

The 4th house, Aries, holds the Sun and Mercury. The Sun is in Aries, a sign of exaltation, and the 4th is the house of land, education and the public. Mercury is the lord of the 6th and 9th and sits close to the Sun. This is read as a state that places weight on its land, its schools and its working population.

Venus, the lord of the 10th (government and standing), is in Pisces in the 3rd, a sign of exaltation, with Mars. The 3rd house governs skill, craft, communication and effort. The tradition reads this as a state whose standing is built on making and trading things, with careful, quiet effort rather than display. Mars, the lord of the 4th and 11th, joins it.

The Moon, for the people, is in Virgo in the 9th, in Hasta. Virgo is the sign of service and detail, and the 9th is the house of law, belief and teachers. Jupiter, lord of the 2nd and 12th, is retrograde in Scorpio in the 11th with Ketu. The 11th is the house of gains. Rahu is in Taurus in the 5th. These are the main notes. They describe a pattern and do not predict.

## Testing the chart against history

The rules were fixed before scoring. An event scores 2 if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies one of the houses for that kind of event (whole-sign, from the ascendant) or is the named planet. Constitutional change uses houses 1, 9 and 10. Economic crisis uses 6, 8 and 12 or Saturn. Leader death uses 8 and 10. Territorial change uses 4, 7 and 12. Natural disaster uses 4, 8 and 12. Boom uses 2, 5 and 11 or Jupiter. Only events dated to the day are scored. "Chance" is the score a random pair of periods would get on the same events with the same chart. The 1960 Anpo protests and the 1995 subway attack are dated, but their kind ("other") has no rule, so they are not scored.

## What this chart cannot tell you

The time is a convention, so the ascendant, the houses and every period date depend on it. Moving the founding from 1947 to 1946 or 1952 changes the ascendant and the periods, and the sources do not agree. In our test the lead chart did worse than a random pair of periods. The best scorer is rectified. We tested only ten events, and the rules are broad. The chart also cannot say how Japan will deal with any event, and does not stand for the views of any person or party.
