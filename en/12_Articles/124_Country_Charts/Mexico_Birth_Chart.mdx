# Mexico Birth Chart: Gemini Rising and a Disputed 1917 Time

The Mexico chart that astrologers cite most is the adoption of the current constitution, dated 31 January 1917 at 4:00 pm. The time comes from Campion's Book of World Horoscopes, as quoted by AstrologyKing. No source says where the chart was cast, so this page uses Querétaro, where the constituent assembly sat, at local mean time. That place is our assumption. In the sidereal zodiac (Lahiri, whole-sign) the chart has Gemini rising and the Moon in Taurus, in Rohini. Confidence is low: only three Western astrologer pages were found, they split three ways, and no Vedic astrologer source was found.

The three pages disagree on which event is the birth. The 1917 chart is the only one with a published time from a reference book, and two pages name it. The 1821 chart comes from one blog that ended the signing at 5:04 pm, put 2 degrees of Pisces on the ascendant, and replaced an earlier Taurus-rising chart. The source of that time is not given on the page. The 1810 date is the one the Astrologydialogues author prefers for the Mexican people, with no time or place. The same blog names 20 November 1910, the start of the Revolution, as another possibility.

Three traps matter here.

**The date.** Astrologers date the constitution 31 January 1917. The research timeline on Wikipedia gives 5 February 1917 for its approval by the assembly at Querétaro. We state both. The chart is cast for the astrologers' date and time, and the scoring below uses 5 February as the event date.

**The place.** The Campion time has no place attached. Querétaro is a reasonable guess, because the assembly met there, but it is a guess. A different city would move the ascendant.

**The clock.** Mexico had no national standard time in 1917. It was adopted in 1922. Every candidate therefore needs the local mean time of its place, which for Querétaro is 6 hours 42 minutes behind UTC. The independence dates have a further complication: Spain did not recognise Mexican independence until 1836, and Britannica also cites the Treaty of Córdoba of 24 August 1821.

## What the chart says about the country

This is a reading in the classical manner, worded as tradition. The time and place are not firm, so the ascendant and the houses are a working frame and nothing more.

Gemini rising is read as a changeable, talkative, many-sided state. The lagna lord is Mercury, and it sits in the 7th house in Sagittarius, in Uttara Ashadha, at 27.0 degrees. The 7th house governs partnerships, treaties and dealings with the outside. Venus (27.9 degrees) and Rahu (26.0 degrees) are in the same sign and the same house, so three bodies are packed into the last four degrees of Sagittarius. The tradition reads a lagna lord in the 7th as an identity formed through relations with other nations, and Rahu there as a taste for foreign influence and unusual alliances. This is a reading and not a proof.

The Moon is in Taurus in Rohini, in the 12th house. The 12th is the house of loss, expense, foreign lands and the hidden. The Moon is the public mood, so a Moon here is read as a people whose feeling is private, guarded or turned toward things beyond the border. Rohini is a nakshatra classically tied to growth, land and abundance. The Moon is in a sign the tradition counts as strong for it.

The Sun and Mars are together in Capricorn in the 8th house, about 6 degrees apart. The 8th is the house of crisis, transformation and sudden events, and Sun with Mars is read as force, heat and a record of upheaval. The 10th house, which stands for government and public standing, is Pisces and holds no planet. Its lord Jupiter is in the 11th, the house of gains and networks, in Aries, in Ashwini. Jupiter makes a square to Saturn, 2.3 degrees apart. Saturn, retrograde in Cancer in the 2nd house, is read as a strict, cautious treasury and a stress on resources. Saturn also stands opposite Rahu, 7.3 degrees apart, which the tradition reads as friction between old structures and foreign or novel pressures.

None of this is evidence. The chart has a quoted time, an assumed place and a disputed date.

## Testing the chart against history

We tested each chart against dated events. The rules were fixed before the charts were cast. An event scores 2 points if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 point if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies one of the houses for that kind of event, or is the named planet. The houses used here are:

- war: 6, 7, 8, or Mars
- economic crisis: 6, 8, 12, or Saturn
- natural disaster: 4, 8, 12
- constitutional change: 1, 9, 10

Only events dated to the day are scored, and only events after the chart's own date can be scored. For c1 that leaves six events and a maximum of 12 points.
