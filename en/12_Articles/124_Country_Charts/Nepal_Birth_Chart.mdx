# Nepal Birth Chart: Why 1768 Has No Ascendant, and What 2008 Shows

Nepal has no agreed birth chart. The date most often given is 25 September 1768, the day Prithvi Narayan Shah entered Kathmandu during Indra Jatra and was crowned. No hour is recorded for it, so that day has no ascendant and no houses. The only timed chart with a source behind it is the declaration of the republic on 28 May 2008 at about 23:26 in Kathmandu, which two Vedic authors use. Our confidence in any single Nepal chart is low, and this article says why.

We do two things. First, we show what holds for the whole of 25 September 1768, from midnight to midnight. Second, we read the 2008 republic chart as a labelled candidate, not as "the" chart of Nepal. All timed sections below belong to that candidate.

The research marks c1 as the consensus only because it is the one moment tied to "founding" by a Nepali source. One source is a very small sample. It is not the same as agreement.

There are traps in the dates. The Wikipedia page on the Battle of Kathmandu says 26 September 1768, while the page on Prithvi Narayan Shah and Nepali Times say 25 September (13 Ashwin 1825 in the Bikram Sambat calendar). The research did not verify a Julian conversion, so these are the dates as the pages give them. Bikram Sambat years run about 56 years and 8 to 9 months ahead of the Gregorian year and must never be typed into software as AD. Kathmandu used local solar time until 1920, so the 1768 offset is local mean time, about +05:41. Nepal then used Indian Standard Time, +05:30, until 1986, and +05:45 since. Entering +05:30 or +05:45 for 1768 would be wrong.

The 2008 chart has its own problem. Its time is a clock time reported by the press, not an official record. Malhotra's page gives both 23:26 and 23:36. K. N. Rao gives 11:26 PM, citing Telegraph Nepal, with Makara (Capricorn) lagna. The date was set by the election timetable, and the research found no sign of an astrological muhurta.

The Nepali source needs its own paragraph, because it is the only one. The research records Santosh Vashistha of the World Astro Federation International Committee, in Nepali Times, saying that Nepal is "a Scorpio" from 25 September 1768. The page does not say which tradition he uses. The research does not record whether "Scorpio" means the Sun, the Moon or the ascendant.

The Moon is the only planet that changes sign on that day. Everything else is fixed to within a few degrees, which is why the day can be described even though the hour cannot. Venus changes nakshatra, from Hasta to Chitra, before the day ends. Mercury changes pada.

In the tropical zodiac the Sun is in Libra (2.16 to 3.14 degrees), the Moon stays in Pisces (9.86 to 24.69), Jupiter is in Libra, Saturn in Cancer at 17.1, and Mars in Taurus.

The Vimshottari clock depends on the Moon, so it depends on the hour. At 00:00 the first mahadasha is Rahu, running to 13 October 1769, followed by Jupiter. At 23:59 it is Saturn, running to 16 September 1786. A chart for this day therefore has at least two different dasha sequences, and no sourced hour to choose between them.

Now the plain comparison. Nepali Times, as recorded in the research, calls Nepal "a Scorpio" from this day. In the engine's positions for 25 September 1768 none of the nine sidereal planets is in Scorpio at any time of day, and none of the tropical Sun, Moon or planets we list is in Scorpio. The Sun is in Virgo (sidereal) or Libra (tropical), and the Moon is in Aquarius or Pisces. So the quoted statement does not match the engine's positions for that day. We make no guess about why. For the record, the only candidate in this article with a Scorpio Moon is the 1767 chart, c2, and we do not claim any link.

## What the chart says about the country

This reading is for the 2008 candidate only. It describes the placements in classical terms and does not predict.

Capricorn rises, with Rahu in the first house at 28.5 degrees. The ascendant is read as the nation itself and its public face. Saturn, the ruler of Capricorn, is the lagna lord, and it sits in the 8th house in Leo, in Magha. The 8th is read as sudden change, hidden matters, and the state's finances and debts. A lagna lord in the 8th is classically read as a country whose identity is tied to upheaval and to long, slow rebuilding. Rahu on the ascendant is read as an unconventional, foreign-influenced or restless national character.

The Moon is in Aquarius in the 2nd house, in Purva Bhadrapada. The Moon is the ruler of the 7th, which governs dealings with other countries, and the 2nd is wealth and speech. A Moon here is read as a public mood tied to money, and to the country's neighbours.

The 10th house, government and the ruler, is Libra, and its lord Venus sits in the 5th house in Taurus with the Sun and Mercury. Three planets in one sign in the 5th house is the striking group of the chart. The 5th is classically the house of ministers and advisers. A 10th lord there is read as government that depends on its advisers, its coalition partners and its parties. 

Mars is in Cancer in the 7th house and is joined by Ketu. Mars in Cancer is the classical weak (debilitated) Mars. The 7th house is read as relations with other nations and as open conflict. Ketu there is read as detachment from, or friction with, that area. Mars rules the 4th and 11th houses (Aries and Scorpio), which links land and the legislature to that 7th-house placement. This is a description of the chart's emphasis, not a statement about any country or event.

Jupiter is in its own sign in the 12th house, retrograde, at 27.8. As lord of the 3rd and 12th houses it is read as a nation whose losses and foreign connections are an important part of its story.

## Testing the chart against history

The method was fixed before any chart was cast. Vimshottari repeats every 120 years; for charts older than that the later cycles come from the same engine. An event scores 2 if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies a house for that kind of event, counted whole-sign from the Vedic ascendant, or if it is the named planet. The houses are: constitutional change 1, 9, 10; territorial change 4, 7, 12; natural disaster 4, 8, 12; war 6, 7, 8 or Mars. Only events dated to the day are scored. "Chance" is what a random pair of periods would score on the same events with the same chart.

The 1767 chart (c2) is the candidate with the most testable history, because eleven dated events fall after its date.

## What this chart cannot tell you

There is no recorded hour for 25 September 1768, so no ascendant, no houses and no divisional chart can be stated for it. The Moon changes sign during that day, so even the Vimshottari clock cannot be fixed. The 2008 time is a press-reported clock time, and Malhotra's own page gives two minutes that differ by ten. The 2008 date was set by an election timetable, and nobody found evidence that a muhurta was chosen. The 1767 Astrotheme chart is not tied to any event we could identify, and may be a calendar-conversion artefact. The 1951 and 2015 dates have no astrologer behind them.

Nepal has no single founding day. The 1768 entry into Kathmandu was one step: Bhaktapur fell in November 1769 and unification went on into the 1790s. The republic of 2008 is the start of a form of government, not the start of the country. Which of these a "birth chart" should mean is a choice, not a fact.
