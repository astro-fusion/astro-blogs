# Bhutan Birth Chart: Taurus Rising at a Midnight Convention

Bhutan has no widely agreed birth chart. The one a Vedic-style astrology site publishes is cast for the India-Bhutan Treaty of Friendship, signed on 8 August 1949, with the time set to 00:00 in Thimphu. That gives a Taurus ascendant at 13.6 degrees and the Moon in Capricorn in Uttara Ashadha. No time of day is recorded for the treaty, so the midnight is a convention and the ascendant should not be relied on. We rate the confidence as low.

The two sources agree on the date and disagree on the time. That is the main finding. With no recorded hour, the chart is a day chart, and the rising sign depends on a clock time that nobody has documented. We found no Western or Vedic astrologer article that discusses Bhutan's national chart at all.

The choice of event is also open. The treaty of 8 August 1949 is the one by which India recognised Bhutan's independence in internal matters, according to our research notes, and some chart databases use it as Bhutan's modern birth. The alternative is the monarchy: Ugyen Wangchuck was enthroned as the first hereditary king at Punakha Dzong on 17 December 1907, which is commemorated as the National Day (first observed in 1971, per a search summary of [the Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Day_of_Bhutan)). One source in [History of Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bhutan) says an assembly elected him in November 1907, which conflicts with 17 December. Without a time for either date, we computed only the 1949 chart.

Bhutanese Tsip astrology, according to a search summary of [Bhutanwiki](https://www.bhutanwiki.org/articles/bhutanese-astrology), produces personal charts (kye tsi), not national ones. We could not open that page.

There is a time-zone trap too. The tz database lists +05:30 for Bhutan until 1987 and +06:00 afterwards. We could not verify this on a dated page, and the sources state no offset. We use +05:30 for 1949.

## What the chart says about the country

This reading rests on a provisional ascendant, so treat it as a description of one candidate. Taurus rises, and Venus is the lagna lord. Venus sits in Leo in the 4th house, in Purva Phalguni at 21.5 degrees. The tradition reads the 4th as the house of the homeland, land and domestic peace. A lagna lord there is read as a country whose identity is tied to its own ground.

The 4th house is crowded. Mercury (3.9 degrees in Magha), Saturn (13.2 degrees in Magha) and Venus all sit in Leo. Three planets in one house is read as a theme that dominates the chart. Saturn rules the 9th and 10th houses for a Taurus ascendant, and classical readers treat it as a strong planet for this rising sign. Here it sits in the 4th, in the sign opposite the 10th house, which is Aquarius. The 10th is the house of government and standing. Its lord in the 4th links public authority with the homeland and with property.

The Moon, the significator of the people, is in Capricorn in Uttara Ashadha, in the 9th house, together with retrograde Jupiter at 1.8 degrees in the same nakshatra. The 9th is the house of dharma, law, long-held tradition and teachers. A Moon and Jupiter pair there is classically read as a popular mood tied to inherited custom and to guidance from elders. Jupiter rules the 8th and 11th houses here, which adds themes of continuity and of gains.

The Sun, in Cancer in Ashlesha in the 3rd, relates to effort, communication and neighbours. Mars, lord of the 7th and 12th, is in Gemini in the 2nd, the house of wealth and speech. Rahu is in the 11th in Pisces and Ketu in the 5th in Virgo. These are traditional associations only. They are not claims that the chart caused or foretold anything.

## Testing the chart against history

We tested the chart against events dated to the day. The rules were fixed before the chart was cast. An event scores 2 if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies one of the houses linked to that kind of event (counted from the Vedic ascendant), or is the named planet:

- War: houses 6, 7, 8, or Mars.
- Economic crisis: houses 6, 8, 12, or Saturn.
- Leader death or assassination: houses 8, 10.
- Constitutional change: houses 1, 9, 10.
- Territorial change: houses 4, 7, 12.
- Natural disaster: houses 4, 8, 12.

## What this chart cannot tell you

There is no recorded time. The ascendant, every house and the dasha dates rest on a convention taken from one unattributed web page, and a second source reportedly uses 12:01 pm. Because the Moon starts the Vimshottari count, a different time shifts every period date.

The event is also open to dispute. The 1949 treaty is a recognition of internal independence by India, as our notes describe it, and the 1907 monarchy is the traditional National Day. We found no astrologer who publishes a chart for 1907 or states the time. The treaty chart may describe a relationship more than a country's origin.

Last, the evidence is thin. Eight scored events, three of them clustered in 2008, cannot separate charts. Bhutan's own Tsip tradition does not seem to cast national charts at all, so there is no local national chart to compare. Treat this page as a description of a candidate, not a reading of the country.
