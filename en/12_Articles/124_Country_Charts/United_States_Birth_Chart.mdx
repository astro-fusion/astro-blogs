# United States Birth Chart: Sagittarius Rising and a Disputed Hour

The chart most astrologers cast for the United States is the Sibly chart: 4 July 1776, 17:10 local mean time, Philadelphia. It has Sagittarius rising in the tropical zodiac and a Cancer Sun. The date is almost never disputed. The hour is, because no document records when the Declaration of Independence was adopted. Our confidence in this chart is medium: it is the default of the best-known chart library and of the one Vedic writer we found who states a preference, but it rests on a deduction and not on a record. Tested against ten dated events it does no better than chance.

The Sibly time is a deduction. Ebenezer Sibly published the chart in 1787 with "ten hours ten minutes PM"; the 17:10 Philadelphia time is a later reading of that. The research also found a claim on astrology.co.uk that Sibly cast the angles for London and did not convert the planets, so the printed chart contains errors. We could not find documentary evidence of a signing at 5:10 pm; the researcher notes that Campion is cited as unable to verify the claims usually made for it.

The Gemini-rising chart has the longer history. The same research found it described as the most popular chart until 9/11, but that came from a search summary we did not open, so we leave it as unverified. Its weakness is plain: the delegates were not working at 2 am, and Greene says it has no historical support. Vedic writers split three ways (Sibly, Kelleher, Angelino), so there is no Vedic convention. Some astrologers use other events entirely: the 8 July public proclamation or the Constitution of 1787 to 1788. Britain's colonies had already adopted the Gregorian calendar in 1752, so the old calendar is not a trap here.

## What the chart says about the country

This is the reading the tradition would give from the placements. It is a description of symbolism and not a finding.

Scorpio rising is read as a country with a guarded, intense and self-made character, one that reacts strongly to pressure. Its lord, Mars, sits in the 8th house at 0.6 of Gemini, the first degree of the sign. The 8th house is read as shared money, debt, crisis and transformation. Mars joined by the Sun, Venus and Jupiter makes four planets in that house. The tradition reads such a crowd as a national life in which public debt, other people's money and recurring reinvention are central themes, and in which the lord of the ascendant is tied to them.

The 10th house, which stands for government and public standing, is Leo and holds no planet. Its lord, the Sun, is in the 8th with the others. So authority in this chart is read as bound up with those 8th-house matters, not as a separate, stable pillar. Saturn in the 11th house, the house of assemblies, networks and gains, is read as lawmaking bodies and long-term institutions that grow slowly.

The Moon is in Aquarius in the 4th house, in Dhanishta. The 4th stands for the land, the home and the people. Aquarius is read as a population that sees itself as a collective of equals. Mercury and Rahu in the 9th house, in Cancer, bring law, higher learning and foreign contact into one house. Rahu in the 9th is read as the openness to outside influence, and the tradition associates Cancer here with an emphasis on home and belonging.

In the tropical chart, the Sun in Cancer in the 7th house is the source of the familiar "Cancer nation" reading: a country defined by its homeland and its idea of family. Both readings depend on the 17:10 hour. At a different hour the ascendant, houses and the Moon's nakshatra change.

## Testing the chart against history

We used fixed rules. An event scores 2 if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies a house linked to that kind of event, or is the named planet. War uses houses 6, 7 and 8 or Mars. A crisis in the economy uses houses 6, 8 and 12 or Saturn. The death of a leader uses houses 8 and 10. A change of territory uses houses 4, 7 and 12. Only events dated to the day count. "Chance" is what a random pair of periods would score on the same events with the same chart. Vimshottari repeats every 120 years; for charts older than that the later cycles come from the same engine.

## What this chart cannot tell you

The hour is the main limit. With no recorded time, the ascendant, every house and the Moon's nakshatra are a convention. The Sibly time is a modern deduction from a printed chart that may itself contain errors. The Moon sign is sturdier than the rest; it changes only if you move the date by a day or two.

A second limit is the choice of event. The adoption of the Declaration, the 2 July vote, the 8 July proclamation and the ratification of the Constitution are separate moments, and each can be argued to be the nation's start. A third is the sample: ten scored events cannot separate five charts. Nothing here forecasts any event. The chart is a symbolic description built on an unrecorded hour.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the United States' rising sign?
In the tropical zodiac used by most Western astrologers, the Sibly chart has Sagittarius rising at 12.4 degrees. In the sidereal zodiac the same chart has Scorpio rising at 21.7 degrees. Other candidate charts give Gemini or other signs, because the hour is not recorded.

### What is the United States' zodiac sign?
The Sun is in Cancer in the tropical zodiac on 4 July 1776, at 13.3 degrees. In the sidereal zodiac it is in Gemini at 22.6 degrees. Most Western articles call the US a Cancer.

### What time was the United States born?
There is no recorded time. The 17:10 hour used in the Sibly chart is deduced from "ten hours ten minutes PM" printed in 1787. Other charts use 02:13, 18:30 and 17:13:55.

### Which dasha is the United States running in 2026?
In the Sibly chart it is the Rahu mahadasha (14 August 2016 to 14 August 2034) and the Mercury antardasha, which ends on 12 February 2027. Ketu follows until 2 March 2028. This depends on the 17:10 hour.

### Is the US chart accurate?
Not in the sense of a verified record. The date is near-unanimous, the time is a convention, and on ten events the chart scores 1.7 points below chance.
