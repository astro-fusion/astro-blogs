# Israel Birth Chart: Virgo Rising at 16:00, and a Score Below Chance

Most astrologers cast the birth chart of Israel for the reading of the Declaration of Establishment on Friday 14 May 1948 at 16:00 in Tel Aviv. In the sidereal system (Lahiri, whole sign) that gives Virgo rising at 29.9 degrees and the Moon in Cancer in the nakshatra Pushya. The date is agreed almost everywhere. The hour is the only real dispute, because some astrologers prefer midnight, when the British Mandate ended. We tested both against nine dated events, and neither scored above chance, so treat every reading below as tradition and not as proof.

The research gives the 16:00 chart high confidence. Five of the seven astrologer pages that show a chart use the 16:00 start. The other two list it and prefer midnight. Western sources agree almost completely. The Vedic evidence is thin: the only Vedic sources found are Cosmologer, who prefers midnight, and Tempora, which uses 16:00 and is a small practitioner site with weak provenance.

The time is a convention in either case. The ceremony ran from 16:00 to about 16:32, and Cosmologer also mentions 16:16 and 16:37 as sub-moments. The Declaration says the state takes effect when the Mandate ends that night, so midnight has a legal argument. Alexander Marr rectified the chart inside the 16:00 to 16:32 span and found an Ascendant about three degrees later than the 16:00 chart (Libra 26 in tropical terms). The Marr chart has no clock time on the page, so we did not compute it.

There is no calendar trap: the Gregorian date is used, and the Hebrew date is 5 Iyar 5708. There is a time-zone trap. Israel was on +02:00 with no daylight time in May 1948, according to the time-zone database. Tempora labels its chart "IST", which a reader could mistake for Indian Standard Time.

## What the chart says about the country

In the Vedic reading, the sign Virgo as ascendant is read as a nation of detail, work, service and constant self-examination. Its lord, Mercury, is in the 9th house in Taurus (Rohini) with the Sun. The 9th is the house of law, belief, teaching and distant places. Mercury also rules the 10th house, so the ascendant lord and the 10th lord are one planet. Tradition reads that as a country whose public standing is tied to its own sense of identity and to argument over what its laws and beliefs mean.

The Moon, which stands for the people, is in Cancer in Pushya, a sign it rules. It sits in the 11th house of gains and networks, with Saturn also in Cancer and the 11th. Saturn at 23.3 degrees is in Ashlesha. Classical texts read a Moon-Saturn pairing in one sign as a serious, watchful, weighed-down public mood, and as strong attachment to land and home (Cancer). This is a description of tradition, not a finding about any group of people.

The 10th house is Gemini and holds Venus (Ardra). Venus is read as the signifier of diplomacy, culture, trade and alliance, so the tradition would read public life as one of negotiation and exchange. Mars, the planet of force and security, stands in Leo in the 12th house, the house of expense, distance and foreign lands. Rahu is in Aries in the 8th, the house of sudden change and shared resources. Ketu is in Libra in the 2nd, the house of wealth and speech. The Rahu-Ketu axis through the 8th and 2nd is read as a theme of sudden turns around resources and security.

Jupiter, retrograde in Sagittarius in the 4th house, is in its own sign. The 4th house is the homeland and its soil. Jupiter is also the 4th and 7th lord for a Virgo ascendant. Tradition reads this as a strong anchor to place, and as partners and neighbours (7th) tied to the question of home (4th).

In the Western chart, the Sun is in Taurus and the Moon in Leo. Saturn, Pluto and the Moon are all in the 10th house in Leo, so the public face is of a state that is conspicuous and under pressure. Western astrologers use these placements in their own ways, and they disagree among themselves.

## Testing the chart against history

We scored each timed chart against dated events, with the rules fixed before scoring. An event scores 2 points if the antardasha lord qualifies and 1 if only the mahadasha lord does. A lord qualifies if it rules or occupies a house linked to that kind of event (whole sign, from the Vedic ascendant), or is the named planet. War uses houses 6, 7 and 8, or Mars. Territorial change uses houses 4, 7 and 12. Assassination uses houses 8 and 10. Constitutional change uses houses 1, 9 and 10. Only events dated to the day are scored, which gives a maximum of 18 for nine events. "Chance" is the average score of random periods on the same events.

## What this chart cannot tell you

The chart is for a meeting, not an instant of birth. The Declaration was read over about half an hour, and the state's legal effect began at the end of the Mandate. The sidereal ascendant lies 0.1 degree from a sign boundary, so a small change of minutes changes the rising sign and the whole house layout.

The Vedic literature on this chart is thin. The page on Barbara Pijan's work (cited in the research) runs only Golda Meir's personal dasha around the proclamation, and does not analyse the state chart. The scoring is crude and one test is not a basis for conclusions. The chart tells you nothing about the future of a country, its wars or its people, and nothing in this article should be read as a judgement on any side of any conflict.
