The 9:15 time is described as the traditional flag-exchange time. We found no documentary source for it. Stockhorst credits the time to Campion, and myAstropedia gives it without a source. That is why the confidence is low: two pages, one time, no record.

The date is a trap of its own. 4 July is also US Independence Day, so a search for a "4 July" chart pulls up American charts that have nothing to do with the Philippines. The Philippine chart is 4 July 1946 in Manila.

The second founding is the Independence Day that Filipinos celebrate, 12 June 1898, when Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed independence from Spain at Cavite el Viejo (Kawit). It has no time and no astrologer chart in what we found. Search results say the proclamation was read between 4 and 5 pm, but that comes from outside astrology, and we did not open it, so we treat it as unverified and cast no chart. A chart for 1898 would also need local mean time, about +08:04, because Manila used local mean time until 6 September 1899, according to Wikipedia's [Time in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_the_Philippines).

For 1946 the offset is not in doubt. The [tz database](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/eggert/tz/main/asia) has Manila at +08:00, with Philippine summer time having ended on 30 November 1945 and the next running in 1954. Wikipedia says no daylight saving was observed in 1946.

## What the chart says about the country

Leo rises, so the Sun is the lagna lord. The Sun is in Gemini in the 11th house, in Ardra. The tradition reads the 11th as the house of gains, networks and fulfilled hopes, and a lagna lord there as an identity tied to alliances and to what the country gathers around it. The Moon and Mars sit in the 1st house itself, both in Purva Phalguni, with the Moon at 22.8 and Mars at 14.8 degrees. The Moon signifies the people, so the people are placed on the ascendant. Mars rules the 4th and 9th houses for Leo rising. A Mars in the 1st is read as a combative, assertive national character. These are classical readings, offered as description.

The 12th house is the most crowded. Mercury, Venus and Saturn are all in Cancer there, in Pushya, Ashlesha and Punarvasu. The 12th is the house of loss, expenses, distance and what lies beyond the border. Three planets there are read as a national story tied to outside powers and to sacrifice. Mercury rules the 2nd and 11th houses, Venus the 3rd and 10th, and Saturn the 6th and 7th. The Moon, which rules the 12th itself, is on the ascendant, so the 12th house theme is tied to the people directly.

The 10th house is Taurus. It holds Rahu in Mrigashira at 26.6 degrees, and Venus, the 10th lord, is in the 12th house. The tradition reads Rahu in the 10th as a theme of rapid change and outside influence on government, and the 10th lord in the 12th as public standing tied to foreign matters. Jupiter in the 2nd house in Virgo, in Chitra, rules the 5th and 8th and is read as support for speech, resources and law. Ketu in the 4th, in Scorpio, in Jyeshtha, is read as detachment from the homeland and its comforts.

## What this chart cannot tell you

The 9:15 time is the foundation, and it has no documentary source. If it is a convention, the ascendant, the houses and the dasha dates are all open. The Moon is at 22.8 degrees of Leo, so the dasha dates depend on the exact hour.

The choice of founding is also open. The 1946 republic is what astrologers use, while Filipinos mark 12 June 1898 as Independence Day, and that date has no time and no chart. The score is below chance on twelve events, which neither supports nor rules out the chart. Several events are political.
