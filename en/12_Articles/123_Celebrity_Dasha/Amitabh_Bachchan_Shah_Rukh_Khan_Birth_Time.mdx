# Amitabh Bachchan and Shah Rukh Khan: Two Clocks, No Timed Reading

Bollywood birth-time pages already do what a dasha article has to do. They print a clock, a lagna, a Moon, a Vimshottari table, and then they attach fame, illness, and marriage to whichever mahadasha that clock produces. The pages disagree with each other, and on these two names the disagreement is large enough that a single timeline would be a choice of source, presented as a calculation.

This note is the comparison. It is not a dasha reading. Neither chart is timed below.

## Amitabh Bachchan: afternoon, dawn, and a one-hour argument inside the afternoon

The date and place are stable: 11 October 1942, Allahabad. The time is not.

Astro-Databank has 15:00 and rates it DD, conflicting or unverified. That claim is stored. The birth time on his row is still the noon placeholder, and no planetary chart is calculated from it. A DD rating with two stories in circulation is not a clock we will run a life through.

AstroSage prints 16:00, source Kundli Sangraha (Bhat), and rates it A. On that page the lagna is Aquarius, the Moon is Libra in Swati, and the Rahu balance at birth is 12 years, 3 months, 8 days, so Rahu runs until 19 January 1955. Their Jupiter then runs to January 1971, and Saturn from 1971 to 1990. A dasha article written on that table puts the 1969 debut and the 1970s films inside Saturn or at the Jupiter–Saturn join, depending on the month the writer cares about.

GrahaSutra prints the same 16:00 and the same place, and a different balance. Their Rahu ends on 27 November 1951, after 9.13 years, and Jupiter runs until November 1967. Saturn, on their table, is already the major period through the debut. The two sites name one clock and produce Rahu balances three years apart. A life-event article that says "he debuted in Saturn" is choosing GrahaSutra's Moon. An article that says "he debuted as Jupiter ended" is choosing AstroSage's Moon. The clock printed at the top of both pages is 16:00.

OurNakshatra uses 16:00 and rates the packet 2 out of 5. They record the further conflict: Harivansh Rai Bachchan's autobiography uses a word for afternoon, which is the 16:00 tradition, and in interviews Amitabh Bachchan has given a time of three to four in the morning. A dawn birth is not a one-hour correction of 16:00. It is a different lagna. House lordship, and with it every sentence of the form "Saturn as 4th lord did this," moves. OurNakshatra leaves the birth dasha unknown for that reason. That is the right scope for a DD record.

Chirag Daruwalla's page uses 16:00, Aquarius lagna, Swati, and then places the 1969 debut in a Jupiter/Rahu period and the 1971 film *Anand* at the start of Saturn. That sequence follows the longer Rahu balance, the AstroSage family, not the GrahaSutra family. The prose treats the period as the cause of the debut. The period is an output of a Moon longitude the sources do not share.

Until a record outranks DD, the nine dated events already in our database for him stay untimed. Mapping them to a noon chart would invent a lagna. Mapping them to 16:00 would pick one of two published Rahu balances, and would ignore the dawn time he himself has used.

## Shah Rukh Khan: the nakshatra boundary sits between the two clocks

Shah Rukh Khan is not in the celebrity table, so there is no row to promote and no chart to recalculate. The public argument is clear enough to state without computing a third Moon.

The date and place are 2 November 1965, New Delhi. Astro-Databank's current time is 06:25, rated A, from memory. The memory is his. On 8 September 2012 he answered a fan on Twitter with "i think 6.25 am." GrahaVaani, AstroNidan, Ishvaram, and Nidarshanavedh use that clock. It gives Libra lagna and the Moon in Capricorn in Dhanishta.

The other clock is 02:30. Naksham still computes Leo lagna from it. Astro-Databank's older note traced 02:30 to a horoscope site whose source was unknown. Nidarshanavedh records AstroSage labeling that data dirty on one of its pages. Libra at 06:25 and Leo at 02:30 are four hours apart. Articles written on Leo discuss a Sun-ruled lagna, a 10th-house Rahu, and a Ruchaka yoga from Mars in a kendra. Articles written on Libra discuss a Venus-ruled lagna and a different house for Mars. Both can sound specific. They are specific about different charts.

The Moon sign is Capricorn on both clocks, and that is the fact that makes the dasha problem easy to miss. Nidarshanavedh puts the Lahiri entry into Dhanishta near 06:07 that morning. Dhanishta is ruled by Mars. Shravana, which is where a 02:30 Moon falls, is ruled by the Moon. The opening mahadasha lord is not a remainder inside one nakshatra. It is a different planet. Their comparison of the two clocks puts Jupiter mahadasha at 4 October 1990 to 4 October 2006 on the 06:25 Moon, and at 5 March 1992 to 5 March 2008 on the 02:30 Moon. Saturn, and the Mercury period that follows it, move with that shift. A paragraph that times *Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge* (1995) to "Jupiter" is true of both calendars and says almost nothing. A paragraph that times a later film to Mercury or to Saturn is true of only one of them.

He said "i think." The A rating means a first-hand memory, not a certificate. We will not publish the Libra-lagna timeline as ours until the person is in the database and the chart is calculated on that one clock, with the 02:30 alternative stated beside it the way the Priyanka Chopra note states 00:30 beside 01:30.

## What a similar article has to contain

The pages that rank for these names are not magic and they are not a PHP trick. They are an indexable URL whose title is the person's name plus "birth chart," a table of planets, and a Vimshottari list long enough to attach a biography to. The weak point in the genre is the clock. AstroSage will rate a compilation A. GrahaSutra will reprint the clock and recompute the Moon. A third site will keep a discarded time because the Leo lagna makes a better story.

A reading we are willing to sign has four lines at the top: the time, the Rodden rating, the source of the time, and the sidereal Moon longitude the periods were counted from. When two published Moons move a mahadasha across the event, the article says so and stops. Bachchan and Khan are that case. The ten charts published with this round are the other case: AA or A, a real clock, a Moon we calculated, and events placed in the periods that Moon produces.
