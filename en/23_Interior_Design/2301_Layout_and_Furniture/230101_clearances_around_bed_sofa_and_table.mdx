# Clearances around a bed, a sofa and a dining table

Furniture fits a room on paper long before it works in it. After reading this you will know how much free floor to leave around a bed, a sofa and a dining table, and you will be able to test your own room with a tape measure before you order anything from the carpenter.

The numbers below are common practice, not rules. Aim for them. Where your room is tight, you can go below them in one place, but not in every place at once.

## Why clearance matters more than furniture size

A bed is 190 cm (6 ft 3 in) long, but you also need to reach it, make it, and walk past it. A chair is 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) deep, but it needs another 60 cm (2 ft) behind it to be pulled out. The free floor around furniture is part of its size. Count it when you plan.

## Common furniture sizes

| Item | Typical size |
|---|---|
| Single bed | 90 x 190 cm (3 ft x 6 ft 3 in) |
| Double bed | 150 x 190 cm (5 ft x 6 ft 3 in) |
| Queen bed | 152 x 198 cm (5 ft x 6 ft 6 in) |
| King bed | 180 x 198 cm (5 ft 11 in x 6 ft 6 in) |
| Three-seat sofa | 190 to 210 cm wide, 85 to 95 cm deep (6 ft 3 in to 6 ft 11 in, 2 ft 9 in to 3 ft 1 in) |
| Four-seat dining table | 120 x 75 cm (4 ft x 2 ft 6 in) |
| Six-seat dining table | 150 x 90 cm (5 ft x 3 ft) |

Sizes vary between makers, so measure the actual piece or the drawing your carpenter gives you.

## Around a bed

- **Side of the bed you get in from:** aim for 60 to 75 cm (2 ft to 2 ft 6 in). A comfortable minimum for one person is 45 cm (1 ft 6 in).
- **Foot of the bed:** aim for 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) if people walk past. If a wardrobe stands at the foot, the gap must also let its doors open, so measure from the door when it is open.
- **Wall side:** a bed pushed against a wall saves floor, but the person on that side climbs over. Fine for a child, awkward for a couple.
- **Wardrobe doors in front of the bed:** hinged doors need their full width as free floor. A 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) door needs 50 cm of clear space plus room to stand.

## Around a sofa and coffee table

- **Sofa to coffee table:** aim for 40 to 45 cm (1 ft 4 in to 1 ft 6 in). That is close enough to reach a cup and far enough to get your legs in.
- **Walking path behind or beside a sofa:** aim for 75 to 90 cm (2 ft 6 in to 3 ft). In a flat, 60 cm (2 ft) is a tight minimum for a path nobody uses with a tray or bags.
- **Sofa facing sofa:** a gap of 2.4 to 3.0 m (8 to 10 ft) between facing seats lets people talk without raising their voices. This is a rule of thumb.
- **Side tables:** keep them within arm's reach of the seat, about 30 to 45 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 6 in) from the sofa arm.

## Around a dining table

The chair is the problem, not the table.

- **Pull-out space:** a person needs about 90 cm (3 ft) from the table edge to the wall or next piece of furniture to sit down and stand up. A comfortable minimum where nobody walks behind is 75 cm (2 ft 6 in).
- **Walking behind seated people:** aim for 105 to 120 cm (3 ft 6 in to 4 ft). If the table is against a wall on one side, that side has no passing path and the person there sits in first.
- **Table to the wall on a side with chairs:** 90 cm (3 ft) is a good target.
- **Table to kitchen counter or fridge door:** keep clear of the fridge door swing, usually about 60 to 70 cm (2 ft to 2 ft 4 in) when open at 90 degrees.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) bedroom

Take a bedroom 3.0 m wide and 3.6 m long with a door on one short wall, a double bed 150 x 190 cm and a wardrobe 60 cm (2 ft) deep.

1. Put the headboard on a 3.0 m wall, centred. The room is 300 cm wide, the bed is 150 cm. That leaves 300 - 150 = 150 cm, or 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) on each side. This meets the target for both sides.
2. The room is 360 cm long. The bed takes 190 cm. That leaves 360 - 190 = 170 cm (5 ft 7 in) beyond the foot of the bed.
3. Put a wardrobe on the foot wall. It takes 60 cm. The remaining gap is 170 - 60 = 110 cm (3 ft 7 in).
4. Check door swing. A hinged wardrobe door about 50 cm wide opens into that gap. Standing room left: 110 - 50 = 60 cm (2 ft). This is tight but workable for one person. A sliding door would leave the full 110 cm.
5. Add a bedside table 35 cm (1 ft 2 in) wide on one side. That side now has 75 - 35 = 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) left, a little below the target. Put the table on one side only. Tables on both sides would leave 40 cm each, which is too tight.

The result: a double bed, a wardrobe with sliding doors and one narrow bedside table fit, but there is no space for a desk.

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Measure the room wall to wall, and note where doors and windows open.
- Mark the bed on the floor with tape or newspaper at its real size.
- Stand at the side of the bed and check you can step out and make the bed.
- Measure the open width of every wardrobe and cupboard door.
- Sit in each dining chair at the table and pull it back; mark the pull-out space.
- Walk your normal path with a bucket or a tray to test the gaps.
- Check that no clearance relies on a door that is open all day.
- Leave a path of at least 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) from the door to the main furniture.

## Common mistakes

- **Measuring the furniture but not the space it needs.** A table that fits the room may leave no space for the chairs.
- **Forgetting door swings.** A wardrobe door, a fridge door and a room door can all want the same piece of floor.
- **Pushing everything to the walls.** It leaves a big empty middle but no usable path. Pull a sofa 10 cm (4 in) from the wall if that helps a curtain or a cable.
- **Buying a larger bed than the room supports.** A king bed in a 3.0 m wide room leaves 60 cm on each side, with nothing else.
- **Ignoring how people really move.** Test with a bucket, a laundry basket or a tray, not with empty hands.

## FAQ

### How much space should I leave beside a double bed?
Aim for 60 to 75 cm (2 ft to 2 ft 6 in) on the side you get in from. A comfortable minimum for one person is 45 cm (1 ft 6 in).

### How far should a coffee table be from the sofa?
About 40 to 45 cm (1 ft 4 in to 1 ft 6 in). Closer and your knees hit it; further and you cannot reach it.

### How much room do dining chairs need?
About 90 cm (3 ft) from the table edge to the wall for sitting and standing. If people walk behind seated guests, aim for 105 to 120 cm (3 ft 6 in to 4 ft).

### Can I go below these numbers in a small flat?
Yes, in one place, for a path used by one person at a time. Use sliding doors, a smaller table or a folding chair to win space instead of squeezing every gap.

### Should the bed be against the wall?
It saves floor, but one person must climb over the other. For a couple, leave both sides open if the room allows.
