# Open living and dining: zoning without walls

Many flats and new houses have one hall that has to serve as both sitting room and dining room. After reading this you will be able to split that room into two clear zones, size each one with real numbers, and keep a walking path between them without building a partition.

## What a zone needs

A zone is an area with one job and enough floor for it. Two zones in one room work when each has its own furniture group, its own light and a path that does not cut through either.

### The dining zone

The table is only part of it. People sit and stand up, so the chairs need room behind them.

- **Table sizes:** a four-seater is about 120 x 75 cm (3 ft 11 in x 2 ft 6 in). A six-seater is about 150 x 90 cm (4 ft 11 in x 2 ft 11 in).
- **Chair room:** aim for 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) from the table edge to the wall or the next furniture, so a chair can be pulled out and a person can sit. A comfortable minimum is 60 cm (2 ft) if no one walks behind the chair.
- **Walking behind a seated person:** you need about 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) from the table edge to the wall if people pass behind them.
- **Table height:** about 75 cm (2 ft 6 in).
- **Light above the table:** a pendant hung at about 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) above the tabletop keeps glare out of eyes and still lights the table. Treat that as a rule of thumb, and check how it looks when someone stands.

### The living zone

- **Sofa depth:** about 90 cm (2 ft 11 in). A three-seater is about 200 cm (6 ft 7 in) wide.
- **Coffee table:** about 100 x 50 cm (3 ft 3 in x 1 ft 8 in), kept 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) from the sofa so you can reach it and still pass your legs.
- **Space between facing seats:** 140 to 250 cm (4 ft 7 in to 8 ft 2 in) is a common range for talking comfortably.
- **Rug:** a rug at least as wide as the sofa and large enough that the front legs of the sofa sit on it is the simplest living zone marker. Common sizes are 160 x 230 cm (5 ft 3 in x 7 ft 7 in) or 200 x 290 cm (6 ft 7 in x 9 ft 6 in).

## Ways to separate the zones

1. **Sofa back as a divider.** The simplest option. Put the sofa so its back faces the dining area. It is 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) deep and about 80 to 85 cm (2 ft 7 in to 2 ft 9 in) high, which defines the space without blocking light.
2. **Low shelf unit.** A unit up to about 110 cm (3 ft 7 in) high separates the zones and still lets you see across. Higher than that it begins to act as a wall and blocks daylight and air.
3. **Rug and ceiling light.** A rug under the sofa group and a pendant over the table show the two zones even when nothing stands between them.
4. **Floor or colour change.** A different tile or paint on one wall marks a zone. This is cosmetic and costs more or less depending on the surface.
5. **Curtain or sliding screen.** Useful if the dining area faces the kitchen or the main door and you want to hide it at times.

Anything that touches the ceiling or is built into the floor is more than furniture. Taking down a wall or putting up a fixed partition can affect structure, so use a licensed engineer before you do either.

## Worked example: a 3.6 x 6.0 m hall

The hall is 3.6 m (11 ft 10 in) wide and 6.0 m (19 ft 8 in) long. The front door is on one short wall and the kitchen opens off the other. A family of four needs a four-seater table and a three-seater sofa.

**Step 1: size the dining zone.** The table is 120 x 75 cm. Add 75 cm of chair room on every side.
- Length: 120 + 75 + 75 = 270 cm (8 ft 10 in).
- Width: 75 + 75 + 75 = 225 cm (7 ft 5 in).

**Step 2: size the living zone.** From the sofa back to the end wall: sofa 90 cm, gap 45 cm, coffee table 50 cm, gap 45 cm, a storage unit 40 cm deep against the wall.
- 90 + 45 + 50 + 45 + 40 = 270 cm (8 ft 10 in).

**Step 3: add the lengths.** 270 for dining plus 270 for living is 540 cm (17 ft 9 in). The hall is 600 cm, so 600 - 540 = 60 cm (2 ft) is left as a buffer between the dining chairs and the sofa back.

**Step 4: check the walking path.** The dining zone is 225 cm wide, and the hall is 360 cm wide. That leaves 360 - 225 = 135 cm (4 ft 5 in) along one side, a path wider than the 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) you need to walk through.

**Step 5: place the table.** Put the table along the length of the hall, close to the kitchen door so food does not travel through the sitting area. Hang a pendant above its centre.

**Step 6: set the sofa.** The sofa's back faces the dining zone, with the rug underneath. The two zones now read as separate without a wall.

## Smells, light and noise

- **Kitchen smells:** if the kitchen is open to the dining area, a chimney or exhaust fan and a window that opens are not optional. Ask your electrician and contractor what suits the hall.
- **Daylight:** keep tall furniture away from the window side so light reaches both zones.
- **Power cuts:** a rechargeable lamp or two on the dining table and sofa arm avoids moving around in the dark.
- **Heat and cold:** in the Terai, keep the dining table where a fan reaches it. In a hill winter, keep the sofa away from a cold window wall.
- **Shoes:** if the door opens straight into the hall, give the entrance its own shoe cabinet so the living zone stays clean.

## Checklist with a tape measure

1. Measure the room in two directions.
2. Mark the table and chair room on the floor with tape.
3. Mark the sofa and coffee table.
4. Walk the path along the side. Check 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) of clear floor.
5. Check the door and window swing do not cross either zone.
6. Check daylight reaches the table.

## Common mistakes

- **No chair room,** so people cannot sit down when the table is against the wall.
- **A tall divider** that blocks daylight and fans.
- **Walking path through the dining zone** because the table sits in the middle of the route.
- **Rug too small,** leaving the sofa legs half on and half off.
- **Zones that are the same size** in a room that cannot hold them. In a small hall, a fold-down or extendable table is the better choice.

## FAQ

### How much space does a four-seater dining table need?

About 270 x 225 cm (8 ft 10 in x 7 ft 5 in) once chair room is included.

### Can I divide the hall without a wall?

Yes. Use the sofa back, a low shelf, a rug and different lights.

### How high can a divider be?

A low unit of about 110 cm (3 ft 7 in) keeps the room open. Taller dividers block light and air.

### Where should the dining table go?

Near the kitchen, away from the front door and the main path through the room.

### Is it safe to remove a wall to make the hall open?

Only if a licensed engineer confirms the wall is not holding up the floor or roof above. Never assume.
