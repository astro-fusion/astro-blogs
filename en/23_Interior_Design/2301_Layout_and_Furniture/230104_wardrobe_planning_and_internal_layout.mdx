# Planning a wardrobe: width, depth and inside layout

A wardrobe is the biggest piece of furniture most people order from a carpenter, and it is the hardest to change later. After reading this you will be able to decide its depth, height and door type, lay out the inside to match your clothes, and give the carpenter measurements to build from.

The sizes below are common practice. Aim for them, and adjust to your clothes and your room.

## Start with what you own, not with the wall

Before you pick a width, sort your clothes into groups and count them.

- Shirts, kurtas and jackets: hang short, about 90 to 100 cm (3 ft to 3 ft 3 in) of drop.
- Dresses, long kurtas, coats: hang long, about 120 to 150 cm (4 ft to 4 ft 11 in) of drop.
- Sarees, trousers, sweaters, T-shirts: fold on shelves or in drawers.
- Bedsheets, blankets and quilts: bulky, top shelf or loft.
- Shoes: usually not in the wardrobe, but a low drawer or rack can hold a few pairs.

A rough planning figure is about 5 cm (2 in) of rod length for each garment on a hanger, and more for winter coats. A rod of 60 cm (2 ft) holds about 12 shirts.

## Depth

- **Hinged doors:** a depth of 55 to 60 cm (1 ft 10 in to 2 ft) lets a hanger sit sideways with room to spare. Hangers are about 40 to 45 cm (1 ft 4 in to 1 ft 6 in) wide.
- **Sliding doors:** add 8 to 10 cm (3 to 4 in) for the track and the door overlap, so plan about 65 cm (2 ft 2 in).
- **Shallow rooms:** a depth of 40 to 45 cm (1 ft 4 in to 1 ft 6 in) works if you hang clothes facing the door, but they stick out, so use sliding or a pull-out rail.

## Height

- Floor-to-ceiling wardrobes are common in flats. A height of 210 to 240 cm (6 ft 11 in to 7 ft 10 in) is typical.
- A person of average height reaches comfortably to about 190 cm (6 ft 3 in). Treat anything above that as a loft for rarely used items.
- Put a top rod no higher than about 170 to 180 cm (5 ft 7 in to 5 ft 11 in), so you can reach hangers without a stool.
- Leave a plinth, a low base, of 7 to 10 cm (3 to 4 in). It keeps the base off a damp floor and makes mopping easier.

## Doors: hinged or sliding

| | Hinged | Sliding |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | The full width is open at once | Only about half is open at a time on a two-door set |
| Floor space | Needs the door width as free floor | Needs none |
| Depth | 55 to 60 cm | About 65 cm |
| Cost | Low to medium | Medium to high |
| Good for | Roomy bedrooms | Narrow rooms, rooms where the bed is close |

Hinged doors wider than about 50 to 55 cm (1 ft 8 in to 1 ft 10 in) are heavy and sag over time. A tall door on cheap hinges is a common source of repair work.

## The inside: work in sections

Divide the width into sections, each about 60 to 90 cm (2 ft to 3 ft) wide. Narrower sections waste divider material. Wider sections can make shelves bend under weight.

- **Double short hang:** two rods, one above the other, each with about 100 cm drop. Doubles the rod length for shirts and kurtas.
- **Long hang:** one rod with 120 to 150 cm drop, for coats and dresses.
- **Shelves:** spacing about 30 to 35 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 2 in) for folded clothes. Closer shelves keep stacks short so you do not pull the whole pile.
- **Drawers:** 18 to 25 cm (7 to 10 in) high, for small items. Check the runner load with the carpenter.
- **Loft:** the top 30 to 50 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 8 in) for quilts, in a box.

## Damp, heat and the monsoon

In humid months, clothes in a closed wardrobe can smell musty. Leave a gap of 2 to 3 cm (1 in) between the back panel and an outer wall, and do not place a wardrobe against a wall with a leak or patch of damp. Fix the leak first; a wardrobe does not cure it. Small vent slots in the doors or sides help air move. Open the doors on dry days. If damp keeps returning, ask a contractor to find the source.

## Worked example: a wardrobe for a couple in a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) bedroom

The room has a double bed 150 x 190 cm with its head on a 3.0 m wall. The wardrobe goes along the 3.6 m wall beside the bed.

1. **Width available:** the room is 300 cm wide. The bed is 150 cm. The wardrobe is 60 cm deep with hinged doors. 300 - 150 - 60 = 90 cm (3 ft) remains between the bed and the wardrobe.
2. **Door check:** a hinged door about 52 cm wide opens into this space. 90 - 52 = 38 cm (1 ft 3 in) is left to stand in. That is tight. Choose sliding doors and the wardrobe grows to 65 cm deep: 300 - 150 - 65 = 85 cm (2 ft 9 in) remains, with no door swinging into it. This is better.
3. **Wardrobe size:** 210 cm wide, 65 cm deep, 230 cm high. It uses 210 of the 360 cm wall, leaving 150 cm for the door and a chair.
4. **Boards:** if the boards are 18 mm (3/4 in) thick and we want three sections, we need four boards across (two sides, two dividers): 4 x 1.8 = 7.2 cm. Usable width = 210 - 7.2 = 202.8 cm. Each section = 202.8 / 3 = 67.6 cm. Each rod holds about 67.6 / 5 = 13 garments.
5. **Section A, double short hang:** two drops of 100 cm plus about 10 cm of rod margin each: 2 x (100 + 10) = 220 cm, which fits in a clear height of about 226 cm. This holds 2 x 13 = 26 shirts and kurtas.
6. **Section B, shelves:** 226 / 30 = 7.5, so seven shelves at about 30 cm spacing, with some height left over. Use for sarees, trousers and sweaters.
7. **Section C, mixed:** long hang of 130 cm, two drawers of 20 cm each (40 cm), and a top shelf of 35 cm. 130 + 40 + 35 = 205 cm, leaving 226 - 205 = 21 cm for rod room. It holds 13 long garments and small items.
8. **Loft:** if you want a loft for quilts, reduce Section B shelves by one and add a 40 cm loft above 190 cm.

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Count your clothes by type and measure their hanging length.
- Measure the wall width at floor level and at 200 cm height.
- Mark the wardrobe depth on the floor and open a door in your mind.
- Check that skirting boards, switches and window sills do not collide with the wardrobe.
- Decide who reaches what: a child needs low shelves.
- Give the carpenter a drawing with width, depth, height, section widths and rod heights.
- Check the floor for slope with a spirit level before the carpenter builds.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing the width from the wall, not the clothes.** You end up with a lot of shelf and not enough rod.
- **Hinged doors in a tight room.** The door hits the bed.
- **Too shallow.** Under about 55 cm, hangers turn sideways and clothes crush.
- **No plinth.** The base sits on a damp floor.
- **Everything above reach.** The top rod or shelf is a stool job.

## FAQ

### What is the standard depth of a wardrobe?
There is no single standard. Aim for 55 to 60 cm (1 ft 10 in to 2 ft) with hinged doors, and about 65 cm (2 ft 2 in) with sliding doors.

### Sliding or hinged: which is better?
Sliding suits small rooms because no door swings out. Hinged shows the whole inside at once.

### How much hanging space do I need?
Count about 5 cm (2 in) of rod for each garment. A 60 cm (2 ft) rod holds about 12 shirts.

### How do I stop a wardrobe smelling in the monsoon?
Keep it off a damp wall, leave a small gap at the back, open it on dry days, and fix any leak.

### Can I build a wardrobe against any wall?
Check the wall for damp first. Do not cut into a wall to fit a niche without asking a licensed engineer.
