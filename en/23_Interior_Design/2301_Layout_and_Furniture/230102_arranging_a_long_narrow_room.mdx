# Arranging a long, narrow room: zones, paths and furniture

Many flats in Nepal and India have a room that is two or three times longer than it is wide. After reading this you will be able to split such a room into zones, keep one clear walking path, and check on paper that the furniture you plan to buy will fit.

The dimensions below are common practice. Aim for them, and adjust where your room demands it.

## What makes a narrow room hard

In a narrow room, furniture on both long walls leaves a corridor, and the room feels like a passage. The usual problem is not lack of floor area but where the walking path runs. If people must squeeze past a sofa or a bed to reach a door or window, the room feels smaller than it is.

Two ideas solve most cases: divide the length into zones, and keep one straight path along one side.

## Step 1: Find the real usable width

Measure the width at three points: near the door, in the middle and at the far end. Walls are rarely parallel. Use the smallest figure.

Then take off what already sits against the long walls: a window sill that projects, a radiator, an air conditioner unit, a pillar or a built-in cupboard. A room labelled 2.7 m (8 ft 10 in) wide may offer only 2.5 m (8 ft 2 in) in places.

## Step 2: Keep a path of 75 to 90 cm

Aim for a path 75 to 90 cm (2 ft 6 in to 3 ft) wide from the door to the far end, along one long wall. A comfortable minimum for one person is 60 cm (2 ft). If two people pass often, or someone carries things, stay near 90 cm.

Put big furniture on the other side of the path. Do not put the path in the middle: a central path splits the room into two strips too thin to use.

## Step 3: Divide the length into zones

Treat the room as two or three short rooms in a row, each about as long as it is wide.

- A room 2.7 m wide works well as zones of about 2.4 to 3.6 m (7 ft 10 in to 11 ft 10 in).
- Mark zone boundaries with a rug, a change in lighting, or the back of a sofa. A tall open bookshelf also works as a divider, but anything that touches the ceiling blocks daylight and air.
- Put the quietest use at the far end, away from the door.

## Step 4: Choose furniture sized to the width

| Furniture | Why it suits a narrow room |
|---|---|
| Three-seat sofa, 190 cm (6 ft 3 in) wide, 85 to 90 cm (2 ft 9 in to 2 ft 11 in) deep | Short enough to leave a path beside it |
| Shallow TV unit, 35 to 40 cm (1 ft 2 in to 1 ft 4 in) deep | A deep unit eats width |
| Desk 120 x 60 cm (4 ft x 2 ft) | Fits a gap of 240 cm along one wall |
| Wardrobe 60 cm (2 ft) deep with sliding doors | No door swing into the path |
| Folding or nesting tables | Come out when needed |

Pick pieces that sit along the long walls, not across the room. A bed in a narrow bedroom goes with its head on a short wall, so its length runs along the room.

## Worked example: a 2.7 x 6.0 m (8 ft 10 in x 19 ft 8 in) living room

The room has a door on one short wall and a window on the opposite short wall. The family wants a sofa and TV, and a small desk. The target is a path of about 80 cm along the right-hand long wall.

1. **Total length:** 600 cm. Split it into a sitting zone of 360 cm and a desk zone of 240 cm. Check: 360 + 240 = 600.
2. **Sitting zone, length:** a TV unit 40 cm deep on the window wall, a viewing gap of 230 cm, and a sofa 90 cm deep. 40 + 230 + 90 = 360 cm. This fills the zone.
3. **Sitting zone, width:** the sofa is 190 cm wide. 270 - 190 = 80 cm. Push the sofa to the left wall. The 80 cm gap on the right is the path (2 ft 7 in), inside the 75 to 90 cm target.
4. **Desk zone, width:** a desk 60 cm deep against the left wall, plus a chair pulled out 90 cm. 60 + 90 = 150 cm. 270 - 150 = 120 cm (3 ft 11 in) is left on the right, so the path is wider here and fine.
5. **Desk zone, length:** the desk is 120 cm long. 240 - 120 = 120 cm of wall remains for a shelf or a shoe cabinet 40 cm deep, which does not block the path.
6. **Door check:** the room door opens against the desk zone. Hang it to open against the wall, not toward the desk, and keep 60 cm free in front of the door.

The result: sofa, TV, desk and a clear path, with the whole room used and the walking line straight from door to window.

## Doors, windows and light

- Keep the window end free of tall furniture so daylight reaches the length of the room.
- A mirror on a long wall, opposite a window, bounces daylight further into the room.
- Light colours on the long walls and a slightly darker colour on the short end wall make the room feel less like a tube. This is a visual effect, not a measurement.
- In hot weather, a path down the middle of the room's length lets a breeze run from window to door. Do not block both ends with tall cupboards.

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Measure width at the door, middle and far end; note the smallest.
- Subtract window sills, pillars and radiators from the width.
- Draw the 75 to 90 cm path with chalk or tape from door to far end.
- Mark each zone with tape on the floor.
- Mark every big piece of furniture at real size and walk around it.
- Check each door and cupboard door swing against the path.
- Sit on the sofa and check the TV distance in your marked zone.
- Test the path carrying a bucket or a laundry basket.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting furniture along both long walls.** It turns the room into a corridor.
- **A central path.** It leaves two strips too thin to use.
- **A deep sofa or deep TV unit.** Each extra 10 cm of depth comes straight out of the path.
- **Hinged wardrobe doors facing the path.** Use sliding doors or leave room.
- **A tall divider across the full width.** It cuts light and air in half.

## FAQ

### How wide should the walking path be in a narrow room?
Aim for 75 to 90 cm (2 ft 6 in to 3 ft). A comfortable minimum for one person is 60 cm (2 ft).

### Should I put the sofa in the middle of a narrow room?
Usually no. Push it to one long wall so the path runs along the other side.

### How do I make a narrow room look wider?
Light colours on the long walls, a mirror opposite the window, and furniture that leaves floor visible. These change how the room looks, not its size.

### Where does the bed go in a narrow bedroom?
With the head on a short wall, so the length of the bed runs along the length of the room. Leave 60 to 75 cm (2 ft to 2 ft 6 in) on the side you get in from.

### Can I use a partition to divide the room?
A low open shelf or a rug can mark zones. A solid full-height partition changes light and air, and if it is fixed, ask a licensed engineer or your contractor whether it is safe.
