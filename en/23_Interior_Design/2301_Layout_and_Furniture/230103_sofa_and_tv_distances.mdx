# Sofa and TV: viewing distance, height and angle

Before you buy a TV or order a TV unit, you can work out how big a screen suits your room, how far away the sofa should be, and how high the screen should sit. After reading this you will be able to check all three with a tape measure and a little arithmetic.

Everything here is a rule of thumb, not a standard. Screens, eyesight and habits differ. Use the numbers as a starting point, then sit in the room and judge.

## Screen sizes in centimetres

TV sizes are quoted as the diagonal in inches. Most screens are a 16 to 9 shape. The table converts the common sizes.

| Diagonal | Diagonal in cm | Width | Height |
|---|---|---|---|
| 43 in | 109 cm (3 ft 7 in) | 95 cm (3 ft 1 in) | 54 cm (1 ft 9 in) |
| 50 in | 127 cm (4 ft 2 in) | 111 cm (3 ft 8 in) | 62 cm (2 ft 1 in) |
| 55 in | 140 cm (4 ft 7 in) | 122 cm (4 ft) | 69 cm (2 ft 3 in) |
| 65 in | 165 cm (5 ft 5 in) | 144 cm (4 ft 9 in) | 81 cm (2 ft 8 in) |

These are screen sizes. The frame and stand add a few centimetres, so check the maker's drawing before you cut a niche.

## How far from the sofa

A common rule of thumb: sit about 1.5 to 2.5 times the screen diagonal away. A closer distance, around 1.2 to 1.5 times, can suit a sharp high-resolution screen. A greater distance suits a person who likes to sit back.

Measure from your eyes to the screen. That is roughly the distance from the front edge of the sofa cushion to the screen, plus about 30 cm (1 ft) for leaning back.

For a 55 in screen with a diagonal of 140 cm, the rule gives:

- 1.5 x 140 = 210 cm (6 ft 11 in) as the nearest
- 2.5 x 140 = 350 cm (11 ft 6 in) as the furthest

If your room only allows about 2.0 m (6 ft 7 in), choose a 43 in or 50 in screen instead of a 65 in one.

## How high

Aim to have the middle of the screen about at eye level when you sit. For most adults on a sofa this is about 100 to 110 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 7 in) from the floor. A TV hung too high makes you tilt your head back through a long programme. If you must mount high, a bracket that tilts down helps a little.

Work it out like this. Take the middle of the screen at 105 cm. For a 55 in screen, half the height is 69 / 2 = 34.5 cm. The bottom edge of the screen then sits at 105 - 34.5 = 70.5 cm (2 ft 4 in) from the floor.

## Angle and seating position

- Aim to have the main seats within about 30 degrees of the straight line out from the screen. In practice, the sofa should roughly face the TV, not sit side-on.
- A side chair at a sharp angle sees a dim, stretched picture on many screens. Give the middle seats to the people who watch most.
- A sofa wider than the screen is fine, since people sit beside each other.

## Glare and daylight

A window behind the TV puts bright light in your eyes. A window facing the screen puts reflections on it. You can cure both without moving walls.

- Place the TV on a wall without a window, or at right angles to the window.
- Use curtains that block most of the light, or a second sheer layer for daytime.
- In a room with strong afternoon sun, check the screen at that hour, not only in the morning.
- Avoid a bright lamp directly behind the TV. A low, soft light on the wall behind is easier on the eyes in the evening.

## Cables, sockets and mounting

If you hang the TV on a wall, ask a competent fitter to choose a bracket and fixings for the wall material, whether brick, concrete or hollow board. Do not guess, because a heavy screen that falls is dangerous. Ask your electrician to put a socket and a cable route behind the TV, so no wires hang down. Do not drill into a wall without checking for pipes and wires. For anything involving a wall's strength or hidden wiring, use a licensed engineer or electrician.

During the monsoon, a damp outer wall is a poor place for a TV. Choose an inner wall if you can.

## Worked example: a 3.3 x 4.2 m (10 ft 10 in x 13 ft 9 in) living room

The TV unit sits on one short wall, and the sofa sits against the opposite short wall. The unit is 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) deep and the sofa is 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) deep and 210 cm wide.

1. **Room length:** 420 cm.
2. **Free floor between the TV unit and the sofa front:** 420 - 40 - 90 = 290 cm (9 ft 6 in).
3. **Eye distance:** add about 30 cm for leaning back against the sofa. 290 + 30 = 320 cm.
4. **Check against the rule for a 55 in screen:** the range is 210 to 350 cm, and 320 cm is inside it. A 43 in screen (109 cm) has a range of 1.5 x 109 = 164 cm to 2.5 x 109 = 273 cm, so 320 cm is beyond it and the picture would look small. Choose the 50 in or the 55 in.
5. **Width:** the room is 330 cm wide and the sofa is 210 cm. 330 - 210 = 120 cm is left. Push the sofa to one side wall and you keep one path of 120 cm, wider than the 75 to 90 cm target. Centred, you get 60 cm on each side, which is tight.
6. **Height:** the target bottom edge of a 55 in screen is 70.5 cm. A TV unit 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) high with the TV on its stand puts the bottom edge near 55 to 60 cm, which is low. Hang the TV on the wall with its bottom edge at 70 cm, or use a unit about 60 cm (2 ft) high.

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Measure from the sofa seat edge to where the TV will stand.
- Add 30 cm for leaning back, and compare with 1.5 to 2.5 times the screen diagonal.
- Sit on the sofa and measure your eye height from the floor.
- Mark the screen's size on the wall with painter's tape.
- Check the window light in the morning, at noon and in the evening.
- Check for glare from lamps behind the screen.
- Ask the electrician for a socket behind the TV.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying the biggest screen first.** Start from the distance, then pick the size.
- **Mounting too high.** The middle of the screen should be near eye level when you sit.
- **A window behind the TV.** It makes the picture hard to see in daytime.
- **Forgetting the depth of the unit.** Each 10 cm of depth takes 10 cm from the viewing distance.
- **Side seats at a sharp angle.** Put regular viewers in the middle.

## FAQ

### How far should my sofa be from a 55 inch TV?
As a rule of thumb, about 210 to 350 cm (6 ft 11 in to 11 ft 6 in).

### What is a good TV height from the floor?
Aim for the middle of the screen at about 100 to 110 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 7 in), which is eye level when you sit.

### Is a bigger TV always better?
No. If the room is short, a big screen makes you move your head. Choose the size to suit the distance.

### Can I put the TV opposite a window?
It is better at right angles to the window. If you cannot, use curtains to cut daytime glare.

### Can I mount the TV on any wall?
Not without checking the wall material and what is hidden inside. Ask a fitter and an electrician.
