# Where to put the bed, and what goes beside it

The bed is the largest piece of furniture in a bedroom, and everything else is arranged around it. After reading this you will be able to choose a wall for the bed, leave enough room to get in and out, pick side tables that suit the bed, and check your plan with a tape measure before you buy.

## Start with the four things that fix the bed's position

1. **The door.** Note where the door opens and how far it swings. The bed must not block the swing, and you should see a clear path from the door to the bed.
2. **The window.** Light and air come from here. A bed under a window is possible but needs thought, covered below.
3. **The longest solid wall.** Beds are usually headboard-first against a wall with no door or window on it.
4. **The wardrobe.** You need to open its doors fully and stand in front of it without touching the bed.

## Common bed sizes

| Bed | Size | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Single | 90 x 190 cm (2 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in) | Children and guest rooms |
| Double | 150 x 190 cm (4 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in) | Common in 10 x 12 ft rooms |
| Queen | 152 x 198 cm (5 ft x 6 ft 6 in) | A little longer |
| King | 180 x 198 cm (5 ft 11 in x 6 ft 6 in) | Needs a wider room |

Allow another 5 cm (2 in) at the head for a headboard, and more for a box bed with a deep frame. Measure the bed you plan to buy, not the mattress alone.

## Space to leave around the bed

These are rules of thumb, not standards.

- **Each side you use to get in or out:** aim for 75 cm (2 ft 6 in). A comfortable minimum is 60 cm (2 ft). Under 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) you will turn sideways to make the bed.
- **Foot of the bed to a wardrobe or wall:** aim for 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) for a path and 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) or more if you open wardrobe doors there.
- **Bed against one wall:** this saves floor space but one person climbs over the other. It can suit a single bed or a child's room; for two adults, keep both sides open.

## Side tables

A side table is a place for a phone, a glass of water and a lamp. Its size depends on the bed and the room.

- **Height:** about the same as the top of the mattress, or a little higher. For most beds that is 45 to 60 cm (1 ft 6 in to 2 ft). A table well above the pillow is awkward to reach from lying down.
- **Width and depth:** 35 to 45 cm (1 ft 2 in to 1 ft 6 in) is enough for a lamp and a glass. A bigger table suits a bigger room.
- **Lamp or switch:** place a lamp, or a switch for the main light, within arm's reach of the bed. If you want sockets beside the bed, tell your electrician where the bed and tables will stand before they fix the points. Leave the wiring to them.
- **Shelf instead of a table:** a narrow shelf at 50 to 60 cm (1 ft 8 in to 2 ft) from the floor saves floor space but cannot hold heavy items.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m bedroom

A bedroom is 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in) by 3.6 m (11 ft 10 in), about 10 x 12 ft. The door sits on one long wall near a corner and opens inwards. You want a double bed 150 x 190 cm, a wardrobe 60 cm deep and two side tables 40 x 40 cm.

**Step 1: choose the wall.** The headboard goes on a 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in) wall with no door or window. The bed then points into the room along the 3.6 m (11 ft 10 in) direction.

**Step 2: check the width.** 300 cm minus the bed's 150 cm leaves 150 cm. Centre the bed and each side gets 150 divided by 2 = 75 cm (2 ft 6 in). That meets the aim.

**Step 3: check the side tables.** Each table is 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) wide and sits at the head end. Floor free beside the table is 75 - 40 = 35 cm, and you step out at the side of the bed past the table, where the full 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) is open.

**Step 4: check the length.** The bed with its headboard takes 190 + 5 = 195 cm (6 ft 5 in). The room is 360 cm, so 360 - 195 = 165 cm (5 ft 5 in) is left at the foot.

**Step 5: place the wardrobe.** A wardrobe 60 cm (2 ft) deep on the opposite wall leaves 165 - 60 = 105 cm (3 ft 5 in) between bed foot and wardrobe doors. That is above the 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) needed to open doors and stand in front of them.

**Step 6: check the door.** A door opening inwards sweeps about 90 cm (2 ft 11 in). Draw that quarter circle on your sketch. If it touches the bed or the wardrobe, move the wardrobe to the wall beside the door or fit a sliding door.

The result: bed centred on the short wall, two small tables, wardrobe at the foot, and a clear path along one side to the door.

## Window, heat and damp

- **Under a window:** you get light but also a draught in a hill winter and sun on the pillow in a hot summer. A curtain or blind helps. If the window sill is low, make sure the window can still be opened and closed from the bed.
- **Against an outer wall in monsoon:** damp can show on cold or wet walls. Leave a gap of about 5 cm (2 in) behind the headboard and the wardrobe so air moves. If a wall stays wet, find the leak first. See a professional for seepage.
- **Under a ceiling fan:** keep the fan centred over the room, not the pillow end. Ask your electrician about fan height and fixing.

## Checklist with a tape measure

1. Measure the room and draw it with the door swing.
2. Measure the bed you plan to buy, with headboard.
3. Mark the bed on the floor with tape or newspaper.
4. Check 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) on each side you use and 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) at the foot.
5. Stand in front of the wardrobe doors fully open.
6. Check the table height against the mattress top.
7. Check the window and door open fully.

## Common mistakes

- **Bed pushed so close to a wall** that one person has to climb over.
- **Side tables taller than the pillow** so you reach up for the water glass.
- **A wardrobe door that hits the bed** when opened.
- **A bed jammed against a damp outer wall.**
- **Forgetting the bed frame**, which is wider than the mattress.

## FAQ

### How much space should be on each side of a double bed?

Aim for 75 cm (2 ft 6 in). A comfortable minimum is 60 cm (2 ft).

### Can the bed go in a corner?

Yes, in a small room, but one side is then against a wall. This suits a single bed or one sleeper.

### How high should a bedside table be?

About level with the top of the mattress, so 45 to 60 cm (1 ft 6 in to 2 ft) for most beds.

### Is a bed under a window all right?

It works, but plan for a curtain and think about draughts in winter and sun in summer.

### What if the room is too small for two tables?

Use one table or a shelf fixed to the wall at 50 to 60 cm (1 ft 8 in to 2 ft), and keep the walking side clear.
