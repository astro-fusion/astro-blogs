# Planning a modular kitchen: shapes, sizes and order of work

A modular kitchen is built from standard-width cabinets, so the plan is mostly a matter of adding widths. After reading this you will be able to choose a shape for your room, set sensible heights, and fit units along your walls with the arithmetic checked before you talk to the supplier.

## Start with the order of work

Food moves through a kitchen in a fixed order: store, wash, prepare, cook, serve. Lay the units out in that order and you walk less and cross your own path less. In most homes that means:

1. Fridge and dry store near the door, so groceries come in and go straight away.
2. Sink next, with a drainer beside it.
3. A clear prepare counter between the sink and the hob. This is the part most plans squeeze.
4. Hob after that, with the serving or plate area beyond it.

A comfortable prepare counter is 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) or more of free worktop between sink and hob. Anything under 60 cm (2 ft) means you chop on the drainer or the hob top.

## The four common shapes

| Shape | Needs a room about | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single run | 1.8 m (5 ft 11 in) wide, one long wall | Small flats, studios | Long walk between fridge and hob |
| Galley (two facing runs) | 2.1 to 2.4 m (6 ft 11 in to 7 ft 10 in) wide | Narrow rooms, one cook | Through traffic if both ends are open |
| L-shape | 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in) on both walls or more | Most 2BHK kitchens | The dead corner cabinet |
| U-shape | 2.4 m (7 ft 10 in) or more in both directions | Larger kitchens, two cooks | Too tight if the room is under 2.4 m (7 ft 10 in) |

These are rules of thumb.

## Sizes that carpenters and suppliers commonly use

- **Base unit depth:** 60 cm (2 ft), with the worktop overhanging slightly.
- **Wall unit depth:** 30 to 35 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 2 in).
- **Counter height:** 85 to 90 cm (2 ft 9 in to 2 ft 11 in). A simple check is to stand with your arms relaxed: the counter should sit roughly 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) below your bent elbow. A shorter cook should lean towards 85 cm (2 ft 9 in).
- **Gap between counter and wall units:** 45 to 60 cm (1 ft 6 in to 2 ft). Less than 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) hits your head and blocks a mixer or kettle.
- **Bottom of wall units from the floor:** therefore about 135 to 150 cm (4 ft 5 in to 4 ft 11 in).
- **Unit widths:** base units usually come in 30, 45, 60, 75, 90 and 120 cm steps (1 ft, 1 ft 6 in, 2 ft, 2 ft 6 in, 2 ft 11 in, 3 ft 11 in).
- **Fridge bay:** about 70 to 75 cm (2 ft 4 in to 2 ft 6 in) wide including a little air gap. Check the fridge's manual.
- **Floor space between facing runs:** aim for 100 to 120 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 11 in). Under 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) two people cannot pass.

The chimney or hood above the hob has its own mounting height, which the maker states in the manual. Follow it. For a gas hob, ask the gas installer about the cylinder or pipe position and how far the hob must sit from walls and cabinets. Do not decide that yourself.

## Worked example: an L-shaped kitchen in a 2.4 x 3.0 m room

Take a kitchen of 2.4 m (7 ft 10 in) by 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in), with the door at one end of the long wall.

**Step 1: find the usable worktop length.** The long run along the 3.0 m wall can use all 300 cm. The short run along the 2.4 m wall loses 60 cm to the corner, which the long run already covers: 240 - 60 = 180 cm (5 ft 11 in). Total run: 300 + 180 = 480 cm (15 ft 9 in).

**Step 2: fill the long run in order of work, starting at the door.**

| Unit | Width |
|---|---|
| Fridge bay | 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) |
| Dry store base unit | 60 cm (2 ft) |
| Sink unit with drainer | 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) |
| Prepare counter | 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) |

Add them: 75 + 60 + 90 + 75 = 300 cm. The run is exactly full.

**Step 3: fill the short run.** The hob unit takes 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) and a plate and spice unit takes the other 90 cm: 90 + 90 = 180 cm. Also full.

**Step 4: check the floor.** The room's clear floor is the room minus the 60 cm counters on two sides: 240 - 60 = 180 cm (5 ft 11 in) one way and 300 - 60 = 240 cm (7 ft 10 in) the other. That is well above the 100 to 120 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 11 in) you need to move and open oven or cabinet doors.

**Step 5: check the order.** The prepare counter has 75 cm (2 ft 6 in), a little under the 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) aim. Shrink the dry store unit to 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) and widen the prepare counter to 90 cm (2 ft 11 in): 75 + 45 + 90 + 90 = 300 cm. The total still works.

## The corner

The dead corner of an L or U kitchen is awkward to reach. Choose a carousel or pull-out unit (high cost, easy reach) or a plain corner for things you seldom need (low cost). Decide before the carpenter starts, because it changes the neighbouring widths.

## Local conditions to plan for

- **Monsoon damp:** keep the carcass of base units a few centimetres off the floor on legs or a plinth, and do not let the back panel touch a damp wall. Plywood with a moisture-resistant grade is common; ask your carpenter what they use.
- **Heat in the plains:** keep the fridge away from the hob and leave the air gap the maker asks for.
- **Power cuts:** ask your electrician how the fridge will be kept running, and leave all wiring to them.
- **Moving a sink or gas point, or opening a wall for a hatch:** involves plumbing, gas or structure. Use a licensed plumber, gas installer or engineer.

## Checklist with a tape measure

1. Measure each wall at floor, waist and ceiling height and note the smallest.
2. Mark the door swing, window sill and any pipe or beam on a sketch.
3. Add up the unit widths for each wall and compare with the wall length.
4. Check 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) of floor between facing counters.
5. Check the counter height against your elbow.
6. Ask for the fridge, hob and chimney manuals before the sizes are fixed.

## Common mistakes

- **No prepare counter.** The sink and hob end up side by side with nowhere to chop.
- **Fridge door hits a wall** and cannot open fully.
- **Counter height copied** without checking it against the main cook.
- **Wall units hung too low,** so the cook hits their head.
- **Ignoring the corner,** leaving a cabinet that swallows things you cannot reach.

## FAQ

### What is the smallest width for a galley kitchen?

Two 60 cm (2 ft) counters and a 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) gap give 210 cm (6 ft 11 in). Between 100 and 120 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 11 in) is more comfortable if two people work.

### How high should the counter be?

Most standard kitchens use 85 to 90 cm (2 ft 9 in to 2 ft 11 in). Choose by your own elbow height and the main cook, not the average.

### How high above the counter should wall units go?

Aim for 45 to 60 cm (1 ft 6 in to 2 ft) between counter and the bottom of the wall unit. The hob area follows the chimney maker's instructions.

### Can I move the sink to another wall?

Often yes, but it means moving water supply and drain lines. Ask a licensed plumber what is possible and what it costs before fixing the layout.
