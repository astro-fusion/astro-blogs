# Door swings and walking paths: planning how you move

A room can have enough floor and still be awkward to live in, because a door hits a bed or a passage is too narrow for a laundry basket. After reading this you will be able to draw door swings and walking paths on a plan, and place furniture so that people can move without squeezing.

The sizes below are common practice. Aim for them, and adjust to your home.

## Door sizes in common use

| Door | Typical width |
|---|---|
| Main entrance | 100 to 105 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 5 in) |
| Bedroom | 80 to 90 cm (2 ft 8 in to 2 ft 11 in) |
| Bathroom | 70 to 75 cm (2 ft 4 in to 2 ft 6 in) |
| Kitchen | 80 to 90 cm (2 ft 8 in to 2 ft 11 in) |

These are frame widths. The clear opening is about 5 cm (2 in) less. If someone in the family uses a walking frame or a wheelchair, aim for a clear opening of at least 80 cm (2 ft 7 in), and ask a professional before you change any opening.

## The swing is part of the door

A hinged door sweeps a quarter circle with a radius equal to its width. An 80 cm (2 ft 7 in) door needs a quarter circle of 80 cm of floor to open fully. Nothing that must stay in place, such as a bed or a table, should stand inside it.

Plan the swing in three steps:

- Mark the hinge side. The door should open against the nearest wall, not across the room.
- Draw the quarter circle on the plan.
- Keep a 60 cm (2 ft) patch of free floor beside the door where a person can stand to open it.

## Inward or outward

- **Inward into a room:** common for bedrooms. It keeps the passage clear but takes room space.
- **Outward into a corridor:** saves room space, but it may hit anyone walking by. Use only where the corridor is wide.
- **Sliding or pocket doors:** save the swing, but need clear wall space and good hardware. Cost is medium to high.
- **Bathroom doors:** outward opening saves a few centimetres inside a small bathroom, but check that the corridor is wide enough. Also check the door can be opened from outside in an emergency.

Do not change a door's position or widen an opening in a wall without asking a licensed engineer. The wall around a door may carry load.

## Walking paths, also called circulation

Circulation is the route you walk through the home, from door to door. Aim for these widths:

- **Main passage or corridor:** 90 to 105 cm (2 ft 11 in to 3 ft 5 in). A comfortable minimum is 75 cm (2 ft 6 in).
- **Path inside a room, used by one person:** 60 to 75 cm (2 ft to 2 ft 6 in).
- **Path where two people pass:** 105 to 120 cm (3 ft 5 in to 3 ft 11 in).
- **Turning space for a wheelchair:** about 150 cm (5 ft) across. This is a guide, not a rule.

Keep the main paths short and straight. A path that zigzags round a sofa and a table every time costs space and bangs shins.

## Special paths in an Indian or Nepali home

- **Shoes at the door:** a shoe cabinet 30 to 35 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 2 in) deep needs about 90 cm (3 ft) of standing space in front. Plan a landing of about 100 x 100 cm (3 ft 3 in x 3 ft 3 in) at the entrance, so people can stand and take off shoes without blocking the door.
- **Wet bathroom:** leave a dry strip outside the door for a mat, so wet feet do not cross the bedroom.
- **Puja space:** keep it away from the main path, so nobody walks across it while someone sits there.
- **Joint family:** more people use the same passages at the same time, usually mornings. Add 15 to 30 cm (6 in to 1 ft) to your path widths if you can.
- **Power cuts:** a passage with no window gets dark. Keep it free of furniture so people can walk it by torchlight.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) bedroom

The door is 80 cm wide, set in the 3.0 m wall, 15 cm (6 in) from the right-hand corner, opening inward, hinged on the right so it swings against the right side wall. The double bed is 150 x 190 cm, with its head on the opposite 3.0 m wall.

1. **Door swing:** radius 80 cm. The quarter circle covers the floor from the door out to 80 cm into the room, near the right wall.
2. **Room length left after the bed:** the room is 360 cm long. The bed is 190 cm. So 360 - 190 = 170 cm remains between the door wall and the foot of the bed.
3. **Beyond the swing:** 170 - 80 = 90 cm (2 ft 11 in). So there is a clear gap of 90 cm between the open door and the foot of the bed, meeting the 75 to 90 cm target.
4. **Path to the bed:** the bed is 150 cm wide, centred, with 75 cm on each side. 75 cm on each side of the bed is the path to the bed, matching the target.
5. **Wardrobe:** if the wardrobe goes on the left side wall, 60 cm deep with sliding doors. The width available to walk is 300 - 60 = 240 cm across the room, minus the bed 150 = 90 cm, so 90 cm remains between the wardrobe and the bed. With hinged doors of 50 cm, the standing room would be 90 - 50 = 40 cm, which is too tight, so slide them.
6. **Check:** nothing stands inside the quarter circle, the path from the door to the bed side is straight, and the wardrobe opens without blocking it.

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Measure each door width and write down the hinge side.
- Draw the quarter circle of every door on the plan, or mark it on the floor with tape.
- Check that each swing is clear of beds, tables and cupboard doors.
- Walk each main route and measure the narrowest point.
- Check that two doors do not hit each other when both are open, such as the bathroom and bedroom.
- Check the entrance has a landing for shoes.
- Carry a laundry basket or a bucket along each route.

## Common mistakes

- **Drawing furniture without the door swing.** It looks fine on paper and fails on the first day.
- **Hinges on the wrong side.** The door opens across the room, not against the wall. It is cheap to change before fitting.
- **A shoe cabinet blocking the entrance.** Put it to the side with a landing in front.
- **Two doors hitting each other.** Check bathroom and bedroom doors, and wardrobe doors beside a room door.
- **Narrow main passages.** A corridor below 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) is hard to use with bags.

## FAQ

### How wide should a bedroom door be?
Common practice is 80 to 90 cm (2 ft 8 in to 2 ft 11 in), and the clear opening is about 5 cm (2 in) less.

### Should bathroom doors open inward or outward?
Inward keeps the corridor clear, but outward saves bathroom space. Choose outward only if the corridor is wide, and make sure the door can be opened from outside.

### How wide should a corridor be in a flat?
Aim for 90 to 105 cm (2 ft 11 in to 3 ft 5 in). A comfortable minimum is 75 cm (2 ft 6 in).

### Can I move a door to make room for a bed?
Moving a door or widening an opening may affect the wall. Ask a licensed engineer first.

### What if I cannot fit the door swing?
Try sliding doors, flip the hinge side, or choose a smaller piece of furniture near the door.
