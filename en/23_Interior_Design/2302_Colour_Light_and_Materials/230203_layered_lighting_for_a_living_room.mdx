# Layered Lighting for a Living Room: Ambient, Task, Accent

One ceiling light in the middle of the room is the usual setup, and it is rarely enough. After reading this you will be able to split your living room light into three layers, place each at a sensible height, and check the total brightness with a simple sum.

## The three layers

**Ambient light** is the general light that lets you move around and see the room. It usually comes from the ceiling, but it can also come from lamps that bounce light off a wall.

**Task light** is focused light for something you do: reading, sewing, homework, a card game. It sits close to the activity and points at it.

**Accent light** picks out something on purpose: a shelf, a painting, the puja corner, a plant or the wall behind the TV. It is lower in brightness and adds depth.

You do not need all three in the same strength. Ambient does the heavy lifting, task is strong in small spots, and accent is gentle.

## Heights that work

Common practice, not a rule:

- **Ceiling lights.** Fixed at the ceiling, usually 2.7 to 3.0 m (8 ft 10 in to 9 ft 10 in) high in Indian and Nepali flats. Spread several small lights across the room rather than a single large one, so no area is left in shadow.
- **Floor lamp for reading.** The bottom edge of the shade should sit below your eye level when you are seated, about 100 to 110 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 7 in) from the floor, so you do not see the bare bulb. Overall height is often 140 to 160 cm (4 ft 7 in to 5 ft 3 in).
- **Table lamp.** Total height of about 55 to 65 cm (1 ft 10 in to 2 ft 2 in) on a side table of about 50 cm (1 ft 8 in), so the shade is near eye level when seated.
- **Reading light distance.** Place it about 60 to 90 cm (2 ft to 3 ft) from the book, to one side so it does not cast your own shadow on the page.
- **Accent lights.** Keep the light source hidden or well shielded so it does not shine into the eyes of anyone sitting down.

## Glare and the television

A bright ceiling light next to a dark TV screen creates a reflection and tiring contrast. Two simple fixes:

- Turn off the strongest ceiling light when you watch, and keep a lower ambient layer on.
- Add a soft light behind or beside the TV, aimed at the wall rather than at you.

Avoid placing a lamp or window directly opposite the screen.

## Keep every layer on its own switch

If all the lights are on one switch, you only have two choices: bright or dark. Ask your electrician to group them in layers: ceiling lights on one switch, lamps on a socket you can switch separately or on a second switch, accent on a third. Wiring and switch planning are the electrician's job. What you can do is tell them how you use the room.

## Colour of light

Use one family of lamp tone in the room. Warm white at 2700 to 3000 K suits most living rooms. If your family reads or sews in the room, a task lamp of about 4000 K is acceptable close to the work, but keep it small and aimed at the page.

## Worked example: a 3.6 x 4.5 m living room

Take a living room of 3.6 x 4.5 m (11 ft 10 in x 14 ft 9 in) with a sofa on the long wall, a TV opposite, one armchair by the window and a puja corner shelf.

**Step 1: floor area.** 3.6 x 4.5 = 16.2 m² (about 174 sq ft).

**Step 2: target ambient light.** Aim for about 150 lumens per m² (about 14 per sq ft). 16.2 x 150 = 2,430 lumens.

**Step 3: build the ambient layer.**

- Four small ceiling lights of 450 lumens each: 4 x 450 = 1,800 lumens.
- Two lamps of 350 lumens each at the sofa ends: 2 x 350 = 700 lumens.
- Total: 1,800 + 700 = 2,500 lumens. That meets the target of 2,430.

**Step 4: task light.** One floor lamp beside the armchair, with a shade bottom at about 105 cm (3 ft 5 in) from the floor and a reading output of about 400 lumens. This is extra to the ambient total, and it is on only when someone reads.

**Step 5: accent light.** A small, warm light on the puja shelf and a soft light on the wall behind the TV, about 150 lumens each. Keep a diya or flame away from curtains and cloth.

**Step 6: switch plan to give the electrician.** Switch A for the four ceiling lights, switch B for the two sofa lamps, switch C for the accent lights. The floor lamp plugs into its own socket.

With this plan, the evening setting is lamps and accent only: 700 + 300 = 1,000 lumens, which is enough for talking and TV. The full ambient setting is for guests or cleaning.

## A short checklist

- List what happens in the room: TV, reading, guests, prayer, homework.
- Measure the floor area and set an ambient target.
- Add up the lumens printed on each lamp.
- Place a task lamp wherever someone reads or sews.
- Put one accent light on a feature you like.
- Keep the same K family on every lamp.
- Group lights in layers on separate switches, and ask an electrician to wire them.
- Check the TV screen for reflections at night.

## Common mistakes

- **A single central light.** It leaves the corners dim and your own head casts a shadow on the page.
- **Bare bulbs at eye level.** The glare is uncomfortable. Use a shade or place the lamp lower or higher.
- **Task lamp behind the reader.** It casts a shadow of your hand across the book.
- **All lights on one switch.** You lose the lower, softer evening setting.
- **Mixed bulb tones.** Wall and furniture colours look uneven.

## FAQ

### How many lights does a living room need?

It depends on size and use. The sum above shows a method: set a lumen target for the room and add lamps until you reach it.

### Is a chandelier enough?

A chandelier is mostly decorative and often gives poor light across a room. Pair it with lamps for reading and a softer evening level.

### Where should a floor lamp go?

Beside the chair or sofa end where someone reads, slightly behind and to one side of the shoulder.

### Can I use cove lighting?

Yes, but the ceiling cove and its wiring are a job for a carpenter and an electrician together. Ask for the light source to be hidden from view.

### Should the TV room be dark?

A completely dark room makes a bright screen tiring. Keep a low, soft light on in the room.

### What if I have power cuts?

Keep one lamp or a small battery lamp in the living room, and ask your electrician about inverter circuits for a few ceiling lights.
