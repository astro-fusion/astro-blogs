# Making a Low Ceiling Feel Higher: Practical Fixes

Many flats and older houses in Nepal and India have ceilings of 2.4 m (7 ft 10 in) or less, and the room can feel close overhead. You cannot raise the slab, but you can change where the eye stops. After reading this you will know what to do with paint, curtains, lights and furniture, and what to avoid.

## What actually works

The eye reads height from the longest unbroken vertical line and from how much of the wall it can see. So the aim is to show more wall and fewer horizontal breaks. Nothing here changes the real height. It changes how the room looks.

### Paint

- Paint the ceiling a light, matt colour. A pale ceiling reflects daylight and lamp light back into the room, so the top of the wall looks less heavy.
- Take the wall colour up to the ceiling with no border or band. A line at the top of the wall cuts the height visibly.
- Avoid a dark ceiling in a small room unless the ceiling is already above 2.7 m (8 ft 10 in).
- Vertical stripes draw the eye upward, but they are busy. Use them on one wall only, or use a subtle tone-on-tone stripe.

### Curtains

Hang the rail or track high, close to the ceiling, and not on the top of the window frame. The curtain then covers a long vertical line from ceiling to floor. Let the curtain fall to about 1 cm (half an inch) above the floor, or just touch it.

### Lighting

- Use flush or slim ceiling lights. A hanging light that drops 40 cm leaves little headroom.
- A pendant over walkways should have its lowest point at least 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in) above the floor, so a tall person does not hit it.
- Wall lights and a floor lamp that shine up onto the ceiling make the ceiling look further away. Ask your electrician about wall points in the right places.
- For a ceiling fan in a low room, ask your electrician about a low-profile fan that sits close to the ceiling. Do not shorten the rod yourself, and do not pick a fan without the electrician confirming the blade height from the floor.

### Furniture

- Choose low furniture. A bed with a low frame, a sofa with a seat height of about 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) and low back, and a low TV unit leave more wall visible above them.
- Run tall pieces to the ceiling. A wardrobe 2.1 m high leaves a 30 cm gap that collects dust and looks unfinished. A box above it, in the same finish, lets the wall line run up in one piece.
- Hang pictures and mirrors in a tall, narrow shape, and keep the centre near 145 to 150 cm (4 ft 9 in to 4 ft 11 in) from the floor.

### Floors and walls

A light, continuous floor with no strong cross pattern lets the room look open. A mirror on a side wall, away from the window, also pulls the eye along the wall and adds depth.

## Worked example: curtains in a 2.4 m room

Take a bedroom 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) with a ceiling at 2.40 m. The window is 1.5 m wide, and its top edge is at 2.05 m.

**Step 1: where the rail goes.** If the rail goes at the window top, the curtain starts at 2.05 m. That leaves 2.40 minus 2.05 = 0.35 m of bare wall above it, a horizontal break.

**Step 2: high rail.** Place the rail 5 cm below the ceiling, at 2.35 m. The curtain now starts at 2.35 m, a rise of 2.35 minus 2.05 = 0.30 m of visible fabric.

**Step 3: curtain length.** The curtain should fall to 1 cm above the floor, so the finished drop is 2.35 minus 0.01 = 2.34 m.

**Step 4: rail width.** The rail should extend 20 cm past each side of the window so the open curtain does not cover the glass. 1.5 + 0.2 + 0.2 = 1.9 m.

**Step 5: fabric.** For soft folds, the fabric is about twice the rail width: 1.9 x 2 = 3.8 m. Add a hem of 10 cm and a heading of 10 cm to the drop: 2.34 + 0.20 = 2.54 m per cut length. With fabric 1.4 m wide, 3.8 / 1.4 = 2.7, so three widths. A pair of matching curtains needs an even number, so use four widths. Total fabric: 4 x 2.54 = 10.16 m, so ask for about 10.2 m.

Before you buy, check that a high rail does not run into a ceiling beam, a curtain box, or an AC unit. Hold the rail up with tape and look.

## Checklist

- Measure the real height at three points, as floors and ceilings are not always level.
- Mark the rail position with tape and step back.
- Choose a pale matt ceiling colour.
- Remove or avoid any border or band at the top of the wall.
- Check the lowest point of each light is at least 2.1 m from the floor over walkways.
- Ask your electrician about a low-profile fan.
- Measure the gap above tall furniture and plan to close it.

## Common mistakes

- **Dark or glossy ceiling in a small room.** It shows every stain and feels heavy.
- **A deep false ceiling for lights.** Each false ceiling takes at least 10 to 15 cm, so use slim lights instead, or keep it to a narrow strip at the edge. Ask a contractor before building one.
- **Short curtains.** Curtains that stop at the window top or hang well above the floor make the room look shorter.
- **A big chandelier.** It dominates a low room and may be a head hazard.
- **Painting a stripe at mid-wall height.** Horizontal lines cut the wall into slabs.

## FAQ

### Does a white ceiling make the room feel taller?
A pale, matt ceiling reflects light, which helps. It does not change the real height.

### Should I use a false ceiling in a low room?
Usually not. It reduces the height. If you need hidden wiring or ducts, keep the drop small and ask a contractor.

### Can I use a ceiling fan in a room of 2.4 m?
Often yes, with a low-profile model, but ask an electrician about the blade clearance and the safe fitting.

### What curtain colour is best?
One close to the wall colour. A strong contrast cuts the wall into strips.

### Do mirrors help?
A mirror on a side wall can make the room look deeper. Hang it so that it reflects light or a pleasant view, and not a clutter pile.
