# Wood, Plywood and Laminates: What Furniture Is Made Of

Your carpenter will ask whether you want plywood, MDF or solid wood, and what finish goes on top. After reading this you will know what each material does well, which thickness to ask for, and how to count the sheets a wardrobe needs.

## The materials in plain words

**Solid wood** is sawn timber. It looks and ages well and can be sanded and repaired. It also swells in the monsoon and shrinks in dry winters, so wide solid boards can warp or split. It costs more than the boards below, and it is best for frames, legs, doors and edges.

**Plywood** is thin layers of wood glued with the grain crossing. The crossing layers keep it flat and strong. Grades differ in the glue used and in how many layers there are. Ask for a grade made for moisture where water may reach it.

**Block board** has a core of wood strips between two veneers. It is lighter than plywood and is often used for long shelves and table tops, but it can sag over long spans if it is thin.

**MDF** is fine wood fibre pressed with glue. It is smooth and takes paint well, so it suits carved doors and painted panels. It is heavy, holds screws less firmly than plywood, and swells badly if water gets into a cut edge. Keep it away from sinks and bathrooms.

**Particle board** is wood chips pressed with glue. It is the cheapest board. It is acceptable for dry, light-duty items such as a headboard back, but it holds screws poorly and does not recover from wetting.

## Surface finishes

A board is rarely left bare. These are the common skins.

| Finish | What it is | Good for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laminate | A thin plastic-resin sheet glued on, about 0.8 to 1.0 mm | Wardrobes, kitchen shutters, table tops | Edges lift if glue is poor or water reaches them |
| Veneer | A thin slice of real wood, under 1 mm | Wood look at medium cost | Needs polish, scratches show |
| Paint or PU | Sprayed coats on MDF or wood | Smooth modern doors | Chips at corners, needs a good primer |
| Acrylic or similar gloss sheet | A glossy sheet on the shutter | Modular kitchen fronts | Shows fingerprints and fine scratches |

Edges matter as much as faces. Ask for the edge band to be glued and pressed on all exposed edges, and ask for the same band on the inside of kitchen and bathroom units where water can splash.

## Thickness: what to ask for

These are common practice figures. Your carpenter may suggest slightly different ones.

- **Wardrobe sides, top, bottom and dividers:** 18 mm (about 3/4 in).
- **Shelves:** 18 mm if the span is up to about 80 cm (2 ft 8 in). For a longer span, add a support in the middle or use 25 mm.
- **Back panel:** 6 mm, fixed into a groove or screwed at close spacing. It keeps the box square.
- **Kitchen base units:** 18 mm for the box. Many people use a moisture-resistant grade for the base units only.
- **Doors and shutters:** 18 mm. Doors wider than about 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) on thin board may bow.
- **Table top for a desk:** 25 mm, or 18 mm with a rail under the front edge.

A full sheet is usually 8 x 4 ft, which is 2440 x 1220 mm. A sheet of 18 mm board is heavy, so ask whether it will go up the staircase or in the lift before it is ordered.

## Worked example: a three-door wardrobe

Take a wardrobe 1.80 m wide, 2.10 m high and 0.56 m deep (6 ft x 6 ft 11 in x 1 ft 10 in), made of 18 mm board, with two vertical dividers and six shelves.

**Step 1: the sides.** Two sides, each 2.10 x 0.56 m.
2 x 2.10 x 0.56 = 2.352 square metres.

**Step 2: top and bottom.** They sit between the sides, so each is 1.80 minus two thicknesses of 0.018 = 1.764 m wide.
2 x 1.764 x 0.56 = 1.976 square metres.

**Step 3: two dividers.** Each is as high as the space between top and bottom, 2.10 minus 0.036 = 2.064 m.
2 x 2.064 x 0.56 = 2.312 square metres.

**Step 4: six shelves.** The inside width is 1.764 m. Two dividers take 0.036 m, leaving 1.728 m. Three equal bays give 1.728 / 3 = 0.576 m each.
6 x 0.576 x 0.56 = 1.935 square metres.

**Step 5: total for 18 mm board.**
2.352 + 1.976 + 2.312 + 1.935 = 8.575 square metres.

**Step 6: count the sheets.** One sheet is 2.44 x 1.22 = 2.977 square metres.
8.575 / 2.977 = 2.88, so three sheets are the least possible. Parts do not fit a sheet perfectly, and the grain or pattern may need to run one way. Ask for a cutting plan from the carpenter. Budget for three or four sheets.

**Step 7: the back.** 6 mm back panel is 1.80 x 2.10 = 3.78 square metres. 3.78 / 2.977 = 1.27, so two sheets.

You have not counted doors, laminate sheets or edge band yet. Ask the carpenter to list these separately, so you can compare quotes.

## Checklist

- Write down the board type and thickness for each part.
- Ask which grade of plywood or MDF is used, and whether it is moisture-resistant where needed.
- Confirm the laminate thickness and that the edge band is on all visible edges.
- Ask for a cutting plan or sheet count before work starts.
- Check that shelf spans over 80 cm have a support.
- Ask what hardware is fitted, and whether hinges are soft close or plain.
- See a sample of the laminate in daylight, not in the shop only.

## Common mistakes

- **Using MDF near water.** One wet edge and it swells and does not shrink back.
- **Thin shelves with long spans.** A shelf of books on 12 mm board over 1 m will sag.
- **Skipping the back panel or using a thin one.** The wardrobe racks sideways and doors stop closing.
- **Ignoring humidity.** In monsoon-damp cities and the Terai, keep wardrobes a few centimetres off an outer wall and leave a gap at the base for air.
- **Judging only by the face.** The inside, the edges and the hardware decide how long the piece lasts.

## FAQ

### Is plywood better than MDF?
Neither is better everywhere. Plywood holds screws and handles damp better, so use it for carcasses and kitchens. MDF is smoother for painted or carved fronts.

### Do I need solid wood for a wardrobe?
No. Boards do the job. Use solid wood where you see and touch it, such as a frame or handle.

### What does laminate thickness mean?
The thickness is the sheet itself, commonly 0.8 to 1.0 mm. A thicker sheet resists chips better, but good glue and edge banding matter just as much.

### How do I know the plywood is the grade I asked for?
Ask to see the stamp or the invoice from the dealer, and ask the carpenter to name the grade on your quote.

### Can I fix a swollen edge?
A little swelling on a dry edge may be sanded and resealed. If the core is soft and crumbling, replace that part.
