# The right order of work in a home renovation

Doing jobs in the wrong order means breaking new work to finish old work. After reading this you will know the usual order, why each step comes where it does, and how to turn it into a plan with days and buffers for your own home.

## The principle: messy and wet first, clean and delicate last

Every job either makes dust and water or must be kept clean. Do the dusty, wet, loud jobs first, and the clean ones last. Work from the inside out and from the top down: ceilings before walls, walls before floors.

## The usual order

1. **Plan and measure.** Decide what you will change. Measure each room. Choose tiles, paint and fittings before work starts, because some take time to arrive.
2. **Strip and demolish.** Remove old tiles, plaster, fittings, false ceilings and cupboards you are replacing. Anything about walls, beams, slabs or balconies is a question for a licensed engineer before anyone picks up a hammer.
3. **Services behind the walls.** Plumbing and electrical routes and points are fixed now, by a licensed plumber and electrician. This is the moment to place sockets, switches and pipes where you want them. Later changes mean opening finished walls. Photograph everything before it is closed.
4. **Repair and plaster.** Fill chases, fix cracks, plaster uneven walls. Let new plaster dry as its supplier advises.
5. **Waterproofing in wet areas.** Bathrooms, balconies and terraces are waterproofed by a specialist, tested, and given time to cure before tiles go on. Do not skip this.
6. **Tiling and flooring.** Floor tiles in wet areas first, then wall tiles. For living rooms, hard flooring goes in before paint, and protect it afterwards. Allow grout to set.
7. **Ceilings.** False ceiling frames and boards are fixed, and light points are placed, before painting.
8. **Carpentry.** Doors, frames, wardrobes and modular units come after wet work and before final paint, so the carpenter can fit and scribe to finished walls, then touch up.
9. **Putty and primer.** Smooth the walls and ceiling, sand, dust and prime.
10. **Paint.** Ceiling first, then walls, then woodwork. Two coats, with each coat dry before the next.
11. **Fittings and finishing.** Switch plates, light fixtures, taps, mirrors, curtain rods, door handles.
12. **Deep clean, then furniture.** Clean last, then bring furniture and curtains in.

Notice that carpentry sits before the final coat of paint, while fittings sit after it. Heavy and dusty goes before paint, small and fragile goes after.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m bedroom and an attached bathroom

Take a bedroom of 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) and a small attached bathroom of 1.5 x 2.1 m (5 x 7 ft). The owner plans new tiles in the bathroom, a new false ceiling in the bedroom, a built-in wardrobe, and paint throughout. These are working days to allow for, not promises.

| Step | Days to allow |
|---|---|
| Strip and demolish | 2 |
| Plumbing and electrical points | 3 |
| Plaster and repair | 3 |
| Waterproofing, then curing and testing | 5 |
| Tiling and grout | 5 |
| False ceiling | 3 |
| Carpentry (wardrobe) | 8 |
| Putty, primer and two coats of paint | 6 |
| Fittings and clean | 2 |

Total: 2 + 3 + 3 + 5 + 5 + 3 + 8 + 6 + 2 = 37 working days. Some jobs overlap: the carpenter can build the wardrobe in his workshop while the plaster dries, which removes some of the 8 days from the site. Say that saves 4 days. The site time falls to 37 - 4 = 33 working days. Now add a buffer for rain, a missing material or a festival. A third of 33 is 11, so plan for about 33 + 11 = 44 working days, which is a little under nine working weeks of five days. Tell the family to allow about two months.

## Why the order matters

- If paint goes on before the carpenter, the carpenter scratches it.
- If tiles go on before the pipes are tested, a leak means breaking tiles.
- If the false ceiling is fixed after paint, the ceiling edge is damaged and repainted.
- If sockets are decided after plaster, walls are chased again.

## Living at home during work

Close doors, seal gaps with plastic sheets, keep one bathroom and one kitchen usable if you can, and move cooking away from dust. If an elderly person, a baby or a patient lives in the home, plan a stay elsewhere for the dustiest days.

## Checklist

- All choices (tile, paint colour, fittings, ceiling lights) made before day one
- Sockets, switches and pipe points marked on the wall with chalk and agreed by the electrician and plumber
- Waterproofing done by a specialist and tested
- Order of trades written on one page and shared with every worker
- A buffer of about a third added to the time plan
- A daily 5-minute talk with the supervisor

## Common mistakes

1. **Painting before the carpenter.** Touch-ups never match.
2. **Skipping the test for wet areas.** Leaks show after the tiles are on.
3. **Deciding sockets after plaster.** Opening walls twice costs time and dust.
4. **No buffer.** Rain, festivals and late materials are normal, not exceptional.
5. **Not protecting finished floors.** Cover them with sheets after tiling.

## FAQ

### Do tiles go before paint or after?
Before. Tiling is wet and rough. Paint comes later, once dust and water are gone.

### When should the false ceiling go in?
After wiring and plaster, before putty and paint, so the paint covers its joints and edges.

### Can I paint first and put in furniture later?
Yes, that is the usual order. Fit fixed units first, touch up, then bring loose furniture last.

### How much extra time should I plan for?
A third is a sensible starting buffer. Increase it in the monsoon or when walls and floors are opened.

### Who decides where sockets go?
You do, with your electrician, before plaster. Mark the places on the wall.
