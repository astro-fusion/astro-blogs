# What to do first on a small budget

A small budget runs out fast if you spend it on the wrong things first. After reading this you will be able to rank the jobs in your home, split your money into parts, and keep a reserve for the surprises that every home has.

## The rule: spend in this order

Money spent on a surface is wasted if the cause under it is still there. A freshly painted wall that has a leak behind it will peel again. So work from the cause to the surface.

1. **Fix what is broken or unsafe.** Leaking taps and pipes, damp patches, loose or damaged sockets and switches, a door that will not lock, a window that will not close. For wiring, gas and anything on the roof or a wall that may be load-bearing, call a licensed electrician or engineer. Do not guess.
2. **Fix what you use every day and that annoys you.** A drawer that jams, a door that rubs, no hook for the towel, a bulb that is too dim at the kitchen counter.
3. **Clean, repair and paint.** Fresh paint on sound walls gives the largest change in how a room looks for a low cost.
4. **Light.** A few better bulbs and one lamp in the right place often change a room more than new furniture.
5. **Furniture, one piece at a time.** Start with the item you use for the most hours: the bed, the sofa or the work chair.
6. **Decoration last.** Curtains, cushions, frames and plants cost little and can wait.

## Why a reserve matters

Once a wall is opened or a floor is lifted, you usually find something: old pipe, uneven plaster, a damp section. Set aside a reserve before you start. A reserve of about a tenth of the budget is a reasonable starting point for a small job. For work that opens walls or floors, keep about a fifth. If you do not use it, spend it on the next item in the list.

## Worked example: a 2BHK with a small budget

Take a family of five in a 2BHK flat of about 74 m² (800 sq ft). Instead of a currency figure, cut the whole budget into 12 equal parts. Whatever the real amount is, the split works the same.

| Job | Parts |
|---|---|
| Repairs: leaking tap, a damp corner, two loose sockets, door lock | 3 |
| Paint for the living room and one bedroom | 3 |
| Better light: bulbs, two lamps, one tube replaced | 1 |
| One piece of furniture: a proper bed base with storage | 3 |
| Reserve for surprises | 2 |

Check the arithmetic: 3 + 3 + 1 + 3 + 2 = 12 parts. Repairs take a quarter of the budget (3 of 12). The reserve is a sixth (2 of 12). That is more than the tenth suggested above, because the flat has a known damp corner, and damp is where surprises hide.

Now suppose the damp corner turns out to be worse and the repair costs one extra part from the reserve. The reserve falls to 1 part and the plan still holds: 3 + 1 = 4 for repairs, and the total is still 12. If the reserve is untouched at the end, the extra 2 parts go to the next item: curtains for the bedroom, then a second lamp.

## Where to save and where not to

**Save on:** decoration, branded finishes you cannot see, extra storage you will not fill, and anything you can do yourself safely, such as cleaning, sanding a small area, or fixing a shelf bracket.

**Do not save on:** surface preparation before paint (scraping, filling cracks, letting damp dry), hinges and drawer runners (cheap ones fail first), anything that touches water, and anything electrical.

**Think about when:** buying for the monsoon is harder because wood swells and paint dries slowly. Painting is easier in the dry months. In the hills, plan outdoor and roof work before the cold or the rains arrive.

## Checklist before you spend

- Walk through every room and write down every fault, with a photo.
- Mark each fault as "unsafe", "daily annoyance" or "looks".
- Ask for a written quote for each repair and put the quotes side by side.
- Split the budget into parts, as in the example, and write the numbers down.
- Keep the reserve in a separate place so it is not spent by accident.
- Measure before you buy furniture: width of the door and the stair, as well as the room.
- Buy the bed or sofa first, then wait a month before buying anything else for that room.

## Common mistakes

1. **Painting over a problem.** Damp, flaking plaster and cracks come back through paint. Find the cause first.
2. **Buying decoration before the basics.** A room with a jammed window and new cushions is still a room with a jammed window.
3. **No reserve.** The first surprise then stops the whole job halfway.
4. **Buying furniture that does not fit the stairs or lift.** Measure the route, not only the room.
5. **Doing everything at once.** A small budget does better in stages. One finished room is better than three half-done rooms.

## FAQ

### Should I paint or buy furniture first?
Paint first if the walls are marked or stained, because furniture is placed after painting and nobody wants paint on a new sofa. If the walls are sound and the bed or sofa is truly unusable, furniture comes first.

### How much should I keep as a reserve?
About a tenth for light work and about a fifth if walls or floors will be opened. Your own home's age and known faults should push the number up.

### Can I do the repairs myself?
Simple things, yes: a tap washer, a loose hinge, a shelf. Wiring, gas, anything that touches the roof or may be structural is for a licensed electrician or engineer.

### Is it cheaper to do one room at a time?
Often yes, because you can see the real cost of one room before you commit to the next. It also lets you keep living in the rest of the home.

### What gives the biggest change for the lowest cost?
Cleaning, repairing and painting, then better light. Both change how a room looks and feels without moving a single wall.

### When should I get a second quote?
Whenever a single job takes more than a quarter of your budget, or when the first quote does not list what is included. Put the scope in writing.
