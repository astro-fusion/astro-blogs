# Renting: improvements you can take with you

A rented flat is often bare, dim and slightly awkward. You do not own the walls, and you will probably move in a year or two. After reading this you will know which changes are worth paying for, which to avoid, how to size things so they fit the next home too, and what to agree with the landlord before you start.

## The rule: spend on things that move

Ask one question about every purchase: can I put it in a box or a truck when I leave? A good lamp, a rug, a free-standing shelf and curtains can all come with you. Paint, a built-in wardrobe and fixed tiles stay behind and belong to the landlord.

Spend most on the things you use every day, such as the mattress, a chair that suits your back and decent light where you read or work. These all move.

## Agree with the landlord first

Before you change anything fixed, ask. Then put it in writing, even if it is a short message that both of you keep. Check local rules and your rental agreement for what a tenant may and may not do.

Things worth asking about:

- Whether you may paint, and whether you must repaint to the original colour when you leave.
- Whether you may put nails or hooks in the wall, and how many.
- Whether the landlord will fix a leak, a broken lock or a faulty fitting, and how to reach them.
- What state the flat is in today. Photograph every room, including the meter readings and any marks, and share the photos.
- What happens to the deposit, and what deductions the landlord expects.

Do not touch wiring, plumbing, gas or anything structural. If a socket is loose or a tap leaks, tell the landlord and let a licensed electrician or plumber deal with it.

## Changes that need no drilling

### Light

Rented flats often have one ceiling light. Add two or three lamps at different heights. A floor lamp beside the sofa and a table lamp by the bed lets you switch off the harsh ceiling light in the evening. Plug-in lamps take their power from existing sockets and leave with you. If power cuts are common where you live, add a rechargeable lamp.

### Window coverings

Tension rods hold light curtains in a window recess without screws. Check the rod's stated load and keep to light fabric. Curtains also hide a poor view and give privacy to a ground-floor room.

### Floors

A rug 1.6 x 2.3 m (5 ft 3 in x 7 ft 6 in) under a sofa group hides worn flooring and makes a cold floor friendlier in a hill winter. Put a non-slip pad underneath, and pick up the corners if children run through the room.

### Walls

Adhesive hooks and picture strips can hold light frames, and they are meant to come off cleanly, though a few surfaces still mark, so test on a hidden corner. A mirror or a large frame leaned on a shelf, a dresser or the floor needs no fixing at all. Heavy mirrors and shelves need proper fixings, so skip them in a rented wall.

### Storage

Free-standing shelves, a trolley, stackable boxes and a clothes rail give you storage that goes with you. Choose pieces no deeper than 30 to 40 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 4 in) for a small room, and fix tall shelves to the wall only if the landlord allows it, because a tall unit can tip. If you cannot fix it, keep it low.

### Smells, damp and monsoon

Rented flats can feel damp in the monsoon. Open windows on opposite sides for a short time on dry mornings, and keep furniture a few centimetres off damp walls so air can move. Tell the landlord about any leak or patch of mould, because fixing the cause is their job, and put your message in writing.

## Measure so it fits the next home

Rooms differ, but doors do not vary much. A standard internal door is about 0.8 to 0.9 m (2 ft 8 in to 3 ft) wide, and the stairs and lifts of most apartment blocks are narrow. Before you buy, measure the door, the stair turn and the lift, and not only the room. Prefer furniture that comes apart, such as a bed with a slotted frame.

### Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) rented bedroom

You want a double bed (150 x 190 cm, 5 ft x 6 ft 3 in), a free-standing wardrobe 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) wide and 55 cm (1 ft 10 in) deep, and a small desk.

1. Put the bed with its head on a 3.0 m wall. Space left across the width: 3.0 - 1.5 = 1.5 m. Split it as 0.75 m on each side, which leaves room for a slim bedside table of 35 cm and still 0.4 m of walking space on that side. If that is too tight, shift the bed to one wall and keep one side at 0.9 m.
2. The bed runs 1.9 m along the 3.6 m wall. Space left at the foot: 3.6 - 1.9 = 1.7 m.
3. Place the wardrobe on the opposite 3.0 m wall, 55 cm deep. Walking space between the foot of the bed and the wardrobe: 1.7 - 0.55 = 1.15 m. A comfortable minimum is 75 cm, and you have well over that.
4. The wardrobe doors need room to open. A hinged door of about 50 cm needs that depth clear, so 1.15 m is enough.
5. A desk 100 x 50 cm (3 ft 3 in x 1 ft 8 in) fits on a free 1 m wall section beside the window, provided the window light falls on your hand side.

Every piece in this plan is free-standing, so every piece moves to the next flat.

## Checklist

- Written permission for any paint, hook or change to a fixed fitting.
- Dated photos of every room and the meter readings.
- Contact for repairs and a written note of who pays for what.
- Lamps at three heights in the living room and one by the bed.
- Curtains or blinds that fit by tension rod or by the existing rail.
- Rug with a non-slip pad.
- Furniture measured against doors, stairs and lift.
- A box of fixings and a small tool kit that move with you.

## Common mistakes

- **Painting without asking.** You may lose your deposit or have to repaint at your cost.
- **Buying built-in wardrobes or custom pieces.** They cannot be taken apart and moved.
- **Drilling for a heavy shelf or television bracket.** Holes in a wall are repairs the landlord will want paid for.
- **Putting a tall unfixed cabinet next to a bed or a child's play area.** Keep tall items low or fixed, if allowed.
- **Spending on the walls and not on the lights.** A single ceiling bulb makes any wall look flat.

## FAQ

### Can I paint a rented room?

Only if the landlord agrees, ideally in writing, including whether you must restore the original colour. Otherwise use fabric, rugs and frames to change the look.

### What can I hang without drilling?

Light frames on adhesive strips, and mirrors or art leaned on a shelf or the floor. Heavy items need proper fixings, so leave them for your own home.

### Who pays for repairs in a rented flat?

That depends on your agreement and local rules. Put the arrangement in writing, and report problems early with photographs.

### How do I protect my deposit?

Photograph the flat on the day you move in and the day you leave, keep receipts for repairs, and agree any marks in writing.

### Is it worth buying new furniture for a rented flat?

Buy pieces you will want in the next home as well. Choose items that come apart and fit through a standard door, and skip anything made to fit this one room.
