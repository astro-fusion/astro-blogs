# Planning switches and sockets: a list for your electrician

Most people notice a missing socket only after the furniture is in. By then, an extension board runs across the floor. After reading this you will be able to walk through each room and write down where a switch or socket would be useful and at what height, so that you can hand a clear list to your electrician before any wall is opened.

This article is about where things are useful. It does not cover wiring, loads, circuits or safety design. All of that belongs to a licensed electrician, who works to local rules. Ask them what they need from you and what they advise.

## How to think about it

Plan with the furniture, not around it. Draw the room with the bed, sofa, wardrobe and desk in place. Then ask three questions for each wall. What will be plugged in here? Which hand will reach for the switch? Will a piece of furniture hide the socket?

Remember that you will probably add devices over the years: a second charger, a router, a purifier, a fan. A socket that is slightly more than you need today is cheaper to install now than to add later. Your electrician will tell you what the circuit can safely carry. Do not decide that yourself.

## Typical heights

These are common practice. Your electrician may suggest different heights for your wall, and rules can differ by place.

| Item | Typical height from the finished floor |
|---|---|
| Light switches | about 120 cm (4 ft) |
| General wall sockets | about 30 cm (1 ft) |
| Bedside sockets | about 60 to 70 cm (2 ft to 2 ft 4 in) |
| Sockets above a kitchen counter | about 105 to 110 cm (3 ft 5 in to 3 ft 7 in) |
| Television socket and cable point | decided by where the set will be, often about 100 to 120 cm (3 ft 3 in to 4 ft) |
| Air conditioner point | high on the wall near the unit, as the installer advises |
| Water heater point | high, away from splashes, as the electrician advises |

Switches at 120 cm sit about at the height of an adult's hand, and a child can reach them if you want that. Put the switches on the latch side of the door, 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) from the door frame, so you do not reach across the swing.

## Room by room

### Bedroom

- A switch by the door for the main light.
- A second switch at the bed, so you can turn off the light without getting up. Ask the electrician about a two-way arrangement.
- One socket on each side of the bed, about 60 to 70 cm (2 ft to 2 ft 4 in) high.
- A socket near the dresser or study desk.
- A socket for the fan or air conditioner point, as the installer says.
- A socket near the wardrobe for a cleaner or an iron.

### Living room

- A switch panel by the entrance with lights and fan together.
- Sockets on both sides of the sofa for lamps and phones.
- A cluster behind the television: power, cable and internet, all at one height. Think about where the router will sit.
- A socket in the middle of a long wall so a cleaner or a fan can plug in without a long lead.
- Light points that match your lamps, so a floor lamp does not have a cable across the walkway.

### Kitchen

- A row of sockets about 20 cm above the counter, spaced so the kettle, mixer and toaster each have their own. That is about 105 to 110 cm (3 ft 5 in to 3 ft 7 in) from the floor if the counter is 85 to 90 cm (2 ft 9 in to 3 ft) high.
- A socket for the refrigerator in a place the fridge will not block.
- A point for the chimney or exhaust fan and a switch within reach.
- A point for the water filter near the sink, kept clear of splashes. Ask the electrician about protection near water.
- Keep sockets away from the stove and the sink.

### Bathroom

- A switch outside the door, so you do not touch it with wet hands.
- A water heater point and an exhaust fan point placed where the electrician advises, away from splashes. Wet areas have specific rules, so follow your electrician's advice and local rules.

### Entrance, balcony and passage

- A light switch at the main door and one at the far end of the passage.
- An outdoor light with a switch inside.
- A socket in the balcony for a plant light or a cleaner, placed where rain cannot reach it.
- A doorbell point.

### Puja space

- A light point for the shrine and a switch placed away from where you will stand with a lamp or incense. Keep it clear of any flame.

## Worked example: counting points for a 2BHK

Take a two-bedroom flat with a living room, a kitchen, two bathrooms and a balcony. Count the sockets you would like, room by room, as a list for the electrician.

1. Bedroom 1 (3.0 x 3.6 m, 10 x 12 ft): 2 bedside sockets, 1 at the dresser, 1 for the air conditioner, 1 near the wardrobe. That is 5.
2. Bedroom 2 (a smaller room used as a child's room and study): 1 bedside, 1 at the desk, 1 for the fan or cooler, 1 spare. That is 4.
3. Living room: 2 beside the sofa, 3 behind the television, 1 mid-wall. That is 6.
4. Kitchen: 5 over the counter, 1 for the refrigerator, 1 for the water filter. That is 7.
5. Bathrooms: 1 each for the water heater point. That is 2.
6. Balcony and entrance: 1 each. That is 2.

Total: 5 + 4 + 6 + 7 + 2 + 2 = 26 sockets.

This is a wish list, not a calculation of what the supply can carry. Hand the list and the furniture plan to the electrician, who will say what can be done, which items need their own circuit and what the work will involve. Ask for a drawing that marks every point, and keep a copy.

## Checklist

- A furniture plan drawn for each room before anything is marked.
- Every switch placed on the latch side of its door.
- Both sides of the bed, both sides of the sofa, and the desk covered.
- Kitchen sockets above the counter and clear of the stove and sink.
- Points for the television, router, air conditioner, fan and heater marked.
- A light switch at the bed, at the main door and at the passage ends.
- A written list given to the electrician, with their drawing returned to you.
- Photos of the walls before they are closed, showing where cables run.

## Common mistakes

- **Planning without the furniture.** A socket behind a wardrobe or a bed head is no use.
- **Too few points.** Extension boards and long leads cause trouble.
- **Switches behind the door.** You will hunt for them in the dark.
- **Putting a socket where water can reach it.** Ask your electrician for advice near sinks, bathrooms and balconies.
- **Not photographing the wall before it is closed.** Later, you will not know where cables run when you want to drill.

## FAQ

### How many sockets does a bedroom need?

There is no single number. A useful starting point is one on each side of the bed, one at the desk or dresser and one for cooling. Your electrician will advise.

### What height should a light switch be?

About 120 cm (4 ft) from the floor is common practice. Confirm with your electrician.

### Can I add sockets myself?

No. Wiring is work for a licensed electrician. You can plan where you would like points, but the installation is theirs.

### Should I plan sockets for an air conditioner I do not own yet?

If you may buy one, ask the electrician about a point in advance. It is easier before the walls are finished.

### What should I give my electrician?

A plan for each room with the furniture marked, a list of points with heights, and the devices you expect to use.
