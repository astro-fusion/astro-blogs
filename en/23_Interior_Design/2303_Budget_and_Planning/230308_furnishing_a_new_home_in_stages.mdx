# Furnishing a new home in stages, in a sensible order

Few families buy everything for a new home in the first week. Money, time and knowledge of the rooms all grow slowly. After reading this you will have a stage-by-stage buying order, a way to check that your first big pieces fit, and a simple split of the budget so that you do not spend it all on the sofa.

## Why stages work

You learn a lot in the first weeks. You find out which wall gets the afternoon sun, where the damp shows in the monsoon, which socket is too far from the bed and how the family really moves through the flat. Furniture bought on day one is bought without that knowledge. Furniture bought in stages is bought with it.

The aim is simple. Buy what you need to live safely and comfortably first, buy what you need to store things second, and buy what makes the place look finished last.

## The stages

### Stage 0: before you move in

Do the jobs that are hard to do once furniture is in place: painting, repairs, deep cleaning, changing locks, fixing leaks and getting a licensed electrician to check the points. If you plan fitted work such as wardrobes or kitchen units, give the carpenter your measurements now. Ask for any warranty on fittings to be put in writing.

### Stage 1: sleep, wash, cook, light

These are the things the household cannot manage without.

- A bed and mattress for every person who sleeps in the flat. A double is about 150 x 190 cm (5 ft x 6 ft 3 in), a single about 90 x 190 cm (3 ft x 6 ft 3 in).
- Curtains or blinds for privacy in bedrooms and bathrooms.
- A fan or the cooling or heating you need for your climate, such as a fan in the Terai summer or a heater for a hill winter.
- Basic kitchen items: stove, gas safety, a few pots, a water filter or storage arrangement.
- Lights in every room, plus one rechargeable lamp for power cuts.
- A clothes rail or a simple temporary cupboard.
- A bin, a mop, a bucket and a shoe rack by the door.

### Stage 2: sit, eat, store

When daily life has settled, buy the main furniture.

- A sofa or seating for the living room, sized to the room.
- A dining table or a small table with chairs, large enough for the household.
- A wardrobe for each bedroom, or fitted storage.
- A study table and chair for children or for working from home.
- A kitchen storage shelf or cabinet, and a place for the puja space if you keep one.

### Stage 3: comfort and character

Add things that make the flat feel like yours: rugs, side tables, extra lamps, a mirror, a bookshelf, plants, pictures and cushions. Add them one at a time, and only when you see a real gap.

### Stage 4: a pause

Leave a few months after Stage 3 before you buy more. Notice which items you never use and which spaces still feel wrong. This pause costs nothing and often saves a lot.

## Split the budget in parts

Without quoting any prices, take your total furnishing budget and divide it into ten equal parts.

- Stage 0 repairs and finish: 2 parts.
- Stage 1 essentials: 2 parts.
- Stage 2 main furniture: 3 parts.
- Stage 3 comfort and character: 1 part.
- Kept aside for surprises: 2 parts.

Check the total: 2 + 2 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 10. Change the split to suit you. A family with a baby may want more in Stage 1, and a tenant may want less in Stage 0. The kept-aside parts are important. Something always comes up, such as a leaking tap, a cracked tile or a bed that does not fit through the door. If you do not use the spare parts, put them toward the next stage.

## Worked example: does the first set of furniture fit?

Take a bedroom 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) where you plan a double bed and a wardrobe 120 cm (4 ft) wide and 60 cm (2 ft) deep.

1. Put the bed with its head on a 3.0 m wall. It is 150 cm wide, so the space across the room is 300 - 150 = 150 cm. Leave 75 cm on each side of the bed.
2. The bed is 190 cm long, so along the 3.6 m wall the space left at the foot is 360 - 190 = 170 cm.
3. The wardrobe goes on the opposite 3.0 m wall at 60 cm deep. The clear walking space between the foot of the bed and the wardrobe is 170 - 60 = 110 cm.
4. A comfortable minimum for a walking path is 75 cm, and 90 cm is easier. 110 cm is enough. The wardrobe door needs about 50 to 60 cm to swing open, so you can open it fully even when walking space is shared.
5. Check the route in: the bed frame, the mattress and the wardrobe all need to get through the main door (about 90 cm, or 3 ft, wide), around the corridor corner and up the stairs or in the lift. If the wardrobe is 120 cm wide, it must travel on its side or be flat-packed.

If the wardrobe were 75 cm deep instead of 60, the clear path would be 170 - 75 = 95 cm, still workable. At 90 cm deep it would be 80 cm, which is tight. This is the kind of check to do before you pay, not after.

## Checklist

- Measure every room, door, corridor turn and lift before you shop.
- Make a list of items by stage, with a note of who will use each.
- Divide the budget into parts and write the split down.
- Photograph each room at different times of day for a week.
- Check the sockets and light points against where furniture will stand.
- Ask for a written warranty or bill for each large item.
- Keep the carton or packing instructions for items that come apart.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying the sofa first.** It is large, expensive and hard to return. Beds, light and storage come before it.
- **Buying fitted furniture before living in the flat.** Wait to see how you use the space, unless the work must be done before you move in.
- **Skipping the door and lift measurements.** A good piece of furniture is useless if it cannot get in.
- **Spending the whole budget at once.** Keep some aside for repairs and surprises.
- **Copying a showroom.** Showrooms use large rooms. Scale the pieces to yours.

## FAQ

### What should I buy first for a new home?

Beds and mattresses, curtains, basic kitchen items, lights and a way to store clothes. Everything else can wait.

### How long should I wait before buying a sofa?

Wait until you know how the living room is used, the size of the room and where the television and windows are. A few weeks of living there tells you a lot.

### Should I buy a full set of furniture?

Usually not. A set is sized for a showroom, and you may only need two pieces from it. Buy pieces separately and match them by colour and material.

### How do I plan for a growing family?

Buy a bed and storage that can change, such as a bed with a drawer base or a study table that adjusts. Leave floor space free in the children's room.

### Do I need to tell the carpenter before I move in?

Yes, if you plan fitted wardrobes or kitchen units. Give measurements early, and agree the drawing and the price in writing.
