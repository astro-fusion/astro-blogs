# Bathroom layout: keeping wet and dry areas apart

After reading this you will be able to arrange the shower, toilet and basin in a small bathroom so that water stays in one zone and the rest of the floor stays dry. You will get the clearances to check with a tape measure and a worked example for a 1.5 x 2.1 m (5 x 7 ft) bathroom, a common size in flats and family houses.

Many Nepali and Indian bathrooms are one wet room: shower, toilet and basin share a floor, and everything gets splashed. A few decisions at the planning stage can change that without making the room bigger.

## Think in two zones

A wet zone is where water falls: the shower and, in many homes, the bucket and tap area. A dry zone is everything else: the basin, mirror, towels and the walk from the door.

The aim is simple. Water should fall in the wet zone, run to the drain there and not travel to the door. You do this with the order of fixtures, a partition and a floor that falls towards the drain.

## The sizes that matter

These are common-practice figures. Check them against the fixtures you actually buy.

- **Shower area.** 90 x 90 cm (3 x 3 ft) is comfortable. 75 x 75 cm (2 ft 6 in x 2 ft 6 in) is tight but works. If you use a bucket and mug, add 30 cm (1 ft) for the bucket.
- **Toilet.** Allow 70 cm (2 ft 4 in) of wall run, which is 35 cm (1 ft 2 in) each side of the centre of the bowl. A clear space of 60 cm (2 ft) in front of the bowl is the comfortable minimum. A bowl commonly projects about 70 cm (2 ft 4 in) from the wall.
- **Basin.** Allow 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) of wall run and at least 70 cm (2 ft 4 in) clear in front. The rim is usually at 80 to 85 cm (2 ft 7 in to 2 ft 9 in) above the floor.
- **Mirror and light.** Put the bottom of a mirror at about 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) above the floor, with light on or beside it, not only behind the user.
- **Shower head.** Commonly at 200 cm (6 ft 7 in) above the floor. Fixed taps are around 100 to 110 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 7 in).
- **Towel hook.** At about 150 cm (4 ft 11 in), placed outside the spray, close to the shower exit.
- **Door.** 75 to 80 cm (2 ft 6 in to 2 ft 7 in) clear is common. Make the door open outward or slide if the room is very small, but check that it does not hit someone in the passage.

## How to keep the dry zone dry

- **Order the fixtures.** Put the shower at the far end, away from the door, and the basin nearest the door.
- **A partition.** A glass panel or a curtain of at least 90 cm (3 ft) wide stops most spray. Glass should be toughened. Ask the supplier.
- **Floor fall.** Ask the plumber and tiler for a gentle fall towards the drain inside the shower area, and a drain that does not stand water. Do not invent the slope yourself.
- **A kerb or a floor drop.** A low edge at the shower stops water, but it is also a step. Where an older person will use it, a flush, gently sloped floor is safer.
- **Waterproofing.** Floors and lower walls need a proper waterproof layer under the tiles. This is a job for a trained applicator, and a leak into the flat below is costly. Ask for the work to be tested by flooding before tiling.
- **Grip.** Choose a matt or textured floor tile in the whole bathroom, and especially in the wet zone.

## Ventilation and damp

A bathroom that never dries grows mould on the grout and ceiling.

- Leave a window or an exhaust fan open after a shower, and let it run for a while.
- In a bathroom with no window, ask the electrician and contractor about a fan that vents to the outside, not into the false ceiling.
- Hot water in a geyser needs a sensible position and an electrician. Keep sockets and switches away from the spray, and use only fittings meant for wet rooms.
- Fix dripping taps early. A small leak damages the wall behind it.

## Worked example: a 1.5 x 2.1 m (5 x 7 ft) bathroom

The room is 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) wide and 210 cm (6 ft 11 in) long. The door is on one short wall. You want a toilet, a basin and a shower.

1. **Choose a wall run.** Use the long 210 cm wall for all three fixtures, leaving a clear strip beside them for walking.
2. **Shower.** Put the shower at the far end, 90 x 90 cm. It takes 90 cm of the long wall.
3. **Toilet.** The toilet needs 70 cm of wall run. After the shower, 210 - 90 = 120 cm (4 ft) remains.
4. **Basin.** The basin needs 50 cm. 120 - 70 = 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) remains, so the basin fits exactly. Check: 90 + 70 + 50 = 210 cm.
5. **Clear space at the toilet.** The room is 150 cm wide and the toilet projects 70 cm. 150 - 70 = 80 cm (2 ft 7 in) of clear space in front, above the 60 cm minimum.
6. **Clear space at the basin.** The basin projects about 45 cm (1 ft 6 in). 150 - 45 = 105 cm (3 ft 5 in) in front.
7. **Beside the shower.** The shower is 90 cm deep and the room is 150 cm wide, so 150 - 90 = 60 cm (2 ft) is left beside it. Use it for the shower entry and keep the towel hook there.
8. **Result.** The basin and mirror are by the door, the wall run is full, and the spray stays at the far end.

## Checklist for the tape measure

- Shower area at least 90 x 90 cm (3 x 3 ft).
- Toilet: 35 cm (1 ft 2 in) from the centre to a side wall or fixture, and 60 cm (2 ft) clear in front.
- Basin: 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) of wall run and 70 cm (2 ft 4 in) clear in front.
- A partition or curtain between shower and the rest.
- A fall to the drain and matt floor tiles.
- A window or an exhaust fan.
- Switches, sockets and the geyser positioned by a licensed electrician.

## Common mistakes

- **Toilet too close to the wall.** A bowl with less than 35 cm (1 ft 2 in) from its centre to the side wall is cramped.
- **Shower next to the door.** Water reaches the passage every time.
- **Polished tiles.** They look clean but are slippery when wet.
- **Skipping the waterproofing test.** Leaks show up months later in the flat below.
- **No ventilation.** Damp builds on grout and ceilings.

## FAQ

### Is a glass partition necessary?
No. A curtain of at least 90 cm (3 ft) also works. Glass is easier to keep clean.

### What is the smallest practical bathroom?
Around 1.2 x 1.5 m (4 x 5 ft) can fit a toilet and a basin. A shower needs more room, so small homes often use one shared wet room.

### Where should the geyser and sockets go?
Ask a licensed electrician. Keep them away from the spray and use fittings made for bathrooms.

### Can I put the toilet in the wet zone?
Many small bathrooms do. Keep the toilet paper, towels and clothes outside the spray, and let the floor fall to one drain.

### How do I stop mould?
Ventilate after each use, fix leaks and wash the grout. If damp keeps returning, call a professional to find the cause.
