# A safe, easy room for elderly parents

After reading this you will be able to lay out one room so that an older parent can get in, sit, lie down, stand up and reach the bathroom with less effort and fewer risks. You will have clear sizes to check with a tape measure and a worked example for a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) room.

This is practical home-planning advice, not medical advice. If a parent uses a wheelchair or has a particular condition, ask their doctor or a physiotherapist what they need.

## Start with the route, not the furniture

Most falls at home happen on the way to somewhere: from the bed to the toilet at night, or through a doorway with a raised step. So plan the route first.

- **Location.** If you have the choice, give parents a room on the ground floor or on the same floor as the main living area, close to a bathroom. Stairs are the hardest part of a home for tired legs.
- **Door width.** A clear opening of 80 cm (2 ft 7 in) is a comfortable minimum for walking with a stick or frame. Aim for 90 cm (3 ft) if a walker or wheelchair may be needed.
- **Thresholds.** The best threshold is flat. If a step cannot be removed, keep it to 1 cm (less than half an inch) with a sloped edge, and mark it with a contrasting strip.
- **Turning space.** A clear circle of about 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) beside the bed lets a person turn with a walker.

## The bed

A bed that is too low or too soft makes standing up hard.

- **Height.** The top of the mattress at 45 to 50 cm (1 ft 6 in to 1 ft 8 in) lets most older adults sit with feet flat and stand without a deep push.
- **Size.** A single bed is 90 x 190 cm (3 x 6 ft 3 in). A double is about 150 x 190 cm (4 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in). Two single beds with a gap of 60 cm (2 ft) between them are often easier than one double for two people who sleep at different times.
- **Open side.** Keep at least one long side of the bed free. A clear 90 cm (3 ft) is a comfortable minimum; 120 cm (4 ft) is better for a frame.
- **Beside the bed.** A small table at 45 to 50 cm (1 ft 6 in to 1 ft 8 in), level with the mattress, holds water, glasses, a torch and a phone. Fix a switch or lamp where it can be reached lying down.

## Floors

Smooth, polished, glossy floors are risky when wet or when someone wears socks.

- Choose a matt or textured finish. Ask the shop for the slip rating of any tile and prefer one made for bathrooms or outdoors near the bathroom door.
- Avoid loose rugs and mats that slide. If you use one, fix it with a non-slip underlay or tape on all four edges.
- Use one floor level and one material in the room and the route to the bathroom.

## Lighting

Eyes need more light with age and take longer to adjust to dark.

- Put a switch at the door and one at the bed, both at 90 to 110 cm (3 ft to 3 ft 7 in) above the floor so they can be reached sitting or standing.
- Add a low, dim light for the night route to the bathroom, so no one has to turn on a bright ceiling light.
- Power cuts happen. Keep a rechargeable torch or emergency light within reach of the bed.
- All wiring, new switches and sockets are work for a licensed electrician.

## Furniture and storage

- **Chair.** One firm chair with arms and a seat height of 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) is easier to rise from than a soft sofa.
- **Wardrobe.** Keep daily clothes between 75 and 150 cm (2 ft 6 in to 4 ft 11 in) from the floor, so no one has to stretch or bend. Sliding or light doors are easier than heavy swinging ones.

## Grab bars and handrails

Grab bars give a firm hold in the bathroom and beside the toilet.

- A bar is usually fitted at 80 to 90 cm (2 ft 7 in to 3 ft) from the floor.
- A bar must be fixed into solid wall or a proper backing, never into a hollow board or a tile alone. Ask a qualified fitter or your contractor how and where, and do not use suction-cup bars as a support.
- On stairs, a handrail on at least one side at about 90 cm (3 ft) above the steps helps. A riser of about 15 cm (6 in) is easier than a tall one. Waterproofing and wall work in the bathroom is for a professional.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) room

Say the room is 300 x 360 cm (10 x 12 ft). The door is 90 cm (3 ft) wide on the short wall, opening outward. One parent needs a single bed and a wardrobe.

1. **Bed.** The bed is 90 x 190 cm. Put its head on the 300 cm wall and one long side against the left wall.
2. **Length check.** 360 - 190 = 170 cm (5 ft 7 in) is free at the foot of the bed.
3. **Open side.** 300 - 90 = 210 cm (6 ft 11 in) is free on the right of the bed.
4. **Bedside table.** Put a table 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) deep at the head on the right. That leaves 210 - 40 = 170 cm (5 ft 7 in) at the head.
5. **Wardrobe.** A wardrobe is 60 cm (2 ft) deep. Place a 120 cm (4 ft) wide wardrobe on the right wall at the foot end, where 170 cm is free, which fits.
6. **Walking space.** Beside the bed, 210 - 60 = 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) stays clear from the bed edge to the wardrobe front. This meets the turning space of about 150 cm and gives well over 90 cm for walking.
7. **Chair.** Put the 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) high chair at the foot of the bed against the left wall, out of the walking line.

## Checklist for the tape measure

- Door opening at least 80 cm (2 ft 7 in), better 90 cm (3 ft).
- No raised thresholds on the route to the bathroom.
- A clear 90 cm (3 ft) beside the bed, with a 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) turning circle nearby.
- Mattress top at 45 to 50 cm (1 ft 6 in to 1 ft 8 in).
- Switch at the bed and at the door, 90 to 110 cm (3 ft to 3 ft 7 in) high.
- Matt, non-slip floor and no loose rugs.
- A night light and an emergency torch.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing a bed for looks.** A tall, soft bed with a thick mattress is hard to leave.
- **Polished floors.** A shiny finish looks clean but is slippery with socks or water.
- **Leaving the room dark at night.** One bright switch across the room is a poor night plan.
- **Fixing grab bars in tile or plaster only.** They can pull out when needed most.

## FAQ

### Should an elderly parent sleep on the ground floor?
If possible, yes, because it avoids stairs on the way to the toilet at night. If it is not possible, keep stairs well lit, fit a handrail and keep each step clear.

### How wide should the door be?
Aim for a clear 90 cm (3 ft). A clear 80 cm (2 ft 7 in) is a comfortable minimum for a stick or frame.

### Is a double bed or two single beds better?
Two single beds with a 60 cm (2 ft) gap give each person an open side and make getting up easier. A double bed is fine if one long side stays free.

### Can I put grab bars on a tiled wall myself?
Ask a qualified fitter. The bar must be fixed into solid wall, and the tile and waterproofing must not be damaged.
