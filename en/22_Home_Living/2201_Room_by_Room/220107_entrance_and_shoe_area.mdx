# The entrance: shoes, bags and a place to sit

After reading this you will be able to plan the few square metres inside your front door so that shoes, bags, keys and wet umbrellas have a place, and so that a person can sit and take off shoes without blocking the door. You will get sizes for a shoe cabinet, a bench and hooks, and a worked example for a narrow foyer.

In most Nepali and Indian homes, shoes come off at the door. That makes the entrance a working space, not only a doorway.

## What the entrance has to do

Think of the entrance as five small jobs:

- Take off and store shoes.
- Hang bags, coats and a wet umbrella.
- Put keys, mask, wallet and post somewhere you will see them.
- Sit for a moment, especially for children and older people.
- Leave a clear floor for the door to swing and people to pass.

If any job has no place, things end up on the sofa, the dining table or the floor.

## The landing space

Start with the floor, because it is the part you cannot add later.

- A clear area of 120 x 120 cm (4 x 4 ft) just inside the door gives room to open the door, step in and turn.
- A walking width of 90 cm (3 ft) is a comfortable minimum for a passage that also carries furniture on one side. Aim for 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) if a pram or a wheelchair comes in.
- A main door is commonly 90 to 100 cm (3 ft to 3 ft 3 in) wide. Check which way it opens and keep the swing area free of furniture.
- Mark the outer side of the door, too. If a corridor or lobby outside is shared, check your building rules before leaving a shoe rack there.

## Shoe storage

Sizes depend on the people and what they wear. These are common-practice figures.

- **Depth.** A shoe cabinet 30 cm (1 ft) deep holds most shoes laid flat. Use 35 cm (1 ft 2 in) if you have large shoes or sports shoes. Shallow tilted shelves can be 20 cm (8 in) deep, but they hold fewer pairs and suit only flat shoes.
- **Shelf spacing.** Allow 18 cm (7 in) between shelves for ordinary shoes and 25 cm (10 in) for boots.
- **Width per pair.** Allow about 25 cm (10 in) of shelf width for each pair of adult shoes laid side by side. Children's shoes need less.
- **Ventilation.** Choose slatted or perforated doors, or leave a gap at the back. Damp shoes in a closed box smell and encourage mould in the monsoon.
- **Floor clearance.** Leave 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) below the cabinet so the floor can be wiped and sandals can sit underneath.

## Hooks, bench and the drop zone

- **Hooks.** Set adult hooks at about 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) and children's hooks at 110 cm (3 ft 7 in). Fix them to a solid wall, not to a hollow panel, and ask your carpenter for a backing board if the wall is weak.
- **Bench.** A seat at 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) height and 35 to 40 cm (1 ft 2 in to 1 ft 4 in) deep is enough for putting on shoes. A bench of 90 cm (3 ft) length seats two people.
- **Drop shelf.** Put a small shelf or tray for keys at about 100 to 110 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 7 in), where a hand reaches without bending.
- **Mirror.** Centre a mirror at about 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) so most adults can see their face.
- **Light.** A switch inside the door and a lamp near the bench help at night and in power cuts. Keep a small torch in the drop tray. Any wiring is for a licensed electrician.

## The wet part of the entrance

In the monsoon, wet shoes, umbrellas and raincoats come in daily.

- Put a tray with a raised edge or a washable mat near the door for umbrellas and wet shoes. A tray of 60 x 40 cm (2 ft x 1 ft 4 in) holds a few pairs.
- Use a floor finish that can be wiped and is not slippery when wet.
- Leave wet items near an opening window or the door gap to dry, not inside a closed cabinet.

## Worked example: a narrow foyer for a family of five

Take a foyer 120 cm (4 ft) wide and 240 cm (8 ft) long, with the front door on one short wall. The family has five people. Each person keeps about three pairs of shoes by the door: 5 x 3 = 15 pairs.

1. **Cabinet depth.** Use a cabinet 30 cm (1 ft) deep. The foyer is 120 cm wide, so 120 - 30 = 90 cm (3 ft) is left for walking. This meets the comfortable minimum.
2. **Cabinet width.** Choose a cabinet 120 cm (4 ft) wide. At 25 cm per pair, 120 / 25 = 4.8, so each shelf holds 4 pairs.
3. **Shelves.** 15 pairs / 4 pairs per shelf = 3.75, so you need 4 shelves.
4. **Height.** Four shelves at 18 cm (7 in) spacing is 4 x 18 = 72 cm. Add 4 boards of 2 cm (about 3/4 in): 4 x 2 = 8 cm. The cabinet interior is 72 + 8 = 80 cm (2 ft 7 in) high. Raise it 10 cm (4 in) off the floor, so the top is at 90 cm (3 ft).
5. **Top surface.** The 90 cm top becomes the key and tray shelf.
6. **Bench.** The long wall is 240 cm (8 ft). 240 - 120 = 120 cm (4 ft) remains beside the cabinet. Put a 90 cm (3 ft) bench there, leaving 30 cm (1 ft) as a gap.
7. **Hooks.** Put hooks on the wall above the bench, at 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) and 110 cm (3 ft 7 in).

## Checklist for the tape measure

- Clear landing of 120 x 120 cm (4 x 4 ft) inside the door.
- At least 90 cm (3 ft) left to walk past furniture.
- Count the pairs of shoes in daily use and divide by pairs per shelf.
- Cabinet depth 30 to 35 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 2 in) and ventilated.
- Bench seat at 45 cm (1 ft 6 in).
- Hooks at 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) and 110 cm (3 ft 7 in).
- A tray for wet shoes and umbrellas.

## Common mistakes

- **A deep cabinet in a narrow foyer.** A cabinet of 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) in a 120 cm (4 ft) wide foyer leaves only 75 cm (2 ft 6 in).
- **A closed shoe box in the monsoon.** No air movement means damp, smelly shoes.
- **No seat.** People balance on one foot, or sit on the floor, in the way.
- **Hooks too high or too low.** Children cannot reach them, and adults cannot see their bags.
- **A door that hits the cabinet.** Always draw the door swing before ordering.

## FAQ

### How many pairs of shoes should a shoe cabinet hold?
Count the pairs you use every week and add a few extra. Keep seasonal shoes elsewhere.

### Can I keep a shoe rack outside the flat door?
Many apartment societies have rules about shared corridors. Check them first, and never block an exit.

### Is a closed cabinet or an open rack better?
An open or slatted cabinet dries shoes better. Closed doors hide clutter. Slats give you both.

### How deep should a shoe cabinet be?
Usually 30 cm (1 ft) deep, or 35 cm (1 ft 2 in) for large shoes.

### Do I need a bench?
You do not need one, but a seat at 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) makes putting on shoes easier for children and older people.
