# A dining space in a small home: sizes and clearances

After reading this you will be able to choose a dining table that fits your room, check that people can sit down and walk past, and decide whether you need a table at all. You will get the sizes to use and a worked example for a 2.4 x 3.0 m (8 x 10 ft) nook, the kind found in many 2BHK flats.

Many families eat on the floor or on a bed, and that is fine. If you want a table, the sizes below keep it comfortable.

## How much room does a diner need?

Each person at a table needs a width of about 60 cm (2 ft). A seat is about 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) high and the table top about 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) high. The difference of 30 cm (1 ft) leaves room for knees.

When someone sits down, the chair is pulled out. A comfortable minimum of 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) from the table edge to the wall or the next piece of furniture lets a person sit and stand. If other people must walk behind the seated person, you need about 100 cm (3 ft 3 in). A tight 90 cm (3 ft) lets a person pass sideways behind a chair that is only pulled out a little.

## Common table sizes

These sizes are common in Nepal and India. Check them in the shop with a tape measure.

| Table | Size | Seats |
|---|---|---|
| Round | 90 cm (3 ft) diameter | 3 to 4 |
| Square | 80 x 80 cm (2 ft 7 in x 2 ft 7 in) | 4 |
| Rectangle | 120 x 75 cm (4 ft x 2 ft 6 in) | 4 |
| Rectangle | 150 x 90 cm (4 ft 11 in x 3 ft) | 6 |
| Rectangle | 180 x 90 cm (5 ft 11 in x 3 ft) | 6 to 8 |

A rectangle of 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) depth works for two plates facing each other, but dishes of food in the middle need 80 to 90 cm (2 ft 7 in to 3 ft).

## Ways to save floor area

- **Table against a wall.** Push one long side to the wall. You lose seats on that side but gain a walking lane.
- **Bench on one side.** A bench of 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) seats two adults, or three if they sit close, and tucks under the table. A bench is lighter than chairs to move.
- **Round table.** A round table of 90 cm (3 ft) diameter has no corners to bump and lets people sit closer.
- **Drop-leaf or folding table.** Fold it flat to 30 to 40 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 4 in) deep and open it at meal times. Fix a wall-mounted one to a solid wall. Ask your carpenter to check the wall and the fittings.
- **Stools or chairs that tuck in.** Four stools under a table take no walking space when not used.
- **Shared with the living area.** Place the table at the edge of the living room and leave a clear lane of 90 cm (3 ft) to the kitchen.

## Where to put it

- Close to the kitchen, so food travels a short way, but not in the cooking line.
- Out of the main walking route from the front door to the bedrooms.
- Near a window or a light, so that food and faces look clear.
- Away from a hot wall in summer. A table by a west-facing window can get the afternoon sun.

## Light and power

A pendant over the table is common. Hang the bottom of the shade about 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) above the table top, so it lights the table and stays out of people's eyes. A plug near the table is useful for a rice cooker or a hot plate, but all wiring needs a licensed electrician. A battery lamp is handy during power cuts.

## Worked example: a 2.4 x 3.0 m (8 x 10 ft) nook

The nook is 240 cm wide and 300 cm long. A family of four eats there, and guests come now and then.

1. **Choose a table.** A 4-seater of 120 x 75 cm (4 ft x 2 ft 6 in) is enough.
2. **Position.** Put one long side against the 300 cm wall. The table is 120 cm long, so the wall length has 300 - 120 = 180 cm (5 ft 11 in) free, which is 90 cm (3 ft) at each end if the table is centred.
3. **End chairs.** A chair at each end needs 75 cm to be pulled out. Each end has 90 cm, so 90 - 75 = 15 cm (6 in) to spare. This works.
4. **Open side.** The room is 240 cm deep. The table is 75 cm deep and sits against the wall, so 240 - 75 = 165 cm (5 ft 5 in) is left on the open side.
5. **Chairs on the open side.** Two chairs there need 75 cm to pull out, leaving 165 - 75 = 90 cm (3 ft) for someone to walk behind. That meets the tight minimum.
6. **Count the seats.** Two chairs on the open side and one at each end give four seats.
7. **Guests.** Use a table of 150 x 75 cm (4 ft 11 in x 2 ft 6 in), such as a drop-leaf that extends. The wall length leaves 300 - 150 = 150 cm (4 ft 11 in), which is 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) at each end. This just fits for the end chairs, and the table seats four comfortably. For six at one meal you need a bigger table and a bigger room.
8. **Light.** Hang a pendant over the table, with its shade bottom 75 cm above the top.

## Checklist for the tape measure

- 60 cm (2 ft) of table width per person.
- 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) from table edge to wall behind a chair, or 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) if people walk behind.
- A clear 90 cm (3 ft) lane to the kitchen.
- Table height 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) and seat 45 cm (1 ft 6 in).
- Mark the table and chair sizes on the floor with masking tape and live with it for a day.
- Check door and window swings.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying the table first.** Measure the room, then choose the table.
- **Forgetting the pulled-out chair.** The table seems to fit until someone sits down.
- **A table in the main walking route.** People then walk around it every day.
- **Heavy chairs.** In a small room, light chairs are easier to move.
- **A fixed table for occasional guests.** A drop-leaf serves both.

## FAQ

### How big a room do I need for a 4-seater?
About 240 x 300 cm (8 x 10 ft) works well, as in the example. A smaller nook can work with a round table or a bench.

### Is a round or a rectangular table better in a small space?
A round table of 90 cm (3 ft) has no corners and seats four in a tight room. A rectangle fits against a wall better.

### Can I use the dining table as a desk?
Yes, if you can clear it. Keep a small shelf or box for papers so meals are not interrupted.

### What if we eat on the floor?
Keep a clear area of about 150 cm (4 ft 11 in) around a mat, and store a low table or a rolled mat in a nearby cupboard.

### How high should the pendant be?
About 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) above the table top is common. Adjust it so no one's eyes meet the bulb.
