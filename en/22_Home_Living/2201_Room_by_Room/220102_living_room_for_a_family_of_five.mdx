# A living room that seats a family of five

After reading this, you will be able to plan seating for five people, plus a few guests, in an ordinary living room. You will know the gaps to keep around sofas and tables, where to put the TV, and how to test a layout with a tape measure before you buy.

## What a family living room has to do

In most homes the living room is used for sitting, TV, snacks, children playing, homework and visitors, often at the same time. It needs clear floor and flexible seating more than extra furniture.

Plan for these things:

- Five fixed seats, so the family can sit together.
- Space for two to four extra seats, such as stools, floor cushions or a folding chair.
- A path from the door to the rest of the home that nobody has to squeeze through.
- A TV that all seats can see.

## The sizes to know

| Item | Common size |
|---|---|
| Three-seater sofa | 180 to 210 cm wide, 85 to 95 cm deep (5 ft 11 in to 6 ft 11 in wide, 2 ft 9 in to 3 ft 1 in deep) |
| Two-seater sofa | 130 to 160 cm wide (4 ft 3 in to 5 ft 3 in) |
| Single armchair | 70 to 80 cm wide (2 ft 4 in to 2 ft 7 in) |
| Seat height | 42 to 45 cm (1 ft 5 in to 1 ft 6 in) |
| Coffee table | 100 x 50 cm, height 40 to 45 cm (3 ft 3 in x 1 ft 8 in, 1 ft 4 in to 1 ft 6 in high) |
| Pouf or stool | 40 x 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) |
| TV unit | 120 to 180 cm wide, 35 to 45 cm deep (3 ft 11 in to 5 ft 11 in wide, 1 ft 2 in to 1 ft 6 in deep) |

## The gaps that matter

- **Sofa to coffee table:** aim for 45 cm (1 ft 6 in), so you can reach the table and still get your legs through. A comfortable minimum is 40 cm (1 ft 4 in).
- **Main walking path:** aim for 90 cm (3 ft). A comfortable minimum for a side path is 60 cm (2 ft).
- **Between two sofas facing each other:** aim for 150 to 180 cm (4 ft 11 in to 5 ft 11 in), with a table in the middle.
- **Door arc:** keep the area the door swings through free, usually about 90 cm.

## Where the TV goes

Put the screen so that it can be seen without turning your head. Do not place it opposite a window, because daylight shows up as glare on the screen. The centre of the screen should be near eye level when you sit, which is about 100 to 110 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 7 in) from the floor for most sofas.

Distance depends on screen size. A common rule of thumb is two to three times the screen diagonal, measured from the seat to the screen. For a 109 cm (43 in) screen, that gives about 2.2 to 3.3 m (7 to 11 ft).

## Worked example: a 3.6 x 4.5 m (12 x 15 ft) living room

The room is 360 cm by 450 cm. The area is 3.6 x 4.5 = 16.2 square metres (about 174 sq ft). The family has five people. We choose a three-seater of 200 x 90 cm and a two-seater of 150 x 85 cm. That gives 3 + 2 = 5 seats.

**Step 1. Put the seating on the long wall.** The long wall is 450 cm. The row along it is a three-seater (200 cm), a side table (50 cm) and a two-seater (150 cm). That is 200 + 50 + 150 = 400 cm. The wall has 450 - 400 = 50 cm left, which is room for a floor lamp or a stool.

**Step 2. Put the TV on the opposite long wall.** The room depth is 360 cm. The sofa is about 90 cm deep and the TV unit is 40 cm deep. The space between the sofa front and the TV unit is 360 - 90 - 40 = 230 cm (7 ft 7 in).

**Step 3. Place the coffee table.** The table is 50 cm deep and sits 45 cm from the sofa. Together they use 45 + 50 = 95 cm. The path left between the table and the TV unit is 230 - 95 = 135 cm (4 ft 5 in). That is more than the 90 cm aim, so two children can pass each other.

**Step 4. Check the TV distance.** When you sit back, your eyes are about 70 cm from the wall behind you. The screen sits about 20 cm in from the front of the TV unit. The view distance is 360 - 70 - 20 = 270 cm (8 ft 10 in). For a 109 cm screen, the rule of thumb gives 2.2 to 3.3 m, so 2.7 m is inside the range.

**Step 5. Add guest seating.** Two poufs of 40 x 40 cm slide under the coffee table when not used. With them, seven people can sit. A rolled mat in a corner gives floor seating for children.

**Step 6. Check the door.** Draw the door arc on the plan. If it opens onto the 50 cm free end, shift the row so the arc stays clear.

If the wall is shorter, replace the two-seater with two armchairs of 75 cm each.

## Choosing seating for a family

- Choose a firm seat that older people can get up from easily. A seat height near 45 cm is easier than a very low one.
- Pick fabrics or covers that can be removed and washed. Dust and monsoon humidity will find a sofa.
- Pick a table with rounded corners if small children run in the room.
- Keep the sofa a few centimetres off an outside wall that gets damp in the rains.
- A cane or wooden bench by the door lets visitors sit to put shoes on.

## Light, power cuts and cooling

Keep a lamp that works on a battery or inverter near the sitting area for power cuts. In the hot plains, do not block a window with a tall cabinet. Ask a licensed electrician about any new socket or fan point.

## Checklist

- Measure the room at floor level, wall to wall, in centimetres.
- Mark the door arc and every window on a sketch.
- Tape the sofas and table on the floor, and walk the path.
- Check 45 cm between sofa and table.
- Check 90 cm for the main path.
- Measure from your seat to the TV, and compare with the screen size.
- Note where the sockets are for the TV and lamps.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying the sofa first.** Measure the room, then choose the sofa, not the other way round.
2. **Pushing every piece against the walls.** This leaves a hole in the middle that nobody uses. A table pulled in front of the sofa helps.
3. **Placing the TV opposite the window.** The glare spoils the view.
4. **A very large coffee table.** A 120 x 70 cm table in a small room blocks the path.
5. **Leaving no spare seating.** A family of five needs something for guests.

## FAQ

### How much floor depth does a sofa and table need?
A sofa is about 90 cm deep. Add 45 cm to the table, 50 cm for the table and a 90 cm path: 90 + 45 + 50 + 90 = 275 cm (9 ft).

### How far should the TV be from the sofa?
About two to three times the screen diagonal. In the example above, a 109 cm (43 in) TV is about 2.7 m from the seat.

### Where do guests sit?
Use stools, poufs and folding chairs that live in another room or under tables. A rolled mat on the floor works well for children.

### Can I put the sofa against a window?
Yes, if the window sill is lower than the sofa back, and the window still opens. Do not block a window you use for air.
