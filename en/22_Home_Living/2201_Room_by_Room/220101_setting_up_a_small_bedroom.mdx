# Setting up a small bedroom: bed, wardrobe and walking space

After reading this, you will be able to check on paper whether a bed and a wardrobe fit your bedroom before you order either. You will know the clearances to keep, how to test a wardrobe door swing, and how to walk through a full layout with a tape measure.

## Start with the three clearances

A small bedroom works when three gaps are right.

| Gap | Aim for | A comfortable minimum |
|---|---|---|
| Along each long side of the bed | 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) | 60 cm (2 ft) |
| Between the foot of the bed and a wall or wardrobe | 90 cm (3 ft) | 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) |
| In front of a wardrobe with hinged doors | Door width plus 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) | Door width plus 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) |

These are common practice, not rules. If two people sleep here, keep both sides open so nobody climbs over the other.

## Know your sizes first

Measure the room, then write down the sizes of what you want to put in it.

- Single bed: about 90 x 190 cm (3 ft x 6 ft 3 in)
- Double bed: about 150 x 190 cm (4 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in)
- Queen bed: about 152 x 198 cm (5 ft x 6 ft 6 in)
- King bed: about 180 x 198 cm (5 ft 11 in x 6 ft 6 in)
- Wardrobe depth: about 60 cm (2 ft) for hanging clothes on hangers
- Wardrobe height: about 210 cm (6 ft 11 in), or to the loft if there is a high shelf
- Bedside table: about 40 x 35 cm (1 ft 4 in x 1 ft 2 in)

Measure the actual bed in the shop, because a thick headboard adds length.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) bedroom

Take a bedroom of 3.0 m by 3.6 m. That is 3.0 x 3.6 = 10.8 square metres (about 116 sq ft). You want a double bed of 150 x 190 cm and a wardrobe.

**Step 1. Put the bed head on a short wall.** The short wall is 3.0 m, which is 300 cm. A bed of 150 cm width leaves 300 - 150 = 150 cm. Centred, that is 75 cm on each side. Both sides meet the aim above.

**Step 2. Check the length.** The room is 360 cm long. The bed takes 190 cm. That leaves 360 - 190 = 170 cm (5 ft 7 in) between the foot of the bed and the opposite wall.

**Step 3. Place the wardrobe on that opposite wall.** A wardrobe is 60 cm deep. The clear space in front of it is 170 - 60 = 110 cm (3 ft 7 in).

**Step 4. Test the door swing.** Choose a wardrobe of 180 cm width with three hinged doors of 60 cm each. With one door open, the space left to stand in is 110 - 60 = 50 cm (1 ft 8 in). That is enough to stand and reach. Now try two doors of 90 cm each. The open door leaves 110 - 90 = 20 cm, which blocks you. In this room, three narrow doors or sliding doors are the better choice.

**Step 5. Add the bedside tables.** A table of 40 x 35 cm sits at the head of the bed. It takes only the first 35 cm of the length, so the full 75 cm stays open along the rest of each side.

**Step 6. Count what is left.** The bed uses 1.5 x 1.9 = 2.85 square metres. The wardrobe uses 1.8 x 0.6 = 1.08 square metres. The floor left over is 10.8 - 2.85 - 1.08 = 6.87 square metres. Not all of that is usable, because the door and window need paths, but it shows the room is not full.

Draw the room door and the window before you finish. A door that swings in needs an arc of clear floor equal to its width, usually 75 to 90 cm. Do not let it cross the wardrobe door arc.

## Make the room work harder

- Choose a bed with drawers or a hydraulic lift for storage under the mattress. Check that the drawers can fully open, which needs about 50 to 60 cm of clear floor on the side they open to.
- Use a loft cupboard above the wardrobe for blankets and suitcases. Reach it with a stable step stool.
- Use light wall colours and a mirror on a wardrobe door to bounce daylight. This makes the room brighter, not bigger.

## Damp, heat and cold

In the monsoon, a wardrobe pushed flat against an outside wall can trap moisture and smell. Leave a gap of about 2 to 3 cm (1 in) behind it, or fit it with a back board and a ventilation gap, and do not store clothes in plastic bags in a damp cupboard. If the wall itself is damp, find the cause first, such as a leak, a cracked pipe or rain coming in through the wall. Call a plumber or a waterproofing professional. Paint will not fix it.

Keep a clear path to the window so you can open and close it. In hill winters, keep the bed a little away from a cold outside wall.

## Checklist

- Measure the room length and width in centimetres at floor level and again at hip height. Walls are rarely straight.
- Mark the door swing and the window opening on the plan.
- Tape the bed size on the floor and walk around it.
- Check 75 cm (60 cm at the least) on each side where people will walk.
- Check 90 cm (75 cm at the least) at the foot of the bed.
- Add the wardrobe depth plus the door width, and check what is left.
- Check that drawers and doors open fully.
- Find where sockets and switches are before you fix the bed head. Ask a licensed electrician about any new point.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying a king bed for a 3.0 m wide room.** 300 - 180 = 120 cm, so only 60 cm on each side, and no space for a table.
2. **Choosing wide hinged wardrobe doors.** A 90 cm door in a narrow gap blocks the room when open.
3. **Pushing the bed into a corner.** One person must climb over the other, and making the bed becomes hard.
4. **Forgetting the door arc.** The bedroom door and the wardrobe door must not open into each other.
5. **Measuring only once.** Measure again before ordering, because plaster and tiles change the real width.

## FAQ

### What is the smallest bedroom a double bed fits in?
A double bed is 150 x 190 cm. With 60 cm on both sides and 75 cm at the foot, you need about 270 x 265 cm for the bed alone. A wardrobe needs more, so 3.0 x 3.0 m is tight and 3.0 x 3.6 m is comfortable.

### Should I choose sliding or hinged wardrobe doors?
Sliding doors need no swing space but open only half the width at a time. Hinged doors give full access but need clear floor equal to the door width.

### How far should a bed be from the wall?
One side can touch the wall if one person sleeps there. For two people, keep both long sides open, at least 60 cm.

### Where can I put a wardrobe if the room is very small?
Look at the wall behind the door, the wall at the foot of the bed, or a fitted wardrobe across a whole short wall. Ask a carpenter about a shallower wardrobe of 45 to 50 cm depth with sliding doors.

### Can I remove a wall to make the bedroom bigger?
Do not do this without a licensed structural engineer. A wall may be carrying the floor above it. Plan the room as it stands first.
