# Kitchen layout: the work triangle and counter heights

After reading this, you will be able to check whether the sink, the hob and the fridge in your kitchen are well placed, and choose a counter height that suits the person who cooks. You will do it with a tape measure and a little arithmetic.

## The work triangle in plain words

A cook moves between three places again and again: the fridge, the sink and the hob. Imagine a line between each pair. The three lines make a triangle. A short triangle saves steps. A very long one means carrying hot pots across the kitchen.

A common aim is:

- Each side between 120 cm and 270 cm (4 ft to 8 ft 10 in).
- All three sides added together between 400 cm and 800 cm (13 ft to 26 ft).
- No cupboard, door or walking path cutting through the triangle.

Measure from the middle of each item. These are guides, not rules. A very small kitchen may have shorter sides.

## Counter height: fit it to the cook

The common counter height is 85 to 90 cm (2 ft 9 in to 2 ft 11 in). You can fit it better in two steps.

1. Stand straight with your arms relaxed, and bend your elbow to a right angle.
2. Measure from the floor to the elbow, then subtract 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in).

A cook with an elbow height of 102 cm gets 102 - 10 = 92 cm and 102 - 15 = 87 cm. So about 87 to 92 cm suits that person. If two people cook, choose the height for the one who cooks most, and use a low stool or a board for the other.

Other common sizes:

| Part | Aim for |
|---|---|
| Counter depth | 60 cm (2 ft) |
| Wall cabinet bottom above the counter | 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) |
| Wall cabinet depth | 30 to 35 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 2 in) |
| Aisle between two facing counters | 100 to 120 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 11 in) |
| Clear counter beside the hob | at least 30 cm each side (1 ft) |
| Clear counter beside the sink | 45 to 60 cm (1 ft 6 in to 2 ft) |
| Clear counter next to the fridge | 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) |

With a counter of 90 cm, the bottom of the wall cabinet is at 90 + 45 = 135 cm (4 ft 5 in) from the floor. Check that the shortest person using the kitchen can reach the second shelf.

## Worked example: a 2.4 x 3.0 m (8 x 10 ft) parallel kitchen

The room is 240 cm wide and 300 cm long. Counters run along both long walls, each 60 cm deep, with a door at one short end.

**Step 1. Check the aisle.** The two counters take 60 + 60 = 120 cm. The aisle is 240 - 120 = 120 cm (3 ft 11 in). That is at the top of the 100 to 120 cm aim, so one or two people can pass.

**Step 2. Give each item a place.** Use the west wall (run A) and the east wall (run B). Measure along the room from the closed short end, which is y = 0.

- Fridge in run A at the closed end. It is 70 cm wide, so its middle is at y = 35.
- Sink in run A, 90 cm wide, from 155 to 245 cm. Its middle is at y = 200.
- Hob in run B, 60 cm wide, from 90 to 150 cm. Its middle is at y = 120.

Across the aisle, the middles of run A and run B are 180 cm apart, because 60 / 2 + 120 + 60 / 2 = 30 + 120 + 30 = 180.

**Step 3. Measure the three sides.**

- Fridge to sink: same run, so 200 - 35 = 165 cm (5 ft 5 in).
- Sink to hob: 180 cm across and 200 - 120 = 80 cm along. The straight line is the square root of 180 x 180 + 80 x 80. That is the square root of 32,400 + 6,400 = 38,800, which is about 197 cm (6 ft 6 in).
- Hob to fridge: 180 cm across and 120 - 35 = 85 cm along. The square root of 32,400 + 7,225 = 39,625 is about 199 cm (6 ft 6 in).

**Step 4. Add them.** 165 + 197 + 199 = 561 cm (18 ft 5 in). Every side is between 120 and 270, and the sum is between 400 and 800. The layout passes.

**Step 5. Check the counter space.** Beside the hob, the counter runs from 0 to 90 on one side, which is 90 cm, and from 150 to 300 on the other, which is 150 cm. Beside the sink, there is 155 - 70 = 85 cm toward the fridge, and 300 - 245 = 55 cm toward the door. All pass the minimums above.

**Step 6. Check the fridge door.** Open the fridge door in the plan. It must open fully, or at least past 90 degrees, without hitting the opposite counter or the wall.

## What cooking needs around the triangle

- **Landing space near the hob.** You need a place to put a hot pot down. Do not put the hob in a corner.
- **A prep area between the sink and the hob.** This is where you cut and mix. Aim for at least 90 cm (3 ft) of clear counter.
- **Storage where you use things.** Pots near the hob, plates near the sink.
- **Chimney or exhaust fan above the hob.** Follow the maker's instructions for the height. A common range is 65 to 75 cm (2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 6 in) above the hob, and the installer sets it.
- **A window or exhaust fan** that moves smoke out of the room.

Gas pipes, the gas cylinder position and all wiring and water pipes need a licensed gas fitter, electrician and plumber. Ask them for the safe distance between the hob, the cylinder and any socket. Do not move a gas line on your own.

## Local points

In the monsoon, wood cabinets near the sink can swell. Leave a gap at the back for air, and have a leaking tap or pipe fixed quickly. With power cuts, keep a small light you can switch on easily. Keep the cylinder in a ventilated place.

## Checklist

- Measure the room length and width, and the door and window positions.
- Mark the sink, hob and fridge on a sketch.
- Measure from middle to middle: three sides and the sum.
- Check each side is between 120 and 270 cm.
- Check the aisle is 100 to 120 cm.
- Measure the main cook's elbow height and pick a counter height.
- Check landing space beside the hob, sink and fridge.
- Open every door, fridge and drawer on the plan to see it does not collide.

## Common mistakes

1. **Putting the hob next to a door or window.** A draft blows out the flame, and a curtain can catch fire.
2. **Forgetting landing space.** A hob at the end of a run leaves nowhere to rest a pot.
3. **One counter height for everyone.** A counter that is too low makes you stoop, and one that is too high lifts your shoulders. Fit it to the main cook.
4. **A fridge in the walking line.** An open fridge door blocks the aisle.
5. **Wall cabinets set too high.** The top shelves are out of reach.

## FAQ

### What is the standard kitchen platform height in India and Nepal?
Most are made at 85 to 90 cm. Use your elbow height minus 10 to 15 cm for a better fit.

### How far should the hob be from the sink?
Keep it within 120 to 270 cm, with about 90 cm of prep counter between them. A hob right beside the sink risks water splashing on a hot pot.

### How high should the chimney be?
Follow the maker's instructions. 65 to 75 cm above the hob is common, but the right figure depends on the model.

### Can I move the sink or gas line myself?
No. Ask a licensed plumber or gas fitter.
