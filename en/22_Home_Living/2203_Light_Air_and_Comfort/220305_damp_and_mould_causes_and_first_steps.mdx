# Damp and mould: causes and first steps

After reading this you will be able to look at a damp patch and say which of four causes is most likely. You will know the simple steps to try first, and the signs that mean you should call a plumber, a waterproofing contractor or a licensed engineer.

This article is about the building and the damp, not about anyone's health. If you have a medical worry, speak to a doctor.

## Why mould grows

Mould needs a surface that stays damp for a long time. It does not need dirt. A wall that is wet or cold, or air that is full of moisture and cannot get out, gives it a place to grow. Remove the damp and the mould has nothing to grow on. Cleaning mould without finding the damp only brings it back.

## The four common causes

### 1. A leak

Water from a pipe, a bathroom, a roof or a balcony reaches the wall. Signs: a patch that grows, stains with a clear edge, a patch that stays after a dry spell, a patch below a bathroom or on a ceiling under a terrace. A leak shows up even in dry weather and can be near a pipe.

### 2. Rain coming in

During the monsoon, water gets in through cracks in outer walls, round window frames, through a leaking roof or from a blocked drain pipe. Signs: patches that appear after rain and dry out in the winter.

### 3. Condensation

Warm, moist indoor air meets a cold surface and turns to water. Signs: droplets on windows in the morning, damp on cold outer corners and behind wardrobes, and a patch that is darkest at the top corners or behind furniture. It grows when many people sleep in a closed room, when clothes are dried indoors and when cooking steam stays in.

### 4. Damp from the ground

Water rises up a wall from the ground or foundation. Signs: a damp band near the floor, often up to about 1 m (3 ft 3 in) high, with salt marks or peeling paint. This is a building defect. Do not treat it yourself.

## First steps you can take

1. **Find the source.** Look for the cause before you touch the mould. Check pipes, taps, the bathroom, the roof and the drains, and watch whether the patch changes after rain or after a shower.
2. **Fix leaks.** Ask a plumber for a pipe or a tap leak. Repair a dripping tap or loose joint early.
3. **Let air in.** Open windows for 10 to 15 minutes in the middle of the day, even in winter. A short, wide opening changes the air better than a small gap left open for hours.
4. **Dry clothes outside.** In the sun when you can. If you must dry them indoors, do it in a room with a window open or near an exhaust fan.
5. **Use extractor fans and lids.** Run the bathroom fan or open the window during and after a shower. Use lids and the kitchen chimney while cooking.
6. **Pull furniture off cold walls.** Leave a gap behind wardrobes and beds on outer walls.
7. **Let the sun in.** Open curtains in the morning. Direct sun dries walls.
8. **Clean the surface once the cause is fixed.** Wear gloves and a mask, wipe with a cloth and soapy water, and let it dry. Do not scrub dry mould, as it spreads spores into the air. Do not mix cleaning chemicals.
9. **Repaint after it is dry.** Paint over a wet wall will peel.

## When to call a professional

Call a plumber when you find a leak from a pipe, tap, or drain. Call a waterproofing contractor for roof, terrace or balcony leaks and for rain coming through walls. Call a licensed engineer if you see cracks that grow, damp coming from the foundation, bulging plaster or rust stains from the reinforcing steel. Call a licensed electrician if water has reached a switch, socket or wiring, and do not touch the wet fittings. Keep this simple: if you cannot find the source in a week, ask.

This article does not give waterproofing design. Ask the professional to explain what they will do, and in what order.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) bedroom

A patch of mould shows behind a wardrobe on a 3.0 m (10 ft) outer wall in a monsoon flat. The wardrobe is 1.8 m wide and 0.6 m deep (5 ft 11 in by 2 ft), and it touches the wall.

**Step 1. Record the patch.** It measures 0.6 m by 0.4 m (2 ft by 1 ft 4 in). Area is 0.6 x 0.4 = 0.24 sq m (2.6 sq ft). Mark the edge with a pencil and note the date.

**Step 2. Check for a leak.** Look at the wall above and beside the patch, and at any pipe in the wall. The patch is high, near the corner, and no pipe runs there. A leak seems unlikely, so it points to condensation or rain.

**Step 3. Check the weather link.** If it is bigger after a heavy rain, outside cracks are the cause. If it is the same after rain and worse after cold nights, it is condensation. Here it is worse on cold mornings.

**Step 4. Make space.** Move the wardrobe 5 cm (2 in) off the wall so air passes behind it. Depth plus gap is 0.6 + 0.05 = 0.65 m (2 ft 2 in). The room width is 3.0 m, so the clear floor is 3.0 - 0.65 = 2.35 m (7 ft 9 in), which is enough to walk.

**Step 5. Ventilate and dry.** Open the window each midday and keep the clothes out of the room.

**Step 6. Watch it.** After seven days, measure the patch against the pencil mark. If it is no larger and the wall feels dry, clean it and repaint when dry. If it is larger, or the paint still lifts, call a professional.

## Checklist

- Mark the patch edge and the date.
- Note whether it is near a pipe, a bathroom, the roof or the floor.
- Note whether it changes after rain, a shower or a cold night.
- Check for a gap of at least 5 cm (2 in) behind wardrobes on outer walls.
- Check that fans and windows work in the bathroom and kitchen.
- Check that clothes are not dried in a closed bedroom.
- Check that rain pipes and drains are clear before the monsoon.

## Common mistakes

- **Painting over damp.** The paint peels and the patch returns.
- **Cleaning without finding the cause.** The mould regrows.
- **Sealing every window in winter.** Moist air has no way out.
- **Pushing furniture hard against cold outer walls.** Air cannot move behind it.
- **Tackling rising damp or roof leaks yourself.** These need a professional.

## FAQ

### How do I tell a leak from condensation?

A leak has a source, grows in dry weather and often sits near a pipe or a bathroom. Condensation shows droplets on cold surfaces and is worse after cold nights or crowded closed rooms.

### Will a dehumidifier help?

It can lower moisture in a closed room, but it does not fix a leak. Use it as a help, not a cure.

### Is the white powder on my wall mould?

Not always. White salts can come out of a damp wall. It is a sign that water is moving through the wall, so look for the source.

### Can I just wipe it off?

You can clean the surface once the cause is dealt with. Wipe it without scrubbing when dry, and let the wall dry fully.

### Why does my wardrobe smell damp in the monsoon?

The air is humid and an outer wall is cool. Leave a gap behind the wardrobe, open it from time to time, and air your clothes in the sun.
