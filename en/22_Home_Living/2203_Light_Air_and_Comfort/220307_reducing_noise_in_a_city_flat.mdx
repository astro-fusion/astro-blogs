# Reducing noise in a city flat: doors, windows and floors

After reading this you will be able to find where the noise in your flat comes from and which low-cost fixes are worth doing first. You will get sizes for sealing gaps, hanging curtains, placing a bookshelf and laying a rug, plus a worked example for a bedroom that faces the road.

You cannot make a flat silent, and a few fixes will not turn a busy road into a quiet lane. You can make a room calmer, mostly by closing gaps and adding soft, heavy things.

## How noise gets in

Noise travels in two ways.

- **Through the air.** Traffic, horns, voices and television reach you through open windows, gaps round frames and the gap under a door.
- **Through the building.** Footsteps, dragged chairs, a washing machine and drilling travel through floors and walls.

The two need different fixes. A heavy curtain does little for footsteps from the flat above, and a rug does little for horns outside. Sit in the noisy room for ten minutes with the window shut and listen. Then close your eyes and decide where it is coming from: the window, the door, the wall or the ceiling.

## Fix the gaps first

Sound goes through any gap that air can pass through, so sealing gaps is the cheapest step with the biggest effect.

- **Window frames.** Run self-adhesive foam or rubber seal strip along the sash where the window closes. A strip 5 to 10 mm (about 1/4 to 3/8 in) thick suits most sliding and casement windows. Check that the window still closes fully.
- **Door edges.** Fit the same strip on the two sides and the top of the door frame.
- **Under the door.** A gap of 10 mm (3/8 in) or more under a bedroom door lets sound in. Fit a door sweep, a strip of rubber or brush fixed to the bottom of the door.
- **Holes in walls.** Look for holes round pipes, AC sleeves and old cable entries. Fill them with a flexible sealant. Ask a licensed electrician before you touch anything near wiring.
- **Letterbox or ventilation grille.** Close what you do not need.

Seal strips wear out, so check them every year.

## Windows

Glass is thin, and a single pane lets in a lot of sound. Heavier or double glazing is a job for a window contractor, and it costs more.

- Close the windows when the road is busiest and ventilate at quieter times.
- A heavy, lined curtain that reaches the floor and is wider than the window by 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) on each side softens sound. It is not a seal, but it helps, especially with echo inside the room.
- If you can put a second sliding window frame inside an existing one, ask a window contractor about secondary glazing. Never remove or cut a frame yourself without checking with the building owner or society.

## Walls and the shared wall

A thin partition or a shared wall carries voices.

- Put a full-height bookshelf against the shared wall, 30 cm (1 ft) deep and filled with books. The weight and the uneven surface break up sound a little.
- A wardrobe on the shared wall does the same job. Put the wardrobe, not the bed head, against it.
- Hang a thick cloth or felt panel on an echoing wall.
- Fix shelves to the wall properly. A tall unit loaded with books can fall, so anchor it to a solid wall. If the wall is hollow or unsure, ask your carpenter.

If noise comes through the wall because it is cracked, damp or very thin, ask a licensed engineer to look at it. Do not drill or cut a wall to fit insulation unless you know it is not structural.

## Floors and the flat above

- Put felt pads under every chair and table leg, 20 to 30 mm (3/4 to 1 1/4 in) in diameter for most chairs.
- A thick rug at least 2.0 x 3.0 m (6 ft 7 in x 9 ft 10 in) under the dining or living area softens footsteps and echo.
- If your flat is above someone else, a rug and slippers help your neighbours more than they help you. If you are below, you can ask your neighbour politely to use rugs and felt pads.
- Put a washing machine on rubber pads and check that it stands level, so it does not hum through the floor.

## Where to put things

Use furniture as a buffer.

- Put the bedroom on the side away from the road if you have a choice, and the kitchen or bathroom on the road side.
- Keep the bed head away from the wall you share with a neighbour's living room.
- Close the bedroom door at night. A solid door blocks more sound than a hollow one, but a gap beats both.

## Worked example: a road-facing bedroom

Take a bedroom of 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) with one window of 1.2 x 1.5 m (4 ft x 5 ft), a door of 0.9 x 2.1 m (3 x 7 ft) and a double bed of 150 x 190 cm.

1. **Seal for the window.** The perimeter is 2 x (1.2 + 1.5) = 5.4 m (17 ft 9 in).
2. **Seal for the door.** Two sides and the top: 2.1 + 2.1 + 0.9 = 5.1 m (16 ft 9 in).
3. **Seal strip needed.** 5.4 + 5.1 = 10.5 m. Add 1 m for waste: 11.5 m, so buy 12 m (39 ft).
4. **Door sweep.** One sweep as wide as the door: 0.9 m (3 ft).
5. **Curtain.** The window is 1.5 m wide. Add 25 cm on each side: 1.5 + 0.25 + 0.25 = 2.0 m (6 ft 7 in). Hang a heavy lined curtain from a rod this long.
6. **Rug.** The floor is 3.0 x 3.6 = 10.8 square metres. A rug of 1.6 x 2.3 m (5 ft 3 in x 7 ft 7 in) covers 1.6 x 2.3 = 3.68 square metres. Place it so the bed stands on its far end and your feet land on it when you get up.
7. **Bookshelf.** The shared wall is 3.6 m (12 ft). A bookshelf 90 cm (3 ft) wide, 30 cm (1 ft) deep and full of books fits beside the door and leaves 3.6 - 0.9 = 2.7 m (8 ft 10 in) of wall for the wardrobe or bed.

The cost stays low: seal strip, a sweep, a curtain and a rug.

## Checklist for the tape measure

- Window perimeter measured and seal strip bought with 1 m spare.
- Door sides and top sealed; sweep fitted if the gap under the door is 10 mm (3/8 in) or more.
- Curtain rod 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) wider than the window on each side.
- Bookshelf 30 cm (1 ft) deep on the shared wall, anchored.
- Felt pads on all chair legs.
- Rug placed under the main walking and sitting area.
- Washing machine level and on rubber pads.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying foam panels for the wall and leaving the door gap open.** Gaps let sound in; panels mostly help echo inside the room.
- **Sealing a window shut with no ventilation plan.** You still need air. Open it at quieter hours.
- **Hanging a thin curtain.** Light fabric does very little. Use something thick and lined.
- **Loose bookshelves.** A tall shelf that is not anchored can fall.
- **Cutting into a wall.** Never open, drill deeply into or remove part of a wall without a licensed engineer.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest fix for road noise?
Seal the gaps round the window and the bedroom door, and fit a door sweep.

### Do egg trays or foam stop noise?
Not much. They soften echo in the room but do not block sound coming through a wall or window.

### Does a heavy curtain help?
A little. It softens echo and some of the sound, but it is not a seal. Gaps matter more.

### How do I deal with footsteps from the flat above?
You cannot fix it from below, other than by talking to your neighbour. Rugs and felt pads in their flat help most. Ask your building society if it has a rule about hard floors.

### Can I put insulation inside the wall?
That is a job for a contractor, and a licensed engineer should confirm the wall is safe to open first.
