# Indoor plants that survive, and where to put them

After reading this you will be able to choose a few hardy plants for your home, place each one where it gets the light it needs, and water it without drowning it. You will get pot and shelf sizes and a worked example for a window shelf.

Most houseplants do not die of neglect. They die of too much water, too little light, or a pot with no way for water to leave. Fix those three things and the rest is easy.

This article makes no claims about plants cleaning the air. It is about keeping plants alive and a home pleasant to look at.

## Start with light

Light decides which plant you can grow and where. Watch a room across a day before you buy.

- **Bright indirect light.** A spot near a window where the sun does not fall on the leaves for hours. An east window or a north window in the plains is usually gentle. Most hardy houseplants like this.
- **Direct sun.** A south or west window in summer can scorch leaves behind glass. Pull plants back or use a sheer curtain.
- **Low light.** A corner far from any window has little. A few plants cope, but they grow slowly. Add a lamp or move them to a brighter spot for a week now and then.
- **Distance.** Aim to keep plants within about 1 m (3 ft 3 in) of a window. Light falls away fast as you move into the room.

A plant that stretches towards the window with long, thin stems is asking for more light. A plant with pale or burnt patches is getting too much.

## Hardy plants to start with

These are commonly grown, forgiving plants. Check each one on a reliable plant-safety list before you bring it home if you have pets or small children.

- **Money plant (pothos).** Trails or climbs. Copes with bright indirect light and some shade.
- **Snake plant.** Upright, thick leaves. Copes with low light and rare watering.
- **ZZ plant.** Glossy leaves, slow growing. Copes with low light.
- **Spider plant.** Arching leaves and small plantlets. Likes bright indirect light.
- **Aloe.** Needs a bright window and very little water.
- **Tulsi.** A sacred and common plant that needs real sun, so keep it on a sunny balcony or terrace, not in a dim room.

Start with two or three, not ten. Learn how they behave before you add more.

## Watering

Overwatering is the most common cause of death.

- **Check with a finger.** Push a finger about 2.5 cm (1 in) into the soil. If it is damp, wait. If it is dry, water.
- **Water slowly until a little drains out of the bottom.** Then stop and empty the saucer after 15 to 30 minutes.
- **Less in cool months.** Plants use less water in a cold hill winter and in low light.
- **More in hot, dry spells.** A plant in a warm, bright place may need water every few days.
- **Avoid a fixed schedule.** Check first.
- **Monsoon.** Air is damp and soil stays wet longer. Check more carefully, and empty saucers daily, because standing water in them can breed mosquitoes.

## Pots, soil and drainage

- **A drainage hole is not optional.** If a decorative pot has no hole, keep the plant in a plain plastic pot with holes and slip it inside, taking it out to water.
- **Pot size.** Choose a pot 2 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) wider than the root ball. A much bigger pot holds wet soil that never dries.
- **Soil.** Use a loose potting mix, not garden clay. Add some coarse sand or small stones to a heavy mix.
- **Saucer.** Pick a saucer a little wider than the pot. Lift the pot clear of it on small feet if the floor is delicate.
- **Floors.** Put a tray or a mat under pots on wooden or marble floors. Water marks are hard to remove.
- **Feeding.** A weak plant feed once a month in the growing season is enough. Check the label.

## Pets and children

Many common houseplants are unsafe if chewed.

- Keep every plant out of reach of toddlers, cats and dogs, on high shelves or hanging pots.
- Check the name of each plant on a trusted plant-safety list, or ask a vet, before bringing it into a home with animals.
- Keep soil and plant feed out of reach, and cover soil with stones if a pet digs in it.
- If a child or pet chews a plant, call a doctor or a vet and take a sample of the plant with you.

## Weight and balconies

Wet soil is heavy. A large pot full of damp soil can weigh many kilos.

- Put big pots on the floor, not on a thin shelf.
- Do not load a balcony or terrace with many large planters without asking a licensed engineer how much it can carry.
- Fix shelves to solid walls. Ask your carpenter what a shelf will hold.
- Keep pots clear of balcony edges so a gust or a knock cannot push them off.

## Worked example: a shelf for a sunny window

Take a living room of 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) with an east window 1.5 m (4 ft 11 in) wide on the short wall. You want a row of small plants on a shelf below the window.

1. **Pot and saucer.** A pot of 20 cm (8 in) across usually has a saucer of about 24 cm (9 1/2 in).
2. **Gap.** Leave 6 cm (2 1/2 in) between saucers so leaves have room and you can reach in.
3. **Space per plant.** 24 + 6 = 30 cm (1 ft).
4. **Shelf length.** The window is 150 cm wide, so the shelf is 150 cm (4 ft 11 in).
5. **Number of pots.** 150 / 30 = 5 pots.
6. **Depth.** Choose a shelf 25 cm (10 in) deep, so a 24 cm saucer fits with a little margin.
7. **Distance from glass.** The plants sit within about 30 cm (1 ft) of the glass, which is inside the 1 m target.

Start by filling three of the five places and leave two empty for plants you add later. In a hot summer, hang a sheer curtain between them and the glass in the afternoon.

## Checklist for the tape measure

- Each plant within about 1 m (3 ft 3 in) of a window.
- Drainage hole in every pot.
- Pot 2 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) wider than the root ball.
- A saucer under each pot, emptied after watering.
- Finger test 2.5 cm (1 in) deep before every watering.
- Plants out of reach of children and pets.
- Large pots on the floor, not a weak shelf.

## Common mistakes

- **Watering by the calendar.** Soil, season and light all change how fast it dries.
- **A pot with no hole.** Roots sit in water and rot.
- **A huge pot for a small plant.** The soil stays wet for weeks.
- **Plants in a dark corner.** They stretch, weaken and drop leaves.
- **Leaving water in the saucer.** It smells and can breed mosquitoes in the monsoon.

## FAQ

### How often should I water?
When the top 2.5 cm (1 in) of soil is dry. That can be every few days in hot weather and every couple of weeks in a cool, dim room.

### Which plant is best for a room with little light?
Snake plant and ZZ plant cope with low light better than most, but they still need some daylight.

### Do I need a pot with holes?
Yes. If you want a decorative pot, put a plain pot with holes inside it.

### Are houseplants safe around pets and children?
Many common ones are not safe if chewed. Check each name on a trusted list, keep plants out of reach, and ask a vet or doctor if unsure.

### Can I put plants on my balcony railing?
Avoid it. A pot can fall. Keep pots on the floor, away from the edge.
