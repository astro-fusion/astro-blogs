# Curtains, blinds and privacy without losing light

After reading this you will be able to choose between curtains, sheers and blinds for each room, set a rod at the right width and height, and work out how much fabric to ask the tailor for. You will get a worked example for a window facing a street or a neighbour's balcony.

The aim is simple. During the day you want daylight and no one looking in. At night you want privacy. In summer you want less heat from the sun on the glass.

## Think in layers

One covering rarely does every job, so many homes use two layers on the same window.

- **A sheer layer.** Thin fabric lets in light and blurs the view in from outside during the day. It does nothing at night, when a lit room is visible from the street.
- **A heavy layer.** A thick or lined curtain gives privacy at night and cuts glare and some heat in the afternoon.
- **A blind.** A roller or slat blind gives control by the half metre. It suits kitchens and small windows, where a curtain gets in the way or catches grease.

For a bedroom, a heavy layer is the main need. For a living room facing a street, a sheer plus a heavy curtain is usual. For a kitchen, a washable blind is easier to keep clean.

## Choosing by room

- **Ground-floor or street-facing rooms.** Passers-by look in at eye level, so cover at least the lower half of the window in the day. A sheer curtain or frosted film on the lower glass works. Keep the upper glass clear for daylight.
- **Facing a neighbour's window or balcony.** Use a sheer with some body, such as a light weave, plus a heavy curtain you can close in the evening.
- **Bathrooms.** Frosted film or a blind that can get wet. Avoid fabric that stays damp.
- **West-facing rooms in a hot place.** Afternoon sun heats the glass. A lined curtain or a blind on the glass side keeps some of it out. Light colours on the outer face reflect more than dark ones.
- **Cold hill winters.** A lined curtain that reaches the floor cuts the cold draught off a window. Close it when it gets dark.

## Rod, track and blind sizes

These are common-practice figures, not rules.

- **Rod width.** Make the rod wider than the window by 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) on each side. When the curtain is open, it then stacks beside the glass, not across it, so the window gets all its light.
- **Rod height.** Fix the rod 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) above the top of the window frame. Fixing it higher, close to the ceiling, makes the room look taller but needs more fabric.
- **Length.** Stop the curtain 1 to 2 cm (about 1/2 in) above the floor so it does not drag and gather dirt.
- **Fullness.** Make the curtain 1.5 to 2 times as wide as the rod, so it hangs in folds. A flat sheet of fabric looks thin and lets light through.
- **Blind inside the frame.** Measure the recess in three places and use the smallest width. Take 1 cm (3/8 in) off so the blind does not catch.
- **Blind outside the frame.** Make it 5 cm (2 in) wider than the window on each side, and mount it above the frame.

Fix rods and brackets into solid wall with plugs. A hollow wall or a false ceiling will not hold a heavy curtain. If the only place to fix is a false ceiling or a weak wall, ask your carpenter for a wooden board behind it or use a free-standing option.

## Fabric and care

- A tight weave blocks more light than a loose one. Hold the cloth up to a window and see how much daylight shows through.
- Cotton and cotton blends are easier to wash in a hot, dusty climate. Wash heavy curtains once or twice a year, according to the label.
- Keep curtains clear of heaters and the cooking area.
- Do not hang curtains where they touch a damp wall in the monsoon. They can pick up a musty smell.

## Worked example: a window facing a street

Take a living room window 1.5 m wide and 1.2 m high (4 ft 11 in x 3 ft 11 in). The sill is 0.9 m (2 ft 11 in) above the floor and the ceiling is 2.7 m (8 ft 10 in) high. The room faces a street, so the family wants a sheer and a heavy curtain.

1. **Top of the window.** 0.9 + 1.2 = 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in) above the floor.
2. **Rod height.** Put the rod 15 cm above the frame: 2.1 + 0.15 = 2.25 m (7 ft 5 in). The ceiling is 2.7 m, so 0.45 m remains above the rod.
3. **Rod width.** 25 cm each side: 1.5 + 0.25 + 0.25 = 2.0 m (6 ft 7 in).
4. **Fabric width.** Use a fullness of 2: 2.0 x 2 = 4.0 m (13 ft 1 in) of fabric across. A typical fabric is about 140 cm (4 ft 7 in) wide, so 4.0 / 1.4 = 2.86, which rounds up to 3 widths. Three widths give 3 x 1.4 = 4.2 m, so the fullness is 4.2 / 2.0 = 2.1.
5. **Finished length.** The curtain stops 2 cm above the floor: 2.25 - 0.02 = 2.23 m (7 ft 4 in).
6. **Cut length.** Add about 25 cm (10 in) for the hem and the heading: 2.23 + 0.25 = 2.48 m (8 ft 2 in).
7. **Fabric to buy.** 3 widths x 2.48 m = 7.44 m. Ask for 7.5 m (24 ft 7 in) for the heavy curtain.
8. **Sheer.** The same arithmetic applies to a sheer on its own rod, so ask for another 7.5 m.

Let the tailor confirm the heading style, as some styles use more or less fabric. During the day, open the heavy curtain and keep the sheer closed. In the evening, close both.

## Checklist for the tape measure

- Window width and height measured, and the sill height noted.
- Rod width: window plus 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) each side.
- Rod 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) above the frame.
- Curtain ends 1 to 2 cm (about 1/2 in) above the floor.
- Fabric width at least 1.5 times the rod, ideally 2 times.
- A solid wall to fix the brackets into.
- A sheer for day and a heavy layer for night where the street is close.

## Common mistakes

- **A rod the same width as the window.** The curtain covers the glass even when open, and the room loses light.
- **A rod fixed right at the frame.** The curtain hangs short, and light leaks over the top.
- **No fullness.** Fabric the same width as the rod hangs flat and looks cheap.
- **A curtain that drags on the floor.** It gathers dust and gets wet in the rain.
- **Only a sheer at night.** A lit room is visible from outside through a sheer.

## FAQ

### Are curtains or blinds better?
Curtains suit bedrooms and living rooms. Blinds suit kitchens, bathrooms and small windows.

### How wide should the curtain rod be?
Wider than the window by 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) on each side.

### Can I have privacy and still get daylight?
Yes. Use a sheer layer in the day, and keep the upper part of the window clear.

### How much fabric do I need?
Work out the rod width, multiply by 1.5 to 2, divide by the fabric width, and round up to whole widths. Multiply by the cut length.

### How do I stop heat coming through a west window?
Use a lined curtain or a blind close to the glass, light on the outside, and close it in the afternoon.
