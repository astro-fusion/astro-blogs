# Cross ventilation: how air really moves through a home

After reading this you will be able to walk through your home and say where the air enters, where it leaves and where it stalls. You will also be able to size and place windows so a breeze passes through a room instead of sitting in it.

## The basic idea

Air moves from higher pressure to lower pressure. Wind pushes air into a window on the side facing the breeze and sucks it out of a window on the sheltered side. Heat adds a second effect: warm air is lighter and rises, so it leaves through high openings while cooler air comes in low.

Cross ventilation means a clear path from an opening on one side of a room to an opening on another side. If there is no path, opening a window does little. Air needs both a way in and a way out.

## Two windows on one wall do not make a breeze

This is the most common surprise. Two windows on the same wall are both in the same pressure zone. Air may move in and out of the same opening, but it does not travel across the room. Aim for openings on opposite walls. Adjacent walls, forming an L, also work, though less strongly.

## Where the openings should sit

- **Opposite walls.** The straight path gives the strongest flow.
- **Different heights.** A low inlet and a high outlet add the rising-warm-air effect. A high window or a ventilator near the ceiling lets hot air out.
- **Outlet at least as large as the inlet.** A larger outlet keeps air moving.
- **Not behind furniture.** A tall wardrobe within 30 cm (1 ft) of a window blocks the flow.

A fan does not replace the opening. A ceiling fan mixes the air in the room, which helps you feel cooler, but it does not bring in fresh air unless a window is open.

## Doors are part of the path

In a flat, the path often runs through a room, a door, a passage and then another room. A shut door breaks the path. Air will squeeze under a door, but a gap of about 2 cm (3/4 in) at the bottom is a small route. For privacy at night, consider a door with a fixed louvre or a ventilation grille at the top, which lets air pass while the door stays closed. Ask your carpenter for it.

For a bathroom or toilet, leave a small gap at the bottom of the door and keep the window or exhaust fan working so damp air leaves.

## Size of openings

A common rule of thumb is that the openable area of windows should be at least one twentieth of the floor area, and more in hot, humid places. This is a guide for comfort, not a legal rule. Note the word "openable". A sliding window with two panels opens only half of its frame. A casement window with hinges can open fully.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) bedroom

The bedroom sits at the corner of a flat. One window is on the 3.6 m (12 ft) outer wall. The other outer wall, 3.0 m (10 ft) long, has a smaller window. The ceiling is 2.7 m (8 ft 10 in) high.

**Step 1. Floor area.** 3.0 x 3.6 = 10.8 sq m (116 sq ft).

**Step 2. Target openable area.** 10.8 / 20 = 0.54 sq m (5.8 sq ft).

**Step 3. Measure the first window.** A two-panel sliding window is 1.2 m wide and 1.0 m high (3 ft 11 in by 3 ft 3 in). Frame area is 1.2 x 1.0 = 1.2 sq m (12.9 sq ft). Only half opens, so the openable area is 1.2 / 2 = 0.6 sq m (6.5 sq ft).

**Step 4. Measure the second window.** It is 0.9 m wide and 0.6 m high (2 ft 11 in by 2 ft), set high on the other wall. A hinged window opens fully, so the openable area is 0.9 x 0.6 = 0.54 sq m (5.8 sq ft).

**Step 5. Compare.** The first window alone gives 0.6 sq m, which is above 0.54 sq m. But one window on one wall gives no through path. With both open, the total is 0.6 + 0.54 = 1.14 sq m (12.3 sq ft), and the air has an inlet and an outlet on different walls.

**Step 6. Check the path.** The windows are on adjacent walls, so the path crosses the room diagonally. Keep the bed and wardrobe out of that line, with at least 60 cm (2 ft) clear between the furniture and each window.

**Step 7. Make it stronger.** Make the high window the outlet and the low sliding window the inlet. Leave the door a small gap or fit a top grille so the room can also draw air from the passage.

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Draw your flat on paper and mark every window and door.
- Measure each window and note whether it opens fully or only half.
- Compute the openable area of the room and compare it with one twentieth of the floor area.
- Mark which walls face the main breeze in your area.
- Find a path from an opening on one side to an opening on another.
- Check that furniture stays at least 30 cm (1 ft) clear of every window.
- Check that doors on the path can be left open or have a grille.
- Check that the bathroom and kitchen can send air out of the home.

## Common mistakes

- **Two windows on one wall.** They do not create a breeze across the room.
- **Opening only the inlet.** A single open window gives weak flow.
- **Shut internal doors.** The path breaks, even if both outer windows are open.
- **Counting the frame as the opening.** Sliding windows open about half of their area.
- **Blocking the outlet with curtains.** Keep the outlet clear or use light mesh.

## Monsoon, dust and noise

In the monsoon a breeze can bring rain in. Keep high windows with a small overhang, or a canopy 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) deep, so they can stay open in light rain. In dusty or noisy places, open windows when the air outside is cooler or quieter, and use a fine mesh against mosquitoes and insects. A mesh slows air a little, so a bigger opening helps.

At night in the plains, outside air often turns cooler than the inside of a flat that has been heated by the sun all day. Open opposite windows then to flush out heat, and close the sun-facing windows in the day.

## FAQ

### Is one big window better than two small ones?

For a breeze, two openings on different walls work better. One big window on one wall can still let in light and some air, but air will not flow across the room.

### Does an exhaust fan count as ventilation?

Yes, if air can enter somewhere else. A fan in a closed room only moves air around. Open a window or leave a door gap so replacement air arrives.

### My flat has windows only on one side. What can I do?

Open the door on the passage side if you can, and ask whether a ventilator on the other side is possible. A fan placed in one window blowing out can help draw air in through the door. Do not cut openings in walls without a licensed engineer.

### Should I keep windows open in the day in summer?

In hot afternoons the outside air may be hotter than inside. Close sun-facing windows then and open them in the evening and early morning.

### Do curtains stop airflow?

Heavy curtains across an open window reduce it. Light curtains or a sheer layer let air pass.
