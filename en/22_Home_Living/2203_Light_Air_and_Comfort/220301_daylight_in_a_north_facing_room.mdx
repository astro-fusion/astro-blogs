# Getting more daylight into a north-facing room

By the end of this article you will know what kind of light a north-facing room really gets, how to check whether its window is big enough, and which cheap changes bring more daylight in. You will need a tape measure and ten minutes on a clear morning.

## What a north-facing room gets

In Nepal and India, which lie in the northern hemisphere, the sun stays in the southern half of the sky for most of the year. A room whose window faces north therefore gets steady, soft, indirect light through the day. It gets very little direct sun, and in winter almost none. A south-facing room gets the most winter sun, because the low winter sun shines straight in.

This has good and bad sides:

- The light is even and has little glare, which suits a study or a bedroom.
- The room can feel dim and cool, because the walls never receive direct sun.
- In summer, when the sun rises and sets slightly to the north, early morning and late evening sun can reach a north window for a short time.

So the job is not to fight the direction. It is to use the sky light you have as well as possible.

## How far daylight reaches into a room

Daylight is brightest near the window and fades with distance. A useful rule of thumb is that a window lights the floor well up to about one and a half to two times the height of the window top above the floor. Take a window whose top edge is 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in) above the floor. It lights the room well up to roughly 3.2 m (10 ft 6 in) from the window, and weakly up to about 4.2 m (13 ft 9 in).

The height of the window top matters more than its width. A tall window sends light deeper into the room than a wide, low one of the same area. If you can choose, raise the top of the window close to the ceiling, leaving only the beam space your engineer says is needed.

## How big should the window be

A common rule of thumb is a window area of about one tenth to one fifth of the floor area. Use the lower figure for a south or east room and aim for the higher one in a north room. This is a guide for comfort, not a legal rule. Measure the glass only, not the frame.

If you want to enlarge or move a window, the wall and the opening above it carry load. Ask a licensed engineer before anyone cuts a hole in a wall.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) bedroom

The room has one window on the 3.0 m (10 ft) north wall. The depth from window to far wall is 3.6 m (12 ft).

**Step 1. Floor area.** 3.0 x 3.6 = 10.8 sq m (116 sq ft).

**Step 2. Target glass area.** One tenth is 10.8 / 10 = 1.08 sq m (11.6 sq ft). One fifth is 10.8 / 5 = 2.16 sq m (23 sq ft). For a north room we aim near the upper end.

**Step 3. Measure what you have.** The window is 1.2 m wide and 1.0 m high (3 ft 11 in by 3 ft 3 in), with its sill at 1.1 m (3 ft 7 in) and its top at 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in). The glass is about 1.1 m by 0.9 m (3 ft 7 in by 2 ft 11 in), so 1.1 x 0.9 = 0.99 sq m (10.7 sq ft). This is just under the lower figure, so the room is likely to feel dim.

**Step 4. Check the reach.** The window top is 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in) high. Good light reaches 2.1 x 1.5 = 3.15 m (10 ft 4 in), and weak light reaches 2.1 x 2 = 4.2 m (13 ft 9 in). The room is 3.6 m (12 ft) deep, so the back wall sits in the weak zone.

**Step 5. Choose the changes.** Replace the window with one 1.5 m wide and 1.2 m high (4 ft 11 in by 3 ft 11 in), with the sill at 0.9 m (3 ft) and the top still at 2.1 m. The glass is then about 1.4 m by 1.1 m (4 ft 7 in by 3 ft 7 in), which is 1.4 x 1.1 = 1.54 sq m (16.6 sq ft). That sits between 1.08 and 2.16 sq m, nearer the upper end.

**Step 6. Add the free changes.** Paint the walls and ceiling a light, matt colour, move the wardrobe away from the window wall, and keep the sill clear. The new window needs an engineer's check first, so do the free changes while you wait.

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Measure the floor area and write down the one tenth and one fifth figures.
- Measure the glass only and compare.
- Measure the height from floor to window top and multiply by 1.5 and by 2.
- Compare those distances with the room depth.
- Check for anything outside the window that blocks the sky, such as a wall 3 m (9 ft 10 in) away or a tree.
- Check the glass is clean on both sides.
- Note which walls and furniture are dark and large.

## Cheap changes that help

1. **Light walls and ceiling.** Pale, matt paint reflects more light into the room. Gloss can throw glare.
2. **Pale floor and rug.** A dark floor soaks up the light that reaches it.
3. **Curtains that clear the glass.** Mount the rod 15 cm (6 in) beyond each side of the window so the curtains rest on the wall when open and no glass is covered.
4. **Sheer layers.** Use a thin curtain for privacy in the day and a heavier one only at night.
5. **Low furniture near the window.** Keep anything taller than 1.2 m (3 ft 11 in) away from the window wall.
6. **A mirror on the side wall.** A mirror at 1.0 to 1.5 m (3 ft 3 in to 4 ft 11 in) high, placed beside the window rather than facing it, bounces light along the wall.
7. **Clean grilles and nets.** A fine mesh or a dusty grille cuts light more than people expect.
8. **Evening light.** A lamp on the far side of the room balances the dim back wall after dark.

## Common mistakes

- **Painting a north room a dark colour.** It makes a dim room dimmer. Keep the largest surfaces pale.
- **Putting a tall wardrobe against the window wall.** It blocks side light and creates a dark corner.
- **Choosing only by width.** A wide, low window lights the room less deeply than a tall one.
- **Heavy curtains left half closed.** They cover the glass even in the day.
- **Cutting a wall opening without an engineer.** Never enlarge a window on your own.

## FAQ

### Is a north-facing room bad?

No. It has soft, even light and stays cooler in summer. It needs more care with colour and furniture because it gets little direct sun.

### Does a north-facing room get direct sun at any time?

Rarely. In summer the sun rises and sets a little north of east and west, so a short stretch of early or late sun can reach a north window. For most of the day the light is indirect.

### Which room should go on the north side?

Rooms that suit steady light with little glare, such as a study, a bedroom or a work corner. Rooms that you want warm in winter, such as a sitting room, do well on the south side.

### Will a mirror really help?

A mirror placed to catch the window light and send it back into the room helps a little. It cannot replace a bigger window or lighter walls.

### Can I add a skylight or light tube?

On the top floor this may be possible, but it cuts through the roof and its waterproofing. Ask a licensed engineer and a roofing contractor.
