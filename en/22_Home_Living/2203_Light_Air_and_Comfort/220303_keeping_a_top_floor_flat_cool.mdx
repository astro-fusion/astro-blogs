# Keeping a top-floor flat cool in summer

A top-floor flat has the sun on its roof from morning to evening. After reading this you will know where the heat comes from, which fixes give the most for the least cost, and which jobs need a roofing contractor or an engineer.

## Where the heat comes from

Heat enters a top-floor flat in three main ways.

- **Through the roof.** A flat concrete slab with no shade soaks up sun all day and gives the heat back into the rooms below, often well after sunset.
- **Through windows.** Direct sun through glass heats a room quickly. West-facing windows are the hardest, because the afternoon sun is low and shines straight in.
- **Through walls.** East and west walls that take the sun heat up and release warmth into the evening.

Heat also stays in. Once the walls and slab are warm, a closed flat cannot lose the heat. So the plan has two halves: keep the sun off, and let the heat out.

## Stop the sun first

### The roof

Shade or reflection on the roof does the most. Common measures are:

- A light-coloured, reflective coat or white tiles on the roof surface, which reflect more sun than dark concrete or bitumen.
- A second shade layer such as a shade net or a light shed over the roof, kept clear of the surface so air flows beneath it.
- Terrace pots and a few plants, which shade a little, if the roof can take the weight.

Be careful with each of these. The roof surface is also the waterproofing layer. A coat or tile laid over it must suit it, or it can crack and leak. A frame, shed or planting adds load and fixings. Before any of this, ask a roofing contractor about the coating and a licensed engineer about the weight and fixings. Do not store extra water tanks or heavy soil on the roof on your own judgment.

### The windows

Shade glass on the outside, where it works best. A curtain inside lets the glass heat up first.

- **Outside shade.** A projecting canopy or a fixed louvre above a south window blocks the high midday sun. For a west window, a vertical screen or a bamboo or cloth screen on the outside blocks the low afternoon sun.
- **Inside shade.** Light-coloured, lined curtains or blinds, closed from late morning, are better than nothing.
- **Reflective film.** Some films cut heat through glass. Ask the supplier for glass that suits your windows.

### The walls

A light-coloured paint on east and west outer walls reflects more of the sun. Trees and tall plants 3 to 5 m (10 to 16 ft) from a west wall shade it without touching it.

## Then let the heat out

At night, outside air is usually cooler than the air inside a flat that has been baked all day. Open windows on opposite sides in the evening and leave them open through the night where it is safe. A high opening lets warm air out and a low opening lets cooler air in. See the article on cross ventilation for sizes.

A ceiling fan does not lower the temperature of the air. It moves air over your skin so you feel cooler, and it helps only when someone is in the room. Switch it off when you leave. Fit the fan at least 2.3 m (7 ft 6 in) above the floor and at least 30 cm (1 ft) below the ceiling, and ask an electrician to fit it. Power cuts are common in many places, so shade and ventilation matter more than any machine that needs power.

A false ceiling with insulation or a gap above it can slow heat from the slab. It is a larger job. Ask the installer how it affects ceiling fans, light fittings and wiring, and have an electrician check any wiring.

## Worked example: a top-floor flat of 9.0 x 8.0 m (29 ft 6 in x 26 ft 3 in)

The flat has two outer walls facing south and west. Each wall is 2.8 m (9 ft 2 in) high, with the south wall 9.0 m long and the west wall 8.0 m long. There are two west windows of 1.5 x 1.2 m (4 ft 11 in x 3 ft 11 in) each.

**Step 1. Roof area.** 9.0 x 8.0 = 72 sq m (775 sq ft). This is the biggest area in full sun, so it comes first.

**Step 2. West wall.** 8.0 x 2.8 = 22.4 sq m (241 sq ft).

**Step 3. West glass.** Each window is 1.5 x 1.2 = 1.8 sq m (19.4 sq ft). Two windows give 3.6 sq m (38.8 sq ft).

**Step 4. Net west wall.** 22.4 minus 3.6 = 18.8 sq m (202 sq ft) of solid wall.

**Step 5. Rank the work.**

| Surface | Area | First action |
|---|---|---|
| Roof | 72 sq m (775 sq ft) | Reflective coat or shade, after a contractor checks the waterproofing |
| West wall | 18.8 sq m (202 sq ft) | Light paint, shade tree if there is room |
| West glass | 3.6 sq m (38.8 sq ft) | Outside screen and lined curtains |

**Step 6. Ventilation.** Plan opposite openings on south and north, or on east and west, and open them at night.

**Step 7. Order.** The glass is the smallest area but the heat comes straight in, so it is also the cheapest to fix first. Screens and curtains can be done this week. The roof needs a contractor, so book that before the peak season.

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Measure the roof, the sun-facing walls and the sun-facing glass, and write the three areas down.
- Note which windows get direct sun after noon.
- Check that each window can be shaded on the outside.
- Check the roof surface for cracks or ponding water, and ask a roofing contractor to look.
- Measure the height of the ceiling fans and the gap to the ceiling.
- Check which windows are open at night and whether they sit on opposite sides.
- Check that curtains and furniture do not block night ventilation.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating only the room, not the roof.** Fans cannot fix a slab that is heating the whole flat.
- **Laying a coat or tiles on a leaking roof.** Fix leaks first, with a professional.
- **Closing all the windows at night.** The heat stays trapped.
- **Heavy dark curtains inside a west window.** They stop light but not heat.
- **Loading the roof without an engineer.** Tanks, soil and sheds all add weight.

## FAQ

### Does painting the roof white really help?

A light, reflective surface takes in less of the sun than a dark one, so the slab stays cooler. Use a product made for roofs and check it with a contractor so it does not harm the waterproofing.

### Is a false ceiling worth it?

It can slow the heat from the slab, but it is not the first step. Shade the roof and windows first, since that stops the heat entering.

### Which side of the flat is hottest?

The west side, in the afternoon and evening, and the roof throughout the day.

### Can I grow plants on the roof?

Some pots are fine, but a garden adds weight and wet soil near the waterproofing. Ask a licensed engineer first.

### Will a cooler or air conditioner fix it?

A machine can cool one room, but it works harder against a hot slab. Shading and ventilation lower the load first.
