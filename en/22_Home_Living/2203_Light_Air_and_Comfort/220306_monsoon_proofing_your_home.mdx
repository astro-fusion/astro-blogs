# Getting your home ready for the monsoon: a checklist

After reading this you will be able to walk through your home a few weeks before the rains and find the small problems that become big ones in July: a blocked drain, a gap round a window, a wardrobe pressed against a damp wall. You will also get sizes for raising belongings off the floor and a worked example for drying clothes indoors.

Most monsoon trouble starts as water that has nowhere to go. Your job before the rains is to let water leave, keep it out of gaps, and keep your belongings away from damp surfaces.

## Start from the top: roof, terrace and drains

Water on a roof or terrace has to run to an outlet. Walk up on a dry day and look.

- Clear leaves, plastic bags and dust from every drain outlet and the mouth of each downpipe.
- Check that the terrace surface slopes towards the outlets. After a short shower, water that stays standing in puddles deeper than a coin means water is not leaving freely.
- Look for cracks in the terrace surface, loose patches and gaps where a wall meets the floor of the terrace.
- Check the roof edge, parapet and water tank stand for stains or loose plaster.
- Make sure the water tank lid fits, so rain and insects stay out.

You can sweep, clear and take photos yourself. Repairing a terrace surface, sealing cracks or doing any waterproofing is work for a qualified waterproofing contractor, and a crack that is wide or growing in a wall or slab should be seen by a licensed engineer. Do not climb onto a wet roof.

## Outside walls and windows

Rain driven by wind finds every gap.

- Run a hand around each window and door frame. A gap you can see light through will let water in.
- Look at the window sill. It should slope slightly outwards, and a small groove or overhang underneath helps water drip away from the wall instead of running down it.
- Check the paint on outside walls. Flaking paint and dark stains below a window or pipe often point to a leak above.
- Clear weep holes and gutters if you have them.
- Trim tree branches that rub on the roof or hang over the terrace.

Fill small gaps around frames with a flexible sealant from a hardware shop and let it cure fully before the rains.

## Inside: keep belongings off damp surfaces

Walls and floors carry damp, especially on the ground floor and on outside walls.

- Pull wardrobes and beds 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in) away from outside walls so air can pass behind them.
- Raise anything stored on the floor by at least 15 cm (6 in). Use bricks or plastic blocks under legs, or a low platform.
- Keep the lowest shelf of a cupboard for things that do not mind damp, such as steel utensils. Store paper, books and cloth higher.
- Keep documents in sealed plastic bags inside a box on a high shelf.
- Open windows on dry spells in the day so rooms can air out. Damp and mould have their own causes and first steps, covered in a separate article.

## Electricity, water and power cuts

Water and electricity do not mix.

- Keep extension boards, chargers and the inverter off the floor.
- If you see wet patches near switches or a wall socket, switch off at the mains and call a licensed electrician. Do not touch it.
- Ask an electrician to check earthing and the circuit breaker before the rains. Do not do this yourself.
- Charge torches, power banks and phones before a storm. Keep a torch where you can find it in the dark.
- Store drinking water in clean covered containers in case a supply is cut.
- If your area floods, know the route to higher ground and where the main switch is.

## Drying clothes in a wet season

When nothing dries outside, the washing line moves indoors, and a room full of wet cloth adds damp air. Dry clothes where there is a draught, with a window or door open a little, and not inside a closed bedroom. A balcony, a stairwell with an opening or a bathroom with a working exhaust fan are all better than a shut room.

## Worked example: a drying line for a family of five

Take a family of five in a 2BHK with a spare room of 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft). Each person has about three items to dry each day, such as a kurta, a towel and a shirt. That is 5 x 3 = 15 items.

1. **Line space per item.** Allow about 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) of line for each item on a hanger so the cloth does not touch its neighbour.
2. **Total line length.** 15 x 40 cm = 600 cm, which is 6.0 m (19 ft 8 in).
3. **Length of one line.** Run lines along the 3.6 m (12 ft) wall. Leave 30 cm (1 ft) free at each end for the hooks and the corner: 3.6 - 0.3 - 0.3 = 3.0 m (10 ft).
4. **Number of lines.** 6.0 / 3.0 = 2 lines.
5. **Spacing.** Put the two lines 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) apart so hung clothes can swing without touching.
6. **Height.** Fix the lines at about 2.0 m (6 ft 7 in) so long kurtas clear the floor and heads.
7. **Air.** Open the window on the opposite wall a hand's width, and leave the door ajar. A small fan pointed along the lines moves the damp air out.

Fix hooks into a solid wall with proper plugs. Do not hang a loaded line from a false ceiling or a light fitting. If the only choice is a thin partition wall, use a free-standing rack instead.

## Checklist for the tape measure and a torch

- Roof and terrace drains clear, and the terrace slopes to the outlets.
- Gaps round windows and doors sealed.
- Window sills slope outwards.
- Beds and wardrobes 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in) off outside walls.
- Stored items at least 15 cm (6 in) off the floor.
- Documents in sealed bags on a high shelf.
- Extension boards and the inverter off the floor.
- Torch, power bank and drinking water ready.
- A drying line planned: 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) per item.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting in the first week of rain.** Roofs cannot be cleared or sealed safely in a downpour, so do it a few weeks earlier.
- **Pushing furniture hard against an outside wall.** The wall behind stays wet and cold, and the back of the wardrobe grows mould.
- **Stuffing a damp cloth into a closed cupboard.** It spreads the smell to the clothes beside it.
- **Painting over a damp patch.** The paint peels again, and the cause is still there.
- **Fixing electrical problems yourself.** Wet wiring is a job for a licensed electrician.

## FAQ

### When should I check the roof and drains?
A few weeks before the rains arrive in your area, on a dry day, so any repair has time to cure.

### Can I seal a roof crack myself?
You can clear debris and note the crack. Sealing a terrace or fixing a crack in a wall or slab is for a waterproofing contractor or a licensed engineer.

### How high should I raise things off the floor?
At least 15 cm (6 in) is a sensible minimum on a ground floor, higher if your street floods.

### How do I dry clothes indoors without making the room damp?
Use a spare room or balcony with some air movement, keep a window open a little, and give each item about 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) of line.

### What should I do if water comes through a wall or ceiling?
Move belongings away, keep clear of any wet switches and wiring, switch off at the mains if it is near electricity, and call a professional to find where it is coming from.
