# Keeping a home warm in a hill winter

After reading this you will know where a house in the hills loses its warmth, which low-cost steps hold it in, and how to use a heater without creating a danger. Most of the work is cheap and you can do it in a weekend with a tape measure.

## Where the warmth goes

A home loses heat in four ways, and they are easy to find.

- **Gaps.** Air leaks round window frames, under doors and through old shutters. Cold air coming in at your feet is the sign.
- **Glass.** A single sheet of glass is a thin barrier, and a large window on a shaded side loses a lot.
- **Walls and roof.** Thin or uninsulated walls and a bare roof let heat through. Changing them is a bigger job for a contractor.
- **Floors.** A bare cold floor draws warmth from your feet and from the room.

The aim is to keep the sun's warmth in during the day and cut the leaks at night.

## Use the winter sun

In Nepal and India the winter sun is low in the south. A south-facing room gets the most direct sun, and a north-facing room gets almost none. So:

- Open south and east curtains fully in the morning and keep them open while the sun shines.
- Keep trees and sheds that shade a south window trimmed, if you can.
- Close curtains as the sun leaves the room, in the late afternoon, to hold the warmth in.
- If you have a choice, sleep and sit in the rooms that get sun, and use the north rooms for storage.

## Stop the draughts

This is the cheapest and most useful job.

1. On a cold evening, hold a thin strip of paper or a lit incense stick near the window and door edges. If it moves, there is a gap.
2. Fit self-adhesive foam or rubber strip along the frame of windows and doors.
3. Fit a draught excluder, a rolled cloth or a door-bottom strip, across the gap under each door. A gap of 1 cm (3/8 in) is enough to matter.
4. Seal small cracks in the frame with a flexible sealant.
5. Close any unused chimney or vent opening, but keep any ventilation that a heater needs.

## Curtains and rugs

Heavy, lined curtains trap a layer of still air against the glass. For them to work:

- The rod should extend 15 cm (6 in) beyond each side of the window and sit 10 cm (4 in) above the frame.
- The curtain should reach the sill or, better, almost to the floor, about 1 cm (3/8 in) above it.
- The fabric width should be about 1.5 to 2 times the width of the rod, so it falls in full folds.

A thick rug on a cold floor, with a mat or pad beneath, helps. In a single-room home, a curtain across the door of an unheated room keeps the warm room warm.

## Moisture and warmth

Drying wet clothes indoors, cooking and bathing add moisture to a cold home. On cold walls this can turn to damp. Where you can, dry clothes in the sun at midday, and open a window for a few minutes in the middle of the day to change the air. A short, wide opening wastes less heat than a window left slightly open all night. See the article on damp and mould for more.

## Heating safely

A heater changes the room, so use one with care.

- **Charcoal, wood or gas heaters.** These burn fuel and use up the air in a closed room. They also give off carbon monoxide, a gas you cannot smell that is dangerous. Never burn charcoal or wood inside a closed room, and never leave a gas heater on while you sleep. Always keep a window open a small amount and follow the maker's instructions.
- **Electric heaters.** These draw a high load. Plug them straight into a wall socket that suits the load, not into a long extension board or a light plug. Ask a licensed electrician if the socket and wiring are suitable. Power cuts are common, so do not rely on electricity alone.
- **Clearances.** Keep a heater at least 1 m (3 ft 3 in) from curtains, bedding and clothes. Keep it on a flat floor, away from children and animals.
- **Hot water bottles and blankets.** These warm the person, not the room, and cost much less to run.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.6 m (10 x 12 ft) bedroom

The room has two windows of 1.2 m wide and 1.5 m high (3 ft 11 in by 4 ft 11 in) and one door of 0.9 m wide and 2.1 m high (2 ft 11 in by 6 ft 11 in).

**Step 1. Tape for each window.** Go round the sash on both sides and top and bottom: 2 x (1.2 + 1.5) = 5.4 m (17 ft 9 in).

**Step 2. Tape for two windows.** 5.4 x 2 = 10.8 m (35 ft 5 in).

**Step 3. Tape for the door.** Two sides and the top: 2.1 + 2.1 + 0.9 = 5.1 m (16 ft 9 in).

**Step 4. Total.** 10.8 + 5.1 = 15.9 m (52 ft 2 in). Buy 18 m (59 ft) so a few joins and mistakes do not leave you short.

**Step 5. Curtain width.** The rod runs 1.2 + 0.15 + 0.15 = 1.5 m (4 ft 11 in) across each window. Fabric at twice that width is 1.5 x 2 = 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in) per window.

**Step 6. Curtain drop.** The sill is at 0.9 m (3 ft) and the window top is at 0.9 + 1.5 = 2.4 m (7 ft 10 in). The rod sits 10 cm (4 in) above the frame, at 2.4 + 0.1 = 2.5 m (8 ft 2 in). A curtain ending 1 cm (3/8 in) above the floor drops 2.5 - 0.01 = 2.49 m (8 ft 2 in), so ask the tailor for about 2.5 m of finished length.

**Step 7. Door strip.** The door is 0.9 m wide (2 ft 11 in), so buy a 0.9 m excluder or cut a rolled cloth to 1.0 m (3 ft 3 in).

## Checklist with a tape measure

- Walk the room on a cold evening and mark every gap.
- Measure each window and door frame and add up the tape you need.
- Measure the curtain rod and fabric width for each window.
- Check that curtains hang almost to the floor and clear of any heater by at least 1 m (3 ft 3 in).
- Check which rooms get winter sun and which do not.
- Check that heaters sit on a proper socket and that a window can stay open a little.

## Common mistakes

- **Sealing every gap with a fuel heater running.** The room needs some fresh air.
- **Short curtains.** They leave a cold draught between the cloth and the floor.
- **Closing sun-facing curtains in the day.** You lose free heat.
- **Using a light extension board for a heater.** It may overheat.
- **Ignoring the floor.** A bare cold floor makes the room feel colder.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest thing that makes the biggest difference?

Stopping draughts around windows and doors, then lined curtains.

### Should I keep windows shut all winter?

Keep them shut most of the time, but open them briefly each day to change the air. Dry air and fresh air keep damp away.

### Is a charcoal heater safe in a bedroom?

No. Do not burn charcoal or wood in a closed room, and never leave a fuel heater on while you sleep.

### Do thick curtains really help?

Yes, lined curtains that reach nearly to the floor and fit close to the wall hold a layer of still air against the glass.

### Can I add insulation to the walls or roof?

That is a larger job. Ask a contractor, and ask a licensed engineer about any changes to the structure.
