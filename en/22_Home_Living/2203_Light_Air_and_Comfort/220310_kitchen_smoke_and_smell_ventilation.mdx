# Kitchen smoke and smell: chimney, exhaust fan and windows

After reading this you will be able to decide between a chimney, an exhaust fan and plain windows for your kitchen, place each one at a sensible height and size, and keep the smell of frying out of the rest of the flat. You will get a worked example that turns your kitchen size into a number you can compare with a chimney's rating.

Cooking with oil and spices makes smoke, steam and smell. The idea is simple: catch it close to the pan, push it outside, and let fresh air come in to replace it.

## How a kitchen clears the air

Three things must work together.

- **Capture.** A hood or chimney above the hob catches the rising smoke and steam. A hood that is too small or too high lets it escape round the edges.
- **Exhaust.** A fan pushes the air out through a duct or a wall opening. The shorter and straighter the path, the better it works.
- **Fresh air in.** When a fan pulls air out, the same amount must come in. If the windows and doors are shut, a fan struggles, and smoke leaves through gaps into other rooms. Open a window or a vent on the opposite side of the kitchen while you cook.

## Chimney, exhaust fan or window

- **Chimney (hood with a fan and filter).** The best choice for frying and strong spices. It sits over the hob and takes smoke where it is made. It needs a point for the fan and usually a duct to the outside.
- **Exhaust fan in a wall or window.** A cheaper and simpler option for lighter cooking. It works best when mounted high, as close to the hob as the wall allows.
- **Windows only.** Fine for light cooking if the kitchen has windows on two sides. Weak when it is still or humid.
- **Recirculating hood.** Some hoods filter the air and send it back into the room. They catch grease but do not take the heat, steam or most of the smell out of the house.

Many homes use a chimney plus a window for fresh air, and an exhaust fan only in a small kitchen with no room for a duct.

## Sizes and heights

These are common-practice figures. Always follow the instructions that come with your hood or fan.

- **Chimney width.** At least as wide as the hob. For a hob of 60 cm (2 ft), use a chimney of 60 cm, or 90 cm (3 ft) for better coverage.
- **Height above the hob.** Fix the bottom of the chimney 65 to 75 cm (2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 6 in) above the hob. Lower and the cook bangs a head, higher and smoke escapes.
- **Counter height.** A common counter is 85 cm (2 ft 9 in). A hob on it puts the chimney bottom near 155 cm (5 ft 1 in) from the floor.
- **Duct size.** Many chimneys use a duct 150 mm (6 in) across. Use the size the maker states.
- **Duct run.** Keep it short, with as few bends as possible, and run it as straight as you can. Each bend cuts the airflow. Use smooth metal pipe, not thin flexible foil.
- **Exhaust fan size.** Common wall fans are 150 mm (6 in) and 225 mm (9 in). Place it high, near the cooking, not beside the door through which the air should enter.
- **Fresh air opening.** A window or vent of about 60 x 60 cm (2 x 2 ft) on the opposite side is a useful starting point.

## Walls, wiring and gas

- A hole in a wall for a duct or a fan is work for a contractor. Never cut a hole in a beam, column or a wall that may carry load. Ask a licensed engineer first, and check the building society rules for outside walls.
- The wiring point for a chimney or a fan is for a licensed electrician. Keep it away from the hob's heat and from splashes.
- Any gas pipe, regulator or cylinder fitting is for a licensed gas fitter. Ventilation does not make a gas leak safe. If you smell gas, open windows, do not use switches, and leave the room.

## Keeping it working

- Wash the grease filter often, more often if you fry daily. A clogged filter drags the airflow down and is a fire risk.
- Wipe the hood and the wall behind the hob.
- Turn the fan on before you start cooking and leave it for a few minutes after.
- Close the kitchen door while cooking so smell does not travel.
- Keep the lid on pots and cover food where you can.
- Empty the bin often, since rubbish also smells.

## Worked example: a 2.4 x 3.0 m kitchen

Take a kitchen 2.4 x 3.0 m (8 x 10 ft) with a ceiling of 2.7 m (8 ft 10 in), a 60 cm (2 ft) hob, a counter at 85 cm (2 ft 9 in) and a window on the far wall.

1. **Volume of the kitchen.** 2.4 x 3.0 x 2.7 = 19.44 cubic metres.
2. **Air to move.** A common rule of thumb for a kitchen with heavy cooking is to change the air 10 to 15 times an hour. 19.44 x 10 = 194.4 and 19.44 x 15 = 291.6, so about 195 to 290 cubic metres of air per hour.
3. **Choose a rating.** Chimney ratings are quoted without a duct. Bends and a dirty filter lose some airflow, so pick a rating clearly above 290 cubic metres per hour. Ask the seller what it is measured with and for the duct size.
4. **Chimney width.** A 60 cm hob suits a 60 cm chimney. Choose 90 cm if you often cook on two burners at the edge.
5. **Chimney height.** Hob at 85 cm, bottom of the chimney 70 cm above it: 85 + 70 = 155 cm (5 ft 1 in) from the floor.
6. **Room above.** The ceiling is 270 cm, so 270 - 155 = 115 cm (3 ft 9 in) is left above the bottom of the chimney for its body and the duct.
7. **Fresh air.** The window on the far wall is the entry. Open it a hand's width or more while the chimney runs.

Ask the installer for the shortest straight route to the outside and the number of bends.

## Checklist for the tape measure

- Hob width measured, and the chimney at least as wide.
- Bottom of the chimney 65 to 75 cm (2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 6 in) above the hob.
- Duct as short and straight as possible, 150 mm (6 in) or the maker's size.
- Opening or window on the opposite side for fresh air.
- Exhaust fan placed high, near the cooking.
- Grease filter on a cleaning routine.
- Wall hole, wiring and gas work booked with licensed professionals.

## Common mistakes

- **A chimney hung too high.** It looks tidy, but smoke slips round it.
- **A long duct with many bends.** The fan works hard and moves little.
- **No way for air to come in.** The fan is weak and smoke goes into the flat.
- **Choosing by looks.** Check width, height and duct before the design.
- **Never cleaning the filter.** Grease builds up and the airflow falls.

## FAQ

### Do I need a chimney or is an exhaust fan enough?
For frying and strong spices, a chimney catches smoke better. An exhaust fan is enough for light cooking in a small kitchen.

### How high should the chimney be above the hob?
Usually 65 to 75 cm (2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 6 in).

### Can I use a chimney without a duct?
Yes, a recirculating type, but it removes grease, not heat, steam and most of the smell.

### How do I stop the smell reaching the bedrooms?
Run the fan during cooking and for a few minutes after, keep the kitchen door shut and a window open.

### Who should fit the duct and the wiring?
A contractor for the duct and a licensed electrician for the wiring. Ask an engineer before any hole in a structural wall.
