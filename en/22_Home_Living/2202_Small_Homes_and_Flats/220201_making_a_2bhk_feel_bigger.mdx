# Making a 2BHK Feel Bigger Without Breaking a Wall

A 2BHK flat feels small mostly because of what stands on the floor and how far you can see, not only because of its size. After reading this you will be able to measure your living room, decide what to move or remove, and make changes a carpenter and a painter can finish without touching any wall.

## What makes a room feel larger

Three things change how big a room feels: how much floor you can see, how far your eye travels, and how much daylight reaches the walls. You cannot add square feet, but you can improve all three.

- **Visible floor.** Furniture on legs, with 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) of space underneath, shows more floor than a solid box that sits on the ground.
- **Long sight lines.** If you can see from the front door to the far window, the flat reads as deeper.
- **Even light.** Light walls and uncluttered windows bounce daylight further into the room.

## Start with the floor

Walk through the flat and stand at the door of each room. Anything that blocks your path or your view is a candidate to move.

### Keep the main path open

A comfortable walking path is 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) wide at the least. In the living room, aim for 90 cm (3 ft) between the sofa and the opposite wall unit where family members cross the room most often. A path narrower than 75 cm makes people turn sideways, and the room feels crowded even when it is not.

### Choose fewer, larger pieces

Many small tables, stools and shelves break up the floor and the eye. One 3-seater sofa, one low table and one wall unit feel calmer than seven small items. Put small items inside the wall unit or on a single tray.

### Raise furniture off the floor

Ask the carpenter for legs of 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) on the TV unit, the sofa and the bed base. A floating or wall-hung unit leaves the floor visible all the way to the wall. Check with the carpenter what the wall is made of before anything heavy is hung on it, and ask a licensed engineer if you are not sure.

## Use light and colour

Light, matt wall colours reflect more daylight than dark ones. Use one main wall colour across the living room, dining area and passage, so that the eye does not stop at each door.

Hang curtains high and wide:

- Fix the rod about 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) above the window frame, or close to the ceiling.
- Make the curtain extend 20 to 30 cm (8 in to 1 ft) beyond the window on each side, so that when it is open it does not cover the glass.
- Use a light fabric in the daytime layer so that sun still comes in when the curtain is drawn.

A mirror placed on the wall beside or opposite a window carries daylight deeper into the room. Do not put it where it reflects the cooking area or a pile of clutter.

## Make the flat read as one space

Doors and passages can be tidier without changing a wall.

- Take off doors that you never close, for example to the dining nook, only if the room does not need privacy or a puja space.
- Use the same floor tile or finish in every connected room, so that there is no line across the floor.
- Do not remove or widen any wall or opening yourself. If you want a larger opening between the kitchen and living room, a licensed structural engineer must check whether the wall carries load before anyone touches it.

## Worked example: a 3.3 x 4.2 m living room

Take a family of five in a 2BHK with a living room of 3.3 x 4.2 m (10 ft 10 in x 13 ft 9 in). That is 13.86 square metres (about 149 sq ft). The room has a 3-seater sofa of 200 x 90 cm (6 ft 7 in x 3 ft), a low table of 90 x 50 cm (3 ft x 1 ft 8 in) and a TV unit 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) deep.

1. Put the sofa and the TV unit on the two long walls, facing each other across the 3.3 m (10 ft 10 in) width.
2. Width left between them: 330 cm minus 90 cm of sofa minus 40 cm of TV unit equals 200 cm (6 ft 7 in).
3. Place the low table so that there are 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) between sofa and table for knees and feet.
4. Table depth is 50 cm (1 ft 8 in). So the table and the knee gap take 40 plus 50 equals 90 cm.
5. Walking path left in front of the TV unit: 200 cm minus 90 cm equals 110 cm (3 ft 7 in). That is more than the 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) minimum, so the family can cross the room without moving the table.
6. Along the 4.2 m (13 ft 9 in) length, the 200 cm (6 ft 7 in) sofa leaves 420 minus 200 equals 220 cm (7 ft 3 in) of wall. Use it for the door side and a single tall shelf, not for another sofa.

A second 90 cm (3 ft) deep sofa would cut that path to 110 minus 90 equals 20 cm. Use pouffes that slide under the table instead.

## Checklist for your tape measure

- [ ] Measure the room width and length, and write them in cm.
- [ ] Measure the main path from door to window. Is it at least 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) wide?
- [ ] Check the gap between sofa and table: aim for about 40 cm (1 ft 4 in).
- [ ] Count loose small items on the floor and move them into closed storage.
- [ ] Measure the space under each piece of furniture, and ask for 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) legs where it is solid.
- [ ] Check the curtain rod: 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 in) above the window frame.
- [ ] Stand at the front door and note what blocks the view to the far window.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying furniture first, measuring later.** A sofa that is 10 cm (4 in) too deep can take away the whole walking path.
2. **Dark colours on all four walls.** They absorb daylight, so the room looks smaller and needs lights earlier in the day. Keep dark colours for one accent wall.
3. **Short curtains hung at the window frame.** They cut the wall into pieces and make the window look small.
4. **Filling every wall.** A bare stretch of wall gives the eye a rest. Leave at least one wall mostly clear.
5. **Treating a wall as a decorative choice.** Removing or widening a wall is a structural question for a licensed engineer, not a design shortcut.

## FAQ

### Can a mirror really make a room look bigger?
A mirror does not add space. It reflects light and the view, so a room with a mirror opposite or beside a window looks brighter and a little deeper. Place it where it reflects something you want to see.

### What is the smallest path I should leave in a living room?
A comfortable minimum is 75 cm (2 ft 6 in). Where several people cross at once, for example between the sofa and the dining table, aim for 90 cm (3 ft).

### Should I use light colours in a flat that gets little sun?
Light, matt colours help a dim room because they reflect the light that does come in. Keep the same colour on the ceiling and walls in a low room, and check the paint in the room at several times of the day before you buy a full tin.

### Can I join the kitchen and the living room to get more space?
Only a licensed structural engineer can say whether a wall can be opened. Do not start work until you have their written opinion, and your housing society may also need to approve.
