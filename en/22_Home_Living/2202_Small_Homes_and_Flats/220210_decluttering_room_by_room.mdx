# Decluttering room by room: a practical order

Most people start decluttering in the wrong place, with the memories and the papers, and stop after an hour. After reading this you will have an order that begins with the easy rooms, a time plan you can fit into three half-days, and a simple way to decide what stays. You will also know where to be careful: documents, medicines, religious items and electronics.

## The rules before you start

Sorting is quicker when you decide the rules first.

- Work one zone at a time, such as one shelf or one drawer. Finish it before you move on.
- Use four boxes or bags: keep, give away, recycle or discard, and decide later. The "decide later" box gets a date on it, and anything still untouched on that date goes out.
- Take everything out of the zone first. You cannot judge what you cannot see.
- Put back only what you keep, and put it back where you will look for it.
- Do not buy storage boxes until you have finished sorting. Measure what is left, then buy.

## The order

Start where decisions are easy and the benefit is quick. Leave the emotional rooms for last, when you have practice.

### 1. Entrance and shoes

Count the shoes. Keep what the household wears in a season and move the rest out of the rack. Broken umbrellas and old bags go. Whatever does not fit on the rack, which is often about 90 cm (3 ft) wide, belongs in a box inside a cupboard, not at the door.

### 2. Bathroom

Check dates on medicines and creams. Bring expired medicines to a pharmacy and ask how to dispose of them safely. Do not throw them in the drain. Bottles that are nearly empty are merged or finished. Keep one towel per person, plus two spares.

### 3. Kitchen

Take out everything from one cupboard at a time. Remove chipped plates, lidless boxes and duplicates of tools that you never use. Check packets of flour, lentils and spices for damp and insects in the monsoon. Keep daily items between knee and shoulder height, about 50 cm to 1.5 m (1 ft 8 in to 4 ft 11 in) from the floor, and heavy pots low. Leave the gas connection and any wiring alone; ask a professional for any repair.

### 4. Wardrobes

Do each person's wardrobe on its own. Try on what you doubt. Clothes that do not fit, are damaged beyond repair, or have not been worn for a full year go out. Keep festival and wedding clothes that you will wear again, and store them in cloth bags rather than plastic.

### 5. Living room

Clear surfaces, then the shelf under the television and the corner heaps of newspapers and cables. Old chargers and cables without a device go to electronic waste. Decorative objects keep only a few at a time.

### 6. Children's room

Involve the child. Sort toys into those used this month, those to keep for later, and those to pass on. Books and school notebooks from earlier years can go, except those the child wants.

### 7. Store room, loft and under-bed boxes

Do these last of the physical rooms, because they hold what you postponed. Pull out one box at a time and open it. If you have not opened it in a year and cannot say what is in it, the contents are probably not essential. Ask an engineer before you load a loft more heavily.

### 8. Papers and documents

Last of all. Keep originals of identity, citizenship or ID papers, property papers, school and degree certificates, insurance, loan and tax records. Do not discard any paper you are unsure about; scan it and keep it safe. Shred old bills and bank papers that you no longer need.

### Religious and sentimental items

Do not bin old puja pictures, idols or worn religious items. Ask an elder, or your priest, how to retire them respectfully. For sentimental items keep one box per person, with a fixed size, and when it is full, sort it.

## A worked example: a bedroom of 3.0 x 3.6 m with a 1.2 m wide wardrobe

Take a household of three bedrooms plus living room and kitchen: eight zones as listed above.

**Step 1, the time.** Allow 30 minutes for the entrance, 30 for the bathroom, 90 for the kitchen, 90 for the wardrobes, 45 for the living room, 60 for the children's room, 90 for the store, and 60 for the papers. The total is 30 + 30 + 90 + 90 + 45 + 60 + 90 + 60 = 495 minutes, which is 8 hours 15 minutes. In three sessions, each is 495 divided by 3, or 165 minutes (2 hours 45 minutes).

**Step 2, the rail in the bedroom wardrobe.** The wardrobe is 1.2 m (3 ft 11 in) wide. Allow 10 cm for the two side panels, which leaves a rail of about 1.1 m (3 ft 7 in). A hanger with a garment takes about 5 cm (2 in) of rail.

- Rail capacity: 110 divided by 5 is 22 garments.
- Packed rails make clothes crushed and hard to find, so aim to leave 4 spaces, which is 20 cm (8 in) empty.
- Target: 22 minus 4 is 18 hanging garments for one person.

**Step 3, the rule.** If the rail holds 25 garments now, 7 must go: 25 minus 18. Take everything off, put back the 18 you wear most, and place the rest in the boxes.

**Step 4, the floor.** The bedroom is 3.0 x 3.6 = 10.8 sq m (116 sq ft). Mark the walking path of 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) from the door to the wardrobe and the bed. If a box or trunk sits in the path, it goes into the "decide later" box.

## Checklist

1. Pick three days and book the time.
2. Prepare four boxes or bags and a marker.
3. Start at the entrance, not the store room.
4. Empty one zone, sort, and put back only what stays.
5. Count items against the space: rails, shelves, shoe racks.
6. Take the give-away bag out of the house the same day.
7. Set a date for the "decide later" box.
8. Measure what remains before buying storage.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting with photographs and papers.** They are the slowest and most emotional.
- **Doing the whole house in a day.** You tire, and the boxes end up in the living room.
- **Buying boxes first.** You buy storage for things you would have discarded.
- **Moving clutter to the store room.** It only changes where the heap sits.
- **Throwing away documents or religious items without thought.** Keep originals and ask elders.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does decluttering a 2BHK take?
Allow about 8 hours in total, spread over three sessions, as in the example. A very full home needs more.

### What do I do with clothes I am unsure about?
Put them in the "decide later" box with a date. If you have not taken any out by then, give them away.

### Where can I give away what I do not need?
Ask relatives, neighbours, a school, or a local charity. Call first to ask what they accept.

### Should I declutter before or after painting?
Before. You can see the walls, and you will not need to cover as many things.

### How do I stop the clutter from returning?
Keep a fixed space for each kind of item and, when the space is full, take something out before you bring something in.
