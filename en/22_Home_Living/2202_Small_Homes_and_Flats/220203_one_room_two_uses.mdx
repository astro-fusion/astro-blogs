# One Room, Two Uses: A Guest Room and Study Together

Most small flats have one spare room, and it has to be a study for most of the year and a bedroom for the weeks when guests stay. After reading this you will be able to choose a bed, a desk and a layout that fit a room of about 3 x 3.3 m, and set the room up for a guest in a few minutes.

## Decide which use comes first

The room has a main job and a part-time job. Ask how many days a month a guest sleeps here.

- If guests come a few times a year, the room is a study. The bed must be easy to hide or fold.
- If guests stay often, the room is a guest room and the desk must be easy to clear.

Whichever you choose, give each use its own place, so that changing from one to the other takes minutes and not an afternoon.

## Choose a bed that does not look like a bed

### Diwan or sofa bed

A single diwan of 90 x 190 cm (2 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in) with a box base works as a sofa by day. Put bolsters along the wall and it reads as seating. The box below holds guest bedding. Ask the carpenter for a seat height of about 40 to 45 cm (1 ft 4 in to 1 ft 6 in) so that adults can sit and stand easily.

### Wall bed

A wall bed folds up into a cabinet. It saves floor but costs more in carpentry and needs strong fixings. Ask a carpenter who has built one, and ask a licensed engineer if the wall that carries it is not a plain, solid wall. For a single bed the closed cabinet is about 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) wide and 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) deep.

### Mattress on the floor

A folding mattress stored in a loft or under the desk is the cheapest option. It needs a clear rectangle of 90 x 190 cm (2 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in) plus a 60 cm (2 ft) strip beside it to get in and out.

## Choose a desk that can be cleared

- **Fixed desk:** 120 x 60 cm (3 ft 11 in x 2 ft) is enough for a laptop, a notebook and a lamp. A depth of 60 cm lets you rest your forearms flat.
- **Wall-fold desk:** 90 to 120 cm (2 ft 11 in to 3 ft 11 in) wide and 40 to 45 cm (1 ft 4 in to 1 ft 6 in) deep. It folds flat against the wall, and the floor is clear for the guest.
- **Chair:** a chair seat is about 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) high. Behind the desk, leave 60 to 90 cm (2 ft to 2 ft 11 in) to pull the chair out and stand up.

Ask the electrician where the sockets will be. A guest needs one by the bed for a phone, and the desk needs a power point at table height. Do not run extension cords across the walking path.

## Make room for a guest's things

A guest brings a bag and clothes. Leave one empty section in the wardrobe, or a rail of 60 cm (2 ft) with five or six hangers. Keep a stool or a rack of 60 x 40 cm (2 ft x 1 ft 4 in) for an open suitcase. Put a bedside lamp that is not the study light near the bed, and keep a curtain or blind that shuts the window at night.

## Worked example: a 3.0 x 3.3 m room

Take a spare room of 3.0 x 3.3 m (9 ft 10 in x 10 ft 10 in). The door is on one 3.0 m wall and the window is on the opposite 3.0 m wall. The area is 3.0 x 3.3 equals 9.9 square metres (about 107 sq ft).

1. Put the diwan of 90 x 190 cm along one 3.3 m wall, starting at the door end. It uses 190 cm of the 330 cm length, so 330 minus 190 equals 140 cm (4 ft 7 in) of that wall is free toward the window.
2. Put the desk of 120 x 60 cm on the window wall, in the corner farthest from the diwan. The window then lights the side of the desk.
3. Across the 300 cm width, the diwan takes 90 cm and the desk takes 120 cm. The walking gap between them is 300 minus 90 minus 120 equals 90 cm (2 ft 11 in).
4. A 90 cm gap is above the 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) comfortable path, so a guest can walk from the door to the window.
5. Check the chair. Pulled out, it needs 60 cm in front of the desk, and that strip lies beside the 90 cm gap, not across it. Nothing blocks the chair, and the diwan is 90 cm away from it.
6. The loft above the door holds the guest quilt and pillows. The diwan box holds the sheets.

By day the family sees a desk, a sofa and open floor. At night the guest takes the bolsters off the diwan, opens the quilt and uses the 90 cm gap to move. If you need a wider gap, choose a desk of 100 x 50 cm (3 ft 3 in x 1 ft 8 in), which gives back 20 cm (8 in) of width and 10 cm (4 in) of depth.

## Checklist for your tape measure

- [ ] Measure the room, wall to wall, in cm.
- [ ] Mark where the door opens and keep that swing clear.
- [ ] Check there is a 90 x 190 cm (2 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in) rectangle for the bed.
- [ ] Check a 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) path between desk, bed and door.
- [ ] Check the daylight at the desk. Does the window sit beside it or behind your screen?
- [ ] Count the sockets near the desk and the bed.
- [ ] Choose where the guest's bag, clothes and lamp go.

## Common mistakes

1. **A screen that faces the window.** Glare on the screen makes reading hard. Place the desk so that daylight falls from the side.
2. **A full-size bed that fills the room.** A double bed of 150 x 190 cm (4 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in) takes 2.85 square metres (about 31 sq ft) and leaves little for a desk in a small room. Choose a single.
3. **A fold-down desk with no clear wall.** The folding leaf needs space to open. Measure it open as well as closed.
4. **No storage for guest bedding.** If the quilt has no home, it ends up on the desk.
5. **Blocking the window with a cupboard.** The room then needs a light all day and gets no cross air.

## FAQ

### What is the smallest room that can be a study and a guest room?
A room of about 3 x 3 m (9 ft 10 in x 9 ft 10 in) can hold a diwan of 90 x 190 cm and a wall desk, if the path stays at least 75 cm (2 ft 6 in). Measure your own room before you buy.

### Is a sofa bed comfortable for a guest?
It depends on the mattress. A diwan with a proper foam mattress about 10 cm (4 in) thick is better than a thin cushion. Sleep on it yourself for a night before you offer it.

### Where should the study desk face?
Aim to have daylight come from the side, so the screen has no glare. Facing a wall gives fewer distractions, but some people prefer to see the door.

### Can I use a wall bed in a flat?
Many people do. It needs a plain, solid wall and careful fixing, so ask the carpenter and, if the wall is of doubtful strength, a licensed engineer.

### How do I keep the room cool in summer?
Open the window at night when the air outside is cooler, use curtains in the day against the sun, and keep a fan on a stand away from loose papers.
