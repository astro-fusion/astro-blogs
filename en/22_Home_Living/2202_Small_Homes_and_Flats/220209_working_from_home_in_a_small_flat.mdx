# Working from home in a small flat

You do not need a spare room to work from home. You need a corner of about 1.2 x 1.5 m (3 ft 11 in x 4 ft 11 in), good light and a clear idea of where the day starts and ends. After reading this you will be able to choose the corner, set the desk and chair at the right heights, and plan light, power and noise before you buy anything.

## Choose the spot

Rooms compete for the desk. Look at each in turn.

- **A bedroom corner:** quiet, but a bed in view makes it hard to switch off. Use it only if no other place works, and turn the chair so that you do not face the bed.
- **A living-room end:** the most common choice. Noise comes from the household, and the sofa becomes the background of your calls.
- **A balcony or a sunroom:** good daylight, but heat in the Terai summer and rain in the monsoon. Ask a professional before you put heavy equipment or a built-in desk on a balcony.
- **A passage or a niche:** a shallow desk of 45 to 50 cm (1 ft 6 in to 1 ft 8 in) deep works in a hall niche if you only need a laptop.

Pick the place with the fewest people walking behind your chair.

## Desk and chair: the sizes that matter

A desk of 120 x 60 cm (3 ft 11 in x 2 ft) is enough for a laptop, a notebook and a mug. A second screen or a lot of paper needs 140 to 160 cm (4 ft 7 in to 5 ft 3 in).

| Item | Aim for |
|---|---|
| Desk height | 72 to 75 cm (2 ft 4 in to 2 ft 5 in) |
| Chair seat height | 42 to 48 cm (1 ft 5 in to 1 ft 7 in), feet flat on the floor |
| Space behind the chair to stand up | 90 cm (3 ft) |
| Eye to screen | about an arm's length, 50 to 70 cm (1 ft 8 in to 2 ft 4 in) |
| Top of the screen | at or a little below eye level |

If the desk is too high for you, raise the chair and use a footrest, or a low stool. If it is too low, a carpenter can add blocks under the legs.

## Light

Put the desk so that daylight comes from the side, not from behind the screen (reflections) or from straight in front of you (glare). A window on your left is good if you write with your right hand. Add a lamp for evening work and aim it at the page, not at your eyes. Use a warm white bulb in the evening and a cooler one if you work in a dark room by day. Curtains or a light blind keep afternoon sun off the screen.

## Power, internet and power cuts

Plan where the cables go before you place the desk. A socket within 1.0 m (3 ft 3 in) saves long leads across the floor. Do not chain extension boards, and do not overload one socket. Ask a licensed electrician how many points you can safely add and whether the wiring suits it. For frequent power cuts, ask the electrician about a battery backup that suits a laptop and router, and have a mobile data plan ready for the router. A router near the work corner, not behind a wall of cupboards, gives a steadier signal.

## Noise and the end of the day

Face a wall or a window rather than the room. Use a rug or a thick curtain to soften echo for calls. A headset helps more than a closed door. In a small flat, define your hours: put the laptop in a drawer or a cloth cover at the end of the day. A fold-away desk or a cupboard with a desk shelf hides the work when it is not in use.

## A worked example: the living room of 3.0 x 4.2 m (9 ft 10 in x 13 ft 9 in)

The room has a three-seat sofa along one long wall. You want a desk in the same room.

1. **Desk zone.** The desk is 1.2 m wide and 0.6 m deep. The chair needs 0.9 m behind it to pull out and stand up. The zone is 1.2 m wide and 0.6 + 0.9 = 1.5 m (4 ft 11 in) long.
2. **Area.** 1.2 times 1.5 is 1.8 sq m (about 19 sq ft). That is less than a fifth of the room, which is 3.0 x 4.2 = 12.6 sq m.
3. **Place it.** Put the desk against one short wall, 3.0 m wide, with its 1.2 m length along that wall, in the corner. The zone runs 1.5 m into the room's 4.2 m length.
4. **Place the sofa.** Choose a 1.9 m (6 ft 3 in) sofa and put it at the other end, along the long wall.
5. **Check the gap.** 4.2 minus 1.5 minus 1.9 is 0.8 m (2 ft 7 in) between the chair zone and the sofa end. That is a comfortable passage.
6. **Check the width.** The desk takes 1.2 m of the 3.0 m wall. 3.0 minus 1.2 is 1.8 m (5 ft 11 in) of free floor beside the desk, so people can pass behind you without touching your chair.

Result: a full desk, a sofa and a passage, all in 12.6 sq m. Use a bookcase or a folding screen of 1.8 m (5 ft 11 in) to separate the desk from the sofa when you are on calls.

## Checklist

1. Choose the spot with the fewest people behind the chair.
2. Measure a zone of 1.2 x 1.5 m on the floor.
3. Mark desk and chair with tape and sit there for ten minutes.
4. Check where daylight comes from and where the glare falls.
5. Find the nearest socket and the router position.
6. Ask an electrician before you add points or a backup supply.
7. Adjust the chair so your feet are flat and your arms are level with the desk.
8. Decide how you will hide or stop work at the end of the day.

## Common mistakes

- **Working on the bed or the sofa.** The posture is awkward and the work has no fixed place. Use a proper desk and chair.
- **Screen facing the window.** It gives glare and a dark face on calls.
- **Cables across the floor.** They trip people. Run them along the wall.
- **Chain of extension boards.** It overloads the wiring.
- **A desk on an open balcony in the monsoon.** Rain and damp ruin equipment.

## Frequently asked questions

### How small can a work corner be?
A desk of 100 x 50 cm (3 ft 3 in x 1 ft 8 in) with a chair works for a laptop. The chair still needs 90 cm to pull out.

### Do I need a special chair?
Not necessarily. Seat height, firm back support and feet on the floor matter. A cushion or a footrest can adjust an ordinary chair.

### Where should my router go?
Near the work corner, off the floor, in the open. Cupboards and thick walls weaken the signal.

### What if the power cuts out often?
Ask an electrician about a small battery backup for your laptop and router. Keep a charged phone as a second connection.

### Can I use the dining table?
Yes, as a start. Its height is usually 75 cm, which suits most people. Keep the work out of sight at mealtimes.
