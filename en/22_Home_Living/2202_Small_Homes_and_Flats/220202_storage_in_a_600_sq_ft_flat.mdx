# Storage in a 600 Sq Ft Flat: Where to Find Space

A flat of 55.7 sq m (600 sq ft) has enough room for a family if the storage uses height and dead corners instead of floor. After reading this you will be able to count how much storage you need, find it in places you already own, and ask your carpenter for the right sizes.

## Start by counting, not buying

Most flats do not lack storage. They hold things that are not used. Before ordering anything, empty one cupboard onto the bed and sort it into things used every week, every season and once a year. Only the first group needs easy reach.

Give each group a place:

- **Every week:** between 60 cm and 150 cm (2 ft to 4 ft 11 in) from the floor. This is the zone your hands reach without bending or stretching.
- **Every season:** below 60 cm (2 ft) or between 150 cm and 210 cm (4 ft 11 in and 6 ft 11 in).
- **Once a year:** the loft above the wardrobe, high shelves and the bed box. Festival items, spare bedding and winter blankets in the plains belong here.

## Where the space is

### Height

Most flat ceilings are about 2.7 to 3.0 m (8 ft 11 in to 9 ft 10 in). A wardrobe of 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in) leaves a band above it that is often empty. A loft cupboard of 50 to 60 cm (1 ft 8 in to 2 ft) closes that band. Use it for light, rarely used items. Keep the heaviest things low.

### Under the bed

A bed with a box base gives a large, dry space. A box of 30 cm (1 ft) inside height holds folded blankets and out-of-season clothes. Ask for a hinged top with a gas lift or a strong prop, because a heavy lid is hard to hold with one hand. In the monsoon, put a cloth bag with a silica sachet or dry neem leaves inside, so that damp does not settle in stored cloth. If the floor is damp or the wall sweats, fix the cause first; storage will not cure it.

### Wall depth

Many shelves are 30 to 35 cm (1 ft to 1 ft 2 in) deep, and that is useful for books, jars and files. A full wardrobe is 55 to 60 cm (1 ft 10 in to 2 ft) deep because clothes on hangers need that width. If a wall is short on space, a shallow 30 cm (1 ft) cupboard can hold shoes, pooja items or tools and takes half the floor depth.

### The back of doors and walls

Hooks, a slim rack or a pocket organiser on the back of a door use space that costs no floor. Use one hook per person near the front door for the daily bag and keys.

### The kitchen and the bathroom

- Kitchen: tall units to 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in) and a pull-out drawer for pots keep the floor and the counter clear. Wall units are usually fitted so that the bottom is about 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) above the counter.
- Bathroom: a wall-hung cabinet above the washing machine or a slim rack above the toilet cistern is useful. Keep cabinets away from the wet area, and ask the contractor to protect the wall behind from splashes.

Anything that needs drilling into the wall, such as a heavy cabinet, depends on the wall material. Ask the carpenter to check it first. Never drill into a wall where you do not know where electrical cables or pipes run; ask your electrician or plumber.

## Worked example: a bedroom wall in a 600 sq ft flat

Take a family of four in a 600 sq ft 2BHK, with a bedroom of 3.0 x 3.6 m (9 ft 10 in x 11 ft 10 in) and a ceiling height of 2.7 m (8 ft 11 in). The bedroom has a double bed of 150 x 190 cm (4 ft 11 in x 6 ft 3 in).

1. **Wardrobe.** Along one 3.6 m (11 ft 10 in) wall, a wardrobe of 240 cm wide, 60 cm deep and 210 cm high (7 ft 11 in, 2 ft and 6 ft 11 in). Volume: 2.4 x 0.6 x 2.1 equals 3.024 cubic metres.
2. **Loft.** The ceiling leaves 270 minus 210 equals 60 cm (2 ft). Take 5 cm (2 in) for the carpenter's board and clearance, giving 55 cm (1 ft 10 in) of usable height. Volume: 2.4 x 0.6 x 0.55 equals 0.792 cubic metres.
3. **Bed box.** The inside of the box is 140 x 180 cm and 30 cm high (4 ft 7 in x 5 ft 11 in x 1 ft). Volume: 1.4 x 1.8 x 0.3 equals 0.756 cubic metres.
4. **Total.** 3.024 plus 0.792 plus 0.756 equals 4.572 cubic metres.

The loft and bed box add 0.792 plus 0.756 equals 1.548 cubic metres. That is more than half the volume of the wardrobe itself (3.024 divided by 2 equals 1.512), and the room has not lost any floor. The footprint of the wardrobe is 2.4 x 0.6 equals 1.44 square metres (about 15.5 sq ft), and the bed footprint stays the same as before.

Check the width of the room. The bed sits with its long side along one 3.6 m (11 ft 10 in) wall, and the wardrobe stands on the opposite 3.6 m wall. Across the 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in) width: 300 minus 150 (bed) minus 60 (wardrobe depth) equals 90 cm (2 ft 11 in) of walking path. That is above the 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) a comfortable minimum asks for. The open doors of a swing wardrobe use that path, so open one 60 cm (2 ft) door at a time, or ask the carpenter for sliding doors, which need no swing space.

## Checklist for your tape measure

- [ ] Measure ceiling height, and the height of each wardrobe or shelf.
- [ ] Note the empty band above every cupboard. Is it 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) or more?
- [ ] Measure the wardrobe depth. Is it 55 to 60 cm (1 ft 10 in to 2 ft) for hanging clothes?
- [ ] Check the bed: can its base be a box, and will the lid open fully?
- [ ] Measure the clear floor in front of each cupboard door.
- [ ] Mark the heaviest items to be kept below 60 cm (2 ft).
- [ ] Check every storage wall for damp before filling it.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying boxes before sorting.** You will carry the same unused things into new boxes.
2. **Putting heavy items in the loft.** Reaching up with a heavy suitcase is how things fall. Keep the loft for light, soft items.
3. **Loft with no way to reach it.** Keep a small, sturdy step stool, and use a ladder only if it is steady.
4. **Deep shelves with no front-to-back order.** In a 60 cm (2 ft) deep shelf, things at the back are forgotten. Use baskets that slide out.
5. **Storing in a damp corner.** In monsoon, cloth stored against an outside wall can grow mould. Leave a 5 cm (2 in) gap behind a cupboard from an outer wall for air.

## FAQ

### How much storage does a 600 sq ft flat need?
It depends on the family and its belongings, so count rather than guess. Sort one cupboard, measure what you keep, and use the heights above to plan where it goes.

### Is a loft above the wardrobe worth it?
Yes, if the ceiling leaves 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) or more above the wardrobe and you only keep light, seasonal items in it. In the worked example it adds 0.792 cubic metres for no loss of floor.

### Can I fix a heavy shelf to any wall?
No. Brick, concrete block and hollow partition walls hold fixings differently. Ask the carpenter to check, and ask a licensed engineer if the wall is a load-bearing one before you change anything.

### How do I keep stored clothes dry in the monsoon?
Keep cupboards a little off the outer wall, air them on a dry day, and put dry neem leaves or a silica sachet in stored bags. If the wall is wet, call a professional to find the leak.

### What goes in the shallow 30 cm cupboard?
Shoes in a tilting rack, tools, files, cleaning supplies, or puja items. It is too shallow for hanging clothes.
