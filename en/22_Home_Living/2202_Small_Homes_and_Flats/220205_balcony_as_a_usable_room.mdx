# Turning a Balcony into a Usable Space

A balcony of 1.2 m (3 ft 11 in) depth is narrow, but it can hold a seat, a few plants and a drying line if you plan the sizes. After reading this you will be able to measure your balcony, decide what it is for, and keep it safe, shaded and dry through summer and monsoon.

## Decide the job first

A small balcony does one or two jobs well. Pick them before you buy anything.

- **A place to sit:** two chairs and a small table for tea.
- **A place to dry clothes:** a line or a fold-out rack.
- **A place for plants:** pots on a shelf or along the rail.
- **A place for storage:** a slim cupboard for shoes, a broom and cleaning things.

Two jobs fit in 3.6 square metres (about 39 sq ft). Four jobs turn the balcony into a store that no one walks into.

## Safety first

### Weight

A balcony is built to carry a certain load, and you do not know it unless an engineer tells you. Wet soil in a pot is heavy: a pot of 10 litres can weigh more than 10 kg. A water-filled tub, a stone floor, a heavy swing or a crowd of guests adds a great deal. Ask a licensed structural engineer what the balcony can carry before you add heavy planters, tiles or a water tank. Keep furniture light and spread it out.

### Railing

A railing height of about 100 to 110 cm (3 ft 3 in to 3 ft 7 in) is common practice. Where there are small children, aim for gaps of 10 cm (4 in) or less, so a head cannot pass. Do not keep a chair, a table or a planter close to the railing, because a child can use it as a step. Check that the railing is firm and not rusted. If it moves or the concrete around it has cracked, call a licensed engineer or contractor.

### Closing in the balcony

Covering a balcony with glass or grill, or joining it to the room, can change the loads and may need permission from your housing society or the municipality. Do not remove the door or any wall between the balcony and the room. These are questions for a licensed engineer.

## Comfort: sun, rain and privacy

- **Summer heat:** in the plains and the Terai, an afternoon sun on a west-facing balcony can make it too hot to use. A bamboo blind, a cloth shade or climbing plants on the rail give shade and let air pass.
- **Monsoon rain:** keep the balcony drain open. Sweep leaves away from the outlet before the rains. If water stands on the floor, enters the room or stains the ceiling below, the slope or the waterproofing may be at fault, and a waterproofing professional should look at it.
- **Winter in the hills:** the balcony may be a sunny spot in the morning. A folding chair and a blanket are enough. Do not store things that a frost can spoil.
- **Privacy:** a screen of slats, a bamboo blind or tall plants in light pots blocks sight lines from the next building without closing the air.

Vastu tradition prefers balconies that face north or east. This is the tradition's view and does not change how you use the space.

## Furniture that fits

| Item | Common size | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Folding chair | 45 x 45 cm (1 ft 6 in x 1 ft 6 in) | folds against the wall |
| Round table | 40 to 50 cm (1 ft 4 in to 1 ft 8 in) across | for two cups and a plate |
| Bench with storage | 35 to 40 cm (1 ft 2 in to 1 ft 4 in) deep | light wood or cane |
| Plant shelf | 20 to 25 cm (8 to 10 in) deep | fix to the wall, not the railing |
| Fold-out drying rack | 50 to 60 cm (1 ft 8 in to 2 ft) deep when open | store folded |

Keep a path of at least 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) wide from the door to the farthest corner if you can. In a balcony 1.2 m (3 ft 11 in) wide, that leaves 120 minus 75 equals 45 cm (1 ft 6 in) for furniture on one side.

## Worked example: a 1.2 x 3.0 m balcony

Take a balcony of 1.2 x 3.0 m (3 ft 11 in x 9 ft 10 in) off a living room, with the door at one end. The area is 1.2 x 3.0 equals 3.6 square metres.

1. **Seating zone.** Along the wall, two folding chairs of 45 cm width and a round table of 40 cm across. Length used: 45 plus 40 plus 45 equals 130 cm (4 ft 3 in).
2. **Path.** Chairs are 45 cm deep. Across the 120 cm width, 120 minus 45 equals 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) is left, exactly the comfortable minimum. When someone sits, their legs reach into that path, so keep the rest of the floor clear.
3. **Plant shelf.** On the same house wall, after the table, a shelf of 25 cm (10 in) depth and 100 cm (3 ft 3 in) length. Across the width: 120 minus 25 equals 95 cm (3 ft 1 in) of path.
4. **Drying rack.** Length used along the wall: 130 plus 100 equals 230 cm (7 ft 7 in). Length left: 300 minus 230 equals 70 cm (2 ft 4 in). The folded rack stands in that 70 cm. On a drying day, fold the chairs against the wall and open the rack in front of the seating zone.
5. **Weight check.** Six pots of 10 kg each add up to 6 x 10 equals 60 kg. Ask the engineer whether that is fine along with people and furniture. If the answer is uncertain, use three pots, or lighter plastic and light soil mixes.

The chairs fold away, so the same floor serves for sitting or for drying.

## Checklist for your tape measure

- [ ] Measure width and length of the balcony floor in cm.
- [ ] Measure the railing height and the gaps between bars.
- [ ] Find the drain outlet and make sure no furniture blocks it.
- [ ] Note which way the balcony faces and when the sun hits it.
- [ ] Check the door opening: how much clear space remains when it is open?
- [ ] List the weight of pots, tubs and furniture, and ask an engineer if you plan more than light items.
- [ ] Look for cracks, rust and standing water.

## Common mistakes

1. **Putting heavy planters or a water tank on the balcony.** Load is a question for a licensed engineer.
2. **Furniture at the railing.** It gives a child a step to climb.
3. **Blocking the drain with pots or a rug.** Rain then stands on the floor and may enter the room.
4. **Drilling into the railing or slab for shades and hooks.** Ask the contractor how and where to fix them.
5. **Using the balcony as storage.** Boxes in the sun and rain spoil, and the space is lost.

## FAQ

### How wide should a balcony be to sit on?
A depth of 1.2 m (3 ft 11 in) holds a chair and a 75 cm (2 ft 6 in) path. Narrower than that, use a single folding chair or a bench of 35 cm (1 ft 2 in) and a wall shelf.

### Can I grow plants if the balcony gets only morning sun?
Some plants manage with morning sun, but ask a local nursery which ones. Keep pots light and watch for water draining onto the neighbour below.

### Can I put a tile or stone floor on my balcony?
It adds weight and can change the slope to the drain. Ask a licensed engineer and a waterproofing professional before you do.

### How do I keep the balcony cool in summer?
Shade it from the afternoon sun with a blind or climbers on the rail, and keep air passing through.

### Is it safe to close the balcony with glass?
It may need permission and an engineer's opinion because of load and wind. Ask your society and a licensed engineer first.
