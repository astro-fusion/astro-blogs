# Under-stair and loft storage done properly

The space under a staircase and the space above a corridor or bathroom are the two biggest pieces of unused room in many Nepali and Indian homes. After reading this you will be able to measure your under-stair space, divide it into zones by height, and decide what belongs in a loft and what does not. You will also know which questions to take to a licensed engineer before anything is fixed to the stair or the slab.

## Start with the staircase, not the cupboard

Under-stair storage fails when someone orders a rectangular cupboard for a triangular space. The height under the stair rises from almost nothing at the foot to full height at the top. Design in zones, and let each zone hold what suits its height.

A typical house stair has a riser (the height of one step) of about 16 to 18 cm (6 to 7 in) and a tread (the flat part) of about 25 cm (10 in). Measure your own. Count the risers from floor to floor, divide the floor-to-floor height by that count, and measure one tread.

### What goes in each zone

- **Low zone, under 90 cm (3 ft) high:** shoe drawers, pull-out boxes on wheels, a school bag shelf. Drawers beat doors here, because nobody can crawl in to find things at the back.
- **Middle zone, 90 cm to 2.0 m (3 ft to 6 ft 7 in):** shelves behind doors for the pantry overflow, cleaning tools, and a small bag of tools. Keep shelves about 35 to 45 cm (14 to 18 in) deep so nothing gets lost behind something else.
- **Tall zone, above 2.0 m (6 ft 7 in):** a full-height cupboard or a narrow broom and mop closet, about 45 to 60 cm (1 ft 6 in to 2 ft) wide.

## A worked example

Take a straight staircase with a floor-to-floor height of 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in), 18 risers and a 25 cm (10 in) tread. Assume the stair slab is 15 cm (6 in) thick, measured vertically under the steps. Ask your contractor for the real figure.

1. **Riser height:** 3,000 mm divided by 18 is about 167 mm, or 16.7 cm (6.6 in).
2. **Run of the stair:** 18 risers have 17 treads. 17 times 25 cm is 425 cm, or 4.25 m (13 ft 11 in).
3. **How fast the underside rises:** 16.7 cm of height for every 25 cm of run, so about 0.67 cm of height for each 1 cm you move away from the foot.
4. **Where the underside reaches 90 cm of clear height:** add the 15 cm slab to 90 cm to get 105 cm. 105 divided by 0.67 is about 157 cm (5 ft 2 in) from the foot.
5. **Where it reaches 2.0 m of clear height:** 200 cm plus 15 cm is 215 cm. 215 divided by 0.67 is about 322 cm (10 ft 7 in) from the foot.
6. **Zones:** the low zone is the first 157 cm. The middle zone runs from 157 to 322 cm, a length of 165 cm (5 ft 5 in). The tall zone is the last 425 minus 322, which is about 103 cm (3 ft 4 in).

Result: drawers in the first 1.5 m, two shelved cupboards in the middle 1.65 m, and one full-height closet of 60 cm with a 40 cm gap beside it for a broom rack in the last metre. Keep the doors off the stair side of the opening so they do not swing into the walkway.

## Loft storage: what suits it

A loft is a cupboard built high above a corridor, a bathroom entry or a wardrobe, usually 60 to 90 cm (2 to 3 ft) high inside. It is for things you need once or twice a year.

### Good loft contents

- Festival items: decorations, extra lamps, thalis kept for large gatherings.
- Out-of-season quilts and blankets, kept in cloth bags.
- Suitcases, empty and light.
- Spare bedding for guests.

### Poor loft contents

- Anything heavy, such as books, tools, stored grain or full water cans.
- Anything you use weekly. If you need a ladder to reach it, you will stop using it.
- Gas cylinders, oil, fuel or anything flammable. Never store these in a closed loft or in a cupboard under the stair.
- Food that attracts pests.

## Heat, damp and access

A loft right under the roof slab of a top-floor flat holds heat in a Terai summer. Paper, plastic and medicines do not like it. Leave a gap at the back of shelves and do not seal the loft tightly. During the monsoon, keep cartons off the floor of the loft and check once a month for damp patches. A patch is a leak or a seepage question for a professional, not something to paint over.

Plan the access. A fixed ladder or folding steps needs about 60 cm (2 ft) of floor to stand on and should not block a door. Things on shelves are comfortable to reach standing up to about 1.8 m (6 ft). Above about 2.0 m (6 ft 7 in), use a stool or step ladder, and only for light things.

## Ask a professional first

A loft or a heavy shelf adds weight to a slab or a wall. A stair cupboard may be fixed to the underside of the stair. You cannot judge these from the surface. Ask a licensed structural engineer whether the slab or wall can take what you plan, and where you may drill or fix. Any new lighting or sockets in a cupboard or loft must be done by a licensed electrician. Also tell the engineer or contractor before you cut or move any part of the stair.

## Checklist

1. Count the risers and measure one tread and the floor-to-floor height.
2. Write down the clear height at 50 cm (1 ft 8 in) steps from the foot of the stair.
3. Mark the low, middle and tall zones on the floor with tape.
4. Decide what goes in each zone before you ask the carpenter for a quote.
5. Ask the engineer what you may fix to the stair, the slab and the wall.
6. Put doors on the side away from the walking path, or use sliding doors.
7. Ask the electrician for a light inside the cupboard if you want one.
8. Plan a stool or step ladder spot near any loft.

## Common mistakes

- **One big door on a triangular space.** The back corners become dead storage. Use drawers and several smaller doors.
- **A loft that is too high to reach.** If the loft floor is above a normal reach, you will not use it.
- **Heavy things in the loft.** Weight is a structural question. Keep it light until an engineer says otherwise.
- **Closing the space tight in the monsoon.** Air must move, or clothes and paper turn musty.
- **Storing cylinders or fuel.** These need open, ventilated space, not a closed cupboard.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I put a toilet or a small kitchen under the stair?
That involves water supply, drainage and sometimes gas. Ask a licensed plumber and your contractor. It also needs enough head height, which is only available at the high end of the stair.

### How deep should loft shelves be?
Aim for 60 cm (2 ft). Anything deeper cannot be reached from the opening.

### Is a loft above the bathroom a good idea?
Only if the space stays dry. Check for leaks and condensation first, and ask the contractor about the waterproofing before you plan any storage there.

### What should I do with a shoe rack under the stair?
Drawers or open racks with air gaps work better than a closed box. Shoes carry damp in from the monsoon street.

### Can I do all this myself?
You can measure, plan and sort by zone. Fixing, cutting and wiring should be done by a carpenter, an engineer and an electrician.
