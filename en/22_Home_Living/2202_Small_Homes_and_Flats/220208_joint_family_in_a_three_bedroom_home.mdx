# A joint family in a three-bedroom home

Many families in Nepal and India live in three generations under one roof, and the home was often built or rented for fewer people. After reading this you will be able to decide who sleeps where, fit two beds into a shared room with real measurements, and plan the morning rush, the kitchen and the storage so that nine people can live in a 3BHK without daily friction.

## An illustration, not a rule

Take a household of nine in a flat of about 1,100 sq ft (about 102 sq m): grandparents, two married sons with their wives, and three children. The flat has three bedrooms, two bathrooms, a living room and a kitchen. Your family will differ, but the method is the same. Decide the needs that cannot move first, then fit the rest around them.

## Step one: place the fixed needs

### The grandparents' room

Put it closest to a bathroom and, where there is a choice, on the ground floor or near the entrance. Avoid a step or a raised threshold between the room and the bathroom. Aim for a clear walking width of 90 cm (3 ft) between the bed and the bathroom door. A night light on a separate switch near the bed is a good idea, and an electrician should fit it. If the elders pray each morning, a small shelf in their room may be enough for a puja space.

### The couples' rooms

A couple with a baby needs the bed plus a cot or a floor mattress. A couple with older children needs a way to give the children their own sleeping place. When two couples share a three-bedroom flat with elders, one couple usually has the third room and the other couple shares a room with the children. This is the case worked below.

### The living room

It becomes a shared room and, at night, sometimes a bedroom. Choose a sofa that can take a guest, and keep one wall free of tall furniture so the room can be rearranged.

## A worked example: a shared bedroom of 3.3 x 3.6 m (10 ft 10 in x 11 ft 10 in)

A couple and two children share this room. The plan is one double bed (150 x 190 cm) and one bunk bed (90 x 190 cm) with a wardrobe.

1. **Width, 3.3 m.** Put both beds with their heads on the same 3.3 m wall. The double bed is 1.5 m wide and the bunk is 0.9 m wide, so together they take 1.5 + 0.9 = 2.4 m.
2. **Gap between the beds.** 3.3 minus 2.4 is 0.9 m (2 ft 11 in). That is enough for an adult to get out of the double bed and for children to climb the bunk ladder from the gap side.
3. **Length, 3.6 m.** Both beds are 1.9 m (6 ft 3 in) long. Put a wardrobe 0.6 m (2 ft) deep on the 3.3 m wall at the foot of the beds.
4. **Walkway at the foot.** 3.6 minus 1.9 minus 0.6 is 1.1 m (3 ft 7 in). That is enough to open the wardrobe doors and walk past.
5. **Door.** Place the room door on the long wall at the foot end, and hang it so it opens against a wall, or use a sliding door. If the door swings across the walkway, the 1.1 m is lost.
6. **Ceiling check.** Measure the ceiling height. A bunk is about 1.6 to 1.7 m (5 ft 3 in to 5 ft 7 in) tall to the top of its frame. Aim to leave at least 90 cm (3 ft) above the frame for the person sleeping up there to sit up. With a 2.7 m ceiling (8 ft 10 in), 2.7 minus 1.7 leaves about 1.0 m (3 ft 3 in).

Result: four people sleep in 11.9 sq m (128 sq ft), which is 3.3 x 3.6. The floor has a 0.9 m central gap and a 1.1 m walkway. Add a curtain on a rail across the gap if the parents want some privacy.

## Step two: bathrooms and the morning rush

Take nine people and two bathrooms. If each person spends about 10 minutes, that is 90 minutes of bathroom time. Split over two bathrooms, that is 45 minutes each, plus gaps. A written order helps more than any extra fitting: elders first, then school children, then adults who leave for work.

Small helps that cost little: a hook rail and a shelf per person, a bucket stand, and a shelf inside each bathroom for towels. Keep wet and dry areas apart. A tall mirror cabinet over the basin saves floor space.

## Step three: kitchen and storage

One kitchen is shared by two or three cooks. Give each of them a clear counter strip, about 90 cm (3 ft), and a separate wall cabinet or basket for personal items. Keep the worktop between the hob and the sink clear.

For storage, assign by person, not by room. A hanging rail of 60 cm (2 ft) per adult and 40 cm (1 ft 4 in) per child is a workable starting point. Use a loft or under-bed boxes for off-season clothes. Ask an engineer before loading a loft.

Shoes are a common source of arguments. Place a shoe rack of about 90 cm (3 ft) at the door, a second one in a back corridor, and keep outdoor shoes out of the bedrooms.

## Noise and privacy

Walls in flats are thin. Put the elders' room and the children's room away from the living room if you can. Put curtains on glass doors and a mat or rug where footsteps carry. Plan one corner of the home where one adult can sit alone for a quarter of an hour. That is worth more than another sofa.

## Checklist

1. List each person, their age, and their needs at night.
2. Put the elders closest to a bathroom.
3. Measure each bedroom and draw the beds to scale.
4. Leave 60 cm (2 ft) beside any bed someone gets out of.
5. Write a bathroom order and pin it up.
6. Assign storage by person, with a rail and a box each.
7. Give each cook a counter strip in the kitchen.
8. Check ceiling height before choosing a bunk.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting the elders far from the bathroom.** A night walk becomes a risk.
- **Choosing a bunk for a low ceiling.** The top sleeper has no head room.
- **One shoe rack for nine people.** It overflows by the first week.
- **No private corner.** Everyone shares everything and tempers shorten.
- **Heavy loft storage.** Ask a licensed engineer first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can three generations share a 3BHK comfortably?
Yes, if elders have a quiet room near a bathroom and children share a room with proper beds. Planning the order of use matters more than the size.

### Is a bunk bed safe for small children?
A top bunk is for older children who can climb and have a guard rail. Use a lower bed for the youngest.

### Where can the puja space go?
A shelf in the living room or the elders' room works. Keep it clear of the kitchen smoke and shoes.

### How do I give teenagers some privacy?
A curtain or a light partition on a rail across the sleeping area is a start. Ask a carpenter for a partition that can be removed.

### Do I need a licensed engineer?
Yes, before you remove a wall, add a loft, or load a balcony. Furniture planning needs no engineer.
