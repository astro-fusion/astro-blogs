# Primary and Secondary Vastu Defects: Which to Fix First

In Vastu Shastra, not every defect is treated as equal. Vastu tradition separates major defects from minor ones, and understanding the difference helps a home owner decide what to look at first. This article explains the two groups, how a building's defects are sorted, and how corrections are put in order.

In this article you will learn:

- What primary and secondary Vastu defects are.
- How defects are classified by severity and effect.
- How corrections are put in order of priority.
- What simple remedies are described for both kinds.

## What Are Primary and Secondary Vastu Defects?

**Primary Vastu defects** are major structural flaws that go directly against the basic principles of Vastu Shastra. Tradition holds that they have a strong and immediate effect on the people who live in the building.

**Secondary Vastu defects** are minor flaws, less severe than primary ones. Tradition holds that they can still cause trouble, but their effect is generally milder.

- **Priority:** Vastu tradition gives first place to correcting primary defects in order to restore balance in a home.
- **Spiritual side:** Attending to defects, primary and secondary, is seen as a way of showing respect for the natural order.

### Historical Background

The classification of defects rests on the old texts of Vastu Shastra, which describe how different features of a building are thought to affect its energy.

## What Decides the Severity of a Defect

- **Direction:** The place of a defect is a key factor in how serious it is judged to be. A defect in the north-east, for example, is considered more serious than a defect in other directions.
- **Flow of prana:** Primary defects are those that severely obstruct the flow of prana, the life energy. Secondary defects cause minor disturbances.
- **The five elements:** The class of a defect also depends on which of the five elements it affects. In Vastu tradition earth belongs to the south-west, water to the north-east, fire to the south-east, air to the north-west and space to the centre.

## How to Classify Vastu Defects

### Step 1: Identify the Defect

First list all the Vastu defects in the building. This can be done by looking at the house yourself and by consulting a qualified person.

### Step 2: Decide the Severity

Once the defects are listed, each is sorted as primary or secondary according to its severity.

- **Primary defects:** A main entrance in the south or south-west, a toilet in the north-east, a kitchen in the north, a master bedroom in the south-east.
- **Secondary defects:** Furniture in a poor position, the use of colours considered inauspicious, clutter in the home.

### Step 3: Put the Corrections in Order

After the defects are sorted, corrections are taken up by severity. Primary defects are addressed first.

## Practical Use

- **Homes:** Go through the house carefully to list and sort all its defects.
- **Shops and offices:** In a commercial space, primary defects are taken up first in the same way.
- **Range of remedies:** Remedies range from simple changes, such as rearranging furniture, to major structural alterations. A structural alteration, such as moving a main entrance, is a large change that belongs to an architect or an engineer, not a casual decision.

## Common Mistakes

- Ignoring secondary defects. Primary defects are more severe, but tradition says secondary ones can add up.
- Trying to correct defects without understanding the principles behind them.
- Choosing expensive remedies when simple and affordable ones are available.

## An Illustration

**Situation:** A business with a main entrance in the south-west.

**Examination:** A Vastu practitioner would treat the south-west entrance as a primary defect, because tradition counts the position of the main entrance as a major factor.

**Traditional suggestion:** Tradition speaks of an entrance in the north and a water fountain in the north-east. Moving an entrance is a large structural change, to be judged by an architect or an engineer first.

This is only an illustration of how defects are examined. It carries no outcome, and no result can be promised.

### Some Practical Pointers

- Always take up primary defects first.
- Where several remedies are described, think about which are practical for your home.
- Review the changes you make and adjust them as needed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Vastu defect should be corrected first?

The position of the main entrance is regarded in Vastu as the most important factor. A main entrance in the south or south-west is counted as a major defect and is taken up first.

### What is the difference between a primary and a secondary defect?

A primary defect is a major structural flaw that strongly obstructs the flow of prana. A secondary defect is a minor flaw, such as furniture placed badly, inauspicious colours or clutter.

### Can I correct Vastu defects myself?

Minor defects, such as rearranging furniture or changing colours, can be handled by the family. For major defects, especially anything structural, consult a qualified Vastu practitioner and an architect or engineer.

### Should secondary defects be ignored?

No. Tradition says that although primary defects are more severe, secondary ones can also add up, so they should not be ignored.

### How long do remedies take to show any effect?

No time can be given. Vastu tradition does not promise results, and they cannot be predicted.

## Conclusion

According to Vastu tradition, knowing the difference between primary and secondary defects is the first step towards an orderly approach to a home. By taking up primary defects first and then attending to secondary ones, a home owner can go about the corrections in a sensible order.
