# Traditional Vastu Remedies Without Demolition

Many people think that correcting a Vastu defect means expensive and disruptive structural work. Vastu tradition also describes remedies that need no demolition. This article sets out what they are, the thinking behind them, and the care they call for.

In this article you will learn:

- The idea behind remedies that need no structural change.
- The kinds of practice described: moving furniture, placing objects, and traditional aids to energy.
- How to decide which remedy suits your case.
- Practical points for using them in a home or an office.

## What Are Non-Structural Vastu Remedies?

Non-structural Vastu remedies are corrections that involve no demolition or structural change to a building. According to tradition they work by redirecting and rebalancing the energy of a space.

- **Practical side:** They are a low-cost and convenient way to deal with a defect. They are described as traditional practices, and no result can be promised from them.
- **Spiritual side:** Using them is a way of working with the subtle energies of a place to make it more sacred and harmonious.

### Historical Background

Remedies of this kind have a long history in Vastu Shastra. The old sages are said to have held that the energy of a space could be influenced by many things, including where objects are placed, which colours are used and whether sacred symbols are present.

## The Basis of Vastu

### The Vastu Purusha Mandala

The placing of objects in a room can be guided by the Vastu Purusha Mandala, so that they sit in harmony with the energies of the space.

### The Five Elements

These remedies are used to balance the five elements in a room. For example, a water fountain is described as a way of strengthening the water element in the north-east. In Vastu tradition earth belongs to the south-west, water to the north-east, fire to the south-east, air to the north-west and space to the centre.

### Flow of Energy

The careful placing of furniture and other objects is said to improve the flow of prana, the life energy, in a room.

## Types of Remedies

### Moving Furniture

- **Belief:** The position of furniture is said to have a strong effect on the energy of a room.
- **Practice:** Heavy furniture is moved to the south and west walls to give a sense of stability. The centre of the room is kept open and free of clutter so that energy can move freely.

### Thoughtful Placing of Objects

- **Belief:** The objects we keep in our homes can add to or take away from the energy of a space.
- **Practice:** Mirrors are said to expand the energy of a space and to correct cuts or extensions. Plants are placed in the corners of rooms to soften the energy.

### Traditional Aids to Energy

- **Belief:** Tradition and popular practice speak of several aids to the energy of a space.
- **Practice:** Yantras are described as a way of drawing positive energy and guarding against negative energy. Popular practice also mentions hanging wind chimes to break up stagnant energy. Pyramids and similar devices are sold today as Vastu remedies; they are sellers' claims, not part of classical Vastu, and are not recommended here.

## Practical Use

- **Homes:** These remedies are used to make a home a place of peace and calm.
- **Shops and offices:** The same ideas are used in a commercial space to make a place orderly and pleasant to work in.
- **Specific defects:** Particular remedies are described for particular defects, such as a toilet in the north-east or a kitchen in the north.

## Common Mistakes

- Using too many remedies. Too many can leave a room cluttered and chaotic.
- Using remedies without understanding their purpose.
- Expecting instant results. Tradition says such remedies work only by gradually shifting the energy of a space, and no result can be promised.

## An Illustration

**Situation:** A person has trouble sleeping and feels anxious.

**Examination:** A Vastu practitioner finds that the bed is in a poor position and that the bedroom is full of clutter.

**Traditional suggestion:** Moving the bed to a more favourable position and clearing the bedroom, as tradition describes.

This is only an illustration of how a practitioner reasons. No outcome is claimed. Persistent sleeplessness or anxiety needs a doctor.

### Some Practical Pointers

- Start with simple remedies and take up more complex ones only if needed.
- Choose remedies you understand and can keep tidy.
- Meditation and yoga are traditional practices that many people combine with these remedies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are remedies without structural change regarded as equal to structural ones?

Tradition treats them as a useful way to correct defects, and in many cases as a substitute for structural work. They remain traditional practices, and no result can be promised.

### How do I decide which remedy to use?

It is best to consult a qualified Vastu practitioner for suggestions that fit your home. Choose remedies that are simple, low in cost and clear in purpose.

### Can these remedies be used in an office?

Yes. They can be used in any space, including offices, shops and factories.

### Can they be used in a rented home?

Yes, since they need no building work. Moving furniture, clearing clutter and placing plants can be done in a rented home.

### What should I watch for when using them?

Do not overuse them, do not use them without understanding their purpose, and do not expect instant results.

## Conclusion

According to Vastu tradition, many corrections can be made without demolition, by moving furniture, placing objects with thought and following simple traditional practices. They are practical and low in cost, but they are traditions, not guarantees, and a large defect is a matter for an architect or an engineer.
