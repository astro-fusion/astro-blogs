# How to Select the Best Plot According to Vastu

Building a home or a shop begins with the choice of land. Vastu Shastra treats the plot as the foundation of the energy of the whole building. Before buying, it is therefore worth checking the plot's direction, shape, slope, soil and surroundings. This article takes those points in order.

You will learn:
- The basic principles of plot selection in Vastu
- What shape, direction and slope a plot should have
- How to check the soil and the surroundings
- Which mistakes to avoid while looking at a plot

## What Plot Selection Means in Vastu

It is a systematic way of judging a plot against the old rules of Vastu Shastra. It looks at the form of the land and its surroundings. Vastu tradition holds that a plot chosen this way supports the health, prosperity, spiritual growth and good relationships of the people who live on it.

- **Auspicious timing:** The tradition also includes choosing an auspicious time (muhurta) for buying land.
- **Vastu view:** The shape of the land, its direction and the conditions around it are held to decide the balance of energy.
- **Spiritual side:** The emphasis is on keeping the land in harmony with the energies of nature.

### Historical Background

The rules for choosing a plot are traced to classical texts such as the Brihat Samhita and to old Vastu Shastra manuscripts. These texts explain the relationship between the surrounding environment and the flow of energy as a principle. Present-day Vastu practice rests on them.

## Why the Choice of Plot Matters

### The Place of the Plot in Tradition

Vastu tradition holds that choosing a plot is no ordinary decision. It is believed that:
- **Time of purchase:** The conditions at the time of purchase can affect the family's future.
- **Choice of muhurta:** Choosing an auspicious day and time reduces obstacles.
- **Vastu adjustments:** If the plot has small defects, Vastu remedies are used to try to balance them.

### The Vastu View

Vastu Shastra regards the plot as the base of energy. The choice of plot affects these matters:
- **Direction:** North-facing and east-facing plots are considered especially auspicious. They are said to bring prosperity, progress and positivity.
- **Flow of energy:** The form and direction of a building are held to carry prana (life force) through the house. Tradition says this brings physical and mental peace.
- **Surroundings:** A quiet place, clean air and plenty of greenery are considered good. Land close to a cremation ground, a rubbish dump or a hospital is best avoided.

## Key Principles of Plot Selection

### Principle 1: Plot Direction and Shape

The direction and shape of the land are the most basic matters. Vastu prefers the following:
- **Direction:** Plots facing north, east and north-east (the Ishan corner) receive sunlight and natural energy well. Tradition considers them good for wealth, health and harmony.
- **Shape:** Square and rectangular plots are symbols of stability and balance. Irregular, triangular or L-shaped plots are said to disturb the balance of energy.

**In practice:**
- Give preference to a main entrance on the north or the east.
- Choose a square or rectangular plot for an even spread of energy.
- If you must take an irregular plot, ask a Vastu practitioner about remedies first.

### Principle 2: Checking the Environment and the Soil

The surroundings and the quality of the soil play a large part in the energy of a plot.
- **Environment:** A green, airy place with clean air is ideal. Do not take a plot near places that tradition considers inauspicious.
- **Soil:** Fertile, unpolluted soil with a pleasant smell is considered a strong base.

**Steps for checking:**
1. Look for greenery around the plot and check that no inauspicious place lies nearby.
2. Check the quality of the soil and how water drains away.
3. Choose a plot with a gentle slope towards the north or east.

Whether the soil can carry a building, and whether the plot drains well or floods, are engineering questions. For those, rely on a soil test and a licensed engineer.

### Principle 3: Energy Flow and Slope

Tradition holds that energy moves with the lie of the land. Vastu stresses a smooth flow without obstruction.
- **Slope:** A gentle slope towards the north or east is considered to bring wealth and harmony. A slope towards the south or west is called inauspicious.
- **Obstructions:** Tall hills close by in the north or east are said to block positive energy, while a hill in the south-west (Nairitya) is considered protective.

**Suggestions:**
- Avoid a plot that faces a T-junction head-on, or one with sharp projections.
- If the plot has a slight irregularity, soften it with plants along the boundary.
- For disturbances from outside, use measures such as fencing or planting trees.

## How to Assess a Plot

### Step-by-Step Process

**First gather this information:**
- The main directions of the plot (use a compass for accuracy)
- The shape and measurements of the plot
- The position of natural and man-made features such as roads, hills and water bodies
- The type and quality of the soil
- How the land was used before

**Order of checking:**
1. **Fix the direction:** Use a compass to identify the direction of the main entrance.
2. **Judge the shape:** Measure the length and breadth. The ideal ratio is close to 1 to 1 for a square and up to 1 to 2 for a rectangle.
3. **Look at the slope:** Make sure the slope is gentle and towards the north or east.
4. **Check the surroundings:** List everything nearby. Parks and water sources count as favourable; hospitals and cremation grounds count as unfavourable.
5. **Examine the soil:** Dig a small pit and check the fertility, colour and smell.

Write down all your findings. If they differ from the ideal standards, take the advice of a Vastu practitioner before the final decision.

## Practical Use

- **Residential plots:** Choose a plot with an entrance on the north or east, square or rectangular in shape, with a gentle slope to the north or east.
- **Commercial spaces:** A Shermukhi plot, that is one whose front is wide, is considered good for business.
- **Spiritual spaces:** The north-east zone is considered best for temples and meditation rooms.
- **Remedies for irregular plots:** Plants, fencing or changes in construction are used to try to improve the spread of energy.

## Common Mistakes

- Taking a plot of irregular shape or with sharp corners without asking a Vastu practitioner
- Choosing land near a cremation ground, a hospital or heavy industry
- Ignoring the slope and buying a plot that slopes towards the south or west
- Overlooking environmental faults such as noise, pollution and poor soil
- Giving no weight to how the land was used before

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a north-facing or east-facing plot the best in Vastu?

In Vastu tradition, north-facing and east-facing plots are considered very auspicious. They are said to bring positive energy and prosperity. The north-east is linked especially with spiritual progress.

### Can I buy a plot of irregular shape?

A square or rectangular plot is held to be better. Irregular plots are said to carry an imbalance of energy, though some defects can be eased with remedies from a Vastu practitioner.

### What should I do if I already own a plot near a hospital or cremation ground?

Use protective measures such as dense greenery or a fence, and consult a Vastu consultant for further remedies.

### How important is the soil of the plot?

It is very important. Fertile, clean soil with a pleasant smell is considered the basis of a positive environment and a strong foundation. Its strength for building should still be confirmed by a soil test.

### Which way should the plot slope?

A gentle slope towards the north or east is considered auspicious. A slope towards the south or west is called inauspicious.

## Conclusion

The right choice of land is the first step towards a settled and balanced life. By paying attention to direction, shape, surroundings and the flow of energy, you can bring your home or workplace into harmony with nature. It is wise to take the advice of a Vastu practitioner before deciding.
