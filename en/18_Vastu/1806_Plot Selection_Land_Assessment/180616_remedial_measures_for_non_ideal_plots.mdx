# Vastu Remedies for Irregular Plots: Boundary, Planting, Entrance

Many plots are not perfectly square or rectangular. Vastu tradition does not ask a buyer to reject such a plot at once. It describes ways to regularise the usable part and to balance the rest. This article sets out those traditional measures and how they are planned.

## What Remedies for Non-Ideal Plots Are

Remedial measures for non-ideal plots are a set of Vastu adjustments for plots with irregular shapes, missing corners, projections or other inherent faults. Rather than refusing a plot that is not perfectly rectangular or square, the tradition aims to correct the imperfection and harmonise the space.

- **Practical use**: adapting landscaping, boundaries, entrances and other features that do not involve altering the structure.
- **Spiritual side**: the aim is to improve auspiciousness without heavy demolition or abandoning the plot.

### Textual background

The idea of correcting defective plots is traced to classical texts such as the Brihat Samhita and the ancient Vastu Shastra treatises, where the sages are said to have advised practical remedies in preference to avoidance.

## Why It Matters in Vastu

- **Direction**: tradition holds that irregularities disturb the flow of positive energy, and that remedial measures restore it.
- **Flow of energy**: corrective landscaping, boundary walls and placements are meant to restore balance.
- **Materials**: tradition uses natural elements to support the remedies.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: Identify the fault and apply a matching remedy

Look for the irregularities of the plot, such as missing corners, odd shapes and slopes. Then plan the remedy.

- Water features can be used in specific zones.
- Boundaries can be reconfigured, or artificial corners created, through fencing or plantation.

### Principle 2: Balance of plot shape

Harmony is sought by changing the perceived or functional shape of the plot.

1. For L-shaped, T-shaped or triangular plots, divide the plot in thought or with fencing so that rectangular buildable sections are formed.
2. For a missing corner, such as a cut north-east, tradition suggests a water feature at the affected zone.
3. Use boundary walls to exclude an inauspicious projection and concentrate construction on the most regular part of the land.

### Principle 3: Entrance and landscaping

Entrances and landscape elements are non-structural Vastu measures.

- Adjust the position of the entrance so as to avoid unfavourable directions, and place main doors in auspicious sectors. For a west-facing plot these are north, east or north-west.
- Plant tall, dense trees and shrubs at certain corners of the plot to act as a buffer against unfavourable influences from outside. Tradition puts them in the south-west or south, and water features or Tulasi, holy basil, in the north-east.

## How to Plan the Remedies

**What you need**

- The exact dimensions and orientation of the plot, and the kind of irregularity.
- The positions of surrounding roads, water bodies and physical obstacles.

**Steps**

1. **Shape analysis.** Map the plot and identify the missing or extended zones.
2. **Directions.** Use a compass to establish the cardinal points and locate the entrances and defects.
3. **Mapping the remedies.** Assign corrective measures according to the severity of the defect and the Vastu principles, for example water features for north-east defects.

**Example**

A triangular plot that narrows towards the north is regularised by building a compound wall around a rectangular area. Trees and planting stabilise the parts left out, and the entrance is adjusted as an additional remedy.

## Practical Applications

- **Boundary treatment**: build new compound walls or fences to regularise the usable shape of the plot, leaving out projections or cuts.
- **Landscaping**
  - Plant trees or shrubs in the zones considered unfavourable, usually the south-west, south and south-east.
  - Add water features or sacred plants such as Tulasi in the north-east where a corner is missing.
- **Entrance changes**: move or shift an entrance to a favourable direction.
- **Non-structural changes**
  - Change the colours of the affected zones, for example lemon yellow or pistachio green in the south-east.
  - Remove clutter, repair broken items and avoid negative images inside the space.
- **Dividing the plot**: an irregular plot can sometimes be divided into two regular plots by an internal compound wall, which is said to reduce confusion of energy.

Any wall, levelling or drainage work should be planned with a licensed architect or engineer and approved under the local building rules.

## Common Mistakes

- Ignoring the orientation and shape of the plot when choosing it or planning remedies.
- Trying to correct complex or multiple faults without expert guidance.
- Placing heavy structures in the north-east or water features in the south-west, which tradition says intensifies the faults.
- Overlooking small defects such as clutter, dead plants and badly placed furniture, which are said to add to the problem.
- Relying only on structural renovation instead of also using landscaping and non-structural measures.

## Examples and Tips

### Example 1: A house on a triangular plot

The north side of the plot is narrow, which tradition says distorts the flow of energy. A compound wall is raised to form a rectangular living zone, dense trees are planted in the south-west and south-east, and the main entrance is moved to the northern sector. This is an illustration of the traditional method, not a promise of any result.

### Example 2: An office on an L-shaped plot

The north-east corner of the plot is missing. A water feature is placed in the north-east, a boundary wall makes the usable plot rectangular, plants are added along the road at the T-junction, and the leader's office is moved to the south-west. Again, this shows the method and not an outcome.

### Professional tips

- Consult a Vastu expert before any large-scale change.
- Combine remedies of different kinds, structural and landscaping, as tradition recommends.
- Align entrances and the main interior elements with the Vastu directions.
- Record every change and watch the results over several months before refining them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a non-rectangular plot be made suitable under Vastu without demolition?

Vastu tradition holds that most non-ideal plots can be harmonised by adjusting the compound wall, landscaping and changing the entrance. These are traditional measures and not proven cures.

### What if my plot has a missing north-east or south-west corner?

Tradition suggests a water feature or Tulasi plant in the north-east. A cut north-east corner is considered particularly inauspicious, so a consultant should look at it.

### Which shapes are the most difficult, and can they be corrected?

L-shaped, T-shaped, triangular and pentagonal plots are the most challenging. Tradition says that dividing them into regular sections, or using boundary and landscaping changes, softens the effect.

### Do these remedies apply to an existing building?

Many of the non-structural measures, such as planting, colour changes and tidying, can be used even after construction.

### Where should trees and where should water be placed?

Tradition puts tall, dense trees in the south-west or south, and water features or Tulasi in the north-east. It advises against heavy structures in the north-east and against water features in the south-west.

## Conclusion

Vastu tradition offers a graded set of responses to an imperfect plot: regularise the shape with a boundary, plant to balance the corners, adjust the entrance and look after the details inside.

None of this is a proven result, and where walls, levelling or drainage are involved a licensed engineer or architect should plan the work. A qualified Vastu practitioner can advise on the particular plot.
