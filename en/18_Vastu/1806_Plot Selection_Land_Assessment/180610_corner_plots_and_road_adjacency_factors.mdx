# Corner Plots and Road Direction in Vastu

A plot that stands where two or more roads meet is called a corner plot. When judging such a plot, Vastu looks at the direction of the roads beside it. This article explains how Vastu tradition reads corner plots and the direction of roads, and which remedies are described for their shortcomings.

## What Corner Plots and Road Direction Mean

Two things are looked at together: the plot standing at the meeting of two or more roads, and the direction and position of those roads. According to Vastu tradition, this decides the nature of the energy of the plot.

- **Use in Vastu:** A thoughtful use of the form of the plot and the position of the roads, so that the place to live agrees with the principles of Vastu.
- **Spiritual side:** Bringing balance among the five elements and the deities of the directions, so that peace and comfort remain.

### Basis in the Texts

The direction of roads and its effect are believed to be discussed in old Vastu texts such as the Brihat Samhita. In Indian building, the importance of plots at crossroads and at the meeting of roads is held to be very old.

## Why This Matters in Vastu

- **Effect of the direction of the road:** According to tradition, the roads beside a plot concentrate the flow of energy from their direction towards the house, and this can be auspicious or inauspicious. For example, a road coming from the north-east (Ishan) is often considered auspicious.
- **Harmony with direction:** Proper fixing of direction increases the favourable effects of a road and reduces the unfavourable ones.
- **Flow of energy:** A corner plot naturally gets more light and air. But if its direction is not right by Vastu, the same openness can also invite unsettled energy.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: The Effect of the Corner Position

A corner plot is open on several sides, so it is believed that the effect of both auspicious and inauspicious energy increases there.

- First see in which directions the meeting roads lie.
- According to Vastu, roads on the north and east are considered ideal, and roads on the south or west less favourable.
- Keep this direction in mind when fixing places such as the main door, the sitting room and the garden.

### Principle 2: Harmony with the Direction of the Road

Different roads create different kinds of energy.

1. First identify the directions of the roads around the plot.
2. Match them with the guidance of Vastu:
   - Roads on the north or east are considered highly auspicious and are linked with progress and prosperity.
   - Roads on the south or west are generally considered less favourable. Tradition links them with financial and mental strain.
3. Choose the direction of the main door and of the rooms so that the favourable effect of the road is the greatest.

### Principle 3: The T-Junction and Its Effect

A plot directly in front of which a road comes to an end is called a T-junction plot. Tradition considers it problematic, because energy is believed to come straight and very fast towards the house. For this:

- If the road comes straight at the house, make a strong and clear boundary, such as a high wall or dense greenery, so that the excess energy is stopped.
- Do not place the entrance or large windows directly facing the T-junction.

## How to Assess the Plot

**Information needed:**

- The exact directions of the plot and of the roads around it.
- The difference in height between the plot and the roads. A high south-west (Nairitya) and a low north-east is considered ideal.
- Other nearby matters, such as water bodies or electric poles.

**Method of assessing:**

1. Look at the direction of each road and match it with the rules of Vastu. Roads on the north and east are the best.
2. See whether there is a T-junction, and form an idea of how strong its effect is.
3. Look at the height of the plot. A high south-west is considered auspicious, and a high north-east inauspicious.

Examples:

- A corner plot with roads on the north and east is considered highly auspicious.
- A corner plot with roads on the south and west is held to need special remedies.

## Practical Use

- **In a corner plot with a road on the south or west:**
  - Do not place the main door on the south or west road.
  - Raise the south-west part with landscaping or a retaining wall.
  - Plant fragrant and hardy plants as a natural screen.
- **In a plot at a T-junction:**
  - Put up a solid boundary wall, a decorative grill or dense hedges in front of the road.
- **In an auspicious plot with a road on the north and east:**
  - Keep the boundary open and welcoming, make a water source in the north-east, and place the entrance so that it gets the most sunlight and fresh air.

Traffic noise, road safety, drainage and flooding at a corner are matters for a licensed engineer or the local authority, not for Vastu alone.

## Common Mistakes

- Buying a plot without checking the direction of the roads and its Vastu.
- Ignoring the difference in height. Plots that are low in the south or west are considered inauspicious.
- Placing a gate, the main door or large windows directly facing a T-junction.
- Overlooking the effect of electric poles or water bodies in sensitive directions.
- Not treating the boundary properly in a plot with an unfavourable road.

## Examples

These examples are only for illustration.

### Example 1: Correction in a House

A family living in a corner plot with roads on the south-east side was struggling with repeated money and health troubles. On inspection, the boundary in the south-east (Agneya) part was found to be poorly made. The south-west corner was raised and shrubs were planted for protection. According to tradition, such corrections balance the atmosphere.

### Example 2: An Office at a T-Junction

In a new office built on a corner plot at a T-junction, employees kept leaving. Because the road came straight at the building, it was taken that unsettled energy was increasing. So a high fence and dense landscaping were made, and the main entrance was moved from directly in front of the road to the side.

### Some Practical Suggestions

- Before giving Vastu advice, always check the directions with a compass.
- Before a big decision, judge the plot by the rules on the direction of the roads.
- If the traffic of the neighbourhood and the layout of the roads change, judge the position again.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are all corner plots inauspicious according to Vastu?

No. Corner plots with roads on the north and east are considered especially auspicious. A corner plot with roads on the south or west needs special remedies.

### How should I make the boundary of a plot at a T-junction?

A high wall and dense shrubs work well. Do not place the main door directly facing the T-junction.

### Are all shortcomings linked to roads removed by remedies?

Not every shortcoming is removed fully, but with proper Vastu planning and treatment of the boundary, negative effects are held to be much reduced.

### What should I look at first in a corner plot?

First see in which directions the roads lie and what the height of the plot is like. A high south-west and a low north-east is considered ideal.

### Where should the main door go in a plot with a road on the south or west?

Do not face the main door towards the south or west road, and raise the south-west part.

## Conclusion

In Vastu, corner plots and the direction of roads are taken as deciding the nature of the energy of any property. By understanding the special features of a corner plot, especially the direction of roads, the T-junction and the make-up of the boundary, you can gain from auspicious energy and guard against shortcomings. With the principles of Vastu and the advice of an expert, even a difficult plot can be made a comfortable place for a family or a business.
