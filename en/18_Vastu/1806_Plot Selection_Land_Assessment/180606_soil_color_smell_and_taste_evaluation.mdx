# Soil Colour, Smell and Taste in Vastu Land Assessment

Vastu tradition judges land not only by the plan and the direction but also by the senses. The colour of the soil and its smell are taken to show whether a place is favourable for a home or a temple. This article describes the method, the traditional meaning of colours and smells, and the precautions to take.

You will learn:
- What the assessment of soil colour, smell and taste is
- The traditional meaning of different colours and smells
- The method of checking
- Common mistakes and precautions

## What the Assessment of Soil Colour, Smell and Taste Is

It is an old method of Vedic and Vastu tradition in which the soil is examined by the senses to form an idea of the suitability of the land and the quality of its energy. The following are looked at:

- **Colour:** The brightness, shade and depth of the soil, especially yellow, red or brown
- **Smell:** Whether there is a sweet or earthy fragrance, or a bad smell that points to decay and negativity
- **Taste:** Some subtle taste tests, often carried out in the context of farming or ritual, to get an idea of the balance of minerals. This is an old practice only. Do not taste soil.

### Historical Background

The roots of this method are traced to classical texts such as the Brihat Samhita. In them, the teachers are described as using the colour, smell and taste of soil to foretell prosperity, fertility and whether auspicious or inauspicious events were likely when building a home or a temple.

## Why This Assessment Matters in Vastu

In Vastu Shastra this assessment is held to help in these matters:
- **Choice of site:** Seeing that the foundation rests on soil that is favourable in energy.
- **Use according to direction:** Deciding the use and place of rooms by looking at the features of the soil in each direction.
- **Identifying corrections:** Tradition speaks of correcting negative energy by rituals of purification or by changes in the structure.
- **Auspicious timing:** Choosing a suitable time for construction together with the quality of the soil.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: Looking at Colour, Smell and Taste Together

According to this principle, the quality of the soil shows the nature of the elements of the place.

**In practice:**
- Collect samples of soil from the proposed site.
- To judge colour, use the Munsell Color System and note colours such as yellow, red, brown and grey.
- Smell the sample. A sweet, earthy smell is taken as a sign of healthy soil, and it is caused by a substance called geosmin.
- Tradition describes tasting as an optional test, in which a mild and pleasant taste is held to be good and bitterness or sharpness bad. Tasting soil can be unsafe, so it is better to rely on colour and smell instead.

### Principle 2: Reading Energy from Colour

In the traditional method, the colours of soil are classed as follows:
- **Yellow or red:** Very favourable, linked with prosperity, fertility and positive energy
- **Brown:** Generally auspicious, a symbol of stability and nourishment
- **Black, grey or blue:** A sign of moisture or decay, often linked with stagnation and negative influence
- **White or chalky:** May be a sign of too much salt or barrenness, and is considered less favourable

**Steps to follow:**
1. Look at the colour of the topsoil with a standard colour card or a Munsell book.
2. For the best energy, choose land of bright yellow or red colour. Avoid plots in which grey, black or chalky colour dominates.

### Principle 3: Smell and Taste as Signs of Energy

- **Sweet, soft and earthy smell:** Linked with vitality and the health of the microbes in the soil (often because of geosmin), and taken as a sign of auspicious energy.
- **Sour, rotten or sulphur-like smell:** Taken as a sign of poor drainage, of bacteria that grow where there is no oxygen, or of negative vibration.
- **Pleasant and balanced taste:** Taken as a sign of a balance of minerals.

**Suggestions:**
- If the soil gives off a pleasant earthy fragrance, it is believed that growth and good energy will find support there.
- Avoid land with a foul or chemical smell, since these are taken as signs of hidden problems or of negative influence.

## Method of Checking

### Information Needed

- Samples of soil from every part of the possible plot
- A chart of the Munsell colour system to look at
- A clean glass jar and water

### Steps of Checking

1. Record the Munsell colour of the sample, together with its lightness or depth (value) and the strength of the colour (chroma).
2. Judge the **smell:**
   - Earthy or sweet: favourable
   - Rotten or chemical: unfavourable
3. The **taste** test described in tradition is best avoided for reasons of safety:
   - Mild and pleasant: favourable
   - Bitter or astringent: unfavourable

Compare the signs for each point, and for construction or worship choose the plot that has the most favourable signs.

## Practical Use

- When choosing a place for a home, a temple or a business, examine the colour and smell of the soil.
- Use the results to help decide the place of the garden, the water feature and the entrance.
- To improve colour and smell, mix organic matter such as compost into the weak parts.
- Check from time to time, so that changes in the soil can be noticed.

**Examples:**
- Land of deep red colour and earthy smell is considered ideal for a home entrance or a place of worship.
- If you must build on less favourable soil, the tradition of purification described in Vastu texts or improving the soil may be considered.

Whether the soil can carry a building, and whether it is contaminated or drains badly, are matters for a soil test and a licensed engineer. Colour and smell cannot settle them.

## Common Mistakes

- Ignoring the smell of the soil, or taking a bad smell lightly
- Choosing a site by colour alone, without attention to smell
- Not looking at the quality of the soil together with auspicious timing
- Ignoring how the land was used before, which is held to affect the energy of the soil whatever its surface looks like

### Suggestions from Practitioners

- Always use clean tools so that the samples are not contaminated
- Take samples from several places for a correct assessment
- Record all the results for later monitoring and reference
- Treat smell as the first sign of the health of the soil

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I rely on colour alone when judging soil for Vastu?

No. A proper assessment should also look at smell. Colour and smell together give a sign of the overall energy of the land. There is also a tradition of tasting, but it is best avoided for reasons of safety.

### Which colour is considered auspicious?

Yellow and red soil is considered the most favourable, and brown generally auspicious. Black, grey or blue soil is taken as a sign of stagnation, and white chalky soil as a sign of barrenness.

### Do modern soil classification systems agree with Vedic methods?

Modern systems such as the Munsell Color System help to record colour in an objective way. The Vedic method adds the side of energy to it.

### What should I do if the soil of my plot has faults?

Soil can be improved by mixing organic matter into it. Tradition also speaks of purification rituals and of astrological advice.

### What should I look at first when checking soil?

Smell is taken as the first sign of the health of the soil. Then look at the colour, and take samples from several places.

## Conclusion

The assessment of the colour, smell and taste of soil is an old Vastu method of understanding the hidden energy of land. By joining old knowledge with modern scientific methods, you can make your home or workplace balanced. Remember to assess the soil as a whole, since colour and smell together point to both the physical and the subtle sides of your surroundings.
