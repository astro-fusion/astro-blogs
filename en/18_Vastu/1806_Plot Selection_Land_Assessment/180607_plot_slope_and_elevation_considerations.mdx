# Plot Slope and Elevation: What Vastu Prefers

Before buying a plot or building on it, Vastu looks at the slope and height of the land. According to Vastu tradition, the natural incline of the ground decides how prana energy will flow across it. This article explains which slope is considered auspicious, how to check it, and what remedies are used when the slope is not ideal.

## What Slope and Elevation Mean

Slope means the natural incline of the ground, and elevation means its relative height. In Vastu tradition both are taken as the basis for choosing a plot, planning the building and making corrections. These matters are believed to affect both the gross and the subtle sides of the lives of the people who live in the house.

### Basis in the Texts

The shape of the ground and its effects are discussed in Vastu texts such as the Brihat Samhita and the Manushyalaya Chandrika. According to tradition, the ancient sages observed that some slopes and heights give auspicious results, while others bring obstacles and unrest.

## Why Slope Matters in Vastu

Slope and elevation are taken as basic matters in Vastu because:

- **Harmony with direction:** The natural form of the plot should increase auspicious energy and not block it.
- **Flow of energy:** A proper slope lets prana energy flow smoothly through the whole house.
- **Prosperity and health:** In Vastu tradition, land sloping towards the north or east is considered the best for comfort and prosperity, while slopes in other directions are linked with difficulties.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: The Right Order of Height

Before buying a plot or starting construction, see whether the south-west (Nairitya) is the highest and the north-east (Ishan) is the lowest. This is the order suggested in Vastu. A gentle slope towards the north or east is considered good. The slope should not be steep.

### Principle 2: Slope According to Direction

1. See whether the plot slopes gently towards the north or east and is high in the south-west.
2. A slope falling towards the south or west is considered inauspicious in Vastu. Tradition links it with troubles of money and health.
3. If the natural slope is not ideal, adopt Vastu corrections such as changes in landscaping, adjusting the soil or fixing the place of a water source.

### Principle 3: Correction When the Slope Is Not Right

- Change the landscaping so that there is more soil or a raised part in the south-west.
- To improve the flow of energy without major demolition, measures such as planting trees, changing the place of the entrance or making an artificial slope are used.
- For advice suited to your site, meet a Vastu practitioner.

## How to Check the Slope

For this you need to know the directions of the plot and the difference in height at each corner.

1. Measure the height at the four corners of the plot, that is the south-west, south-east (Agneya), north-west (Vayavya) and north-east.
2. The ideal order is this: south-west highest, south-east and north-west medium, north-east lowest.
3. The direction in which the lowest point of the ground lies is the direction of the slope. For example, if the north-east is the lowest, the slope is towards the north-east.

How to read the result:

- Slope towards the north or east: considered highly auspicious in Vastu.
- Slope towards the south or west: considered inauspicious, and according to tradition it can bring losses and difficulties.

## Practical Use

- **Before buying:** Check the natural slope of the land with a level. Give preference to a plot with a gentle slope towards the north or east.
- **In an existing house:** If needed, raise the south-west by adding soil. Remove extra soil from the north-east and do not leave pits there. Keep the flow of water such that it leaves from the north or east, and not from the south or west.
- **In a house on a hill or sloping ground:** Keep the slope of the roof and the terrace towards the north or east as well, and not towards the south or west.

How rainwater drains from a plot, and whether it can flood, are engineering questions. Before changing levels or the flow of water, have a licensed engineer check the plan.

## Common Mistakes

- Not paying attention to the natural slope of the plot before building.
- Choosing land only by location or price and ignoring the slope.
- Levelling the plot wrongly, so that an inauspicious slope is created.
- Overlooking small pits or mounds, which tradition also holds to affect the flow of energy.

## Examples

These examples are only for illustration.

### Example 1: Correction in a House

A family had been struggling for a long time with money troubles and repeated illness. On inspection it was found that the plot sloped from the south-west to the north-east and that there was a pit in the north-east. In keeping with Vastu, the south-west was raised by adding soil, the pit in the north-east was filled and the flow of water was turned towards the north. According to tradition, such corrections balance the atmosphere of the house.

### Example 2: Correction in an Office

A new company was in a state of stagnation, with disagreements in the team. The office building stood on ground sloping towards the west. There the south-west was raised by landscaping, a water source was placed in the north-east and the main entrance was turned towards the east.

### Some Practical Suggestions

- Check not only the flatness but also the small ups and downs of the ground.
- After the corrections, write down the changes seen in the home or the business.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does Vastu advise a gentle slope towards the north or east?

According to Vastu tradition, a slope towards the north and east is considered helpful in the coming of auspicious energy and prosperity.

### What should I do if my plot slopes towards the south or west?

Vastu remedies such as raising the south-west, planting trees and placing a water source in an auspicious direction are used, to put the flow of energy right.

### Do these rules apply only in India?

Their roots are in Indian tradition, but with small changes according to the geography and climate, they are also adopted in other places.

### Can the trouble of a bad slope be fixed without pulling down the house?

In many cases it can be improved by changing the landscaping, correcting the spread of soil and turning the flow of water. For your site, take the advice of a Vastu practitioner.

### What should I watch for in a house on hilly ground?

Keep the slope of the roof and the terrace towards the north or east as well, and not towards the south or west.

## Conclusion

The slope and elevation of a plot are considered very important in Vastu. Choosing a plot with a gentle slope towards the north or east, or making suitable corrections in an existing house, is considered favourable to comfort and peace in the home and the workplace. Every piece of land is different, so along with tradition, do a practical check of the land, and take the advice of a Vastu practitioner if needed.
