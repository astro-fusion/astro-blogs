# Using a Vastu Compass to Measure Directions Accurately

Every Vastu rule depends on direction, so a plan marked with the wrong north gives the wrong answers. This article explains how to find the directions of a plot with a compass, why magnetic north and true north differ, and how the Vastu Purusha Mandala is laid over a plan.

## What Measuring Directions With a Vastu Compass Means

Using a Vastu compass for directional accuracy is the systematic use of a magnetic or digital compass to find the cardinal directions during the assessment of a plot or building, so that the Vastu principles are applied to the correct directions.

- **Practical use**: making sure that every room, entrance and activity zone is placed according to Vastu guidelines.
- **Spiritual side**: Vastu tradition aims to tune the built space to the subtle forces of the cosmos.

### Textual background

The idea is traced to classical Vedic texts such as the Brihat Samhita and to Vastu Shastra manuscripts. These texts are said to stress exact orientation and the tools needed to find it.

## Why It Matters in Vastu

- **Direction**: each of the 16 Vastu zones is considered sensitive to its exact direction. Vastu tradition holds that even a deviation of a few degrees can move a zone out of its place.
- **Flow of energy**: tradition holds that correct direction-finding supports the flow of prana, the life energy, through the property.
- **Layout decisions**: the main entrance, rooms and objects are placed using the direction map.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: Find the main directions with a reliable compass

The basis of a Vastu plot assessment is the correct identification of north, south, east and west.

- Stand at the geometric centre of the plot and place the compass on a flat, non-magnetic surface.
- Wait for the needle to settle, then mark true north and map all other directions from it.
- Transfer these directions onto your plan before construction or interior arrangement.

### Principle 2: Correct for magnetic declination

The magnetic north of the Earth and the geographic or true north are not always the same. The angle between them is called magnetic declination.

1. Find the magnetic declination for your area from government sources or online maps.
2. Correct your compass readings accordingly.
   - If your compass has an adjustable declination, set it before use.
   - If not, add or subtract the declination angle by hand when marking directions.
3. Always take the true cardinal directions as your reference for a Vastu assessment.

### Principle 3: Map the Vastu Purusha Mandala

The Vastu Purusha Mandala is the sacred grid that divides a plot into zones linked with particular energies and deities.

- Lay the grid so that the central Brahmasthan and all 16 zones sit on the true cardinal axes found with your compass.
- Mark the entrances, main rooms and the sensitive points called marmasthans on the grid.

## How to Fix Directions Step by Step

**What you need**

- An accurate plan of the plot or house.
- The centre point of the property.
- A reliable compass, whether analogue, digital or an app.

**Method**

1. Place the compass at the geometric centre of the plot.
2. Keep it on a wooden or other non-metallic surface and away from electronic devices, so that there is no electromagnetic interference.
3. Let the needle settle and mark magnetic north.
4. Correct for magnetic declination to turn magnetic north into true north.
5. Draw the north-south and east-west axes.
6. From these axes, draw the 16 or 32 zones of the Vastu Purusha Mandala.

## Practical Applications

- **Plot assessment**: before buying or designing a property, map each direction carefully and see whether the Vastu zones can be respected.
- **Placing rooms**: assign the use of each room on the basis of true direction. For example, the prayer room goes in the north-east and the master bedroom in the south-west.
- **Main entrance**: mark the main entrance in an auspicious zone after laying the Mandala on the correctly mapped plan.
- **Marma points**: avoid placing heavy objects or digging at the marma points identified on the Mandala.
- **Renovation**: use the compass and the Mandala to re-plan partitions, furniture and decor.

## Common Mistakes

- **Using a compass near metal or electronic objects**, which can distort the reading.
- **Ignoring magnetic declination**, which can cause an error of up to 20 degrees in some places.
- **Misjudging the centre of the plot.** Always use careful measurements.
- **Marking the inside and the outside differently**, which leads to Vastu being applied to the wrong directions.
- **Overlooking the zones of the Vastu Purusha Mandala.**

## Examples and Tips

### Example 1: A family house

A family finds, on checking with a compass, that the main entrance sits away from its intended zone. The entrance is moved to the true north-east, and heavy furniture is cleared from the Brahmasthan, the central zone. This is an illustration of the method and not a promise of any result.

### Example 2: An office

A compass check shows that the conference area of an office does not fall in the zone the Mandala suggests for it. The workspace is re-planned using the true cardinal directions. Again, this shows the method and not an outcome.

### Professional tips

- Always check the local magnetic declination.
- Use the compass at different times of the day and cross-check with a map.
- Mark directions with permanent reference points so that they can be used again.
- Note the changes you see after any adjustment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use my smartphone compass for Vastu directions?

Yes, provided it is calibrated and corrected for magnetic declination. Make sure there is no metal or electronic interference while you take the reading.

### How often should I re-check the directions of my property?

Re-check when you renovate, when you move large metal objects, or when a particular zone keeps giving you trouble.

### Do homes in the northern and southern hemispheres use the same directions?

The principles remain the same. Always correct for your local declination and your actual geographic position.

### Where should the compass be placed?

At the geometric centre of the plot or house, on a flat wooden or non-metallic surface, away from electronic devices.

### Why is the correction for magnetic declination necessary?

Magnetic north and true north can differ, and in some places the difference is as much as 20 degrees. A Vastu assessment should rest on the true directions.

## Conclusion

Accurate direction-finding is the first step of a Vastu assessment. Using the compass carefully, correcting for magnetic declination and laying the Vastu Purusha Mandala over the plan correctly are the steps this article describes.

Tradition holds that a layout placed on the true directions follows Vastu as intended. Where a surveyor's plan is available, it is worth checking it against your own readings.
