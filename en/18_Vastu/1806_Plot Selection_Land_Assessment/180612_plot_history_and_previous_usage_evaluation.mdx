# Plot History and Previous Use: What to Check Before Buying

What stood on a piece of land before you came to it matters to a buyer, and Vastu tradition also takes note of it. This article explains how to find out the history of a plot, how tradition reads different former uses, and which checks belong to engineers rather than to Vastu.

## What Plot History Evaluation Means

Plot history evaluation is a systematic look at the past activities on a piece of land, its earlier condition and its history, before deciding whether to build there.

- **Practical use**: finding out the past of a plot when choosing a site for a home or a workplace.
- **Spiritual side**: the connection between the earlier use of the land and its present state, as the tradition understands it.

### Textual background

The idea is traced to classical texts such as the Brihat Samhita and to Vastu Shastra manuscripts. Vastu tradition holds that land keeps an imprint of earlier occupants and activities, and that this affects those who live there later.

## Why It Matters in Vastu

- **Layout**: tradition holds that the history of a plot can guide how rooms are placed.
- **Flow of energy**: the earlier uses of the land are believed to leave their mark on it.
- **Materials**: tradition suggests building materials and colours chosen to balance an unfavourable past.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: Look into the history of the land

- Research the earlier use of the land through public records and local accounts.
- Apply the Vastu guidance that goes with that history, whether a hospital, a cemetery, a temple or something else.
- Note any changes after remedial measures have been carried out.

### Principle 2: Balance of energy

1. Write down the known earlier uses of the property.
2. Map the matching Vastu zones on the plan of your proposed home or office.
3. Apply the traditional remedy that goes with the past use.

### Principle 3: Purification of the land

- Tradition combines auspicious timing with Vastu adjustments during land purification rites.
- It also recommends daily practices that keep the place clean and well kept.

## How to Investigate a Plot

**What you need**

- The present position and orientation of the plot.
- Records of earlier use of the land.
- A list of your specific concerns.

**Steps**

1. **Collect the history**
   - Check municipal records for earlier structures.
   - Ask long-time local residents about how the land was used.
   - Study old maps and aerial photographs to see how the land looked in the past.
2. **Map the plot**
   - Divide the plot into a 9 by 9 grid, like the traditional Vastu Purusha Mandala.
   - Record any unusual observations in each square.
   - Note areas where plants grow very poorly or very heavily.

## Practical Applications

### Judging the history by observation

Before buying land, check it in these ways.

1. **Direct observation**
   - Look at the soil quality and composition in different parts of the plot.
   - Note unusual patterns of vegetation or places where plants do not grow.
   - Note unexplained differences of temperature across the plot.
2. **Visiting at different times**
   - Visit the plot at different times of the day.
   - Pay attention to how you feel in different parts of the plot. This is a personal impression and not a measurement.
3. **Local enquiry**
   - Look through old records at local government offices.
   - Ask neighbours about earlier uses of the land.
   - Speak to local historians or elderly residents about important events.

### Traditional responses by earlier use

**Former farmland**

- Generally considered favourable, especially if it was productive.
- Usually little remedial work is said to be needed.
- Planting fruit trees is traditionally suggested to keep the nurturing quality of the land.

**Former industrial sites**

- Have the soil tested thoroughly for contaminants. This is a matter for a licensed environmental or geotechnical engineer.
- Tradition describes a havan or yagna to purify the elements.
- A water feature in the north-east is traditionally used to balance an excess of the fire element.

**Land with a sad history**

- Tradition holds special purification rites on auspicious days.
- Protective markers at the corners of the plot are traditionally used.
- Some practitioners advise delaying construction until several purification ceremonies are complete.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Ignoring small signs.** Many buyers look only at the physical plot and the price and overlook hints of a troubled history.
2. **Too little research.** Relying only on what the seller says, without checking independently, can mean learning about an unfavourable past after the purchase.
3. **Rushing remedies.** Trying to purify land without proper timing or a full understanding of its history is traditionally said to give limited results.
4. **Overlooking geology.** Natural features such as underground water streams or mineral deposits affect a site and should be part of the evaluation. Ask a qualified geologist or engineer.
5. **Misreading impressions.** Not every unusual feeling on a site comes from its past. It may be a natural variation or simply the visitor's own mood.

## Examples

### Example 1: Trouble in a newly built house

A family has persistent problems in a newly built house. Enquiry shows the land was once a small medical clinic. They carry out the traditional purification before further work. This is an illustration of the approach, not a promised result.

### Example 2: A shop

A retail business has unexplained stock discrepancies and disputes among staff. The site turns out to have been a police station with cells in the south-west part. The owner redesigns the layout so valuable goods are not kept in the south-west. Again, the example shows the method, not an outcome.

### Practical tips

- Check the history against more than one source before drawing conclusions.
- Allow for seasonal and geographical differences when you observe a site.
- Combine physical checks, local enquiry and Vastu assessment.
- Note the results and adjust your plans as the occupants report back.
- Keep the premises clean and well maintained.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far back should I research a plot before buying?

Ideally the research should cover about 100 years. The most recent 50 years are the most important, since tradition holds that recent imprints are the strongest. Pay special attention to any major event or change of use in that period.

### Can an unfavourable history be removed completely?

Complete neutralisation is difficult. Vastu tradition holds that remedies can balance the energy of the land, and that the result depends on how serious the past events were and how thoroughly the measures are carried out. These are traditional beliefs, not certainties.

### How can I find the history of a plot if there are no records?

- Ask local elders and long-time residents.
- Study aerial photographs from different decades.
- Ask a Vastu consultant for an on-site assessment.
- Look at how the surrounding land is used for clues.

### Do natural disasters affect a plot differently from human activity?

Tradition holds that they leave a different kind of imprint. Floods and fires are said to clear unfavourable energies in some cases, while land that has had earthquakes or landslides is said to stay unstable. Stability of such land is an engineering matter, so consult a licensed engineer.

### How does earlier farming use affect land?

Agricultural land is generally regarded as favourable, especially if it was productive and well cared for. Land where heavy chemicals were used or animals were kept in poor conditions is said to need cleansing. Tradition associates farmland with a strong earth element, which supports stability.

## Conclusion

Vastu tradition holds that the past of a plot should be understood before a new building is placed on it. Researching its earlier use, reading the history against the traditional framework and carrying out the matching remedies are the steps it recommends.

Land has a physical history too, and soil contamination, ground stability and legal records are matters for qualified professionals. Whether you are buying a home or setting up a business, a careful look at the history of the plot is part of a sound decision.
