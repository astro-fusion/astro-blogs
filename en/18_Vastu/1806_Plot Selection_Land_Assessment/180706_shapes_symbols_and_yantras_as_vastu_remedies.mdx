# Shapes, Symbols and Yantras in Vastu Tradition

In many homes and offices the construction cannot be changed. For such cases Vastu tradition describes certain shapes, symbols and yantras as remedies that need no demolition. The Vastu yantra is the most common of these. This article explains the belief behind them, which yantra is named for which fault, where it is traditionally placed and what mistakes to avoid. It describes tradition. It does not recommend buying anything.

## What Shapes, Symbols and Yantras Are

In Vastu and Vedic tradition these are regarded as a means of correcting an imbalance in the energy of a home. Three aspects are described:

- **The astrological side:** It is believed that the energy of the planets can be directed through particular geometric shapes.
- **The Vastu side:** It is said that by placing yantras properly, an energy imbalance can be corrected without changing the construction.
- **The spiritual side:** A relationship between cosmic energy and the living space is assumed through sacred geometry.

A yantra is a geometric design that begins at the centre and spreads outward. It is regarded as a symbol and vessel of particular cosmic energies. Instead of changing the construction, such measures are said to balance the subtle energy field, which is why they are used in modern homes and offices where changing the structure is difficult.

### Historical Background

It is said that the idea of using sacred geometry as a Vastu remedy comes from ancient texts such as the Brihat Samhita and the Vastu Shastra manuscripts. In tradition, yantras were kept near the image of a deity in temples, or placed at the altar and the entrance of a home. In South India there has been a custom of drawing yantra-like patterns as rangoli at the door of the house during festivals.

## Shapes and Yantras in Vastu and Astrology

### From the Astrological Side

- **A means of directing energy:** Tradition holds that geometric shapes can give direction and balance to the influence of the planets.
- **Correcting doshas:** It is said that they reduce the negative effect of faulty construction or layout.
- **Planetary measures:** They are regarded as a means of balancing cosmic influences without demolition.

### From the Vastu Side

- **Energy balance:** Their use is described for correcting an energy imbalance in the eight directions of a property.
- **Harmony of the elements:** Yantras are divided according to the five elements, earth, air, fire, water and space, and in tradition they have been made in gold, silver or copper.
- **Correction without changing the building:** They are regarded as a means of correcting an imbalance without structural change to a home or office.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: The Energy of Shapes

According to tradition every shape has its own energy, which can be used in Vastu correction.

- A triangle with its point upward stands for Shakti (feminine energy) or the fire element, and a triangle with its point downward stands for water.
- A circle (chakra) stands for rotation and perfection and indicates the air element.
- A square (bhupura) stands for the earth element and the physical form. It is believed to complete the evolution of creation from the subtle to the gross.

### Principle 2: Placing a Yantra Properly

The thought is that placing a yantra in the right spot balances the flow of energy through the whole home.

**Order of steps:**

1. Identify the Vastu doshas of your home or workplace.
2. Choose the yantra that tradition names for the directional fault.
3. Install it by the traditional method, with the ritual of pran pratishtha.

It is said of the Vastu Dosh Nivaran Yantra that it is meant for serious Vastu faults, such as a wrong direction of the house, wrongly placed rooms or the absence of a part of the Vastu plan. It is named for homes and offices with money trouble, illness or constant quarrels. This is a traditional belief and not a promise of any result.

### Principle 3: Pyramids

The pyramid is a shape that some sellers and practitioners present as a means of focusing energy. It is not part of classical Vastu. There is no independent verification of any effect, and this article gives no purchase advice. Where space is small, as in a flat, flat versions of the pyramid are marketed.

## How to Choose a Yantra

### Steps of Assessment

You need this information:

- The existing plan of the house, with the directions marked.
- The areas of concern or the aims of the correction.
- The Vastu faults that are to be corrected.

**Method:**

1. **Map the directions:** Identify the eight directions of the property.
2. **Identify the fault:** Look for the particular Vastu dosha in each direction.
3. **Choose the measure:** Decide which yantra or shape is named for each fault.
4. **Plan the installation:** Decide the most suitable place for it.

### Types of Yantra

Different yantras are named for different purposes.

**Vastu Yantra (Krityanashak Vastu Yantra):**

- Described as a general measure for an overall energy imbalance.
- It is believed to reduce negative influences and increase the flow of positive energy.
- It is said to be placed in the north-east (Ishan) or in the centre of the house.

**Vastu Dosh Nivaran Yantra:**

- Named for serious Vastu faults.
- It is said that heavy faults can be handled with it without breaking the structure.
- Placing it in the north-east, the west or near the main entrance is said to be suitable.

**Vastu Purush Yantra:**

- Regarded as bringing energy into step with the Vastu Purusha.
- It is said to bring harmony among the five elements.
- It is said to be installed in the centre (Brahmasthan), the north-east or at the main entrance.

## How They Are Used in the Home

### The Main Entrance

The main door is the principal path by which energy enters a home. It is customary to place yantras here.

**Door yantra:** Hanging a Vastu yantra on the main door is both decoration and, in tradition, a means of keeping negative energy away. It is like the old custom of placing symbols such as the swastika or Om at a door.

### The Bedroom

Tradition does not give a yantra for the bedroom. What it stresses is the position of the bed: sleep with the head towards the south or east and avoid the north. A bedroom that is calm and uncluttered is what the tradition asks for.

### The Workplace

**Copper Bhaum Yantra:** It is named for a fault in the south direction of a workplace. In tradition it is regarded as reducing the negative effect of the planet Mars and as removing obstacles to success.

## Common Mistakes

### Placing in the Wrong Spot

**Mistake:** Placing a yantra in an unsuitable place or direction.

**What to do:** Follow the rules of direction carefully. For example, the Vastu yantra is placed in the north-east or the centre of the house, and the Vastu Dosh Nivaran Yantra in the north-east, the west or near the main entrance.

### Skipping the Pran Pratishtha

**Mistake:** Putting up a yantra without the ritual of pran pratishtha.

**What to do:** In tradition a yantra is regarded as activated through pran pratishtha. In it, special mantras are chanted, flowers and incense are offered and a lamp is lit.

### Lack of Care

**Mistake:** Not looking after the cleanliness and sanctity of an installed yantra.

**What to do:** Clean the yantra regularly with rose water or Ganga water and keep the surroundings pure. Tradition asks for daily worship of it with flowers, incense and a lamp.

### Mixing Incompatible Energies

**Mistake:** Placing a yantra near a mirror, glass or a source of opposing energy.

**What to do:** Do not place a yantra near a mirror or glass, because they are believed to reflect and distort the pattern of energy. Keep a proper distance between different yantras so that their energies do not clash.

- Always do a full Vastu check before choosing a measure.
- Take account of both the birth charts of the residents and the nature of the place.
- Write down the changes in energy, mood and circumstances after installation.
- If you see a crack or damp in the building, call an engineer. No shape or symbol replaces repair.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Vastu yantras are regarded as the main ones for a home?

The Vastu Yantra, the Vastu Dosh Nivaran Yantra and the Vastu Purush Yantra are regarded as the main ones. The Vastu Yantra is named for general correction, the Vastu Dosh Nivaran Yantra for serious faults and the Vastu Purush Yantra for balancing energy with the Vastu Purusha.

### Can a Vastu yantra be kept in any room of the house?

It can be placed in different rooms, but the north-east corner is named as most suitable, which is good for worship and for morning sunlight. As an alternative it can be placed on the south-east wall with enough light.

### Is it necessary to bury a Vastu yantra in the ground?

Burying it in the ground at the time of construction is the traditional way. In a house that is already built, or in a flat, keeping it in the north-east corner or on a table in the sitting room is also considered acceptable. Tradition says it is better to install it before construction and under the guidance of a knowledgeable practitioner.

### How is a yantra looked after?

A yantra kept above ground should be looked after by regular cleaning and worship.

### Can pyramids replace structural corrections?

Pyramids are sellers' products and are not part of classical Vastu, and no independent verification of their effect is known. They should not be taken as a firm solution for a serious construction fault.

## Conclusion

In Vastu tradition, shapes, symbols and yantras are regarded as means of correction that do not change the structure. By understanding the identity of the triangle, circle and square and the significance of the different yantras, you can see what the tradition holds about them and decide what your home needs.

Keep reverence and purity in mind while adopting them, keep the yantra clean and keep watching the changes. These measures stand as a bridge between old Vastu tradition and present-day living, but they are traditional beliefs and do not ensure any benefit.
