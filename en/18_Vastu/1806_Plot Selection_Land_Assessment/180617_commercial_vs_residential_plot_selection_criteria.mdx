# Commercial and Residential Plot Selection Under Vastu

A shop or an office and a family home do not ask the same things of a plot. Vastu tradition gives different guidance for each, and a buyer also has practical questions such as access, utilities and budget. This article sets out both sides.

## What Plot Selection Means for Commercial and Residential Use

Plot selection is the choice of a site according to its purpose. Vastu lays down guidelines for residential and commercial properties, with specific points on shape, orientation and the flow of energy.

- **Practical use**: choosing and arranging a plot according to whether it will serve a family or a business.
- **Spiritual side**: the idea that the built environment shapes the lives of the people in it.

### Textual background

The idea of site selection is traced to classical Vedic texts such as the Brihat Samhita and to Vastu Shastra manuscripts, which discuss the relationship between the choice of site and the harmony of its environment.

## Why It Matters in Vastu

- **Direction**: placing rooms according to the purpose of the property.
- **Flow of energy**: tradition holds that it affects both living and commercial spaces.
- **Materials**: choosing suitable building materials and colours.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: Plot shape

**Commercial properties**

- The ideal shape for a commercial site is a perfect square or a rectangle with a ratio of width to length of 1 to 2.
- A commercial plot should have right-angled corners in the south-west and the south-east.
- Commercial plots are traditionally Shermukhi, the lion-faced plot, which is wider at the front. It is seen as a symbol of power and authority.

**Residential properties**

- Square or rectangular plots are said to bring balance and harmony to a home.
- Residential plots are traditionally Gomukhi, the cow-faced plot, which is narrower at the front. It is seen as a symbol of nurture and abundance.
- Avoid irregularly shaped plots for both uses, since they are believed to disturb energy.

### Principle 2: Balance of direction

**For commercial use**

1. Heavy furniture and machinery should go in the south.
2. The main structure or plant of the commercial property should be in the south-west.
3. Light machinery can be placed towards the north.
4. If possible, make the south and north boundaries of thick stone or concrete.

**For residential use**

1. The longer sides of the plot should face east-west or north-south.
2. The main entrance should be in an auspicious direction: east, north or north-east.
3. The plot should be higher in the south and west than in the north and east, so that the ground slopes towards the north-east.
4. Build closer to the south and west boundaries and leave open space to the north and east.

### Principle 3: Choosing the location

**Commercial considerations**

- Access and transport matter. Nearness to highways, public transport and airports adds to the business potential.
- Visibility from main roads and the chance to put up signage affect a business.
- Find out whether similar businesses already operate in the area.
- Assess the utilities, the state of the roads and plans for future development.

**Residential considerations**

- Nearness to schools, parks and community amenities.
- Noise levels and privacy.
- Natural light and ventilation.
- Long-term development plans for the neighbourhood.

## The Selection Process

### For commercial properties

**Step 1: Identify potential markets.** Evaluate markets on these factors:

- Access to the existing supply chain.
- Cost and availability of labour.
- Availability and stability of electricity.
- Local and regional taxes and regulations.
- Quality of infrastructure, including roads.
- Site preparation and construction costs.
- Permit and zoning requirements.

**Step 2: Evaluate the site.** Once the best markets are identified, evaluate the sites in them.

- Analyse access and visibility on the site.
- Assess the population and the area covered.
- Look at the road frontage and the traffic.
- Look at how vehicles enter and leave.
- Consider whether the site can be expanded.

**Step 3: Assess financial feasibility.** Work out whether the project makes financial sense.

- The initial investment, including purchase and construction.
- Expected running costs, such as utilities, maintenance and rent.
- Expected income from the location.
- The return over a defined period.

**Step 4: Ask about incentives.** Raise this alongside the review of the location. Ask what the local authorities offer, such as utility concessions.

### For residential plots

**Step 1: Set the basic requirements**

- The minimum area you need.
- Whether zoning allows residential use.
- Your budget for purchase and development.
- The size and lifestyle of the family.

**Step 2: Check against Vastu**

- Plot shape, preferably square or rectangular.
- The boundary, for regularity and soundness.
- The surroundings, avoiding nearness to hospitals and graveyards.
- Water bodies, which are considered favourable in the north-east or north.
- Elevation, with the south and west higher.

**Step 3: Assess the environment**

- Exposure to natural light.
- The direction of the prevailing wind.
- The soil, for foundation and gardening.
- The depth and quality of the water table.

Soil, foundation and water-table questions are engineering ones. Ask a licensed engineer.

## Examples and Tips

### Example 1: A commercial complex

A retail complex has a good location but its entrance and orientation are not in line with Vastu. The owners shift the main entrance to face north-east, add water features in the north-east, build stone walls on the south and south-west boundaries, and move heavy equipment and storage to the south. This is an illustration of the traditional method, not a promise of any result.

### Example 2: Choosing a residential plot

A family moving to a new city has three plots to choose from. They apply Vastu principles in this way.

- They choose a rectangular plot oriented north-south.
- They check for a gentle slope, with the south and west higher.
- They note where tall trees stand on the plot, since tradition places them in the south-west.
- They select the plot that is open to the east for morning light.

This shows how the criteria are applied, not what the outcome will be.

### Professional tips

- Visit the site at different times of day before you decide.
- Look at how the sun path and the shadows change with the seasons.
- Combine traditional Vastu with modern needs of access and convenience.
- Note the conditions at the start so that you can compare them later.
- For a commercial property, balance the Vastu principles against the particular needs of the business.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does plot shape matter differently for commercial and residential properties?

Tradition prefers a Shermukhi plot, wider at the front, for business, and a Gomukhi plot, narrower at the front, for a home. In both cases a regular shape, square or rectangular, with proper proportions is preferred.

### What are the most important Vastu points when choosing a commercial plot?

The main points are the position of the entrance, preferably north or east, the orientation of the plot with the longer sides facing east-west or north-south, strong south and north boundaries, and heavier structures in the south and south-west. Road access and visibility matter too, and the main structure is ideally in the south-west.

### Which way should the ground slope?

For both kinds of plot, tradition prefers the south and south-west to be higher, with the ground sloping towards the north-east, which is the lowest part.

### Can modern business needs override Vastu in commercial plot selection?

Practical needs such as access, visibility and the customer base are essential. The sensible course is to meet the needs that cannot be given up and to apply as much Vastu as possible, adapting the interior layout and the entrance where the plot falls short.

### How much do the surroundings matter?

For a business, neighbouring businesses, traffic and competitors bear directly on access and customers. For a home, nearness to green spaces and schools, and the absence of hospitals or crematoriums, comes first. In both cases tradition prefers water bodies to the north or north-east.

## Conclusion

Vastu tradition treats a commercial plot and a residential plot differently. A business is advised to look at access, visibility and strength in the southern directions, with heavy infrastructure placed accordingly. A home is advised to look at balanced proportions, a pleasant surrounding and natural light.

In either case, practical requirements and Vastu guidance should be weighed together. Consult a Vastu practitioner and a real estate professional, and a licensed engineer where the ground is concerned.
