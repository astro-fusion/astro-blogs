# Plot Size and Proportions: How Vastu Assesses a Plot

The shape and size of a plot are among the first things a buyer looks at, and Vastu has its own rules about them. This article explains the ideal shapes and proportions, how a plot is measured, the sizes traditionally given for different buildings, and the usual treatment for plots that do not fit the ideal.

## What Plot Size and Proportions Assessment Means

It is a systematic assessment of the dimensions and geometry of a piece of land according to Vastu principles.

- **Practical use**: deciding the ideal dimensions of a plot for a home or a workplace.
- **Spiritual side**: the relationship that tradition sees between cosmic energies and the proportions of a living space.

### Textual background

The idea is traced to classical Vedic texts such as the Brihat Samhita and to Vastu Shastra manuscripts, which give guidelines for the proportions of land that are considered auspicious.

## Why It Matters in Vastu

- **Direction**: the orientation of the plot to the cardinal directions.
- **Flow of energy**: Vastu tradition holds that correct proportions distribute energy evenly across the property.
- **Materials**: choosing materials that suit the characteristics of the plot.

Vastu tradition holds that square and rectangular plots are the most auspicious. For a rectangle, the ideal ratio of length to width is taken as 1 to 2 or less.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: Analysing the dimensions

According to Vastu, the ideal plot is square or rectangular, with its sides facing squarely on the four cardinal directions. A square plot symbolises perfect balance and is considered the most auspicious. A rectangular plot should keep a proportion of 1 to 2 between width and length.

**Practical use**

- Take the exact dimensions of the plot and work out the ratio of length to width.
- Apply the Vastu guidance that goes with the shape and orientation of the plot.
- In a rectangular plot, the longer sides ideally run north to south rather than west to east.

### Principle 2: Balance of shape

Vastu classifies plot shapes into several categories, each with its own believed effect on energy.

- Square and rectangular plots: the most auspicious, said to bring balance and prosperity.
- Octagonal plots: considered auspicious for all-round prosperity.
- Hexagonal plots: generally favourable if the other Vastu principles are observed.
- Triangular, circular and irregularly shaped plots: often considered inauspicious without remedial measures.

**Steps**

1. Decide the geometric class of your plot.
2. Note any departures from the ideal shape, such as corners that are not 90 degrees, projections and cut corners.
3. For a non-ideal shape, apply the traditional treatment through landscaping or changes to the boundary.

### Principle 3: Choosing the size

The traditional measures for different kinds of buildings run as follows.

- 60 by 40 feet (2,400 square feet) for small structures.
- 100 by 100 feet (10,000 square feet) for larger buildings and estate homes.

The right size is said to depend on the intended use, the size of the family and the person's own considerations.

**Placement of rooms**

- Tradition places the master bedroom in the south-west and the prayer room in the north-east.
- It places the kitchen in the south-east.

## How to Assess a Plot

**What you need**

- The exact measurements of the plot, length and width.
- The position of the plot relative to the cardinal directions.
- Any irregularity in the shape or the angles.

**Steps**

1. **Ratio of length to width.** Divide the length by the width. The ideal ratio is 1 to 1 for a square or up to 1 to 2 for a rectangle.
2. **Corner angles.** Measure every corner. The corners ideally measure 90 degrees.
3. **Alignment with directions.** See how the plot lines up with the cardinal directions.
4. **Slope.** Measure the natural slope of the land. Tradition holds that the south-west and the south should be higher than the north-east, so that the ground slopes towards the north-east, which is the lowest part.

### Ayadi calculation for a plot

The Ayadi calculation is said to be taken from the Viswakarma Prakash text. It is used to decide how auspicious a particular length and breadth are for a plot.

1. Divide the full 360 degrees into 16 equal parts of 22.5 degrees each.
2. Evaluate nine Ayadi points: Aya, Var, Ansha, Dravya, Vyaya, Nakshatra, Tithi, Yog and Aayu.
3. On this basis, decide the length to breadth ratio for the particular plot.

## Practical Applications

### Ideal measures by type of building

Different buildings are given different measures.

**Residential buildings**

- Small homes: 60 by 40 feet (2,400 square feet).
- Medium homes: 80 by 60 feet (4,800 square feet).
- Large estates: 100 by 100 feet (10,000 square feet).

**Commercial properties**

- Small offices: 40 by 30 feet (1,200 square feet).
- Medium businesses: 80 by 60 feet (4,800 square feet).
- Large commercial complexes: custom dimensions, with the 1 to 2 ratio kept.

**Usual room sizes**

- Bedrooms: 10 by 12 feet to 14 by 16 feet.
- Living rooms: 16 by 20 feet.
- Kitchens: 10 by 12 feet.

### Treating irregular plots

These are the traditional remedies for plots that depart from the ideal.

**Triangular plots**

- Build a boundary wall around a rectangular portion of the land.
- Plant trees outside the boundary in the south-west and south-east corners.
- Use parking and landscaping to balance the plot.

**L-shaped plots**

- If possible, buy additional land to fill in the missing corner.
- A cut north-east corner is considered particularly inauspicious and calls for significant remedial work.
- Use landscaping and built elements to make the missing portion appear complete.

**Plots with projecting corners**

- A projection towards the north-east is considered auspicious.
- A projection on other sides, particularly the south-west, should be avoided or treated.
- A portion that projects beyond an ideal rectangle can be left unused or given away.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Ignoring corner angles

Many people look only at the size of the plot and forget the importance of right angles. Vastu says all four corners should ideally be 90 degrees, with special attention to the north-east corner.

**Remedy**: if the corners are not right angles, set boundaries within the plot that give the main building proper 90 degree corners.

### Mistake 2: Buying a plot with a slope against Vastu

Vastu says the south-west and west should be higher than the north-east and east, so that the ground slopes towards the north-east. Buying land with the opposite slope is a common error.

**Remedy**: have the ground levelled properly before construction, or create a suitable slope with landscaping. Levelling and drainage should be planned with a licensed engineer.

### Mistake 3: Choosing a plot with an unfavourable surrounding

Many buyers look only at the plot and ignore the things around it that are believed to affect energy.

**Avoid plots near**

- Graveyards, cemeteries or tombs.
- Foul-smelling areas, within 80 to 100 feet.
- Dead-end roads pointing directly at the plot.
- Hills or mountains on the east or north sides.

**Favour plots with**

- Water bodies such as rivers and canals to the north or east.
- Hills or elevated land to the south or west.
- Open space to the north and east.

## Examples and Tips

### Example 1: A house on a triangular plot

A family buys a triangular plot with sharp angles and a missing north-east portion. They build a rectangular boundary within the plot, soften the sharp edges with planting and reposition the main entrance. This is an illustration of the traditional method, not a promise of any result.

### Example 2: An office of irregular shape

A company occupies an office whose building is not rectangular. The workspace is re-planned so that the proportions inside the existing structure follow the ideal as closely as possible. Again, this shows the method and not an outcome.

### Professional tips

- Have the dimensions of a plot verified by a professional surveyor before purchase.
- Allow for seasonal and geographical differences when you assess the plot.
- Combine more than one way of checking.
- Note the results and adjust your approach as the occupants give feedback.
- For complex cases, consult a qualified Vastu consultant who understands both the tradition and modern building constraints.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the ideal plot size according to Vastu?

The ideal depends on the use. For homes, traditional sizes range from 60 by 40 feet (2,400 square feet) for smaller homes to 100 by 100 feet (10,000 square feet) for larger estates. Shape is equally important, and square and rectangular plots with a ratio of up to 1 to 2 are the most auspicious.

### Can an irregular plot be made suitable under Vastu?

Tradition says an irregular plot can be improved by setting a rectangular boundary within the irregular shape, balancing it with landscaping and the placing of rooms, and applying the correction that goes with the particular irregularity.

### How does the orientation of the plot matter?

Plots facing north or east are generally considered more auspicious than plots facing south or west. The ideal orientation allows morning sunlight into the house. The orientation to the cardinal directions also decides where rooms and functions are placed.

### What does Vastu say about living on a plot considered inauspicious?

Tradition associates an inauspicious plot, left untreated, with difficulties, and names particular irregularities with particular troubles. For example, a triangular plot is linked with legal trouble and a plot cut in the north-east with a loss of peace of mind. These are traditional beliefs, not proven results.

### How much deviation from the ideal proportions is acceptable?

Vastu texts are said to accept a tilt of the land of up to 20 degrees off square. For a rectangular plot, a length to width ratio of up to 1 to 2 is considered auspicious. Beyond these limits, tradition says remedial measures become more important.

## Conclusion

Vastu tradition places the size and shape of a plot at the base of its assessment. Square plots, and rectangles up to a ratio of 1 to 2, set squarely on the cardinal directions, are the ideal it describes.

Even a plot that departs from the ideal can be worked with using the traditional remedies described above. Plot size is only one part of a Vastu assessment, and a qualified consultant can look at the particular site.
