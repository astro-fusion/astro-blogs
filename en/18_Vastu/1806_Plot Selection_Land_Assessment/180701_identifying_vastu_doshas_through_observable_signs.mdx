# Spotting Vastu Doshas From Everyday Signs at Home

Sometimes the same kinds of trouble keep returning to a home and no cause is obvious. Vastu tradition holds that such patterns can point to a fault in the way a building is laid out or arranged. This article describes the everyday signs that tradition associates with Vastu doshas and how to check them.

## What a Vastu Dosha Is

Identifying a Vastu dosha means recognising faults in the layout and energy of a home or workplace from clear signs seen in daily life. In the Vedic tradition these doshas are seen as disruptions in the natural flow of energy. They are said to show up as illness, unstable finances, friction in relationships, obstacles at work, or a general lack of peace in the home or office.

A dosha is said to arise when the design or arrangement of a building does not match the five elements: earth, water, fire, air and space. Vastu Shastra, the ancient Indian science of building, sets out how these elements should be balanced. Tradition holds that an imbalance appears as recurring difficulties in the lives of the people who live there.

### Historical Background

The idea of the Vastu dosha comes from classical Vedic texts such as the Brihat Samhita and the foundational Vastu Shastra manuscripts. In these, the sages described harmony with the environment and the relationship between a building, its energies and human life.

## Vastu and Astrology

### From the Astrological Side

- The layout and orientation of a home are thought to strengthen or weaken the effect of the planets in a birth chart.
- When reading a birth chart, some astrologers also look at Vastu doshas to explain lasting difficulties with health, money, relationships and career.
- Along with corrections to the building, an auspicious time (muhurta) and planetary remedies are sometimes suggested. These are best understood as tradition.

### From the Vastu Side

- Vastu treats doshas as the root of discord and negative energy. The positions of the entrance, bedrooms, kitchen and toilets are mapped so that energy can flow well.
- Placing rooms by direction is very important. For example, an entrance or kitchen in an unfavourable direction is said to block prosperity and harmony.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: Recognise the Visible Signs

Persistent health problems, sudden financial loss, daily quarrels, restlessness and a lack of progress are the signs that show on the surface. Vastu tradition says they may point to deeper doshas that disturb the energy balance of the home.

- Watch the home for these recurring patterns.
- Link an area of concern to a part of the house. For example, tradition connects a toilet in the north-east (Ishan) with long illness and a south-facing main entrance with money trouble.
- Make Vastu corrections and notice what changes in daily life.

### Principle 2: Dosha Symptoms and Balance

Knowing the symptoms is necessary for creating a balanced environment. They may include:

- Constant health problems despite treatment. If someone is ill, see a doctor first.
- Unexplained financial instability or sudden expenses
- Conflict in relationships, frequent misunderstandings or a lack of harmony in the family
- Stagnation in a career, repeated failures or lost opportunities
- Persistent restlessness, sleeplessness or mental fatigue

**Steps to follow:**

1. Note how often these symptoms occur among the residents and what sets them off.
2. Link the symptoms to particular parts of the house, such as the north sector for finances and the south-west (Nairitya) for relationships.
3. Consult a Vastu practitioner to understand how the symptoms relate to flaws in the building.

### Principle 3: Restoring Balance

Practical measures are used to bring balance back.

- Pay attention to correct Vastu orientation, meaning the placement of rooms and the direction of the entrance.
- Indoor plants and colour changes are used to balance the five natural elements.
- Keep the house clean and clear out unneeded items regularly so that energy can move freely.

## How to Check for a Dosha

### Information You Need

- An accurate plan of the property with its orientation and dimensions
- The symptoms noticed so far

### Method

- Using the floor plan and a compass, identify the problem areas and their directions.
- Match the symptoms with the traditional signs of a dosha. Examples given in the tradition:
  - Toilet in the north-east: loss of health and money
  - Entrance in the south-west: financial instability and career obstacles
  - Kitchen in the north-east: digestive and health problems
- Judge the dosha as mild, moderate or severe according to how long the symptoms have lasted and how strong they are.

## Practical Corrections

Based on the signs observed, these measures are used to correct a Vastu dosha:

- Rearranging furniture to open up pathways for energy
- Moving the main entrance, or placing traditional Vastu symbols such as the swastika near the problem area
- Moving the bed or kitchen to match auspicious directions
- Using colour: light and pastel shades are said to increase positive energy
- Placing mirrors properly to improve light and the flow of energy
- Keeping indoor plants to freshen the air
- Cleaning regularly and removing clutter

## Common Mistakes

- Dismissing persistent symptoms as coincidence and not linking them to the flow of energy
- Overlooking the importance of direction for rooms and entrances
- Keeping heavy objects or clutter in the north or north-east, which is said to block prosperity
- Building toilets or kitchens in unfavourable directions and doing nothing to correct them
- Placing a mirror directly facing a bed or an entrance, which is believed to disturb energy

## Illustrations and Tips

### Illustration 1: A Home

In a household where long illness and money worries were present, a toilet in the north-east and a south-facing entrance were identified as the sources of the dosha. Vastu symbols were placed, major furniture was moved, the entrance was kept clear and pastel colours were used. This is a general illustration, not a promise of any result.

### Illustration 2: An Office

In a new company, the team was low in morale and work was slow. The office had an irregular shape and a pile of electrical equipment in the north-east. Plants were added, the north-east was cleared, and workstations were arranged according to favourable directions.

### Practical Tips

- Check the date, time and place details before matching them with any chart.
- Take account of the season and geography, such as the direction of sunlight.
- For a complete solution, consider both the astrological and the building-related views.
- Write down any changes and review the progress honestly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the most commonly cited signs of a Vastu dosha at home?

Recurring health problems, persistent money trouble, frequent quarrels, restless sleep and a lack of harmony are the main signs tradition names.

### Can a Vastu dosha be corrected without major demolition?

In many cases, changes such as moving furniture, using traditional Vastu measures, changing colours and clearing clutter are the measures people use.

### How do I check whether these signs apply to my home?

Write down the symptoms and see which part of the house they link to. Use a plan and a compass to fix the directions, and consult a Vastu practitioner if needed.

### Is a dosha also linked to the birth chart?

Tradition says a chart can be read so that Vastu measures are chosen to suit the person.

### What should I do about illness at home?

Illness needs a doctor. Vastu is a traditional way of arranging a home and does not replace medical treatment.

## Conclusion

Recognising the signs of a Vastu dosha is a first step for anyone who wants a peaceful and pleasant home. By noting symptoms such as poor health, money trouble, strained relationships and a lack of peace, you can link them to flaws in the layout that tradition says practical Vastu measures can correct. Use these old principles as a guide to making every corner of your home or workplace better.
