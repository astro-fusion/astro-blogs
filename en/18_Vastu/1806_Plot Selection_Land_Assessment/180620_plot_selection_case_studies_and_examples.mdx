# Plot Selection Examples: Choosing and Correcting Under Vastu

Rules on plot selection are easier to follow when they are shown at work. This article sets out how the Vastu guidelines on direction, shape, slope and surroundings are applied when a plot is chosen or adjusted. The examples are plain illustrations of the method. They are not reports of outcomes.

## What the Examples Show

The examples describe how Vastu guidelines can be applied to select, evaluate and refine property choices for homes, offices and commercial spaces. The aim is to see whether the attributes of the plot fit the tradition's ideas of harmony.

- **Practical use**: applying the traditional guidelines to the choice of a site and to the layout on it.
- **Spiritual side**: connecting the land, the property and the people with what the tradition calls universal energies.

### Textual background

The origins of plot selection are traced to the Brihat Samhita and the traditional Vastu Shastra treatises. These texts are said to record the effects of the orientation, shape and energy of a site on those who live there.

## Why It Matters in Vastu

Tradition holds that the choice of a plot is more than a physical one.

- **Direction**: an ideal entrance and layout, based on the orientation of the plot.
- **Flow of energy**: the smooth movement of prana, the life energy, through the design.
- **Materials**: the use of elements and colours that suit the natural character of the plot.

Vastu tradition holds that following these principles supports harmony and peace in a home. That is a traditional belief and not a proven result.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: The choice of plot and its effect

The plot chosen shapes the later life of the building. Tradition says that following Vastu closely benefits the occupants in many ways.

- Analyse the facing direction, the shape and the slope of the plot.
- Match the choice to the Vastu recommendations.
- Note any changes after the work is done.

### Principle 2: Harmony of direction

The orientation of a plot is said to decide the main inflows of energy to the property.

1. Decide which directions suit the property.
2. Select or modify plots so as to take in as much natural sunlight as possible from the north and the east.
3. Use traditional remedies for plots that are not naturally aligned.

### Principle 3: Shape and balance

Regular plot shapes, square or rectangular, are believed to bring balance, while irregular shapes call for remedies.

- For regular plots, follow the standard Vastu layouts.
- For irregular plots, tradition uses remedies such as changes of colour and internal partitioning to harmonise the flow of energy.

## How to Assess a Plot

### What you need

- A compass reading, to find east, west, north and south.
- The dimensions and shape of the plot.
- The direction of the slope and the elevation.
- Surrounding features such as water bodies, roads and obstructions.

### Steps

1. **Direction.** Use a compass to find the primary facing direction of the plot.
2. **Shape.** Confirm whether the plot is square, rectangular or irregular.
3. **Slope and surroundings.** Note whether the ground slopes towards the north-east, which tradition prefers, and look at the neighbouring features. Avoid plots near cemeteries or hospitals.
4. **Mapping.** Lay the Vastu Purusha Mandala, the energy grid, over the plan to mark the key zones.
5. **Remedies.** For any flaw found, such as a cut corner or a wrongly placed entrance, note the traditional remedy, such as a colour change or an architectural correction.

## Practical Applications

- **Square or rectangular plots** are chosen for stability and ease of energy flow.
- **A main entrance facing north or east** is considered favourable.
- **A plot sloping towards the north-east** is said to help the movement of energy. Slope and drainage on the real plot should be planned with a licensed engineer.
- **Correcting irregular plots**: tradition says that careful planning and the traditional remedies can harmonise such properties.

**Example**: a triangular plot is turned into a workspace by realigning the entrances, arranging the utilities and balancing the interiors.

## Common Mistakes

- Choosing plots with irregular shapes or sharp projections.
- Ignoring the direction of the main entrance.
- Failing to look at the surroundings, for example land near waste dumps, heavy traffic or hospitals.
- Overlooking the slope and natural drainage of the plot.
- Not consulting an experienced Vastu practitioner before finalising the purchase.

## Examples and Tips

### Example 1: Choosing a house

A family finds that their chosen plot has an unfavourable orientation and an irregular shape. They switch to a plot with a north-facing entrance and a regular shape, in line with the Vastu guidelines.

### Example 2: A business on an irregular plot

An entrepreneur buys a triangular plot for commercial use and is concerned about its shape. The plot lacks symmetry but has good frontage. The main entrance is realigned, the core utilities are repositioned and interior changes such as colour schemes and partitioning are made.

### Example 3: Health and a household

A homemaker's family has moved into a new home where the kitchen sits in an unfavourable position and structural change is not possible. Following the Vastu guidelines, a temporary kitchen is set up in the south-east, the direction tradition gives to the kitchen.

### Example 4: An office on an irregular plot

An office has been built on an irregular plot, and the staff find it uncomfortable. The furniture is arranged strategically so as to balance the space and direct movement towards collaboration.

None of these examples promises a result. They show the sequence in which the traditional guidelines are applied.

### Professional tips

- Check all measurements and directions on site.
- Note the changes you make, and compare the situation before and after.
- Be open to practical adjustments for difficult plot shapes.
- Where an astrological chart is used, a Vastu consultant may read it alongside the plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do Vastu remedies work in an already built house?

Vastu tradition offers several non-structural measures, such as colour changes and the arrangement of furniture, which can be used after construction. They are traditional practices and not proven cures.

### Is it possible to harmonise an irregular plot?

Tradition says that with the guidance of an experienced consultant and the traditional remedies, even an irregular plot can be balanced. See the article on remedies for irregular plots for the details.

### How much do the date and time of purchase matter?

In Vedic astrology an auspicious time, or muhurta, is chosen for the purchase. It is a traditional practice, and the legal and financial checks come first.

### Does Vastu apply only to homes, or to commercial property as well?

Vastu principles are applied to residential and commercial plots alike.

## Conclusion

The examples above show how the Vastu guidelines are applied when a plot is chosen or adjusted: look at the direction, the shape, the slope and the surroundings, map the zones, and note the traditional remedy for each flaw.

Vastu tradition offers this as guidance and not as a guarantee. For the engineering of a plot, such as drainage and foundations, consult a licensed engineer, and consult a qualified Vastu practitioner for the rest.
