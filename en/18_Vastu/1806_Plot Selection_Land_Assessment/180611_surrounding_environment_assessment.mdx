# Assessing the Surroundings of a Plot Under Vastu

A plot does not end at its boundary wall. Vastu tradition also looks at the water bodies, roads, hills, neighbouring buildings and soil around it. This article explains how those surroundings are assessed and what a buyer can check on a visit.

## What Surroundings Assessment Means

Surroundings assessment is a systematic look at everything around a property, judged against Vastu principles, to see what effect it is believed to have on the people who will live there.

- **Practical use**: keeping the surroundings in mind while planning a home or a workplace.
- **Spiritual side**: the relationship between cosmic energies, the natural elements and the living space.

### Textual background

The idea is traced to classical texts such as the Brihat Samhita and to Vastu Shastra manuscripts. These texts hold that the setting of a plot strongly affects the flow of positive energy (prana) into and around the house.

## Why It Matters in Vastu

- **Direction**: choosing a property by looking at the natural features around it.
- **Flow of energy**: Vastu tradition holds that the nearness of water bodies, hills and other structures affects the energy of the site.
- **Materials**: choosing building materials and colours that suit the surroundings.

## Key Principles

### Principle 1: Water bodies

Water bodies carry great weight in Vastu. Tradition holds that the presence, direction and distance of a river, lake or pond near the plot influence the harmony of the home.

**Traditional results by direction of the water body**

| Direction of water body | Traditionally believed result |
|---|---|
| North-east | Good fortune and financial strength |
| East | Monetary gain |
| North | Money and joyfulness |
| South-east | Quarrels, harm to the son |
| South | Harm to the wife |
| South-west | Harm to the house owner |
| West | Monetary gain |
| North-west | Mental disorder, enmity |
| Centre | Lack of wealth, self-destructive tendency |

This table records a traditional belief. It is not a proven or certain outcome.

**Practical use**

- Identify the rivers, lakes or ponds near the prospective plot.
- Use a compass to see on which side of the plot each one lies.
- Vastu tradition prefers water bodies to the north, east or north-east of the plot.

### Principle 2: Roads and traffic

Roads are seen as the way energy and opportunity reach a plot. The direction, width and type of the roads around the plot are believed to affect its Vastu.

1. Look at the roads next to the plot in every direction.
2. Prefer plots with roads to the north and the east.
3. Be careful of a T-junction where a road ends pointing straight at the plot, which tradition says sends a concentrated force at it.
4. Look at the quality and upkeep of the surrounding roads. Well-kept roads are taken as a good sign.

### Principle 3: Natural features

Mountains, hills and other natural features can act as a protective wall or as a source of imbalance, depending on where they stand.

- Prefer plots with higher ground and hills to the south or west and open, lower land to the north and east, which tradition links with protection and stability.
- Look at the soil of the plot. Fertile soil is a traditional symbol of growth and prosperity.
- Look at the slope. The ground is traditionally preferred to slope gently towards the north-east, with the north-east lowest and the south-west highest.

## How to Assess the Surroundings

**What you need**

- The location and orientation of the plot.
- A compass for directions.
- A map showing the features within about 1 km of the plot.
- A list of the points you want to check.

**Steps**

1. **Fix the directions.** Use a compass to find true north, south, east and west, and mark every surrounding feature on a map against those directions.
2. **Note the distance.** Measure how far each important feature is from the plot. A feature close to the plot is taken as more significant than one far away.
3. **Look at the whole picture.** Consider all the features together and note where they support each other and where they conflict.

## Practical Applications

### Water element

Water is considered a powerful element in Vastu, and tradition places it to the north, east and north-east.

1. **Natural water bodies**
   - Water to the north or east is considered favourable.
   - Do not choose a plot so close to water that flooding or damp is a risk. That is an engineering question, so ask a licensed engineer.
2. **Artificial water features**
   - Tradition places a fountain or other water feature in the north-east of the plot.
   - Keep any water feature in proportion to the size of the plot.

### Neighbouring structures

1. **Height**
   - Tall buildings to the south or west are considered protective.
   - Avoid a plot overshadowed by tall structures to the north or east.
   - Try to keep building heights balanced on all sides.
2. **Use of neighbouring buildings**
   - Look at what goes on in the buildings around the plot.
   - Avoid sites close to hospitals, cemeteries or garbage dumps.
   - Neighbourhoods with schools and parks are preferred.

### Light and air

1. **Sunlight**
   - See that the plot gets plenty of morning sun from the east.
   - Avoid obstructions that block natural light, especially from the north-east.
   - Remember that sunlight changes with the seasons.
2. **Air**
   - Look at the prevailing winds around the plot.
   - Prefer unobstructed airflow, particularly from the north and east.

## Common Mistakes

### Ignoring a T-junction

Many buyers study only the plot and overlook a T-junction facing it.

- A road pointing straight at the plot is traditionally seen as concentrating force on one part of the building.
- It is also traditionally linked with financial instability and strain between people.
- Have a Vastu consultant assess the actual effect on the plot.
- Planting or a change in the entrance position are the usual traditional responses.

### Skipping the soil check

The soil under a property is considered to shape its foundation.

- Poor soil can lead to structural problems.
- Barren or rocky land may need extra work.
- Contaminated soil is a concern for health and building.
- Have the soil tested before buying. Soil strength and contamination are engineering questions for a qualified geotechnical engineer, not matters Vastu can settle.

### Misreading the effect of water

Water bodies are generally regarded as favourable, but their effect depends on where they lie.

- Water in the south-west or south-east is traditionally seen as unfavourable.
- Stagnant water is seen as a place where negativity gathers.
- An oversized water feature is said to dominate the property.
- Judge the direction of a water body with a proper compass, note whether the water flows or stands still, and keep artificial features in proportion to the plot.

## Examples

### Example 1: A suburban house

A family buying a house in a suburb finds a large water body to the south-west of the plot and a cemetery about 500 metres to the south. On Vastu grounds they plant trees along the southern boundary, place a water feature in the north-east and move the main entrance. This is an illustration of how the points above are applied, not a promise of any result.

### Example 2: An office in a commercial complex

An office faces a T-junction at its entrance, and tall buildings block the morning light from the east. The occupants redesign the entrance so that it does not face the road directly and keep any water feature to the north. Again, this shows the method and not an outcome.

### Practical tips

- Check directions with a proper instrument, not only a smartphone app.
- Visit in different seasons, since sunlight and water levels change.
- Use more than one way of checking before you decide.
- Think about the comfort of the people who will live there as well as the Vastu rules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the ideal surrounding environment according to Vastu?

The ideal setting has a gentle slope towards the north or north-east, water bodies to the north, east or north-east, well-kept roads to the north and east, and higher ground or hills to the south and west. The surroundings should be clean and open, and away from cemeteries and garbage dumps.

### How do hospitals or temples nearby affect a property?

Tradition holds that a temple, especially to the north-east or east, is favourable. A hospital is traditionally considered a less ideal neighbour. If one is nearby, a buffer of trees or plants between it and the plot is the traditional suggestion.

### Can Vastu remedies offset an unfavourable surrounding?

Vastu tradition offers adjustments such as planting trees, keeping the site clean and changing the entrance. They are traditional practices, not proven cures. For a serious problem, consult a qualified Vastu consultant.

### How important is the soil?

Fertile soil is a traditional symbol of growth, while rocky or barren land is said to need extra effort. Whatever the tradition says, have the soil tested by a qualified engineer before you finalise the purchase.

### What is the significance of a T-junction?

Tradition holds that a T-junction facing the plot concentrates force on it. Practitioners say the effect depends on other factors, such as the layout of the building and the quality of the entrance, so it should be assessed on site before any change is made.

## Conclusion

Vastu tradition holds that a home is influenced by what surrounds it. Water bodies, roads, hills, neighbouring buildings and soil all form part of the picture that is examined before a plot is chosen.

Vastu is about balance rather than perfection, and a plot with some unfavourable features can still be worked with through careful planning. Questions of soil, drainage and flooding belong to a licensed engineer. For guidance on the Vastu side, consult a qualified consultant who can look at the actual site.
