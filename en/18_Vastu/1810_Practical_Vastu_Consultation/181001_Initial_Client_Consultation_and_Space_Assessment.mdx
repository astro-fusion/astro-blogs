# First Meeting and Space Assessment in Vastu Consultation

A Vastu consultation begins with the first meeting and an assessment of the space. In this step the consultant understands what the client needs, collects the details of the property, and walks through the building to see its main Vastu features and possible defects. Everything that follows, including the audit, the report and the corrections, rests on this first assessment.

This article explains what happens in the first meeting, the principles on which a space is examined, the mistakes to avoid, and what a home owner should have ready.

## What the first meeting and space assessment involve

This is the foundation of a Vastu consultation. In a structured way, the client's needs are understood, the details of the building are collected, and the consultant walks through the property to note its Vastu features and possible defects.

Three things go together in this process.

- **The astrological side:** In Vastu tradition many consultants also look at the client's birth chart, to see how planetary positions are thought to relate to the place where the person lives.
- **The Vastu side:** A careful analysis of the building's design and of the way energy is said to move through it.
- **The spiritual side:** According to tradition, the aim is to bring cosmic forces and the physical space into harmony so that the surroundings feel balanced.

### Historical context

The practice of assessing a space carefully and consulting the client is believed to be linked to classical texts such as the Brihat Samhita and to the wider Vastu Shastra tradition. These texts stress how astrology, the design of a building and a balanced way of living fit together.

## Why this step matters

### The astrological side

According to tradition, the assessment at the first meeting helps with the following.

- **Matching with the birth chart:** Understanding how the person's chart and the directions of the building relate to each other.
- **Choosing a time (muhurta):** Deciding an auspicious time for moving in, for renovation or for a ritual.
- **Choosing corrections:** Selecting Vastu corrections that suit the client's situation.

### The Vastu side

In Vastu this step is important for the following reasons.

- **Assessing directions:** Mapping the rooms and parts of the plot against the main directions and the zones of the elements. For example, water is associated with the north-east and earth with the south-west.
- **Analysing the flow of energy:** Finding the places where energy is considered blocked or unbalanced.
- **Practical suggestions:** Advising where things should be placed in the home or office and which materials suit each place.

## Main principles and how they are applied

### Principle 1: Studying the client and the property together

In this principle both the client's personal details and the physical features of the property are examined.

In practice it is done like this.

- At the first meeting, ask the client for birth details and their main concerns.
- Review the property documents, the building plans and the history of earlier occupants.
- Turn what emerges into practical Vastu suggestions that fit the client.

### Principle 2: A balanced check of the space

Here the direction of the building, the zones of the elements and the layout of the rooms are examined systematically, so that the whole building can be seen in balance.

The steps are as follows.

1. Look at the facing of the plot, the position of the main entrance and the division of rooms by direction.
2. Look at the zones of the five elements as Vastu describes them: water in the north-east, fire in the south-east, earth in the south-west, air in the north-west and space in the centre.
3. Identify and write down Vastu doshas (defects), such as a blocked north-east corner, a kitchen in the wrong place or a cluttered centre.
4. Suggest adjustments, such as placing heavy furniture in the south-west or keeping the north-east light in colour with a place for water.

### Principle 3: The Vastu audit and its corrections

This involves a detailed inspection of the whole property, and each important area is rated on how well it follows Vastu.

Consultants commonly advise the following.

- Use a Vastu score sheet or checklist to rate each zone and feature.
- Put first the corrections that match both the client's wishes and what the property allows.
- Keep all findings and planned changes in writing, so that they can be monitored and followed up later.

## How to analyse direction and space

### Information needed

- The exact facing of the building and of each room
- The plan of the building, showing the positions of doors and windows
- The client's birth details (date, time, place), if the consultation will also look at the chart
- The client's specific goals, such as health, finances or relationships

### Steps of the analysis

1. Align the plan with the main directions using a compass or a compass app.
2. Mark the main zones, such as the north-east and the south-west, on the plan and see how far the principles of Vastu are followed.
3. If the chart is also being studied, compare these findings with it and note where they agree and where they conflict.
4. Record the position of each feature, such as the main entrance, kitchen, master bedroom and toilets, using scores or a checklist.
5. Write a summary of the main problems, set the priority of the corrections and prepare for the detailed Vastu audit.

## What is done at the first meeting

- **Talking with the client:** Start with a structured conversation to understand needs, concerns and what the client hopes for.
- **Collecting documents:** Gather building plans, plot maps and earlier reports on the property.
- **Inspecting the site:** Walk through the property yourself and note the directions, the way energy is said to flow and any departures from Vastu rules.
- **Photographs and measurements:** Take photographs and measurements for a precise analysis later.
- **Suggestions that can be made at once:** Offer easy changes that need no demolition, such as removing unnecessary things, shifting furniture a little or changing colours.
- **Plan for what comes next:** Discuss the next steps, the timing of the detailed audit and what the client expects.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Relying only on plans or digital maps without visiting the site.
- Overlooking the client's specific concerns, or not asking detailed questions at the first meeting.
- Ignoring the earlier occupants or the history of the property.
- Going ahead without checking directions with a proper instrument.
- Suggesting costly structural changes before trying simple, light Vastu measures.

## Examples and practical tips

### Example 1: A change in a home

A family was facing repeated money difficulties and disagreements. The assessment found a lot of clutter in the south-west part and no heavy objects there. Heavy furniture was placed in it, unnecessary things were cleared from the centre and the colours were corrected. Vastu tradition associates money trouble and quarrels with such a south-west and a cluttered centre, but this is what tradition holds and not a diagnosis.

### Example 2: Use in an office

In a young company's office, staff kept leaving and work was stuck. The inspection found storage material in the north-east corner. That corner was cleared, the lighting was increased and a place for water was made. Tradition regards the north-east as the zone that should stay open and light.

### Practical tips for the consultant

- Always check the directions on the spot with a compass, because drawings of a building can mislead.
- Hold the consultation in daylight for a better reading of the space.
- Use checklists, score sheets and understanding built from experience together.
- Keep a full record for follow-up work and for the client's reference.

## Frequently asked questions

### What information should I keep ready for a Vastu consultation?

Keep the plan of your property, details about earlier occupants and your main concerns or aims ready.

### How long does the first meeting and inspection take?

Most first meetings take 1 to 2 hours, depending on the size and complexity of the property.

### Do I always have to demolish or rebuild to follow Vastu?

No. Many Vastu corrections are simple changes, such as moving furniture or changing colours. They do not require a change to the structure.

### Is a Vastu assessment useful in a rented home too?

Yes. Even in a rented home, temporary measures such as rearranging things can be used to improve harmony, according to tradition.

### Will the consultant also look at my birth chart?

Many consultants combine the chart with the Vastu assessment when making suggestions. It depends on the method of the consultation, so ask at the first meeting.

## Conclusion

The first meeting and the space assessment are the foundation of a Vastu audit. By understanding the client's needs in an orderly way, collecting full details of the property and carrying out a structured inspection, the consultant keeps the audit accurate and complete. This step shows the layout of the building and prepares the ground for the work that follows.

Giving priority to consultation and assessment opens the way to improvements in a home or office. Resting on tradition and practical understanding, this first step helps in creating a living environment that follows Vastu.
