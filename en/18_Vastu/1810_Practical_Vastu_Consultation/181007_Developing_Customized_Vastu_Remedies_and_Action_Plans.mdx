# Making Vastu Remedies and an Action Plan to Suit a Home

This article explains how Vastu remedies and an action plan are prepared for a particular home or office, according to its situation. These are measures that need no demolition, such as moving furniture, correcting colours, auspicious symbols and the placing of objects. The aim is for solutions that are easy to adopt and low in cost. The measures here are described as practices followed in tradition. They are not a promise of any result.

## What a tailored remedy and action plan is

This is an orderly process of identifying the Vastu imbalances (doshas) of a space and deciding, without changing the structure, practical remedies fitted to that space, so that the flow of energy is harmonised according to tradition.

Three things go together in it.

- **The astrological side:** In some traditions the consultant also considers how the person's birth chart and planetary positions relate to the energy of the home or workplace.
- **The Vastu side:** Moving objects to new places, fixing where things go and using colours to correct imbalances.
- **The spiritual side:** Using sacred symbols and thoughtful decoration to bring personal and cosmic energies into harmony.

### Historical context

The roots of such tailored action plans are believed to lie in ancient texts such as the Brihat Samhita and in the core texts of Vastu Shastra, where the sages set out both the philosophy and the practical methods of bringing the energies of the surroundings into line with the lives of individuals.

## Why it matters

### The astrological side

In astrology, tailored Vastu remedies are considered important for the following.

- **Analysis of the chart:** Identifying, from planetary positions and their directional influence, the areas of life believed to be affected by Vastu doshas.
- **Choice of time (muhurta):** Choosing an auspicious time to apply the remedies or make changes.
- **Matching remedies:** Balancing planetary doshas with the corresponding Vastu corrections.

### The Vastu side

From the Vastu point of view, a personal action plan does the following.

- **Links the use of rooms with the elements:** Tradition links each Vastu zone with a particular element and aspect of life.
- **Corrects energy imbalances:** Through remedies that need no demolition, such as correcting the position of things, placing auspicious symbols and using colours.
- **Makes the most of the flow of good energy:** Making sure that every member of the family can benefit from a harmonious space without major renovation.

## Main principles and how they are applied

### Principle 1: Remedies fitted to the person

Action plans are made on the basis of the person's chart, if it is being considered, and of the particular Vastu doshas present in the surroundings.

In practice it is done like this.

- Carry out a full analysis of the space and, if wanted, of the chart.
- Identify the priority zones and the elements affected (fire, water, earth, air, space).
- Choose remedies that need no demolition to suit the needs of each zone, such as moving furniture or changing colours.

### Principle 2: Balancing the zones with low-cost solutions

The north-east is regarded as the zone of prosperity and the south-west as the zone of stability. These zones are balanced with practical measures that do not need costly construction.

The steps are as follows.

1. Map the Vastu zones of the home against the compass directions.
2. Use simple measures.
    - Placing heavy furniture in the south-west, for stability.
    - In tradition, blue elements in the north are linked with growth in a career.
    - Keeping indoor plants in the north-west, which are considered good for social relations.
3. Make colour changes, such as yellow in the south-west and green in the east.
4. Decorate with things that are easy to find, such as paintings or auspicious symbols.

### Principle 3: Balancing energy with symbols and objects

Sacred symbols and carefully chosen objects are believed to change the atmosphere of a space.

These practices are common in tradition.

- Setting up a Kuber Yantra or a Shree Yantra in the north or east, for prosperity.
- Placing the Om, Swastika or lotus symbols, for peace, good fortune and spiritual progress.

These practices do not need costly objects, and they should not be thought of as compulsory.

## Method of analysis and of the action plan

### Information needed

- An accurate plan with the directions
- The exact position of rooms, objects and entrances
- The birth details of the main residents, if the match with astrology is to be examined

### Steps of the analysis

- Divide the plan into 16 Vastu zones (each of 22.5 degrees).
- Lay the Vastu Purusha Mandala over the plan to identify the strong and weak zones.
- If the chart is to be examined, compare with it to fix the priority corrections.

### Checklist for the action plan

- **What:** Which remedy is to be carried out.
- **Where:** The exact Vastu zone, such as the north-east corner.
- **How:** The method of carrying it out, such as placing, hanging or painting.
- **Who:** The person responsible, such as the home owner or the decorator.
- **When:** If wanted, an auspicious time according to muhurta.
- **Cost:** List affordable options that are easy to find.

## Practical uses in Vastu

**Remedies that need no demolition include the following.**

- **Moving things:** Place heavy furniture in the south-west, and keep the north and east free of clutter for the flow of energy.
- **Colour changes:** Paint or decorate the walls of a zone in matching colours, such as yellow in the south-west and green in the east.
- **Symbols and objects:** Place auspicious symbols such as a yantra or a Swastika in the zones suggested.
- **Artworks and paintings:** Hang artworks of spiritual significance in line with the Vastu directions, for peace of mind and progress.

**A few more suggestions:**

- Avoid mirrors in the south-west corner or at the main entrance, because they are believed to let energy flow out.
- Remove unnecessary things regularly and keep pathways clear, so that the flow is unobstructed.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Assuming the same remedies suit everyone: every home and every occupant needs a different solution.
- Ignoring the effect of colours and symbols: a wrong choice can increase the imbalance.
- Placing mirrors, water decor or metal objects in the wrong zone without asking a Vastu expert.
- Forgetting regular checks of the arrangement and corrections in the light of feedback.
- Making structural changes without trying practical, low-cost options.

## Examples and practical tips

### Example 1: A change in a home

A family had been struggling for a long time with money trouble and repeated health problems. The inspection found a Vastu dosha in the south-west, where the construction projected outwards and the colours were also wrong. As remedies, yellow and brown decor was adopted, heavy furniture was placed in the south-west and mirrors were avoided in that zone. Tradition regards these changes as suited to stability, but no result can be promised, and health problems need a doctor.

### Example 2: Use in a business

The team of a young company lacked cooperation and its work was stuck. The seating was changed to face east, a Kuber Yantra was placed in the north and green plants were put in the east.

### Practical tips

- Always check the accurate plan and the compass directions.
- For a remedy fitted to the person, match the chart details as well.
- Start with the least intrusive and most economical step.
- Write down all the changes and look from time to time at how far they have been useful.
- In complex cases, consult a qualified Vastu and astrology expert.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Vastu doshas be corrected without demolition?

Yes. Most Vastu imbalances are dealt with by moving things, changing colours, placing auspicious symbols and fixing where objects go. A change of structure is not necessary.

### Are Vastu remedies expensive?

No. Most remedies use cheap things such as colour, decor and auspicious symbols. In a consultation, only low-cost options that are easy to find are listed.

### Is it necessary to consult an astrologer for Vastu remedies?

It is not necessary. Consultants who also bring in an understanding of astrology fit the remedies to the person.

### What should an action plan contain?

It should state what the remedy is, in which Vastu zone, by what method, who is responsible, at what time and at what cost.

### What should I try first when choosing remedies?

Try the least intrusive and most economical step first, such as moving furniture, changing colours and clearing things away. Keep a change of structure as the last option.

## Conclusion

By preparing remedies and an action plan to suit the home, you can follow the old tradition of Vastu Shastra in modern life without large structural changes. Accurate analysis, practical measures and a written plan make the work clear. The measures remain practices of tradition and not a promise of results.
