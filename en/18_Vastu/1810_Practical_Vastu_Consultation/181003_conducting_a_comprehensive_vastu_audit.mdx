# How to Conduct a Full Vastu Audit of a Home, Room by Room

A comprehensive Vastu audit is a systematic evaluation of the layout and arrangement of a home on the principles of Vastu Shastra. This traditional Indian science of architecture aims at a balanced living environment by arranging the five elements: earth, water, fire, air and space. A thorough audit helps to identify Vastu doshas (defects), which tradition associates with trouble in health, money and relationships. This is what tradition holds and not a diagnosis, and illness always needs a doctor. This article gives a detailed room-by-room process for carrying out a Vastu audit.

## Part 1: The main entrance, the gateway of energy

In a Vastu audit the main entrance is the most important point, because tradition holds that energy enters the home here.

- **Direction:** The most auspicious directions for the main entrance are considered to be north, east and north-east. Use a compass to find the exact direction the main door faces.
- **Appearance:** The entrance should be well lit, clean and welcoming. Avoid clutter such as shoes or dustbins near the main door.
- **The door:** A wooden door is considered ideal. It should be the largest door in the house and should open inwards in a clockwise direction. Make sure it does not creak.

## Part 2: Room-by-room Vastu analysis

### a. Living room: the social space

- **Location:** Ideally the living room should be in the north, east or north-east.
- **Seating:** Arrange the furniture, especially the sofa, along the south or west walls. Then, when you sit, you face north or east.
- **Decor:** Use light and bright colours on the walls. Paintings of flowing water or of sunrise on the east wall are considered to improve the feel of the room.

### b. Kitchen: the source of nourishment

- **Location:** The south-east corner of the house is the best place for the kitchen, because it is linked with the fire element. If that is not possible, the north-west is the next best choice.
- **Stove:** The cooking stove should be in the south-east corner of the kitchen, and you should face east while cooking.
- **Sink and water:** The sink and water taps should be in the north-east corner, which keeps the fire and water elements apart.

### c. Bedroom: the place of rest

- **Location:** The master bedroom should be in the south-west, which is said to give stability and peace.
- **Bed:** The bed should be in the south-west corner of the room, with the head towards the south or east while sleeping. Avoid sleeping with the head to the north.
- **Colours:** Use soothing, light colours such as shades of white, yellow or green.

### d. Pooja room (prayer room): the sacred space

- **Location:** The north-east is the most sacred and ideal direction for a pooja room.
- **Idols:** The idols should be placed in the north-east part of the pooja room and should face east or west.
- **Direction of prayer:** When you pray, you should face east.

### e. Bathroom and toilet: the place of disposal

- **Location:** The north-west part of the house is the best place for bathrooms and toilets.
- **Placement:** Avoid a toilet in the north-east or south-west corners.
- **Cleanliness:** Keep bathroom doors closed and the space well ventilated.

## Part 3: Identifying Vastu doshas and traditional remedies

During the audit you may find parts of the home that do not follow Vastu. These are called Vastu doshas. Here are some common ones and the remedies traditionally mentioned.

- **Entrance in the wrong place:** If the entrance faces a direction considered inauspicious, such as south or south-west, tradition has the practice of placing a Swastik symbol near the door.
- **Toilet in the north-east:** One traditional remedy for this dosha is to place a bowl of sea salt in the toilet and replace it every week.
- **Kitchen in the north or north-east:** To balance the elements, a partition or the use of particular colours is suggested.
- **Mirrors:** Avoid placing a mirror directly opposite the bed, because it is believed to cause restlessness. Mirrors on the north or east walls are considered beneficial.

## Part 4: Quarterly and annual reviews for ongoing harmony

A Vastu audit is not a one-time fix. To keep harmony going, the space needs regular review.

- **Quarterly review:** Every three months, make a quick walk through the home to check for clutter, cleanliness and any new object that may be upsetting the balance.
- **Annual review:** Once a year, carry out a more detailed audit, paying close attention to any changes in your life or surroundings. This is also a good time to reassess your goals and make any adjustments to the remedies.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the most common Vastu doshas?

The most common doshas include a main entrance in the wrong place, a toilet in the north-east, and a kitchen in the north or north-east. They can be dealt with by simple changes that do not touch the structure.

### Can I carry out a Vastu audit myself?

Yes, you can do a basic audit yourself using the guidelines in this article. For a deeper analysis and corrections suited to your home, it is best to consult a professional Vastu consultant.

### How often should I review the Vastu of my home?

A quick review every quarter and a more detailed audit once a year are advised. This keeps the home balanced.

### What should I check at the main entrance?

Check the direction with a compass, keep the entrance clean and well lit, and do not keep shoes or dustbins near the door. The door should be wooden, the largest in the house, and should open inwards in a clockwise direction without creaking.

## Conclusion

A room-by-room Vastu audit is an orderly way to examine the layout of a home and bring it closer to Vastu. By following this guide you can identify Vastu doshas and arrange a space that suits the way you live. Regular reviews keep the arrangement in order over the years.
