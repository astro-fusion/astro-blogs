# Vastu Purusha Mandala: 45 Deities and the 81-Square Grid

## Introduction

The Vastu Purusha Mandala is the foundational diagram and philosophical centre of Vastu Shastra, the ancient Indian science of architecture and design. It is regarded as the all-pervading soul of every building, and this cosmic diagram expresses the principle that human structures and the universe follow similar patterns. Understanding the mandala is the first step in learning Vastu. Vastu tradition treats it as a map of the cosmic energies that act on living spaces. The mandala has guided Indian architecture for thousands of years, so that human dwellings are built in line with nature and the directions.

## What Is the Vastu Purusha Mandala?

The Vastu Purusha Mandala is a sacred geometric diagram. It symbolises the cosmic being (Purusha) who represents the underlying energy of all architectural spaces. At the centre of the diagram is a human figure held down by 45 deities, with his head in the north-east (Ishanya) and his feet towards the south-west (Nairritya). This figure, lying face down, is the Vastu Purusha. The parts of his body correspond to different areas of a building or plot.

The mandala is usually divided into 81 parts (a 9x9 grid). Each square is the place of a deity who holds down a particular part of the body of the Vastu Purusha. These deities include gentle gods (devas) and demonic forces (asuras). According to this plan, the energy of each area of a building is believed to be influenced by its deity. This organised arrangement of divine energies is the basis of every architectural and design decision that follows Vastu.

## Key Aspects

### Mythological Origin

According to ancient texts such as the Vishwakarma Prakash, the origin of the Vastu Purusha Mandala is a fascinating story. A fierce battle was fought between the gods (devas), led by Lord Shiva, and the demons (asuras), led by the demon Andhaka. During it, drops of sweat fell from the forehead of Lord Shiva to the earth. From these drops a gigantic being arose, who terrified both gods and demons with his endless hunger and his wish to devour the three worlds (Triloka). To contain this threat, Lord Brahma, with the help of the other deities, pinned the giant face down on the earth, with his head pointing north-east and his feet south-west. This immobilised being became known as the Vastu Purusha, and the pattern of the deities who restrain him formed the Vastu Purusha Mandala.

### The 45 Energy Fields

The Vastu Purusha Mandala represents 45 separate energy fields. Each is ruled by a particular deity with his own qualities and influences. Brahma is at the centre. On the outer perimeter there are 32 deities, and in the inner ring there are 12. According to tradition, each deity governs a different aspect of human life through his divine qualities.

For example, in the Deva Vithi (path of the gods) there are four deities: Bhudhar, Aryama, Vivaswaan and Mitra. They extend outward from the place of Brahma. In the tradition they are linked with self-manifestation, forming connections, gaining success and inspiration, respectively. This complex arrangement means that different areas of a building or plot have different energetic qualities, which are to be respected during design and construction.

### The 81-Square Grid

The classical Vastu Purusha Mandala is usually shown as a perfect square divided into 81 smaller squares (a 9x9 grid). The grid works as a precise map for architects and Vastu consultants. The central area, the Brahmasthana, is the most sacred space and is ruled by Lord Brahma. Each of the four cardinal and four intermediate directions has its ruling deity, with particular qualities that are believed to influence the activities carried out there. Understanding this grid is important for deciding where rooms, entrances, windows and other elements should be placed.

## How to Lay the Mandala on a Plot

To apply the Vastu Purusha Mandala to a building or plot, these main steps are followed:

1. **Fix the directions of the plot.** First, determine the four main directions (north, east, south, west) of the plot exactly, using a compass or other surveying tools.
2. **Lay the mandala over the plan.** Place the 9x9 grid of the mandala on the plan of the plot so that the north-east corner of the mandala meets the north-east corner of the plot.
3. **Divide in proportion.** Whatever the shape of the plot, divide it into 81 equal parts (a 9x9 grid). For irregular plots, adjustments are made while keeping the proportions of the mandala intact.
4. **Map the energy.** Once the mandala is laid, identify which deity rules each part of the plot. This shows the energetic quality of each area.
5. **Analyse each zone.** On the basis of this energy map, decide the proper function for each part of the building. For example, the north-east (ruled by Isha, or Lord Shiva) is considered auspicious for a meditation space or prayer room.

For multi-storey buildings the calculation becomes more complex. Each floor has to stay in harmony with the whole mandala, and the vertical flow of energy has to be considered too.

## Placing Rooms According to the Mandala

To use the Vastu Purusha Mandala in planning a building:

**Understand the positions of the deities.** Each of the 45 deities in the mandala has its own qualities, which affect the energy of its area. Areas of the gentle deities (devas) are generally considered auspicious for positive activities. Areas ruled by the asuras need careful planning to neutralise energies that may cause obstruction.

**Respect the hierarchy.** The central Brahmasthana (ruled by Lord Brahma) is the core energy of any structure. According to tradition, it should be kept open or lightly occupied, so that energy can move freely through the building.

**Activities by direction.** Different directions suit different activities, depending on their ruling deities. For example:

- North-east (Ishanya, Isha): ideal for a prayer room and meditation space
- East (Indra): favourable for the main entrance and living room
- South-east (Agneya, Agni): suitable for the kitchen and fireplace
- South (Yama): suitable for storage and heavy furniture
- South-west (Nairritya, Pitru or Nirriti): best for the master bedroom and heavy construction
- West (Varuna): good for the dining area and water storage
- North-west (Vayavya, Vayu): suitable for guest rooms and less-used spaces
- North (Kubera): considered favourable for keeping wealth and valuables

**Balance of the elements.** The mandala also represents the five elements (Pancha Mahabhutas). Make sure each element is placed in its proper direction, to keep harmony.

## Practical Applications

### Architectural Design

The Vastu Purusha Mandala is the basic plan for the design of homes, temples, commercial buildings and whole cities. Architects trained in Vastu principles use the mandala to decide:

- The best place for rooms according to their function
- The ideal positions of doors, windows and other openings
- The most auspicious directions for different activities
- The proportional relationships between different spaces

According to Vastu tradition, following the guidance of the mandala keeps the built spaces in harmony with these energies.

### Correcting and Adapting a Space

For buildings that were not designed according to Vastu, the Vastu Purusha Mandala is a diagnostic tool for finding imbalances of energy. Vastu consultants use the mandala to:

- Assess problem areas where energy may be blocked or disturbed
- Suggest changes in the use of a room, based on its position in the mandala
- Describe remedial measures such as placing certain elements, colours or symbols to reduce the effect of negative energies
- Arrange furniture in line with the basic patterns of energy

It is believed that even in buildings already constructed, these adjustments can bring the space into harmony with these energies.

## Common Misconceptions

**Misconception 1: The Vastu Purusha Mandala is only superstition**

Its mythological origin may seem mysterious to some people. Still, the mandala holds principles of sacred geometry and harmony of proportion. Its supporters say that many of its recommendations agree with good use of natural light and space.

**Misconception 2: Vastu needs a perfectly square or rectangular plot**

The ideal mandala is shown as a perfect square. Even so, its principles can be applied to plots of irregular shape. What matters is keeping the proportional relationships and the balance of energy, rather than forcing a rigid geometric template.

**Misconception 3: The Vastu Purusha Mandala is only for Hindu temples**

The mandala has deep roots in Hindu cosmology, but its principles of harmony of space and flow of energy can be applied to any space. Its geometric proportions and directional guidelines can be used in a space of any kind, whatever the religion of the person.

**Misconception 4: All 45 deities must be worshipped during construction**

According to the story, a ritual is performed to honour the Vastu Purusha. But the practical use of the mandala is mainly about understanding and respecting the energy qualities of the different zones, not about religious observance. The architectural principles work independently of ritual worship.

## Examples

**Example 1: Design of a residential home**

Suppose a family is building a two-storey house on a square plot. After laying the Vastu Purusha Mandala over the plan, they take these decisions:

- The prayer room in the north-east corner (the place of Isha)
- The kitchen in the south-east (Agneya), the region of fire
- The master bedroom in the south-west (Nairritya), for stability and grounding
- An open courtyard in the middle, matching the Brahmasthana
- The main entrance on the east or north side (auspicious directions)

According to Vastu tradition, such a house is in line with the directions shown in the mandala.

**Example 2: Temple architecture**

The ancient temples of India are often cited as examples of the use of the Vastu Purusha Mandala. At the Brihadeeswara Temple in Thanjavur it is said that:

- The main sanctum (garbhagriha) is at the Brahmasthana
- The temple complex spreads outward in full proportional harmony, following the grid of the mandala
- Different deities stand in their proper directional places in the mandala
- The whole structure is a three-dimensional form of the cosmic diagram
- The proportions of the different parts follow the mathematical relationships contained in the mandala

This work of architecture shows how the theoretical rules of the Vastu Purusha Mandala can become grand sacred architecture.

## Conclusion

The Vastu Purusha Mandala is a system that joins mythology, sacred geometry and practical architecture. As the basic diagram of directions and deities in Vastu Shastra, it provides a framework for creating living and working spaces. Its picture of a cosmic being held down by 45 deities, laid out on an 81-square grid, is more than a story. According to the tradition it holds teaching about the relationship between space, energy and human life.

Some people look at these ideas from a spiritual point of view and others from a practical one. Modern architecture also recognises the importance of natural light and good use of space, and the ancient ideas contained in this plan can still interest designers and home owners today.

### How many deities and how many squares does the mandala have?

The mandala has 45 deities. Brahma is at the centre, 12 deities are in the inner ring and 32 are on the outer perimeter. It is usually shown as 81 squares in a 9x9 grid.

### What is the Brahmasthana, and how should it be kept?

The Brahmasthana is the central area of the mandala, believed to be ruled by Lord Brahma. According to tradition, it should be kept open or lightly occupied.

### Can the mandala be applied to a plot of irregular shape?

Yes. The ideal mandala is shown as a perfect square, but on irregular plots adjustments are made while keeping the proportions intact. What matters is keeping the proportional relationships and the balance.

### Do all 45 deities have to be worshipped during construction?

No. According to the article, the practical use of the mandala is mainly to understand and respect the qualities of the different zones, not religious observance.

### How should a beginner use the mandala?

First understand the four main directions (north, east, south, west) and the elements and qualities linked with them. Then make simple changes step by step, in line with Vastu guidelines, such as moving a bed, a desk or kitchen appliances. For this you do not need a birth time or a horoscope.
