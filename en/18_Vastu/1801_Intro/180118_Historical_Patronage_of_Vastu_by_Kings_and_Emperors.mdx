# How Kings and Emperors Patronised Vastu in History

## Introduction

The historical patronage of Vastu by kings and emperors shows how the ancient rulers of India actively supported Vastu experts, had grand buildings made, and encouraged the growth of architectural traditions in line with cosmic principles.

Understanding this patronage brings out the deep link between Vedic philosophy, harmony in architecture and royal power.

## What is the historical patronage of Vastu by kings and emperors?

It means the systematic and enthusiastic support given by kings and emperors to Vastu experts and to the science of architecture. Vastu Shastra is a Vedic system that sets down rules for harmony in space. According to tradition, it was respected as essential for the prosperity and spiritual well-being of kingdoms.

With royal help, temple complexes, palaces, settlements and even whole cities were built on Vastu principles. As a result Vastu was established not only in architecture but also in the spiritual traditions of Vedic India.

Through history, rulers were the chief patrons. They had construction projects carried out and gave resources for the preservation, teaching and further development of Vastu knowledge. This shaped not only the physical landscape of India but also its cultural and religious practice.

## Key aspects of the historical patronage of Vastu

### Royal construction and grand architecture

- Kings and emperors had grand temples, palaces and cities built, and their plans are believed to have followed the principles of Vastu Shastra.
- The major buildings of historic dynasties such as the Cholas and the Chandelas are often cited as examples of Vastu. These include the Brihadeeswara Temple and the city plan of Hampi.
- Under the patronage of the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan, the inlay work of pietra dura was raised to new heights.

### Preservation and transmission of architectural knowledge

- Rulers gave patronage to Vastu scholars and craftsmen. Because of this, traditional knowledge was preserved through texts, inscriptions and royal grants.
- Many ancient texts, including the Matsya Purana and the Agama Shastra, record the names of royal patrons. They are also said to link them with pioneering architects and sages of Vastu.
- Institutions supported by the court were centres for training future Vastu experts.

### Cultural and socio-religious influence

- Royal patronage turned Vastu from a practical discipline into a respected science with spiritual and social importance.
- Projects funded by kings promoted the arts, education and shared identity. Temples, libraries and settlements are believed to have been laid out in harmony with cosmic forces.
- Patronage also helped bring the principles of Vastu into everyday life and made statecraft follow the cosmic order.

## How Vastu was applied in royal projects

Vastu Shastra is not applied through numbers. The way it was applied in royal projects was as follows.

- Consulting Vastu experts (Sthapatis), who analysed the orientation of the plot, its proportions and the zones of the elements.
- Turning to classical texts (such as the Kasyapa Silpa) and watching for an auspicious time, a muhurta, for laying the foundation stone.
- Planning the construction with mandalas and geometric diagrams, so that the prescribed rules for directions, proportions and energies were followed.

## How to understand this patronage

Those who know the tradition see this patronage in the following way.

- Important evidence of the value that ancient rulers gave to cosmic harmony.
- A model for understanding how architecture was used to express power, prosperity and spiritual merit.
- Royal buildings made on the principles of Vastu are believed to spread auspicious energies and leave a lasting legacy of well-being.

## Practical applications

### Application 1: Building temples and palaces

- Modern temple and palace projects often refer to the plans and principles laid down by ancient kings. They try to repeat the spiritual and functional benefits seen in historic sites.

### Application 2: Town planning and public spaces

- Lessons from the historical patronage of Vastu inspire today's architects and town planners to combine traditional knowledge with modern needs and create harmonious urban spaces.

## Common misconceptions

- *Misconception*: Vastu was used only for religious buildings.
  - **Clarification**: Royal patronage carried the principles of Vastu beyond temples to palaces, forts, water systems and the layout of cities.
- *Misconception*: Only a few dynasties followed Vastu.
  - **Clarification**: It is believed that many dynasties adopted it widely over centuries.

## Examples

- The Brihadeeswara Temple at Thanjavur was built by Rajaraja Chola. It is often cited as an example of state-level patronage of temple building based on Vastu. It includes alignment with the cardinal directions, proportional mandalas and elaborate gateways.
- The ancient city of Mohenjo-daro, with its orderly streets and drainage, shows an early use of Vastu Shastra. It is believed that the same tradition later appeared in the royal town planning of classical India.

## Conclusion

The historical patronage of Vastu by kings and emperors is regarded as proof that the ancient rulers of India shaped the country's architectural, spiritual and cultural heritage through planned support of Vastu experts and knowledge systems. Their initiatives both preserved and enriched the place of Vastu. This is how it became a lasting pillar of the Vedic tradition.
