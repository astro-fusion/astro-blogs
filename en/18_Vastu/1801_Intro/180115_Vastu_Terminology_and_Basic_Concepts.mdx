# Vastu Terminology and Basic Concepts Explained

## Introduction

Vastu Shastra is the ancient Indian science of architecture. It is believed to be about 5000 years old. It is counted among the most refined systems for making a home or workplace balanced and pleasant. The ancient texts of this tradition set out principles for the structure of a building, its plan and the arrangement of spaces. Their aim is to link the building with nature and cosmic energies.

For this reason it is useful for anyone who builds or improves a home to understand the terminology and basic concepts of Vastu. The tradition holds that cosmic energies affect human life and well-being.

## What are Vastu terminology and basic concepts?

These terms and concepts are the foundation of Vastu Shastra. Vastu Shastra literally means "science of dwelling". Here Vastu means dwelling or house, and Shastra means science or knowledge. This terminology gives us the language and framework to understand how energy flows through a space and what effect it has on those who live there.

The Sanskrit word Vastu comes from the root vas, which means to dwell, to stay, to reside. This shows its focus on creating an ideal environment for living. This architectural science is preserved in ancient Sanskrit manuals called Vastu Shastras. They contain the whole body of knowledge about building and organising an ideal dwelling, so that positive energy grows and negative influences are fewer.

## Key aspects of Vastu terminology

### Brahmasthana

In Vastu Shastra the Brahmasthana is the central point of any building or space. It is regarded as the navel of the building. It is the most sacred area, where cosmic energy gathers.

According to the principles of Vastu, this central part should stay open and unobstructed, so that energy can move freely through the whole building. When the Brahmasthana is properly looked after, it balances the energies of the whole building and contributes to the well-being of those who live there.

### Marma points

The marma points of Vastu are like the vital energy points of the human body that Ayurveda recognises. They are the important meeting places in a building where the pathways of energy join.

A standard Vastu plan is believed to have several marma points, and their importance varies. According to tradition, if there is a disturbance or obstruction at these points, the flow of energy can be disrupted and an imbalance can arise in the space. Understanding these marma points and respecting them is regarded as necessary for keeping harmony throughout the building.

### Pada Vinyasa

Pada Vinyasa is the grid system that forms the basic framework of a building plan in Vastu Shastra. Two grids are commonly used: Manduka (8 by 8 = 64 padas) and Paramasayika (9 by 9 = 81 padas). Each square is called a pada.

Each pada is assigned to a particular deity and is linked with different functions and energies. This grid helps decide where rooms, doors, windows and other building elements should be placed, so that balance and harmony are kept in the building.

## How the plot grid and Aayadi are worked out

This Vastu process begins with making the right grid system (Pada Vinyasa) from the measurements of the plot. Ideally the plot is square, and it is divided into 9 smaller squares to make a particular grid. In relation to the four main directions, north, east, west and south, each square shows a particular function of the space.

The process is as follows.

1. Fix the main directions exactly with the help of a compass.
2. Establish the central point, the Brahmasthana.
3. Divide the plot according to the suitable Pada Vinyasa grid.
4. Calculate the Aayadi formulas, which include the following.
   - Aaya (income): Shows the financial prosperity linked with the building.
   - Vyaya (expenditure): Indicates a possible outflow of money.
   - Yoni (source): Relates to compatibility and harmony.
   - Tithi (lunar day): Links the building with the rhythm of the cosmos.
   - Nakshatra (constellation): Matches the building with the influence of the stars.

According to tradition, these calculations show whether a plot is auspicious, and what changes are needed to improve the flow of energy.

## Basic rules of Vastu

When applying Vastu principles to a space, keep a few main points in mind.

1. **Importance of direction**: The north-east (Ishanya) is regarded as the most sacred direction. It should be kept light and clean. The south-west (Nairitya) corner is regarded as suitable for the main and heavy construction of the building.

2. **Relation to the elements**: Each direction is linked with a different element. The south-west (Nairitya) is linked with earth, the north-east (Ishanya) with water, the south-east (Agneya) with fire, the north-west (Vayavya) with air and the centre with space. Rooms and functions should match their elements.

3. **Flow of energy**: Energy in a building should flow clockwise. It is good if the entrance is in the north, east or north-east.

4. **Balance and proportion**: The size and proportion of the different parts should follow the guidelines of Vastu, so that the balance of energy is kept.

5. **Correcting defects**: When an imbalance is identified, it can often be corrected with remedies, without changing the structure. This is the approach usually taken in existing buildings.

## Practical applications

### Home plan and construction

The principles of Vastu guide the whole process of building a home, from choosing the right plot to deciding the final interior arrangement. For example, the kitchen should ideally be in the south-east (Agneya), the direction of fire. Bedrooms should be in the south-west (Nairitya) or north-west (Vayavya), and the prayer room in the north-east (Ishanya). Following these guidelines is believed to create a home atmosphere that supports health, prosperity and good relations between family members.

### Business spaces

In commercial spaces such as shops or offices, Vastu is used to increase the success of the business and the well-being of employees. This includes the right position of the main entrance, which is best in the east or north. The owner's desk should be placed in the south-west (Nairitya) area, facing north or east. Accounts papers should be kept in the north or south-east (Agneya). These arrangements are believed to increase prosperity, authority in leadership and financial stability.

## Common misconceptions

The first common misconception is that Vastu is only superstition. Many Vastu principles are linked with matters that modern architecture also accepts, such as natural light, movement of air and efficient use of space. Its subtle-energy claims are matters of tradition.

The second misconception is that an existing building must be demolished or completely changed to correct a "defect". In practice many Vastu imbalances are removed by simple remedies, such as correcting colours, changing the placement of objects or making small changes.

Besides this, many people take Vastu Shastra for feng shui. Both systems speak of the flow of energy in living spaces, but their roots are in different cultural traditions, one Indian and the other Chinese. Their methods are also different, although some of their basic principles about harmony with nature are similar.

## Examples

**Example 1: Analysis of a home's Brahmasthana**

In a square house of 30 by 30 feet, the Brahmasthana would be the central part of 10 by 10 feet. In an ideal home that follows Vastu, this part is kept open, or is used as a courtyard or a central hall with minimal furniture. According to Vastu tradition, if this central part holds a toilet, a kitchen or storage for heavy goods, it creates a block in the path of energy.

For example, if a home has a toilet in its Brahmasthana, Vastu regards this as a defect. The tradition suggests moving the toilet elsewhere and opening up that part.

**Example 2: Using Pada Vinyasa in a business**

The new layout of a retail shop was made according to the 9 by 9 grid. The cash counter was placed in the north zone, which is linked with prosperity. The owner's room was made in the south-west (Nairitya), which is regarded as the position of authority. The north-east (Ishanya) part was kept empty for customers to enter.

## Conclusion

Vastu terminology and basic concepts give a framework for understanding how, according to Vastu tradition, the places where we live and work affect our lives. By learning key terms such as Brahmasthana, marma points and Pada Vinyasa, we can understand how this tradition links the physical space with cosmic energies.

This architectural science, believed to be thousands of years old, still gives guidance for creating a harmonious environment in tune with the forces of nature. Whether it is the plan of a home, the layout of an office or the building of a temple, these principles help to create places that support our life journey and do not obstruct it.

## FAQs

### What is the Brahmasthana and why is it kept open?

The Brahmasthana is the central point of a building, regarded as the navel of the building. According to Vastu it should be open and unobstructed, so that energy can move freely through the whole building. It should not hold a toilet, a kitchen or storage for heavy goods.

### What does Pada Vinyasa mean?

Pada Vinyasa is the grid system that forms the basic framework of a building plan. Two grids are commonly used: Manduka (8 by 8 = 64 padas) and Paramasayika (9 by 9 = 81 padas). Each pada is assigned to a deity, and this decides where rooms, doors and windows are placed.

### What are marma points?

They are the important meeting places in a building where energy pathways join, like the energy points of the human body. According to tradition, a disturbance or obstruction at them can cause an imbalance.

### Does a home have to be demolished for Vastu?

No. According to the article, many Vastu imbalances are removed by simple remedies, such as correcting colours, changing the placement of objects or making small changes.

### Which room should go in which direction?

The tradition suggests the kitchen in the south-east (Agneya), bedrooms in the south-west (Nairitya) or north-west (Vayavya), and the prayer room in the north-east (Ishanya). The entrance is considered good in the north, east or north-east.

### Where should a beginner start?

First learn the importance of each direction, then understand the concept of the Brahmasthana. Removing clutter from the north-east (Ishanya) corner, making sure there is good air and natural light, and keeping the centre of the home unobstructed are good first steps. After that, Pada Vinyasa and the marma points can be studied in more depth.
