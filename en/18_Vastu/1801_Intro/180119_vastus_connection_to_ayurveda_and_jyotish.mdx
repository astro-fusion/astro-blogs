# How Vastu Relates to Ayurveda and Jyotish

## Introduction

The relation between Vastu, Ayurveda and Jyotish shows the deep harmony of three ancient Vedic sciences. In tradition all three rest on holistic well-being. They are Vastu (the science of buildings and space), Ayurveda (the science of health and medicine) and Jyotish (Vedic astrology). Tradition holds that together they bring harmony to the energies of our surroundings, our body and our destiny.

Understanding how these sciences are regarded in tradition as woven together helps to see Vastu in a larger context.

## What is the relation between Vastu, Ayurveda and Jyotish?

It describes the interplay of Vastu Shastra, Ayurveda and Jyotish within the ancient Vedic tradition. All three disciplines come from the same philosophical base. That base is the balance of the five elements, namely earth, water, fire, air and space (the Panchabhutas). The only difference is that each science looks at it in a different field.

- **Vastu** balances these elements in living spaces.
- **Ayurveda** balances them within the human body and mind.
- **Jyotish** explains their influence at the cosmic and personal level through the positions of the planets.

According to tradition, these sister sciences together form a holistic approach to well-being. It carries the idea of bringing surroundings, body and destiny into line together.

## Key aspects of the relation between Vastu, Ayurveda and Jyotish

### The principle of the five elements

All three sciences work on the principle of the five elements. Ayurveda focuses on balancing them within the body to keep health. Vastu balances them in the home or workplace. Jyotish analyses their cosmic and planetary influences.

- According to tradition, an imbalance in the home (Vastu) can mirror or worsen an imbalance in the body (Ayurveda), and it can also show in a person's birth chart (Jyotish).

### Alignment and flow of energy

Vastu, Ayurveda and Jyotish all stress the flow of energy. In Vastu it is called prana, in Ayurveda vata, pitta and kapha, and in Jyotish the energies of the planets.

- A well-built home is believed to support a healthy movement of energy.

### Personalisation and harmony

Each person's Ayurvedic constitution (dosha) and Jyotish chart can guide Vastu advice. The ideal arrangement of a space can be adapted to a person's dosha and planetary influences, so that harmony and well-being increase.

## Bases for joining the three sciences

There is no single formula that joins the three sciences. In tradition their mutual relation is seen on these bases.

- **Analysis of the chart**: Each direction and each room of a home is linked with a planet and an area of life. This can be mapped from the positions of the planets in the birth chart.
- **Checking the type of dosha**: Ayurveda identifies a person's main dosha (vata, pitta or kapha). This helps in choosing colours, materials and plans in Vastu.
- **Vastu assessment**: Evaluating the building's alignment with the main directions and the position of the elements (water, fire and so on), so that they support both the resident's dosha and the strengths and weaknesses of the planets.

## How tradition reads these signs

- **Balanced elements**: According to tradition, balance in living spaces and in a person's constitution is regarded as a positive sign.
- **Reflection of imbalance**: Problems that persist in areas of life (health, wealth, relationships) may be a sign of an imbalance of elements both in the space (Vastu) and in the self (Ayurveda). These often also show in challenging planetary positions (Jyotish).
- **Remedies**: Bringing harmony to the living space according to Vastu, changing lifestyle and diet according to Ayurveda, and following the remedies of Jyotish: looking at all three together is held by tradition to reach the root of disharmony.

## Practical applications

### Designing a space to suit the person

According to tradition, changes in one's home or workplace can be made not only on general Vastu but also with one's dosha (Ayurveda) and planetary chart (Jyotish) in mind. For example, it is believed that a vata-dominant person may need more of the earth element and warmth in their space.

### A whole-picture approach to problems

Are challenges in some area of life continuing? Tradition speaks of looking for an imbalance of the elements in all three places: the living space, the body and the birth chart. For example, in the case of repeated financial strain, Vastu remedies for the north (the direction of wealth) are considered, a change of diet is advised for kapha balance, and Jyotish remedies are suggested for the planets. For any health decision, take the advice of a qualified physician.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Vastu, Ayurveda and Jyotish are independent and unrelated."**
  In fact these sciences are deeply linked and should be taken together for an all-round view of well-being.
- **"Only the building or only the medicine matters."**
  Tradition holds that ignoring environmental or planetary factors may leave the balance incomplete, which is why it speaks of attending to all three areas.

## Examples

- In one example a person often has anxiety and restlessness, which Ayurveda regards as an imbalance of vata. Clutter is found in the north-east (Ishanya) of the home, which is a Vastu imbalance. The Moon is also weak in the person's birth chart. According to tradition, work is done on all three levels, that is, removing the clutter, adopting a supportive diet and looking at astrological remedies.
- In a second example a person faces repeated obstacles in a career. The Vastu analysis finds the north area (the direction of wealth) blocked, Ayurveda identifies the sluggishness of kapha, and Jyotish shows the challenges of Saturn. Tradition advises looking at the remedies of all three sciences together.

## Conclusion

The relation between Vastu, Ayurveda and Jyotish is regarded as the foundation of the Vedic holistic view. By recognising the energy links between our homes, our bodies and our destiny and bringing them into harmony, the tradition shows the way to a balanced life. This view stresses looking at our inner and outer surroundings together. Its claims are matters of tradition and belief, not proven science or medicine.

## FAQs

### What do Vastu, Ayurveda and Jyotish have in common?

All three are sciences of the Vedic tradition, and all three rest on the balance of the five elements: earth, water, fire, air and space. Vastu looks at them in living spaces, Ayurveda in the body and mind, and Jyotish through the positions of the planets.

### Does an imbalance in the home affect the body?

According to tradition, an imbalance in the home can mirror or worsen an imbalance in the body. This is a Vastu belief, not proven medicine. If you have a health problem, see a qualified physician.

### How do the doshas (vata, pitta, kapha) relate to Vastu?

Ayurveda identifies a person's main dosha, and in tradition this is used to help choose colours, materials and plans in Vastu. For example, it is believed that a vata-dominant person may need more of the earth element and warmth in their space.

### Is there one formula for all three?

No. There is no single formula that joins the three sciences. In tradition the chart, the type of dosha, and the building's direction and element positions are looked at separately and then brought together.

### Is Vastu alone or Ayurveda alone enough?

According to the article, tradition speaks of attending to all three. Even so, the principles of Vastu and Ayurveda can be used without a birth time.

### How should a beginner start?

Learn the basic principles of Vastu for the home, find out your Ayurvedic dosha (with the help of a knowledgeable physician) and learn your Sun and Moon signs in astrology. Then see how tradition looks at the three together.
