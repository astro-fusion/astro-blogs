# Choosing a Commercial Plot by Vastu: Shape and Ratio

## Introduction

Choosing a commercial plot is not only a real estate decision. Vastu Shastra, the traditional Indian system of architecture and design, gives guidelines for choosing commercial property, and tradition holds that the shape, direction and proportions of a plot affect business and the wellbeing of the people who work there. In the competitive business world, knowing these principles gives an entrepreneur one more point to weigh when arranging a workplace. Vastu is not a substitute for legal checks, soil and engineering reports, sound business practice or good location, and it makes no promise of results.

## What is an ideal commercial plot and its proportion?

It is the orderly process of examining and choosing land for business on the basis of Vastu principles. Vastu holds that a plot of a particular shape, dimension and direction carries energy in balance and supports business success. Mainly square or rectangular plots are considered, with proportions usually given as 1 to 1 for a square and 1 to 2 for a rectangle. Such shapes are believed to spread energy evenly through the whole property.

The choice also looks at the direction, the surroundings, the slope of the land and the nature of the neighbouring buildings. According to Vastu these together decide how energy flows through the property, and this is said to affect business prosperity, the efficiency of employees and the success of the entrepreneur. Instead of taking a plot only by availability or price, this method looks at how well a plot can support a particular business.

## Key aspects

### Shape and proportion

In Vastu Shastra the shape of the plot is the most basic consideration. Square or rectangular plots are specially recommended, because energy is distributed in balance in them. According to Vastu practitioners a full square is a symbol of wholeness and stability, and a rectangle whose width and length are in the ratio 1 to 2 is regarded as equally auspicious for business. Such regular shapes are said to support prosperous energy and give stability to the structure. Irregular shapes such as a triangle or a hexagon are advised against, because extended or missing corners are believed to upset the flow of energy. Such irregularities are linked with obstacles in business and financial instability. The ideal shape may vary a little for different businesses, but the principle of regularity is the same everywhere.

### Facing direction of the plot

According to tradition, the side on which the plot faces decides what kind of business it suits. Each direction is believed to be linked with a different energy that supports particular professions.

- East-facing plots are described as suitable for educational institutions, research organisations and professions connected with learning.
- West-facing plots are suggested for trade and general business.
- South-facing plots are suggested for manufacturing and industrial use.
- North-facing plots are regarded as suitable for administrative offices and businesses connected with government work.

This is a traditional belief of Vastu, not a universally accepted rule. Choose the direction according to the nature of the business. In addition, the corners of the plot, especially the south-west and the south-east, should be right angles, so that energy is held properly.

### The surroundings

The surroundings of a plot matter a great deal in Vastu. Avoid a plot at a T-junction or a Y-junction, because such a layout is believed to bring negative energy towards the plot. In the same way, plots near waste disposal sites, hospitals or cremation grounds are advised against, because tradition holds that the energy there affects the growth of a business. Natural features matter too. Avoid plots with hills or mounds in the east or the north, while a solid rocky hill in the south-west is regarded as giving stability and support. Water bodies are good in the north, the north-east or the east, not in the south or the south-west, because water in a wrong place is held to reduce the energy of wealth.

## How to examine the plot

1. Take the measurements properly. Have a professional survey give the exact length, width and area of the plot in square metres or square feet. In a rectangular plot the ratio of width to length should ideally be 1 to 2.
2. Work out the area and check the proportions. Multiply the length by the width to get the total area.
3. Check the direction. With a compass see how the plot lies against the true directions. Note which direction the front faces.
4. Look for flaws in the geometry. Measure all the corners and see that they are 90 degrees, especially the south-west and south-east corners, which are held to be particularly important in commercial Vastu.
5. Check the slope. In tradition the ideal slope of a plot is low towards the north or the east, that is towards the north-east. Measure the difference in height between the corners to see whether the natural slope matches this recommendation.
6. Check the load-bearing capacity. For commercial construction, test the quality of the soil and its load-bearing capacity, especially in the south and west parts where heavy construction is advised.

On the basis of these measurements a full assessment of the suitability of the plot from the Vastu point of view can be made.

## Reading the result

A perfectly square plot is a symbol of wholeness and is regarded as good for general business. According to tradition it gives a steady flow of energy and balanced growth in every aspect of the business. Rectangular plots with a ratio of 1 to 2 are regarded as equally beneficial and may give more flexibility for some business layouts.

The advice is that the facing direction of the plot should match the kind of business. In tradition an east-facing plot is described as suitable for intellectual and creative business, a west-facing one for trade and commerce, a south-facing one for manufacturing and production, and a north-facing one for administrative and service businesses.

A raised south and west part of the plot is regarded as a sign of financial stability and strong leadership. The main building or the heavy parts of the business should be kept in these parts. The north-east corner should be the lowest and be kept open and clean, so that prosperity and positive energy come in.

Sharp corners, awkward extensions or cuts in the plot are taken as signs of challenges in the corresponding areas of business. A cut in the north-east is linked with obstacles to progress and growth, and a cut in the south-west with a loss of authority and stability. Extensions in the north, the north-east and the east are regarded as auspicious, while extensions in the other directions are said to create imbalance.

Surrounding features such as roads, buildings and natural things are also held to influence the energy of the plot. Roads on all sides are said to be good for the circulation of auspicious energy, while a T-junction pointing straight at the plot should be avoided.

## Practical applications

### Choosing a site for different businesses

According to tradition, different businesses gain from different features of a plot. For educational institutions and research centres, give preference to an east-facing rectangular plot, which is said to support intellectual growth and innovation. The east is regarded as the direction of knowledge and learning, so it is described as ideal for knowledge-based enterprises.

For manufacturing and industrial business, a south-facing plot is suggested, with enough area for the machinery and the work. The south is regarded as giving stability and energy for production work. Place heavy machinery in the south and south-west parts of the plot, and lighter equipment in the northern part.

For retail and trading business, a west-facing rectangular plot is described as suited to commercial exchange and the coming and going of customers. Keep the main entrance in the north, the north-east or the east so that prosperity is welcomed, and place the cash counter in the north.

For corporate offices and administrative centres, a north-facing plot that is square or has a 1 to 2 rectangular ratio is regarded as better. Keep the executives' rooms in the south-west or the west, the meeting rooms in the west, and the reception in the north-east or north part of the building.

### Planning the layout to use the plot's energy

Once a suitable plot is found, a thoughtful plan of the layout is used to draw the most from its energy. For stability and authority, keep the main building in the south and south-west parts of the plot. These parts should carry the greatest weight of construction.

Keep the north-east corner for open space, a water feature or the reception area, so that the flow of positive energy is formed. This part should be lighter and more open than the rest.

Place electric generators, electrical rooms and transformers in the south-east part of the plot, because this direction is held to belong to the fire element.

Build staff facilities and rest areas in the north-west or south-west corner of the plot.

Keep vehicle parking in the north-west or the north part of the plot, and leave the north-east and the east open so that the flow of energy is not blocked.

## Common misconceptions

One common misconception is that a plot which departs even a little from a perfect square or rectangle is wholly unsuitable. Regular shapes are ideal, but small irregularities can often be put right by proper construction methods or by landscaping. Instead of rejecting a plot with minor faults outright, Vastu practitioners can suggest corrective measures.

Many people think that when choosing a plot Vastu matters less than price and location. In fact, keeping Vastu in mind from the start can avoid costly difficulties and demolition later. For this reason the advice is to consider Vastu at the beginning, not afterwards, together with price, location, legal title and the engineering of the site.

It is also a misconception that every direction is equally suitable for every kind of business. According to tradition different business activities match the energy of particular directions, and the better choice depends on your industry. A plot that is excellent for manufacturing may not be ideal for education or retail business.

Some people think that a larger plot is always better from the Vastu point of view. But regularity of shape and proportion are held to matter more than area alone. A smaller plot with the right proportion is often regarded as having a better energy balance than a large irregular plot.

Many people overlook the importance of what surrounds the plot. The roads, buildings, water bodies and natural formations around the property are held to affect its energy, so they should be looked at carefully when choosing.

## Examples

These are plain illustrations, not records of outcomes.

### An office plot for a technology company

A technology start-up chose a rectangular east-facing plot of 600 square metres, with a ratio of 1 to 2 (20 metres by 30 metres). The corners of the plot were regular right angles and the slope was gentle. Following Vastu principles they placed the main building in the south-west and the executives' rooms in the west. The reception and client meeting area was placed in the north-east, and the development team sat in the eastern part. The server room and the electrical facilities were in the south-east corner.

The east-facing direction was regarded as suiting the innovation and creative thinking that a technology business needs, and the proper distribution of weight in the south-west was linked with the stability of the organisation.

### Assessing a plot for a manufacturing unit

A manufacturing business examined two plots for its new unit. Plot A was an awkward pentagon facing west, while Plot B was a rectangular south-facing plot of 3000 square metres (50 metres by 60 metres), with a slight rise in its south-west corner. On Vastu principles they chose Plot B.

They placed the heavy machinery in the southern part, storage in the west, the administrative offices in the south-west and the main entrance in the east. Staff facilities were in the north-west, and a small garden was made in the north-east corner. The south-facing direction was regarded as suiting their manufacturing process, and the rectangular proportion as a means of balanced energy flow through the whole unit.

## Conclusion

Seen through Vastu Shastra, choosing a commercial plot of the right proportion is a basic foundation of a business. Tradition stresses that square or rectangular plots with proportions of 1 to 1 or 1 to 2 make harmonious energy patterns. The facing direction should suit the kind of business: east for knowledge-based enterprises, west for trade, south for manufacturing and north for administrative services.

Beyond shape and direction, a full assessment must include the surroundings, the natural slope and the neighbouring structures. Modern considerations such as location, access, soil, legal title and the market remain important. Vastu can be taken as an additional guide alongside them, never in place of them.

## FAQ

### How does choosing a commercial plot differ from choosing a residential plot in Vastu?

Both share basic principles such as preferring square or rectangular shapes, but a commercial plot needs particular consideration according to the business. Commercial Vastu gives different facing directions for different industries (east for education, south for manufacturing and so on), while residential Vastu is more general. A commercial plot also needs attention to the weight of machinery, public access and staff facilities, which do not arise in a home.

### Can Vastu be used to predict the success of a business?

No. Vastu is a traditional guide to choosing and arranging a plot, not a prediction. Business success still depends on the market, management decisions and the quality of operation, so treat Vastu as one part of a wider approach to business planning.

### Which cuts and extensions of a plot should I watch for?

Tradition links a cut in the north-east with obstacles to progress and a cut in the south-west with loss of authority and stability. Extensions in the north, the north-east and the east are regarded as auspicious, and extensions in other directions as creating imbalance.

### Do I need exact measurements of the plot to check it by Vastu?

Yes. The exact length, width, corner angles and direction are needed to judge the proportions of the plot. A professional survey is recommended, especially for a large investment.

### What should I do with a plot that has a flaw?

Do not reject it outright. Small irregularities can often be corrected by proper construction methods or landscaping, and a Vastu practitioner can suggest corrective measures.

### What should a beginner do first?

Learn to recognise the shapes and proportions Vastu recommends, squares and rectangles of 1 to 1 or 1 to 2. Practise finding the direction of a plot with a compass and look at the surroundings. For an important investment, consult a qualified Vastu practitioner as well as the surveyor and the engineer.
