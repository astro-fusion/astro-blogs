# Industrial Land Slope and Elevation in Vastu

## Introduction

Vastu Shastra is a traditional Indian system of building and the design of space. It is not limited to homes: rules are also given for factories and industrial complexes. In these, the shape of the land, its levels and the direction of its slope are given special importance. According to Vastu tradition, these matters influence the flow of energy and the steadiness of the business. Understanding the natural form of the land, the differences in height and the direction of the slope is held to give a better basis for production, for the comfort of workers and for the long life of the business. These principles are not a substitute for engineering surveys, safety rules and sound business practice.

## What Industrial Land Slope and Elevation Means

It means examining the natural ups and downs, the slope, the levels and the direction of land chosen for an industry by the rules of Vastu. This is used to decide whether the land suits manufacturing, processing or other industrial work.

Ordinary selection of an industrial site mostly looks at transport and cost. The Vastu approach also asks how the energy of the earth (bhu-shakti) moves through the property, given the form of the land. By tradition, differences in height, the direction of the slope and the natural landforms work together with the five elements, earth, water, fire, air and space, to form a pattern of energy that is either good or disturbed.

## Key Aspects

### Natural Direction of Slope

According to Vastu, an ideal industrial plot slopes gently towards the north-east, so that the land is lower in the north and east and higher in the south and west. This is considered auspicious because it is said to suit the natural flow of energy. Tradition holds that such a slope lets positive energy flow into the property and keeps negative energy away from the main working areas.

This slope also has a practical benefit. Rainwater and drainage are handled properly, which is necessary for industry. On plots whose slope runs the opposite way, corrective measures or special design are considered necessary.

### Elevation Zones and Heavy Structures

Vastu divides an industrial property into separate zones by height. The earth zone is generally taken to be the south-west part, and the south side is also kept high. All heavy construction, taller buildings and heavy machinery are advised to go in this part. By tradition, this gives the company a firm foundation and a lasting business.

It is also held that a higher earth zone supports production capacity and allows the machinery to be used fully.

### Position of the Water Element

The place of water has special importance in industrial Vastu. A large underground water tank, in proportion to the size of the premises, should be built in the water zone. Tradition holds that this place suits the flow of money into the business.

Many industrial units today also adopt rainwater harvesting in designs that follow Vastu. The underground tank stays in the water zone, which is the north-east, and the overhead tank is built on the opposite side so that the balance of the elements is kept. This arrangement agrees with the traditional rule and also with the goals of sustainable design.

## Assessing the Land

The height and slope of an industrial site are checked in these steps.

1. **Measuring the slope.** Work out the percentage of slope as the rise divided by the run, multiplied by 100. For an industrial site a gentle slope is considered suitable, falling towards the north-east. Heavy machinery needs level ground or a very gentle slope.

2. **Survey of levels.** Have a topographic survey made to identify the high and low points across the plot. This gives a three-dimensional picture of the ground.

3. **Assessing parts by direction.** Divide the plot into nine equal parts, a simple three by three grid, and compare the height of each part with the others. The south-west part should be the highest, falling gradually towards the north-east.

4. **Allotting by element.** Decide the proper place for each element. For example, the zone of the fire element is the south-east, where the electrical systems are kept. The zone of the earth element is the south-west, where heavy machinery is kept.

5. **Map of energy flow.** From the height data, look at how energy (prana) moves across the property and where it may be blocked or stagnant. The design can be improved at those places.

## Reading the Results

- **Importance of the slope.** A slope falling towards the north-east is regarded as very auspicious, so give it priority when choosing land. If the natural slope is the opposite way, it can be corrected by regrading the ground or by deciding the position of buildings with care.

- **Order of heights.** The south-west corner should generally be the highest, with the heavy machinery and main buildings. The north-east should be comparatively low, with the water arrangements or lighter work. This is considered to give a balanced distribution of energy through the premises.

- **Check of balance.** See whether the two extremes of height are in balance. A very steep slope is taken as a sign of imbalance. Completely flat ground with no natural fall is taken as a sign of stagnant energy. In both cases the design needs careful thought.

- **Care for the boundary.** The compound wall is made of different heights according to direction. On the south and west sides a more solid and higher wall is usually kept.

- **Needs of each part.** The south-east and south-west zones are held to benefit from height, while the north-east water zone should be low so that water can collect there.

## Practical Applications

### Planning a Production Unit

According to Vastu, a production unit whose slope and levels have been considered offers a better base for operations. Heavy machinery generally needs level or gently sloping ground. Placing the production area in the south or south-west on slightly higher ground gives a steadier foundation for machines that vibrate.

### Storage and Resource Management

According to Vastu, the store for heavy raw material is placed in the south-west part of the premises, which is the higher, firmer ground. A store for finished goods is considered properly placed in the north-west. In this way material moves clockwise.

The order of receipt of material in the east, production in the south or south-west and distribution in the north-west agrees with both the traditional rule and efficient industrial design. Drainage and storage of water should also follow the slope of the land and respect the Vastu zones. The main water arrangement stays in the north-east, on low ground.

## Common Misconceptions

The first misconception is that completely flat land is always best for industry. It is true that level land lowers the initial cost of construction and leaves the building design open. Even so, Vastu prefers a gentle slope with a particular direction. A gentle slope towards the north-east is said to suit both the flow of energy and the drainage of water.

The second misconception is that industrial Vastu rules are only ritual or superstition with no practical benefit. The truth is that several of these rules agree with good engineering. Placing heavy machinery on slightly higher ground in the south-west improves stability and the control of vibration. The Vastu suggestions on water can also complement modern drainage design.

The third misconception is that the whole plot must be dug up and changed to correct an unfavourable slope. Land that suits Vastu is better, but experienced consultants suggest improvements that work with the existing landform. These can include the position of buildings, changes in the internal plan and balancing with the elements of the building.

## Examples

These examples are plain illustrations. No outcome is promised.

### Example 1: A Production Unit in Western India

A medium-sized company had a factory on land sloping from west to east. Production was interrupted and the machines often broke down. On meeting a Vastu consultant, the company learned that the heavy machinery stood in the north-east, the water zone, on the lowest part of the plot, which is against Vastu advice.

The company moved its main production machines in stages to the south-west, on the highest point of the plot. Water storage and treatment were also brought to the lowest part of the north-east. In this way the slope of the land worked with the flow of operations and not against it.

### Example 2: Developing an Industrial Park

A developer had a detailed topographic survey made before building a 50-acre industrial park. Together with civil engineers and Vastu consultants, a grading plan was prepared that kept the natural slope of the land and made proper level ground for each building.

The naturally higher south and south-west parts were given to heavy manufacturing units, the middle part to mixed industrial use, and the lower north and north-east parts to warehouses, water management and green areas.

## Conclusion

The slope and elevation of industrial land form a link between the ancient Vastu tradition and modern industrial development. According to Vastu tradition, keeping heavy machinery and main buildings in the high south-west, the water arrangements in the low north-east and a slope falling towards the north-east makes a plot balanced in both energy and practice.

Behind these points are also solid needs such as drainage, strength of the structure, flow of material and efficiency of operation. Premises built with attention to the relation between land, energy and human activity are regarded as supporting both immediate needs and long-term balance.

## FAQs

### In which direction should the slope of an industrial plot run?

According to Vastu, the natural slope should fall gently towards the north-east, so that the land is lower in the north and east. It is considered good for the flow of energy and also for draining water.

### In which part should heavy machinery be placed?

In the earth zone of the south-west, together with the south side, which should be the highest part of the plot. This gives machines a steadier foundation.

### Where should the water tank be built?

The underground tank is built in the water zone in the north-east, in proportion to the size of the premises. The overhead tank goes on the opposite side.

### What if the slope of the land runs the wrong way?

Tradition mentions measures such as grading, careful positioning of buildings or changes in the internal plan. Changing the whole plot is not considered necessary.

### Where should raw material and finished goods be kept?

Heavy raw material in the south-west. A store for finished goods is considered properly placed in the north-west.

### Is perfectly flat land acceptable?

Building on flat land is easier, but Vastu prefers a gentle slope in the right direction. Completely flat ground is taken as a sign of stagnant energy.
