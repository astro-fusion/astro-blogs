# Boss Cabin Placement and Seating Direction in Vastu

## Introduction

The position of a boss's cabin in the office, and the direction it faces, is not only a question of interior design or hierarchy. The ancient Vastu Shastra tradition holds that the physical arrangement of a workplace affects the flow of energy, the style of leadership, the success of the business and prosperity. In today's competitive business world, many executives and business owners turn to these old principles to arrange a workplace that supports their leadership goals. Vastu is not a substitute for sound business practice, and it makes no promise of results.

## What is boss cabin placement and orientation?

It means the deliberate position of an executive's room or work area within the whole office plan, and also the direction the executive faces while sitting at the desk. Vastu tradition holds that certain positions and directions strengthen leadership qualities, decision making and authority. The idea behind this is that the energy of the directions influences human behaviour and the results of a business. For those who want to arrange their workplace for success, it is one more point to weigh in office design.

## Key aspects

### The cabin in the south-west corner

According to Vastu principles, the south-west corner of an office is regarded as ideal for the boss's or executive's cabin. This direction stands for stability, strength and authority, which are qualities needed for effective leadership. Placing the main executive's room in this corner creates a natural position of power within the space. The south-west is linked with the earth element, which is said to give steadiness and to strengthen the decision maker's grip and ability to command respect. In this way the foundation of the company's leadership is made firm and the executive's position secure.

### Facing north or east

Even if the cabin is in the south-west corner, the executive should face north or east while sitting at the desk. Facing north is considered especially good for bringing in business and new opportunities, because this direction is associated with growth, prosperity and positive energy. Facing east places the executive towards the energy of the rising sun, which is said to bring clarity of thought, freshness and a new outlook. These are qualities needed for strategic decisions. Both directions are held to support concentration and financial success, while facing south or west is regarded as draining.

### A solid wall behind

An important part of the right orientation is that the executive has solid support at the back. The desk should be arranged so that the boss sits with the back against a solid wall, not towards a window, an open space or a door. This gives a feeling of mental security and protection. Without proper support at the back, the executive may feel insecure or unsupported, which can quietly weaken confidence and decision making. If a solid wall is not possible, tall plants or a heavy piece of furniture placed behind the chair can create similar support.

## How to decide the direction and the position

1. Identify the directions. First fix the true directions (north, east, south and west) in the office with a reliable compass. Every later decision rests on this.
2. Identify the south-west part. Divide the office plan into nine equal squares (a grid of 3 by 3). When north is at the top of the plan, the south-west is the bottom-left square. This part is about one ninth of the total office space and is the ideal zone for the boss's cabin.
3. Set the direction of the desk. Once the position of the cabin is fixed, place the desk so that the person sitting faces north (0 degrees) or east (90 degrees). The angle can be measured from true north to fix this exactly.
4. Consider the auspicious angle. Some Vastu practitioners advise facing 10 to 20 degrees towards the north-east of true north rather than exactly north, because it is thought to combine the energy of north and north-east. This is one opinion, not a universally accepted rule.
5. Keep a distance from the walls. It is considered right to keep the desk at least 2.5 to 3 feet away from any wall, so that energy can move, while the support at the back described above is still kept.

## Reading the arrangement

- A cabin in the south-west: according to tradition, a cabin in the right place is expected to bring greater authority, steadier leadership and respectful relations with staff. If leadership still feels weak or unstable although everything else is in order, the position of the cabin can be reconsidered.
- The facing direction: an executive facing north is expected to be better at attracting new business opportunities and clients. For those facing east, tradition speaks of more creativity and clearer decisions. If this is not seen, check whether anything in the line of sight is an obstruction or is considered inauspicious.
- The support at the back: proper support should give a feeling of security and confidence. If the executive is restless, distracted or easily startled while working, the arrangement behind the chair may be incomplete.
- The overall flow of energy: the whole office should show a harmony of rank, with energy moving from the south-west (leadership) towards the other parts. If there is tension in relationships within the organisation, look at how the boss's cabin relates to the other work areas.
- Business results: tradition holds that a correct arrangement should show good effects in business measures such as profit, team unity and lasting growth. Such results are taken as a confirmation of correct practice, though they depend on many other things as well.

## Practical applications

### Supporting leadership and authority

A cabin in the south-west corner creates a natural position of power for leadership and makes the leader's authority firm within the organisation. Tradition holds that it helps the executive to win respect and to decide with confidence. To strengthen this effect, keep the cabin slightly raised if possible, and let the lighting bring out the executive's position without glare. The furniture should be heavy and durable, preferably of natural material such as wood and in earth colours, to support the steadiness of the earth element linked with the south-west. In difficult business conditions this helps the executive keep a firm hold and a strong presence.

### Helping decisions and bringing in business

Sitting facing north or east connects the executive with the energies held to support the main functions of a business. Facing north is described as especially useful for those whose main responsibility is to bring in new business, add clients or widen market reach. This direction encourages outward-looking energy and helps draw opportunities towards the business. Facing east is held to support innovation, strategic planning and clear communication, so it suits executives who must make complex decisions or start new initiatives. Where there are several executives, each one's facing direction can be set according to the main responsibility, so that the leadership team is balanced.

## Common misconceptions

One common misconception is that all this is mere superstition with no practical basis. The aim of these principles is to improve the sense of space, mental comfort and the surroundings. The south-west corner, for example, gives the widest view of the whole office, so the leader can follow what is happening in the workplace. This practical advantage exists apart from any talk of energy.

A second misconception is that these principles work only in large offices built for the purpose. In fact, a small office or a home workspace can apply them in proportion to the space available. The main point is to keep the relative positions, not to insist on exact measurements.

Many also wrongly believe that these principles need costly demolition or special furniture. An ideal application may involve structural changes, but simple rearrangement of furniture and small changes in the direction of the desk can be tried without a large expense.

## Examples

These are plain illustrations, not records of outcomes.

### A financial services company

A mid-sized financial services company faced problems of leadership and staff leaving. On consulting a Vastu practitioner, it found that the chief executive's room was in the north-east corner of the building, which tradition links with spiritual growth rather than leadership, and that the executive sat with the back towards the door. The executive's room was moved to the south-west corner and the desk set so that the executive faced north with the back against a solid wall. The company said it felt an improvement: staff had more trust in the leadership and the executive's decisions seemed more efficient and decisive. See it only as an example.

### A home office

An entrepreneur running a consulting business from home was not winning new clients despite her expertise. Her table was in the middle of the room, with no support behind it, and she faced west while working. She moved the table to the south-west corner of the home office, put a solid wall behind her and turned to face north. She then reported more enquiries. She also said that her concentration was better and that she felt less nervous making important business calls, which she put down to the feeling of support from the solid wall. This too is an example only.

## Conclusion

The placement and direction of the boss's cabin is one meeting point between ancient knowledge and modern workplace design. By placing the executive's room in the south-west corner of the workplace, with the executive facing north or east and with proper support at the back, an environment can be created which Vastu tradition regards as favourable to leadership, decision making and prosperity. These principles come from Vastu Shastra, but their value goes beyond spiritual thinking to the practical psychology of the workplace and the layout of space. Adopting them gives a chance to improve the workplace at the leadership level, alongside sound management and safe working conditions.

## FAQ

### Where should the boss's cabin be in the office?

According to Vastu, the south-west corner is ideal for the boss's or executive's cabin. This direction stands for stability, strength and authority, and it is linked with the earth element.

### Which direction should the boss face while sitting?

North or east. North is considered good for bringing in business and new opportunities, and east for clarity of thought and freshness of ideas. Facing south or west is advised against.

### Why is a solid wall behind the chair important?

Tradition holds that solid support at the back gives mental security and confidence. With a window, an open space or a door behind, the executive may feel insecure. If there is no wall, tall plants or heavy furniture can be placed behind the chair.

### Do these rules apply to a small office or a home office?

Yes. In a small office or home workspace they can be applied in proportion to the space available. What matters is to keep the relative positions, not to insist on exact measurements.

### Do I need costly changes to follow them?

No. You can begin with rearranging furniture and small changes in the direction of the desk.

### What should a beginner do first?

Make three simple changes. First, move the desk to the south-west corner of the office if possible. Second, place the chair so that you face north or east while working. Third, have a solid wall behind your back instead of a window or a door. After that, you may look at finer points such as colours, balance of the elements and the measurements of the desk.
