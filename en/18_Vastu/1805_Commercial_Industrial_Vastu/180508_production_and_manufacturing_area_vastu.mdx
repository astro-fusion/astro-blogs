# Factory Production Area Vastu: Machines and Layout

## Introduction

Vastu for the production and manufacturing area is a special use of Vastu Shastra, the ancient Indian tradition of building. This branch gives attention to keeping the flow of energy right on the production floor of a factory, in assembly lines and in industrial units. According to Vastu tradition these guidelines are held to help raise the pace of work, cut down faults and keep the atmosphere of the workplace harmonious. For a factory owner, understanding these principles may be useful in keeping operations running smoothly. Vastu is not a substitute for engineering, safety rules, quality control or sound management, and it makes no promise of results.

## What is meant by Vastu of the production and manufacturing area?

It means applying the traditional Vastu principles to the planning, layout and running of production facilities, manufacturing areas and assembly spaces. Vastu Shastra applies to every kind of built space, but this branch pays particular attention to factories and industrial spaces. Unlike the Vastu of an ordinary building, industrial Vastu takes into account the special work of a production area, its machines, the flow of work and its energy needs. The aim is to balance the five elements (earth, water, fire, air and space) and the energy of the directions, so that work is efficient and successful.

## Key aspects

### Where machines go

- The place of a machine should be decided by its function, not only by its weight.
- For example, heating equipment such as boilers, furnaces and generators is kept in the south-east, which tradition calls the zone of fire. This place is held to be right for safety and smooth working.
- Machines for crushing or heavy striking work, such as a stone crusher, can be placed in the north-west. It is believed to match the energy of Rudra in the Vastu Purusha Mandala.
- Avoid placing heavy machines in the north-east or in the central part. According to tradition this obstructs the flow of good energy and may bring hindrances in the work.

### Seating and direction of staff

- The cabins or offices of the owner and senior managers should ideally be in the south-west, with the face towards the east. According to tradition this keeps control in place.
- It is regarded as good to carry out administrative work in the north or east parts of the factory.
- It is regarded as good for general staff and assembly line workers to sit in the north or to face the north-east, for concentration and pace of work. However, the right direction depends on the role. For people connected with sales, the south-east (to close a deal) or the north (for new opportunities to come) is regarded as favourable.

### Energy flow, layout and zoning

- The ideal factory plot should have a gentle slope towards the north or the north-east. Tradition regards this as good for growth and prosperity.
- Keep the entrance in the north or the east, so that energy arrives properly.
- Finished goods are better kept in the north-west or the west, while raw material should be kept in the east or the south-east.
- The production process should move clockwise. It is believed to resemble the flow of the cosmos and to keep the work cycle smooth.
- Having a temple or place of prayer in the north-east is said in tradition to bring spiritual energy and a good atmosphere in the workplace.

## How to apply this in practice

Vastu has no numerical scoring for a factory. It has clear rules of direction and element. The main steps are as follows.

- Draw the layout of the factory according to the Vastu Purusha Mandala.
- Identify the places of the main items, such as the entrance, the machines, the stores, the staff seating and the power and water arrangements.
- Decide the function of each zone on the basis of the five elements and the directions.
- If needed, change the physical layout, the slope and the division of space, so that the flow of energy is balanced.

## Reading the rules

Check whether the present items in the factory match the places given.

- The south-east heating zone: when heating equipment is in the right place, the process is regarded as safe and efficient and breakdowns of machines are held to be fewer.
- Cleanliness of the north-east: this part should be open and clean, and it is good to have the prayer place here. Tradition speaks of confusion or a standstill in the work if this part is obstructed.
- Heavy machinery: placing it in the south or the south-west is regarded as giving stability and strength in operation. Placing it in the north or the north-east is said to cause financial or technical problems.
- Direction of staff: when the seating is right, attention, enthusiasm and the overall atmosphere of the company are said to be good.

## Practical applications

### Raising output and cutting faults

- According to tradition, following the Vastu rules lessens the chance of machine breakdowns and accidents at work, so production of better quality with fewer faults is possible.
- The right place for production lines and assembly areas keeps the flow of work in order, so that both speed and quality may improve.

### Worker welfare and business growth

- A right seating direction and a comfortable division of space help to keep the health, morale and efficiency of the workers. Absence may be less and output greater.
- Entrances and open spaces made according to Vastu are regarded as drawing opportunities and prosperity, and as helping the steady growth of the business.

## Common misconceptions

### The south-west is the only place for machines

This is not right. Heaviness in the south-west is regarded as good, but equipment should be kept according to its function, for example heating equipment in the south-east, not merely to balance weight.

### One rule applies to everyone

Factory Vastu should be adapted to the type of industry and its special processes. Running the same layout everywhere may bring inefficiency.

### Vastu is superstition

It is a traditional system. Well-thought-out spaces can help the flow of work, safety and morale, and on this basis Vastu links the old tradition with modern industrial layout. No study is named here in support of it.

## Examples

These are plain illustrations, not records of outcomes.

- Example 1: in a textile factory the boilers broke down often. According to Vastu the heating equipment was moved to the south-east, the zone of fire. It was reported that faults became fewer and production ran smoothly.
- Example 2: in a metalworking factory the seating of the assembly line workers was changed to face north, and the owner's cabin was moved to the south-west, so that the authority of leadership and positive energy would be kept. It was reported that the efficiency of the workers improved and absence fell.

## Conclusion

According to Vastu tradition, Vastu of the production and manufacturing area is a way to make a factory efficient, prosperous and harmonious. By placing machines thoughtfully, deciding the direction of the staff and keeping the flow of energy right, steadier quality of production, fewer faults and growth over the long term are hoped for. For industrialists these guidelines are a traditional way of linking physical space with cosmic order and the productivity of work. They work alongside engineering and safety practice, never in place of them.

## FAQ

### Where should boilers and furnaces be installed in a factory?

In the south-east, which tradition regards as the zone of fire. Heat-producing equipment such as generators is also kept here.

### In which direction should the owner's cabin be?

The cabins of the owner and senior managers should ideally be in the south-west, with the face towards the east.

### Which side should the factory entrance be on?

In the north or the east, so that energy arrives properly. A gentle slope of the plot towards the north or the north-east is regarded as good.

### Where should raw material and finished goods be kept?

Raw material is regarded as better kept in the east or the south-east, and finished goods in the north-west or the west.

### Should all heavy machines be placed in the south-west?

No. The place of a machine depends on its work. Heat-producing machines are kept in the south-east. Heavy machines should not be placed in the north-east or the central part.

### How should one begin?

Look at the layout of your factory and note the places of the entrance, machines, stores and staff. Compare them with these guidelines and make changes slowly and thoughtfully. If needed, take advice from a Vastu practitioner.
