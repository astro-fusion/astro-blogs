# Using the Brahmasthan Well: Understanding the Home's Centre

## Introduction to the Brahmasthan

In Vastu Shastra the Brahmasthan is regarded as the spiritual and energetic centre of a building. This central zone is named after the creator deity, Lord Brahma. According to Vastu tradition, the energies of all eight directions meet at this place.

General Vastu knowledge says that this place should be kept open and free of obstruction. This article discusses a few ideas that go a little further, which suggest using the centre with thought instead of only leaving it empty. They include octagonal plans, circular seating arrangements, sacred geometric patterns, courtyards and natural materials.

## The Importance of the Brahmasthan

Based on the ancient Vedic texts, the Brahmasthan is believed to receive cosmic energy and distribute it through the whole building. The tradition holds that its influence extends to the physical, mental and financial life of the residents. In a balanced Brahmasthan the flow of prana, the life force, is regarded as smooth. A flaw in this part is said to bring stagnation, quarrels and misfortune.

This way of thinking stresses not only protecting the Brahmasthan but keeping it open, light and balanced. For that it is considered necessary to understand the geometry of the space, the materials and the relation between the five elements, the Pancha Bhutas.

## Ways of Using the Brahmasthan

### 1. Octagonal and Circular Plans

In Vastu, square and rectangular plots are generally regarded as the best. Some ideas nevertheless describe octagonal and circular forms as useful for the Brahmasthan.

- **Octagonal plan**: an octagon makes a clear and protected space at the centre. It is believed that this shape divides energy equally in all eight directions. The angled walls are regarded as reducing the stagnation that can gather in sharp corners.
- **Circular seating arrangement**: even in a square or rectangular Brahmasthan, furniture can be arranged in a circle or a half circle. This is regarded as helping the flow of energy and of conversation. It is said that furniture in a straight line can create a face-to-face, confrontational mood.

### 2. Sacred Geometric Patterns

- **Mandalas**: the Vastu Purusha Mandala is regarded as a diagram of cosmic energy, and a mandala is the traditional pattern at the centre of a building.
- **Patterns in the floor**: a flower or lotus mandala pattern can be made in the floor of the Brahmasthan, using contrasting materials such as marble and brass. The tradition regards this as keeping the energy auspicious.

### 3. The Vertical Axis: The Role of the Courtyard

In a multi-storey home the Brahmasthan runs upwards through all the floors.

- **Central courtyard and skylight**: a courtyard open to the sky above the Brahmasthan, or a large skylight, is regarded as a way of connecting with the space (Akasha) element. It brings natural light and openness to the middle of the house. Any opening in a roof or floor slab must be designed and checked by a licensed engineer.
- **Water fountain**: the tradition speaks of a small, quiet water fountain towards the north-east of the Brahmasthan. The gentle sound of flowing water is regarded as calming. The water should always be clean and flowing.

### 4. Materials and the Balance of Elements

Vastu also pays attention to the materials used in the Brahmasthan.

- **Natural materials**: natural, breathable materials such as wood, stone and lime plaster are believed to strengthen the connection with the earth element and give a feeling of stability. The tradition advises avoiding artificial materials.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the biggest mistake people make with the Brahmasthan?

The commonest mistake is to build heavy structures such as pillars, staircases or thick walls in the Brahmasthan. Vastu treats this as a major defect, because it blocks the flow of energy at the centre.

### Can I put a dining table in the Brahmasthan?

Yes. A dining table is one of the few accepted uses of the Brahmasthan, provided it is light and, preferably, round or oval. Eating together as a family is regarded as building a feeling of unity. The area must always be kept clean and tidy.

### What can I do if there is a toilet in the Brahmasthan?

Vastu treats a toilet in the Brahmasthan as a major defect. The best solution is to move it elsewhere. When this is not possible, it is sensible to consult an experienced Vastu consultant and a licensed plumber or engineer.

### Which colours are considered suitable for the Brahmasthan?

Light colours such as white, cream, beige or very pale yellow and blue are regarded as suitable, because they give a feeling of openness and purity. It is advised to avoid heavy colours such as black, dark grey or deep red, which are said to make the space heavy and stagnant.

## Conclusion

The Brahmasthan is the centre of Vastu. Going beyond the basic advice of keeping it empty, one can think about an octagonal plan, sacred geometry, a courtyard and natural materials. These are beliefs of the Vastu tradition and not a promise of any result. Before adopting them in your own home, it is good to take advice from an experienced Vastu consultant.
