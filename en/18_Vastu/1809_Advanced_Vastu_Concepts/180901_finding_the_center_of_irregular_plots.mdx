# How to Find the Brahmasthan of an Irregular Plot

## The Challenge of Irregular Plots

In an ideal world every plot would be a perfect square or rectangle, and applying Vastu Shastra would be straightforward. In modern towns and cities, plots are often irregular, which is a real difficulty for home owners and architects. One of the most important tasks in Vastu is to identify the Brahmasthan, the spiritual centre of the property.

Vastu tradition holds that a wrongly identified Brahmasthan leads to Vastu doshas (defects) and disturbs the flow of energy in the property. This article describes traditional and modern ways of locating the centre of a plot of any shape.

## What the Brahmasthan Is and Why It Matters

The Brahmasthan is the centre of the plot. It is regarded as the abode of Lord Brahma, the creator. According to tradition, prana, the life force, spreads from this point in all directions and energises the whole property. Vastu principles say that this central zone should be kept open, clean and free of heavy structures or obstructions, and its sanctity is placed above everything else.

- **Energetic core**: the Brahmasthan is called the "cosmic lung" of the house, which takes in positive energy and distributes it.
- **Source of stability**: Vastu tradition links a balanced Brahmasthan with structural, financial and emotional stability.
- **Health and harmony**: an undisturbed centre is believed to support harmony among family members.

In the Vastu tradition any obstruction at the Brahmasthan is treated as a serious defect, which is why identifying it correctly is the first step of a Vastu analysis.

## Methods for Finding the Centre of an Irregular Plot

The centre of a square or rectangle is simply where its two diagonals cross. An irregular plot needs a more careful method.

### 1. The Triangulation and Geometric Method

This is a traditional technique that breaks a complex shape into simple geometric forms.

1. **Make a scaled drawing**: take the exact dimensions of the plot and draw it to scale on paper or on a digital canvas.
2. **Divide it into triangles**: split the whole irregular shape into several triangles that do not overlap. The more complex the shape, the more triangles you will have.
3. **Find the centroid of each triangle**: the centroid is the point where the three medians meet. A median is a line from a corner to the midpoint of the opposite side.
4. **Calculate the weighted average**: the Brahmasthan of the whole plot is the weighted average of the centroids of all the triangles. For this you work out the area of each triangle and then find the centre of mass of the entire shape. The method gives a very accurate geometric centre.

### 2. The Centre of Gravity (Cardboard) Method

This practical method lets you see the balance point of the plot with your own eyes.

1. **Make a model**: draw the plot to scale on a sturdy, flat material such as cardboard or thin plywood.
2. **Cut it out**: cut out the shape of the plot carefully.
3. **Find the balance point**: try to balance the cutout on a sharp point such as a pin or the tip of a pen. The one point where it balances is its centre of gravity, and this point is the Brahmasthan.

The physical method is surprisingly accurate and gives a tangible way to understand the centre of the plot.

### 3. Modern Software (CAD)

For those who want precision, Computer-Aided Design (CAD) software such as AutoCAD gives the most accurate answer.

1. **Draw the plot**: recreate the exact dimensions of the plot in the software.
2. **Use the centroid function**: the built-in functions, often called Mass Properties, Centroid or Center of Gravity, calculate the geometric centre of the shape automatically and precisely.

This method removes human error and suits very complex or valuable properties.

## Vastu Rules for the Brahmasthan

Once the centre is found, it should be treated with respect. The main guidelines are these.

- **Keep it open**: avoid building walls, pillars or staircases in this zone.
- **No heavy furniture**: do not place heavy objects such as safes, machinery or large storage units here.
- **Avoid toilets and kitchens**: a toilet, kitchen or septic tank at the Brahmasthan is considered a major Vastu defect.
- **Keep it clean**: the area should always be clean and free of clutter.
- **Suitable use**: a courtyard, an open hall, or a small, light element such as a plant or a small water feature is considered acceptable.

## Traditional Remedies for an Irregular Plot

Finding the centre is only the first step. The next is to correct the imbalance that the shape of the plot is said to create.

- **Create a regular shape**: the most effective remedy in the tradition is to build a compound wall, a "Vastu fence", that forms a regular square or rectangular plot inside the irregular one. The main construction is then placed within this regular shape. The leftover odd-shaped areas can be used for a garden, parking or utility structures.
- **Level the plot**: the plot should be properly levelled. Ideally it should slope gently from the south-west down towards the north-east. If the middle of the plot is raised, earth from it can be moved to the south-west to achieve this balance.
- **Consult an expert**: for a severe irregularity, speak to a qualified Vastu consultant, and have a licensed engineer or architect check levels, boundaries and any structural question before you build.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What if a corner of my plot is cut or extended?

A cut corner is regarded as a missing element, while an extended corner can cause an imbalance of elements. For example, a cut in the north-east, the water corner, is considered inauspicious in Vastu tradition. An extension in the north-east is considered good, but an extension in other corners such as the south-east or south-west is said to need correction. The way to do this is the one described above: build a compound wall that forms a regular shape.

### Can I keep a Tulsi plant at the Brahmasthan?

Yes. Tulsi (holy basil) is considered very auspicious and light, and keeping it at the Brahmasthan, especially in a courtyard, is acceptable in the tradition.

### My plot is circular. How do I apply Vastu?

Vastu generally does not recommend a circular plot for a home, because it is said to be unsettled. Its centre is easy to find, but the energy there is considered unstable. It is better to create a square or rectangular living space inside the circular plot.

### Does the method change for a multi-storey building?

The Brahmasthan is fixed from the ground floor plan. In a multi-storey building, however, the Brahmasthan on every floor should also be kept open and free of heavy structures, so that the vertical flow stays clear.

## Conclusion

A perfectly regular plot is the Vastu ideal, but modern land is often irregular. Finding the Brahmasthan of an irregular plot is the foundation of a sound Vastu analysis. Traditional geometry, the practical cardboard method or modern software can all locate the centre accurately.

Identifying the Brahmasthan is only the beginning. Keeping it open and clean, and correcting the irregularities of the plot, matter just as much. Approach Vastu with respect and, when in doubt, ask a qualified professional.
