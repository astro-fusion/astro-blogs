# Vastu for High-Rise Buildings: Skyscraper Design Ideas

## Introduction

The principles of Vastu Shastra rest on balancing the five elements and living in harmony with the Earth's magnetic field. Applying them to the design of modern skyscrapers is the hardest challenge. A very tall building meets the elements, especially air and space, far more intensely than an ordinary building. So some extra points are added to the usual Vastu rules, so that a building can be thought of as stable in the Vastu sense as well as structurally.

This article sets out the Vastu tradition on subjects such as stepped massing, the placement of skybridges and the handling of energy at the roof. These are beliefs. They cannot take the place of engineering. The structure of a high-rise, including its foundation, wind load and safety, is the work of the structural engineer.

## The Particular Challenges of Skyscrapers

- **A weaker link with the earth element**: According to Vastu tradition, the higher the floor, the weaker its link with the steadying energy of the earth element (Prithvi). People on the upper floors are said to be able to feel unsteadiness, restlessness and a sense of being cut off from their roots.
- **An excess of air and space**: Skyscrapers stand under heavy wind pressure and are surrounded by open sky. Tradition speaks of an excess of these elements as bringing a restless mind, poor concentration and scattered energy.
- **Energy that collects**: Tall, pointed buildings are thought of as acting like antennas that draw energy from the sky. The belief is that if this energy is not let out properly, it stays and weighs on the whole building.

## Vastu Principles for Skyscrapers

### 1. Stepped massing: giving the building a steady base

One main technique for making a building steady is stepped massing, in which the building grows lighter and narrower as it rises.

- **Heavier lower floors**: Heavier massing and materials are kept in the lower floors. This means more stone and concrete, a wide base, and uses that need stability, such as commercial space, parking and the building's services. This strong base links the building with the energy of the earth.
- **Lighter upper floors**: The building should be lighter as it goes up. This can be done with a narrower shape, more glass, and terraces and open spaces. It is like the natural shape of a mountain, which is wide at the bottom and pointed at the peak. In Vastu such a shape is considered stable by nature.

### 2. Careful placement of skybridges

Skybridges are now common in projects with several towers. In Vastu tradition, their position is considered important.

- **Auspicious pairings**: It is good if a skybridge joins buildings that stand in auspicious directions. If a heavy, stable tower in the south-west (Nairitya) is joined with a light, spiritual tower in the north-east (Ishanya), the flow of energy is regarded as good.
- **Pairings to avoid**: A skybridge joining the north-west (Vayavya, air element) and south-east (Agneya, fire element) should be avoided. Tradition holds that this makes an unstable fire and wind tunnel, which it links with conflict, accidents and instability for the businesses or residents it connects. The height and materials of the skybridge should also be considered so that the overall balance of the elements is not upset.

### 3. The roof

The roof of a skyscraper is regarded as its most active point, where cosmic energy is said to meet.

- **The problem with a pointed roof**: It is believed that a plain, pointed roof (shikhar) can gather and concentrate energy like a needle. That can be stressful for people on the top floors.
- **A rooftop garden**: A rooftop garden with flowing water in the north-east part of the roof is also regarded as calming and balancing the strong energy at the top of the building.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which floors are better for living or working in a tall building, according to Vastu?

The lower to middle floors are generally considered better, because their link with the earth element is stronger, and tradition speaks of steadiness and peace there. If you have to live on a very high floor, pay more attention to Vastu inside your flat or office. The advice is to emphasise steadying elements in the south-west, such as earthy colours and heavy furniture.

### Does the shape of the building matter?

Yes. A plain square or rectangular base is always regarded as ideal. Complex and irregular shapes are thought to make the flow of energy disorderly. The whole design should be symmetrical and balanced.

### Where should the building's water tanks be?

The main water tanks of the building should be in the south-west or west part of the roof, so that the expected heavy energy of that zone is kept. A smaller second source of water for drinking, or a rooftop pool, is considered better in the north-east. Where tanks actually go is also settled by the engineer, because of the weight and the plumbing.

### How can Vastu faults be corrected after the building is complete?

Major structural faults are very hard to correct. The Vastu inside each flat or office can, however, be improved a good deal through colours, materials and the arrangement of furnishings, so as to balance the influence of the large building.

## Conclusion

Vastu for high-rise buildings is a special subject within modern architecture. Architects and builders who follow Vastu tradition can use stepped massing, careful skybridge placement and thought about the roof to build towers that are not only feats of engineering but also give a balanced, steady atmosphere. As cities grow upward, the old teaching of Vastu Shastra offers one way of building vertical communities on a base of balance. It remains a tradition, and the structural safety of the building stays with the engineer.
