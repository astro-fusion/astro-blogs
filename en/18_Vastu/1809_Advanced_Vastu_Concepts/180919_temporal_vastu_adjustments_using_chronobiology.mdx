# Vastu and Time: Matching Your Home to the Day and Season

## Introduction

Vastu Shastra has traditionally attended to the three dimensions of space. It aligns a home with the directions and balances the five elements. An idea now being discussed adds a fourth dimension to this: time. It can be called time-based Vastu, and it links Vastu principles with chronobiology, the study of the body's internal clocks.

This idea holds that the energy of a place does not stay fixed but changes with the cycle of the day and of the seasons. By making small adjustments in the home to follow these changes, a person can create an atmosphere that fits their natural daily routine. It is a modern idea built on Vastu tradition, and it makes no promise of results.

## Chronobiology and the Vastu View

Vastu tradition regards people as creatures of cycles, deeply affected by the cycle of sunlight and darkness. The body is described as made to do different work at different times of the day.

- **Morning**: Cortisol is higher and alertness is greater. It is regarded as a good time for concentration and activity.
- **Afternoon**: Coordination and speed of reaction are described as best.
- **Evening**: The body begins to make melatonin and prepares for sleep.

From this understanding comes the picture of a home planned with thought not only for direction but also for time.

## Using Space by the Hour: A Corner for Each Part of the Day

The core of the idea is that the use of a room can change during the day, so that it suits the strongest energy of that time. It moves away from the fixed thought that "this room is the bedroom" to a more flexible way.

- **Morning (Brahma Muhurta, about 4 AM to 6 AM)**: According to tradition, the north-east (Ishanya) corner of the house is filled with the most spiritual, sattvic energy. This is the right time and place for meditation, yoga or prayer. A corner that is a quiet reading spot in the day can become a place of worship in the morning.
- **Forenoon (9 AM to 12 noon)**: The south-east (Agneya) zone, which belongs to the fire element, is regarded as the most active. It is the time for work linked with vigour and digestion, such as cooking the main meal of the day.
- **Evening (after 6 PM)**: The south-west (Nairitya) zone, linked with rest and relationships, comes into the centre. It is the time to sit with the family, relax and wind down the day.

## Movable Partitions: Architecture for Time

Some modern architectural measures are thought of for this flexible use of space.

- **Sliding walls and movable partitions**: Light, removable partitions can make flexible spaces. A large open living room can be divided in the morning to make a separate meditation corner in the north-east. In the evening the partitions can be moved again to make an intimate dining place in the west.
- **Smart glass**: Glass that changes from clear to frosted at the press of a button can be used in windows or inside partitions. A zone can then be made private at once, without building a solid wall.

## Vastu Muhurta: Timing the Start of Work

Muhurta is the Vedic tradition of choosing an auspicious time to begin a task. Time-based Vastu applies this principle to the routine inside the home.

- **Starting new work**: It is considered better to begin a new project or an important task at an auspicious muhurta, facing north or east.
- **Deep cleaning and clearing clutter**: It is considered good to do this on a Saturday. Saturday is the day of Saturn, who is regarded as the ruler of order and discipline. Tradition calls it a time for removing old, stagnant energy.

## Seasonal Changes in Vastu

The belief is that the energy of a home also changes with the seasons. The traditional way of marking this is by the two courses of the sun.

- **Uttarayana**: This is the sun's northward course, which begins at the winter solstice and runs roughly from mid-January to mid-July. Tradition holds that the energy is then more active and fiery. During this time, use the north and east parts of the house more, and keep cooler colours such as blues and greens in the decoration.
- **Dakshinayana**: This is the sun's southward course, roughly from mid-July to mid-January. Tradition holds that the energy is then more inward and calm. During this time, spend more time in the south and west parts of the house, and use warm colours such as red, orange and yellow to wake the fire element.

The match of these two courses with the hot and cold seasons is only approximate, so use your own local weather as the guide for what feels comfortable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### This sounds complicated. How can I start simply?

Begin by noticing your own energy through the day, and try to fit your tasks to your natural rhythm. Have your morning tea in the east part of the house, where you get the morning sun. In the evening, wind down in a comfortable chair in the south-west. You do not need movable walls to start. You only need to change where you yourself sit.

### How does artificial lighting relate to this?

It is relevant. Smart lights that can change colour temperature can be used. They can be set to be bright and cool-toned in the morning and warm and dim in the evening, which suits the natural rhythm of day and night. Light and colour are not medical treatment.

### Can this improve my sleep?

Keeping your routine and your light in step with the sun's cycle may make it easier to fall asleep at night, but this cannot be assured. Vastu advises that the bedroom be in the south-west, south or west, and that it be dark and quiet at sleeping time.

### What if my home's plan is fixed and has no such flexibility?

You can follow these ideas even in a fixed plan. Set aside particular corners of a room for tasks at particular times. For example, a yoga mat can be rolled out in the north-east corner of the living room in the morning and put away afterwards. The main thing is work done at the right time, in the right direction, with attention.

## Conclusion

Time-based Vastu is a reminder that we live not only in space but also in time. By staying aware of the rhythm of the day and the seasons, and by making small changes in the home and in our habits, a way of living can be built that fits the rhythm of nature. It is a modern idea based on Vastu tradition. Do not take it as an assurance of health or happiness.
