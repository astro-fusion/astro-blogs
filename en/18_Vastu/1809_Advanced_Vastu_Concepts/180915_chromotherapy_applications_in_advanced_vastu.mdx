# Colour and Light in Vastu: Lighting Through the Day

## Introduction: The Role of Colour in Vastu

Vastu Shastra holds that colour has a deep effect on the energy of a space. Tradition has long named fixed colours for the different directions, to balance the five elements. These colours are permanent. Some present-day ideas carry this further and think of light that changes colour with the time of day.

This idea goes beyond suggesting the same colour for everyone. It uses devices such as smart LED bulbs to change the colour of a room's light according to the time of day. Smart lighting is a modern idea and not part of classical Vastu. This article introduces it without claiming any health benefit.

## Understanding Colour and Light

Every colour is a wavelength of visible light. In this Vastu way of thinking, each colour is linked with the energy of an element, so that the atmosphere of a home is not just attractive but also restful.

Common experience is that a cool white light with a bluish tint feels alert and awake, while a warm yellow-orange light creates a relaxed mood in the evening.

## Colour Through the Day

Here the light is arranged to imitate the sun's movement through the day, so that the indoor atmosphere follows the natural cycle outside. This is the basic layer of the idea.

- **Morning (6 AM to 9 AM)**: A cool, bright white light with a slight bluish tint. In the Ayurvedic tradition, this is linked with the Kapha period of the morning. It is believed to reduce drowsiness and prepare one for the day. It is associated with the east, the zone of new beginnings.
- **Midday (10 AM to 2 PM)**: The light becomes a more neutral white. It is linked with the Pitta period. It is considered suitable for work without too much stimulation.
- **Evening (from 6 PM)**: A warm, dim, soft yellow-orange and soft white light with no bluish tint. It is considered suitable for rest and for getting ready for sleep. It is linked with the calm energy of the west and the south-west (Nairitya).

## Directions and Colours in Tradition

Tradition links some colours with directions and planets. Green is associated with the north (Mercury). Violet or deep blue is linked with the north-east (Ishanya), the zone of spirituality, which is why these colours are mentioned for meditation. A light green or soft blue for a moment of calm belongs to the same tradition. All of this is traditional belief.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a full smart-home system for this?

No. You can begin with simple tools. Smart bulbs controlled from a phone app are easy to find. You can also change the colour and brightness by hand according to the time of day.

### Is this different from an ordinary coloured bulb?

An ordinary coloured bulb gives one colour, often a deep one. Smart LED lighting lets you change the colour and brightness in fine steps. That is the difference on which this idea rests.

### Can it be used to treat health problems?

No. Light and colour are not medical treatment, and this Vastu tradition of colour makes no claim to cure. If you have a problem with sleep or mood, consult a doctor.

### Where should I begin?

The bedroom is a sensible place to start, because the sleep cycle is fundamental to our health. A simple start is to keep the bedroom light warm and dim in the evening, after sunset, and to switch on brighter light when you get up in the morning.

## Conclusion

The Vastu tradition of colour has moved from fixed colours to the idea of changing light. It tries to keep a home's lighting in step with the daily cycle and the elements of the directions. The idea combines Vastu tradition with modern devices, and it is not a promise of any result. When you use it at home, let your own comfort and convenience be the measure.
