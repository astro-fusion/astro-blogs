# Corporate Vastu: Office Departments and Workstation Layout

## Introduction

Vastu Shastra has been used for centuries for the atmosphere of homes. Many companies now also ask for Vastu advice on how an office is laid out. This article uses two illustrations to explain how Vastu tradition thinks about dividing an office into departments and arranging the seats of employees.

The illustrations are only there to explain. No proven figures are given with them, and no definite benefit from Vastu is promised. They are ways of looking at an office according to Vastu tradition.

## Illustration 1: A Large Technology Company

**The problem**: Suppose employees in a large technology company are very tired, nothing new gets made despite good people, and there is friction between departments. The headquarters is a modern open-plan office, but its atmosphere feels disorderly and unproductive.

**The Vastu review**: From the viewpoint of Vastu tradition, the review notes a few things.

- The Research and Development (R&D) department, the centre of innovation, is in the south-west (Nairitya). This is the direction of stability, and it is linked with new ideas staying where they are.
- The Finance and Accounts department is in the north-west (Vayavya), the zone of movement. Tradition links this with financial instability and an uncertain flow of cash.
- The CEO's room is in the south-east (Agneya), the fire zone. It is believed that decisions there become aggressive and often hasty.

**The Vastu suggestion: placing departments by direction**

The main point is that each department is placed again according to its work and the elements of the directions.

1. **R&D and innovation in the north-east (Ishanya)**: The north-east is the zone of the mind, clarity and new ideas, and is linked with the water element. The whole R&D department would be kept in this part. The surroundings would be open and airy, and a small water feature could be placed there for the flow of creative thought.
2. **Finance and Accounts in the north (the direction of Kubera)**: The north is regarded as the direction of wealth, and Mercury, the planet of trade, is linked with it as its lord. The finance department would be kept here. In decoration, green, the colour of Mercury, and metal elements are regarded in tradition as favourable for wealth.
3. **The CEO's room in the south-west**: The south-west is the zone of the earth element, which gives stability, dignity and control. It is believed that this keeps leadership steady and supports long-term thinking.
4. **Marketing and Sales in the north-west (Vayavya)**: The north-west is the zone of the air element and is linked with movement and communication. Keeping the sales and marketing teams here is regarded as favourable for their contacts and for the pace of selling.

**The outcome of this suggestion**: No proven figure is given here. According to tradition, the aim is to give each department the direction that suits its nature.

## Illustration 2: Arranging Workstations

**The problem**: Suppose employees of a financial services call-centre company have low morale, make many mistakes and are often absent. The workplace is laid out in dense, identical rows.

**The Vastu suggestion: attention to each seat**

This time the attention goes to the arrangement of each employee's seat.

1. **Facing an auspicious direction**: All seats are placed so that the employee faces north or east. According to tradition, facing north increases concentration, and facing east is linked with health and creativity.
2. **Removing the unease of having one's back to the door**: Nobody's back should be directly to the main aisle or a door. In such a position a person can feel unprotected and uneasy without knowing why.
3. **A solid wall behind**: Where possible, seat the employee with a solid wall behind. It gives a feeling of support and steadiness.
4. **Controlling cables and mess**: Keep a rule on every desk to keep cables tidy and clear away unneeded items, because Vastu tradition regards clutter as an obstacle to the flow of positive energy.

**The outcome of this suggestion**: No measured result is given here either. All that can be said is that the aim of improving the seating is to raise each person's concentration and ease.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are these changes not just ordinary good management and design?

Some points, such as a solid wall behind an employee, also agree with modern ergonomics and psychology (for example the prospect and refuge idea). Vastu, however, offers a whole framework for them and gives its reasons in terms of energy. Placing whole departments by direction is a feature of Vastu itself, and it goes beyond ordinary design.

### Will every company get such results?

No result can be promised. The principles are the same everywhere, but how they work out in practice depends on the existing plan, how serious the Vastu faults are and how fully the management is willing to carry out the changes.

### What should a company do first if it wants to try corporate Vastu?

The first step is to have the premises reviewed by a qualified, experienced corporate Vastu consultant. The consultant can identify the strengths and weaknesses of the workplace and suggest a plan for improvement.

### Do the departments have to move to follow this?

Not necessarily. Moving a department is a large decision with a real cost, and it is for the management to weigh against how the business actually works. Smaller steps, such as the seating directions, a solid wall behind each desk and tidy cables, are easy to try on their own.

## Conclusion

Corporate Vastu looks at an office on a large scale, in how departments are placed, and on a small scale, in the seat of each employee. According to tradition, it is an effort to make the workplace fit the directions and elements. It is best seen not as a promise of business success or productivity but as a traditional view of an orderly, thoughtfully arranged environment.
