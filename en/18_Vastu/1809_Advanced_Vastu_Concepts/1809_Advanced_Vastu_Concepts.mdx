# Advanced Vastu Concepts: A Guide for the Modern Home

## Introduction

Vastu Shastra is the ancient Indian tradition of architecture, and for centuries it has guided the making of harmonious places to live. Many people know the basic rules of Vastu, but beyond them lies a deeper and finer system that deals with the management of energy. This article introduces advanced Vastu concepts that relate to the challenges of modern architecture, such as irregularly shaped plots, tall buildings and smart homes.

All of this consists of beliefs of the Vastu tradition and of modern suggestions. Take them as guidance and not as rules. They do not promise any result.

## 1. Finding the Centre of an Irregular Plot

The Brahmasthan, the energy centre of a plot, is regarded as the heart of a building that follows Vastu. In a plot that is perfectly square or rectangular, the Brahmasthan is easy to find: draw the two diagonals. Modern town planning, however, often produces irregularly shaped plots, and finding the centre in them needs some more advanced methods.

### The Triangulation Method

To find the Brahmasthan of an irregular plot, the triangulation method is used. The plot is divided into a series of triangles, the centroid of each triangle is found, and then the weighted average of these centroids is calculated to fix the true centre of the plot. This identifies the Brahmasthan accurately and helps in deciding the right position of the building.

### The Importance of the Brahmasthan

The Brahmasthan is regarded as a sacred space and should be kept open, free of any structural blockage. By identifying it correctly in an irregular plot, the centre can be kept undisturbed, so that, in the tradition's view, positive energy moves freely through the property.

## 2. Door Balancing Theory and Its Use

In Vastu, doors are regarded as the main routes by which energy comes and goes. The door balancing theory aims at a harmonious flow of energy by paying attention to the position, size and manner of opening of all the doors of a building.

### Main Points of Door Balancing

- **Ratio of doors to windows**: A ratio of 3:5 between the total area of doors and the total area of windows is suggested for a balanced flow. This ratio is a popular suggestion and not a rule laid down in any ancient text.
- **Doors opening clockwise**: It is considered good for a door to open clockwise, so that it welcomes positive energy.
- **Avoid a straight line**: The main entrance and exit doors should not lie in one straight line, because in that case energy is believed to leave the property quickly.

### Use in Modern Homes

In today's open-plan homes, door balancing can be applied with light measures such as screens, partitions or the arrangement of furniture. These make a slow, winding path for energy.

## 3. Careful Placement of Utilities in Modern Homes

Water tanks, electrical panels and air-conditioning are necessary for modern life. According to Vastu tradition, if they are not placed correctly, they are regarded as obstructing the energy of the home.

### Harmony by Element

- **Water tanks**: To link them with the stability of the earth element, it is advised to keep them in the south-west (Nairitya) zone. This is the usual suggestion for an overhead roof tank.
- **Electrical panels**: The south-east (Agneya) zone, the zone of the fire element, is regarded as right for electrical panels and inverters.
- **Air-conditioning**: The north-west (Vayavya) zone, the zone of the air element, is regarded as the most suitable for air-conditioner units and exhaust fans.

### Keeping Electromagnetic Fields (EMF) Low

Along with the elements, it is good to keep the EMF from electrical systems low. For this, attention can be given to shielded cables, a safe distance between electrical panels and living areas, and proper earthing of all electrical appliances.

## 4. Flow of Energy in Multi-Storey Buildings

Applying Vastu to multi-storey buildings has its own challenges. Three-dimensional Vastu looks at the flow of energy in the vertical direction, so that each floor stays in step with the others.

### Main Points for Multi-Storey Buildings

- **Position of staircases**: Staircases should be in the south or west part of the building and should be built to turn clockwise as one goes up.
- **Position of lifts**: The lift shaft should not cut through any important energy centre, and its door should not face the main entrance of a floor directly.
- **Agreement between floors**: The position of heavy furniture and structural parts should be decided with care, so that no imbalance is created between floors.

## 5. Vastu for Small Urban Flats

Applying Vastu in a small urban flat needs creativity and flexibility. The attention goes to making the fullest use of the space available while still respecting the basic principles of Vastu.

### Measures for Small Spaces

- **Convertible furniture**: Furniture that serves more than one purpose and is easy to rearrange keeps a room open and free of clutter.
- **Mirrored walls**: Mirrors placed with thought make a space look larger and can be used to reduce a structural imbalance to the eye.
- **Several zones in one space**: Screens, rugs or lighting can mark out areas for different tasks in a single room, which gives a feeling of order and harmony.

## 6. Non-Invasive Measures for Structural Faults

When the structure cannot be changed, non-invasive measures are mentioned for correcting Vastu faults. Tradition holds that these lighten inauspicious energy and increase the flow of positive energy. Understand them only as traditional beliefs. None of them requires buying costly items.

### Ways of Lightening Energy

- **Wind chimes**: Their gentle sound is believed to break up stagnant energy and make the atmosphere pleasant.
- **Use of colour**: Particular colours are chosen in the decoration to balance the energy of the elements.

## 7. Joining Vastu with Contemporary Architecture

There is a growing trend of joining Vastu with contemporary architecture, because architects and designers want to make places that are both beautiful and balanced. This mixed approach joins an understanding of modern design with the old principles of Vastu.

### Parametric Modelling and Vastu Compliance

Advanced software such as parametric modelling can produce complex building forms that also follow Vastu. With these tools, architects can run simulations and make quick changes to see whether a design suits the principles of Vastu.

### Choice of Materials

The choice of materials is another important part of this combination. Natural materials such as wood, stone and bamboo are preferred, because they are regarded as good for a healthy, pleasant atmosphere.

## 8. Advanced Ways of Using the Brahmasthan

The Brahmasthan should be kept open and free of structural elements, but some advanced ways are also mentioned for raising its positive energy.

### Octagonal Floor Plans

An octagonal plan is regarded as very auspicious in Vastu, because it is said to carry the energy of the Brahmasthan equally to all corners.

### Rotating Furniture Arrangements

Placing furniture in a circular or rotating order around the Brahmasthan is believed to raise its energy and make the atmosphere of the home livelier.

### Symbols of Sacred Geometry

There is also said to be a tradition of installing symbols of sacred geometry such as the Sri Yantra in the Brahmasthan. The belief is that this makes a centre of positive vibrations there.

## 9. Vastu for Mixed-Use Projects

Projects in which commercial and residential spaces are together need a different approach in Vastu. The basic point is that there should be a clear separation of energy between the two kinds of space, and that both should be in harmony with the whole structure.

### Buffer Zones in Between

Making buffer zones, such as landscaped courtyards or water features, between the commercial and residential parts is believed to stop their energies from mixing.

### Separate Entrances

Separate entrances for the commercial and residential sections are described as necessary to keep the energy boundary of each space.

### Difference in Materials

Using different materials in the commercial and residential parts also helps to make a clear difference. For example, stone and metal in the commercial parts and wood and natural fabrics in the residential parts give a feeling of balance.

## 10. Identifying and Handling Geopathic Stress

Geopathic stress means natural radiation from the earth that is regarded as harmful. Its causes are said to be underground water veins, mineral deposits or geological fault lines. This idea is not established by science, so see it only as a belief of Vastu.

### Dowsing Rods

In tradition, dowsing rods are used to identify the affected parts of a plot. Experienced Vastu consultants use them to map a property and mark such parts. This method is disputed and cannot be taken as firm proof.

### Measures of Correction

After identification, the traditional measures include planting certain trees and plants.

## 11. Solar Energy Systems in Vastu-Compliant Design

Adding solar energy systems to a design that follows Vastu is a good way of using the sun's energy while the balance of the elements is kept.

### Placement of Solar Panels

One Vastu suggestion is to put solar panels on east-facing roofs so as to receive the morning sun. The actual tilt and direction of the panels should be decided by the engineer or installer. For electricity output, the right tilt differs with the latitude of the place, and in South Asia panels normally face south.

### Solar Water Heaters

It is suggested to place a solar water heater in the south-east (Agneya) zone, the zone of the fire element, so that the water is heated efficiently and the fire element in the property stays balanced.

## 12. Arrangement of Yantras for Correcting Energy

Yantras are sacred geometric figures, and tradition uses them to correct imbalances of energy. In advanced Vastu, arranging yantras in a special grid (matrix) is mentioned.

### The Sri Yantra Mandala

The Sri Yantra is counted among the most powerful yantras in Vastu. When arranged as a mandala, it is described as useful for addressing many Vastu faults.

### Installation by Planetary Position

It is suggested that yantras be installed with the positions of the planets in mind. For this, experienced Vastu consultants use astrological calculations to decide the proper place of yantras for a particular property. These are traditional practices.

## 13. Landscape Vastu

Landscape Vastu takes the principles of Vastu to the outdoor spaces of a property. According to tradition, a harmonious landscape increases the positive energy of the home and forms a protective ring against inauspicious influences.

### Water Features in Sacred Geometry Shapes

Water features such as ponds or fountains are regarded as auspicious in the north-east (Ishanya) zone. It is also suggested to build them in sacred geometry shapes such as an octagon or a spiral.

### Spiral Herb Gardens

The idea is to plant a herb garden in a clockwise spiral. Herbs should be chosen for their medicinal properties and their ability to balance the elements of the property.

### Fractal-Patterned Paths

Paths with fractal patterns are regarded as slowing the movement of energy and making the atmosphere calmer and more reflective.

## 14. Acoustic Vastu for a Pleasant Sound Environment

Acoustic Vastu is a special branch of Vastu that attends to making a pleasant sound environment. Improving the acoustics of a space can help to reduce noise and stress and to improve concentration. This is ordinary acoustics and not a classical Vastu rule.

### Reverberation Time

The ideal reverberation time for a living space is between 0.8 and 1.2 seconds. It can be controlled by a mixture of sound-absorbing and sound-reflecting materials. It is better to take the advice of an acoustics specialist for this.

## 15. Colour and Light in Advanced Vastu

Colour therapy uses colours to balance the energy centres of the body. In advanced Vastu, its use is thought of for making a supportive and harmonious atmosphere. It is not medical treatment. It is an idea about decoration and atmosphere, and light and colour are not a cure for any condition.

### Smart LED Systems

Smart LED lighting can be programmed so that the colour of a room changes with the time of day, the season or the mood of the people living there. This is a modern idea, not classical Vastu.

### Light by Colour

It is said that light of particular colours can balance the chakras or energy centres. In a study room, for example, yellow light is regarded as good for concentration. In a bedroom, warm and dim light in the evening is more suitable for night-time rest.

## 16. Smart Homes and Vastu Principles

Joining smart-home technology with Vastu principles is a new experiment in conscious living. By paying attention to the place and use of smart devices, a home can be made that is advanced in technology and also balanced.

### Placement of a Wireless Router

It is advised to keep the wireless router in the south-east (Agneya) zone, so that its effect on the balance of the home is kept low. Keep it away from bedrooms and other places where you spend a lot of time.

### Shielded Cables

Where possible, use shielded cables to cut down the EMF given off. This matters especially for devices near your bed or workplace.

## 17. Balancing Elements with Materials Science

In advanced Vastu there is an idea of using the principles of materials science to balance the elements in a building, with special materials used to raise the energy of the zone of each direction.

### Nano-Coated Surfaces

The idea is to apply a titanium dioxide coating to the walls of the north-east (Ishanya) zone so that the zone can be kept clean. This is a modern idea, not one from traditional texts.

### Piezoelectric Flooring

Piezoelectric flooring produces an electric charge when mechanical pressure is applied. The idea is to lay it at the entrance of a home, so that the pressure of footsteps turns into charge and the entrance feels lively. This too is a modern idea.

## 18. Vastu for Skyscrapers

Applying Vastu to skyscrapers and other tall buildings is a complex, special subject. The Vastu of tall buildings takes into account particular challenges such as their height, their exposure to the elements and their effect on the surroundings. The structure of a tall building is the engineer's field.

### Stepped Massing

Stepped massing, in which the lower floors are heavier and more solid than the upper floors, is a basic principle of high-rise Vastu. It is believed to steady the building and make a balanced field of energy.

### Placement of Skybridges

Skybridges should be built so that no imbalance arises between the buildings they join. They should be made so that the passage of natural light and air is not blocked.

## 19. Changes in Vastu with Time

Time-based Vastu is a flexible approach that takes account of energies that change with time. The idea in it is to make changes in your surroundings by the time of day, the season or the transits of the planets, so that the place goes with the rhythm of the universe.

### Vastu Muhurta Timing

Vastu muhurta is the tradition of choosing an auspicious time for construction and other important tasks. The belief is that doing tasks by Vastu muhurta gives them the support of the natural flow of energy.

### Movable Partitions

Movable partitions can make flexible spaces that are easy to change as the needs of the people living there change.

## 20. Corporate Vastu Illustrations

The aim of corporate Vastu is to make a place of work pleasant and supportive. No proven effect is claimed here. It is a way of thinking based on tradition.

### Placing Departments by Direction

The place of departments in an office should be decided by Vastu principles. For example, the Research and Development (R&D) department would be kept in the north-east (Ishanya), to support innovation and creativity, and the finance department in the north, which is regarded as the direction of wealth.

### Arranging Workstations

The seat of each employee should also follow Vastu principles. For example, employees would sit facing their auspicious direction, and their desks would be free of clutter and other obstructions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I apply advanced Vastu principles to my existing home?

Yes. Many advanced principles can be taken up in an existing home without large structural changes. Non-invasive measures such as the use of colour, the arrangement of belongings and better use of space are simple ways of making the atmosphere of a home fit Vastu.

### How do I find a qualified Vastu consultant?

When choosing a consultant, look for a person with a deep understanding of both the traditional principles of Vastu and the challenges of modern architecture. Choose a consultant who is linked with a professional body and whose earlier work has been good.

### What is the difference between Vastu and Feng Shui?

Vastu and Feng Shui are both ancient traditions of architecture, and both aim to make a harmonious place to live. Their cultural and philosophical roots differ, however. Vastu is linked with the Vedic tradition of India, and Feng Shui with the Taoist tradition of China.

### How soon does Vastu show its effect?

There is no definite answer, and no result can be promised. Some people say they feel a difference at once, and for others it takes several months. It depends on how serious the faults of the home are and how many of the suggested changes are carried out.

## Conclusion

Advanced Vastu concepts offer many ideas for making a supportive and harmonious environment in modern times. Going beyond the basics and taking up the finer points of this ancient tradition can give a direction when building a new home, renovating an old one or improving an existing space. Do not see them as a miracle. Take them as suggestions linked with tradition, and adopt them thoughtfully and with your own judgement.
