# Marma Points in the Vastu Purusha Mandala

## Introduction

Vastu Shastra is counted among the oldest attempts to bring homes and other spaces into step with cosmic energies. At its centre is the Vastu Purusha Mandala, a map of the energy fields within a building. On this map a few points carry special weight. They are called marma points. They are regarded as the nervous system of any building, and they are believed to influence the well-being of those who live in it. Knowing them is not only a matter of theory. Before building or furnishing a home it is useful to know where heavy construction is best avoided.

## What Are Marma Points?

Marma points are the points on the grid of the Vastu Purusha Mandala where lines of energy (sutras) meet. In Sanskrit, "marma" means a sensitive or vulnerable place, one that should not be hurt. These points are regarded as the vital centres of the Vastu Purusha, the cosmic being believed to dwell in every building.

The mandala symbolises a cosmic man lying face down, with each section under the care of a deity. The marma points are the most sensitive parts of this being's body, much as the human body has important pressure points. Vastu tradition holds that when pillars, beams or heavy structures are built on these points, the flow of energy is upset and the occupants may meet various troubles.

There are mainly 64 points of intersection, called marmasthanas. Nine of them are the maha marmasthanas (great vital points), which are given special attention when a building is planned. They are fixed by the crossing of six energy lines of the mandala, called vanshas or rajju.

## Key Aspects

### The Six Main Marma Points

The Vastu Purusha Mandala has six especially important marma points, called the Shanmahanti. They correspond to the main parts of the cosmic being's body:

1. **Shir (head):** This lies in the northeast (Ishana) part and is regarded as the centre of intellect and spiritual energy of the building. By tradition, an obstruction here can hinder clarity of mind and spiritual progress.
2. **Mukha (mouth):** This is regarded as the centre of communication, expression and the arrival of resources. Disturbing it is said to bring problems of communication and financial difficulty.
3. **Hridaya (heart):** This lies near the centre of the mandala and is regarded as the centre of emotional well-being and harmony. A construction fault here is believed to cause emotional strain and trouble in relationships.
4. **Nabhi (navel):** This is the centre of the core energy, likened to the solar plexus in a person. It is linked with vitality and life force. A disturbance here is believed to affect the general level of energy.
5. **Payu (anus):** This point is linked with elimination and letting go. It is believed to control the release of negative energy and waste. An obstruction here is said to bring financial loss and an inability to free oneself from negative habits.
6. **Vama sthana and Dakshina sthana (the left and right breasts):** These come as a pair and are linked with nourishment and support. They represent the nurturing energy of the building.

### The Nine Maha Marmasthanas

The maha marmasthanas are formed where six lines (vanshas) crossing the Vastu Purusha Mandala meet. These lines join the following pairs of energy fields:

1. Shikhi to Pitra
2. Aditi to Sugriva
3. Jayanta to Bhringaraja
4. Roga to Anila
5. Mukhya to Vrisha
6. Shosha to Vitatha

Where these lines cross, the nine maha marmasthanas are formed. They are regarded as the most powerful and sensitive energy points of the whole mandala. They are considered especially sacred, and construction should never obstruct them.

### Principles of Energy Flow

The marma points work as junctions of energy within the mandala. They are compared to the nerve plexuses of the human body. The horizontal, vertical and diagonal lines of the mandala grid are regarded as energy channels, like the veins of the Vastu Purusha, carrying cosmic energy through the whole building.

These lines of energy are called sutras. They form a complex network of flow, and where they meet, points of concentrated energy, the marmasthanas, are formed. The idea is likened to acupuncture in traditional medicine, in which certain points on lines of energy have special importance.

Vastu tradition holds that the proper flow of energy through these points keeps balance and harmony in the building. When the marma points stay open, cosmic energy is believed to flow without hindrance and the surroundings become nourishing for the occupants.

## How the Marma Points Are Located

The marma points are identified by a few geometric rules. The steps for finding them are these:

1. **Draw the base mandala:** Draw the Vastu Purusha Mandala as a perfect square. By tradition it is divided into a grid of 8x8 or 9x9 smaller squares.
2. **Fix the main directions:** Set the mandala by the directions, with north at the top.
3. **Draw the six vansha lines:** These lines join the points of the mandala named above (Shikhi and Pitra, Aditi and Sugriva, Jayanta and Bhringaraja, Roga and Anila, Mukhya and Vrisha, Shosha and Vitatha).
4. **Find the crossing points:** Where these lines cross, the nine maha marmasthanas are formed.
5. **Mark the six main points (Shanmahanti):** These correspond to particular parts of the Vastu Purusha. The head is towards the northeast, the mouth towards the east, the heart at the centre and the navel south of the centre. The payu is taken to be in the southwest (Nairitya). Traditional sources are needed for the exact places of the pair of breasts.
6. **Work out the 64 ordinary marmasthanas:** These are formed where the horizontal and vertical grid lines of the mandala cross.

This geometric working identifies the marma points correctly, so that architects and Vastu consultants can avoid placing structural parts at these sensitive places.

## How the Marma Points Are Read

Understanding the importance of the marma points is necessary for applying Vastu correctly. The main guidelines are these:

**Seriousness of an obstruction:** Not all marma points carry equal weight. The maha marmasthanas are the most important, followed by the six Shanmahanti points. The ordinary marmasthanas deserve attention but are less sensitive.

**Matching body parts:** Each marma point is linked with a part of the Vastu Purusha's body. By tradition, a disturbance at these points can affect the matching areas of the occupants' lives:

- Head marma: intellectual and spiritual well-being
- Heart marma: emotional health and relationships
- Navel marma: vitality and general energy
- Mouth marma: financial prosperity and communication

**Checking the flow of energy:** Look at how energy flows through these points in the building. Ideally the points stay open and free of obstruction, so that energy moves easily.

**Remedies:** Where an obstruction on a marma point cannot be avoided, as in buildings that already stand, Vastu tradition describes some balancing remedies, such as special materials or geometric adjustments. These are traditional beliefs.

**Working with other Vastu principles:** The marma points should be understood together with the energy of the directions, the influence of the elements and the 45 deities of the Vastu mandala.

## Practical Applications

### Planning and Building

The main use of marma point knowledge is in planning a building. Architects who know Vastu avoid placing load-bearing pillars, beams or columns on marma points at the design stage. In the same way they avoid drilling, hammering in nails or fixing heavy fittings at these places.

For new construction:

- Mark the marma points clearly when the building plan is made.
- Design the structural parts so that they avoid these points.
- Carry out the foundation work with care for the integrity of these energy centres.
- Route water and electrical lines away from the important marma points.

In older buildings that already have a disturbance on the marma points, tradition describes balancing remedies, such as special materials or geometric adjustments. They are regarded as lessening the adverse effect.

### Using Space in a Home or Office

Even after the first construction, knowledge of the marma points helps in placing furniture and using space:

- Do not place heavy furniture directly on marma points.
- Arrange sleeping places so that the head or heart part of the body does not fall over a marma point.
- Work desks and productive areas are considered best placed near supportive marma points, but not directly on them.
- Meditation spaces are considered more effective when placed in relation to the spiritual marma points.

## Common Misconceptions

**Misconception 1: All crossing points are equally important**

Many people think all the crossing points on the grid of the Vastu mandala are marma points of equal importance. In fact there is an order of importance. The nine maha marmasthanas come first, then the six Shanmahanti points, and then the ordinary marmasthanas.

**Misconception 2: A fault on a marma point cannot be corrected**

Some people believe a building with construction on its marma points is flawed for ever. Avoiding such construction is ideal, but Vastu tradition also speaks of balancing remedies that reduce the effect.

**Misconception 3: Marma points apply only to temples**

Some think only temples or sacred buildings need to observe the marma principle. By tradition, these energy centres apply to every kind of building and its occupants, whatever the purpose of the building.

**Misconception 4: Marma principles cannot be used in Western-style buildings**

Many think the idea of marma points applies only to traditional Indian architecture. But these principles concern the flow of energy and not any particular aesthetic style, so they can be applied in any style of building.

Keep in mind also that the marma point theory is a traditional Vastu belief. It should be understood as tradition and not as scientific proof.

## Examples

### Example 1: Planning a Home

Imagine an ordinary home of about 2,000 square feet planned on a square plot. The Vastu consultant first lays a perfect square mandala over the plot and marks all the marma points. During planning it turns out that a proposed load-bearing pillar falls on a maha marmasthana where the Shikhi to Pitra and Jayanta to Bhringaraja lines cross.

The architect changes the plan and moves the pillar by two feet. The strength of the structure is kept and the important marma point is also spared. The courtyard is also placed in line with the flow of energy from the unobstructed marma points. Tradition holds that this improves the overall energy of the home.

### Example 2: Renovating an Office

Suppose a corporate office had very frequent staff turnover and constant quarrels. A Vastu expert found that the conference table in the middle stood over the heart marma of the building's Vastu mandala, with its heavy metal supports resting on that sensitive point.

The renovation involved these steps:

- The conference table was moved elsewhere.
- The employees' desks were rearranged in line with the supportive flow of energy.

By tradition, a change like this is believed to reduce quarrels and improve the working atmosphere. The example shows that the marma points can be kept in mind in today's offices as well.

## Conclusion

The marma points of the Vastu Purusha Mandala are counted among the subtlest aspects of the ancient building tradition. They are regarded as the nervous system of a building, influencing the well-being of the occupants through the flow of energy. By understanding and respecting these sensitive points, especially the six Shanmahanti points that match the head, mouth, heart, navel, payu and breasts of the Vastu Purusha, a living or working space can be made to nourish and not to drain.

Tradition does not regard the careful working out and protection of marma points during construction as mere superstition. Behind it lies the view that the environment we build is a living energy system, connected with our own biological and subtle energy fields. Whether a new building is to be raised or an old one improved, attention to these points is one of the most important applications of Vastu Shastra in modern life.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are marma points?

They are the points in the Vastu Purusha Mandala where the lines of energy (sutras) meet. They are regarded as the most sensitive parts of the Vastu Purusha's body, so heavy construction on them is avoided.

### How many marmasthanas are there?

In all, 64 marmasthanas are recognised. Of these, the nine maha marmasthanas are the most sensitive. Besides these there are six main points, called the Shanmahanti.

### What happens if a pillar or beam falls on a marma point?

By tradition, the flow of energy is upset and the occupants may meet troubles. For this reason pillars, beams and columns are kept away from these points at the planning stage, as the pillar was moved by two feet in the example above.

### Is there a remedy for a marma fault in a house that is already built?

By tradition, balancing remedies are described for such buildings, such as the use of special materials or geometric adjustments. The easiest step is to move heavy furniture away from these points.

### How can I keep this in mind in everyday life at home?

Do not place heavy furniture directly on marma points, do not let the head or heart part of the body lie over a marma point when sleeping, and place a meditation space in relation to the spiritual marma points.

### Where should a beginner start?

First learn to draw a simple Vastu Purusha Mandala and identify the main marma points. Begin with the nine maha marmasthanas and the six Shanmahanti points. Then check in your home or office whether heavy furniture or a structure is pressing on these points. A small change such as moving furniture is a good first step.
