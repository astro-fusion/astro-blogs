# The 45 Deities of the Vastu Mandala and Their Zones

## Introduction

The 45 deities of the Vastu mandala are regarded as the heart of the energy scheme of Vastu Shastra, the Vedic tradition of building and spatial harmony. Brahma stands at the centre, 12 deities are in the inner ring and 32 on the outer perimeter. Each deity is believed to preside over a particular domain and to influence different parts of a building and the lives of those who live in it. By understanding their places and meanings, a home can be planned in a way that tradition regards as favourable to health, prosperity and well-being.

## What Are the 45 Deities of the Vastu Mandala?

They are the spiritual forces believed to be placed on the Vastu Purusha Mandala. The mandala is a spiritual square grid, the basis for drawing up a building plan according to Vastu. On this grid, 32 deities are on the outer perimeter, 12 are in the inner ring, and Brahma, the creator, holds the central Brahmasthan. Each deity is taken to be the lord of a separate energy field (pada), and the character of that part of the building is thought to be tied to him. This idea is the foundation of Vastu Shastra, whose purpose is to create harmony between human dwellings and the universe.

## Key Aspects

### The Cosmic Map and Its Structure

- The Vastu mandala is usually drawn as a grid of 9x9 (81 squares) or 8x8 (64 squares), with fixed positions for the 45 deities.
- The central square, the Brahmasthan, is ruled by Brahma. From here energy spreads outward to the other 44 deities, each with a domain of his own.
- The 32 outer deities hold the boundaries and protect the building. The 12 inner deities look after important energies within.

### Domains and Powers of the 45 Deities

- **Central deity:** Brahma, the source of creation and of the ultimate energy.
- **Inner deities:** These include Bhudhar (manifestation), Aryama (relationships), Vivasvan (success and fame), Mitra (motivation) and others. Each is linked with a basic human experience.
- **Outer deities:** These are spread across the main and intermediate directions and are linked with matters such as health, wealth, justice, art, communication and protection. For example, Indra is regarded as the lord of expansion and prosperity, Varuna of water and emotions, Yama of discipline and morality, and Surya (the Sun) of vitality and leadership.

### Influence on Life and the Building

- The place of each deity in the mandala corresponds to a part of the building, such as the entrance, kitchen or bedrooms.
- It is believed that wrong design or wrong use of a deity's zone weakens his favourable influence and can upset the balance of that area of life.
- Vastu tradition holds that proper alignment keeps the energy flowing in harmony and supports comfort, health and creativity.

## How the Zones Are Fixed

These steps are followed to work out the influence of the 45 deities:

- **Dividing the plot:** The building site is laid over the grid of the mandala and divided into 81 padas (squares). Each square is linked with a deity.
- **Allocating functions:** The main functions of the building, such as the entrance, kitchen or bedrooms, are placed in particular squares, keeping in mind the deity regarded as most suitable for that purpose.
- **Following Pada Vinyasa:** This traditional process gives each pada to a deity so that the space has order and the energy has harmony.
- **Respecting the sacred zone:** The central Brahmasthan is ideally kept open or lightly used, so that the core energy of the building is preserved.

## How the Zones Are Read

- **Favourable match:** When a function such as the entrance falls in the zone of a benevolent deity such as Indra or Surya, tradition holds that the blessings tied to that deity, such as wealth, health and fame, are received.
- **Unfavourable match:** Deities linked with difficulties, such as Roga for illness and Papayakshma for misfortune, have zones where important rooms are avoided or a remedy is applied, because an adverse effect is believed.
- **Overall assessment:** Practitioners look at the main rooms, the entrance and the shape of the plot in relation to the mandala, and suggest changes to balance the energies.

## Practical Applications

### Planning a Harmonious Home

Placing the main parts of the home, such as the entrance, kitchen and bedrooms, in the zones of favourable deities is believed by tradition to reduce everyday obstacles.

### Traditional Corrective Measures

If a building does not follow the Vastu mandala, tradition describes some remedies, such as changing the position of rooms or choosing colours. Placing yantras is counted among such traditional practices. They are believed to lessen unfavourable influences and keep the deities concerned content. These remedies rest on traditional belief.

## Common Misconceptions

- **It is only about religion:** The 45 deities are not merely religious figures. They are also taken to stand for cosmic principles and natural energies, which are said to affect the occupants whatever their personal faith.
- **Vastu is very rigid:** Vastu guidelines are strict, but remedies and adaptations exist for modern life. This keeps it practical in today's homes too.
- **Only the centre matters:** The Brahmasthan is important, but all 45 deities are believed to influence different areas of life. Neglecting the outer deities is said to give an incomplete result.

## Examples

- **Entrance and Indra:** For a home whose main entrance falls in the square of Indra in the east, tradition holds that growth, prosperity and chances to build contacts increase, because Indra is regarded as the deity of expansion and leadership.
- **Kitchen and Agni:** A kitchen in the southeast (Agneya), in the zone of Agni, is considered proper, because Agni is the lord of fire and digestion. By contrast, placing the kitchen in the zone of Roga, the deity of illness, is considered improper in tradition.

## Conclusion

Understanding the 45 deities is necessary for reaching the depth of Vastu Shastra. Vastu tradition holds that when the layout of a building follows their zones, the place becomes harmonious and supportive. As guardians and givers of energy for each zone, these deities are part of any serious discussion of Vastu.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How are the 45 deities of the Vastu mandala distributed?

Brahma is at the centre, 12 deities are in the inner ring and 32 are on the outer perimeter.

### Whose zone is the Brahmasthan, and how should it be kept?

The Brahmasthan is the zone of Brahma, and all energy is believed to spread from it. It is ideally kept open or lightly used.

### Is it enough to look only at the centre when planning a home?

No. The Brahmasthan is important, but all 45 deities are believed to influence different areas of life. Neglecting the outer deities may leave the result incomplete.

### In whose zone are the entrance and the kitchen placed?

By tradition, an entrance in the east, in the zone of Indra, is regarded as favourable, and a kitchen in the southeast (Agneya), in the zone of Agni. Placing important rooms in the zones of deities regarded as unfavourable, such as Roga, is avoided.

### What if the house was not built to the mandala?

Tradition describes remedies such as changing the position of rooms or changing colours. Placing yantras is also counted among these traditional practices.

### How can a beginner learn this?

First learn the place and character of each deity, then lay your home over the grid and see which zone belongs to which deity. Seeking advice from reliable guides or experts helps.
