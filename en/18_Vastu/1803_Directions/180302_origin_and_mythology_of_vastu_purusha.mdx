# Origin and Legend of the Vastu Purusha

## Introduction

According to ancient Hindu tradition, the Vastu Purusha is the cosmic being who presides over the harmonious design of buildings. His story is a fine blend of myth and practical wisdom about living. It is said that for centuries this legend has inspired the building of temples, homes and towns across the Indian subcontinent. A being born from a conflict became in time the kindly guardian of buildings. The story teaches that the places we build should be in tune with the energies of creation.

## What Is the Legend of the Vastu Purusha?

It is an ancient Hindu legend that explains how a cosmic being became the presiding deity of every building. According to several texts, including the Matsya Purana and the Brihat Samhita, the Vastu Purusha was a powerful being born during a vast conflict. He is pictured lying face down on every plot of land. The different parts of his body are linked with different directions and elements. His presence and position are the basis of Vastu Shastra, the traditional Hindu system of architecture that seeks to bring buildings into step with natural energies.

Vastu is believed to have developed between roughly 6000 BC and 3000 BC. At first it was a purely technical subject, confined to certain sections of society and handed down from generation to generation, either by word of mouth or in handwritten texts. Because it is so old, the legend of the Vastu Purusha exists in several versions. All of them share one core: a divine being who presides over the harmony of buildings.

## The Main Stages of the Legend

### The Divine Birth

In the most widely told version, during a fierce battle between Lord Shiva and the demon Andhaka, drops of Shiva's sweat fell to the earth. From those drops arose a gigantic being of immense strength. He claimed to be Shiva's son and received permission to satisfy his great hunger by consuming the three worlds (Triloka). Some texts give another account, in which the Vastu Purusha came out of Shiva's bow after Andhaka was defeated, and looked like a terrifying Bhuta (spirit) or Yaksha.

The Shilpa Grantha Prakashika gives yet another form. In it the Vastu Purusha was born from a sacrifice performed by Shukracharya, the teacher of the demons (Asuras), during the long war between the gods and the demons. All these accounts agree on two things: his immense power and his threatening nature at the start.

### The Divine Intervention

As the huge being began to devour everything in his path and threatened to swallow the whole universe, the gods grew alarmed and went to Brahma. Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva and the other gods together planned how to bring him under control. The guardians of the eight directions (Ashta Dikpalas) also joined in overpowering him.

Together the gods threw the Vastu Purusha to the ground and pinned him face down. Brahma sat at the centre of his body, and the other gods held down different parts of his form. Each god took charge of one particular part. This cosmic restraint gave the pattern that later became known as the Vastu Purusha Mandala, the sacred grid that guides all construction.

### The Divine Compromise

Once subdued, the Vastu Purusha cried out to the gods and asked why he was being punished for being what he had been made to be. Moved by this argument, Brahma offered a compromise. The Vastu Purusha was made immortal as the presiding deity of all buildings raised on earth. From then on, anyone who built would have to honour and worship him for harmony and prosperity in the home.

This agreement changed the Vastu Purusha from a destructive force into a kindly guardian. His body became the cosmic plan of every building, with each part linked to an element, an energy and a function. The gods who had pinned him down became the presiding deities of their own directions and places on the Vastu grid.

## From Legend to Mandala

The practical form of the legend is the Vastu Purusha Mandala. It is a spiritual plan that is believed to include the movements of the heavens and unseen forces. This sacred diagram is the basis of the measurements Vastu Shastra uses in construction.

The mandala is usually drawn as a square divided into smaller squares (padas). For temples an 8x8 grid of 64 squares is often used, while homes generally use a 9x9 grid of 81 squares. Each square is believed to be the seat of a deity and to be linked with a part of the Vastu Purusha's body.

First the main directions of the plot are fixed, and then the Vastu Purusha Mandala is laid over it. The Vastu Purusha is taken to lie with his head in the northeast (Ishana) and his feet in the southwest (Nairitya). His face and stomach touch the ground. This is understood to mean that he carries the weight of the building.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who is the Vastu Purusha?

The Vastu Purusha is a mythical cosmic being who is pictured lying face down on every plot of land. In tradition he is the presiding deity of all buildings on earth.

### How was the Vastu Purusha born?

In the most widely told story, drops of Shiva's sweat fell to the earth during his battle with the demon Andhaka, and this huge being arose from them. Some texts say he came from Shiva's bow or from a sacrifice performed by Shukracharya.

### Why did the gods pin him down?

He began to eat everything to satisfy his hunger, and there was a danger that he would swallow the whole universe. Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva and the guardians of the directions then pinned him face down on the ground.

### Why is the Vastu Purusha honoured when a building is raised?

In the legend, Brahma made him the presiding deity of all buildings and said that whoever builds must honour and worship him. This was meant to bring harmony and prosperity to the home.

### In which directions are the head and feet of the Vastu Purusha placed?

The head is in the northeast (Ishana) and the feet are in the southwest (Nairitya).

### What is the difference between the grid for a temple and for a home?

Temples often use an 8x8 grid (64 squares), while homes generally use a 9x9 grid (81 squares).
