# Vastu Sub-Directions: NNE, ENE, ESE and SSE Explained

## Introduction

Vastu Shastra gives great importance to directions. Most people know the eight principal directions: north, south, east, west, northeast (Ishanya), northwest (Vayavya), southeast (Agneya) and southwest (Nairutya). Between these eight lie sixteen sub-directions, which fewer people know.

This article looks at four of them: NNE (north-northeast), ENE (east-northeast), ESE (east-southeast) and SSE (south-southeast). Vastu tradition holds that these sectors influence the atmosphere of a home, the ability to work and peace of mind. Understanding them lets a home owner apply Vastu rules with more precision.

## What Are Sub-Directions?

Sub-directions are a finer division of the eight principal directions. Each principal direction spans 45 degrees. Halving it gives smaller sectors of 22.5 degrees. The four sub-directions in this article lie on the compass between the principal directions:

- NNE lies between north and northeast.
- ENE lies between east and northeast.
- ESE lies between east and southeast.
- SSE lies between south and southeast.

Each sub-direction is held to blend the qualities of the two directions beside it, which is why tradition treats its influence as distinct. Sub-directions are used in building plans, in the placing of furniture and in the allotment of rooms.

## Key Aspects

### Energy Flow

Vastu tradition holds that each sub-direction carries its own flow of life energy. NNE is said to blend the qualities of the north and the northeast, which tradition links with clear thinking and spiritual growth. ESE combines the rising energy of the east with the prosperity side of the southeast, and so is linked with wealth and creative inspiration. Understanding these subtle flows helps in choosing the right place for an activity.

### Elemental Associations

Each sub-direction is given its own mix of qualities:

- NNE is linked with the water element and is taken as a foundation for knowledge and spirituality.
- ENE is linked with growth and decision-making.
- ESE is taken as nourishing wealth and creativity.
- SSE is linked with the fire element, and with fame, recognition and active energy.

Some descriptions of these sectors also mention a wood element for ENE, ESE and SSE. Wood belongs to the Feng Shui tradition. In Vastu the five elements are earth, water, fire, air and space, so treat the elemental descriptions as a general indication only.

### Governing Deities

Vastu tradition holds that each sub-direction has its own presiding deity, and that honouring the deity through a correct arrangement of space brings the favourable qualities of that direction. The sub-directions carry modified forms of the symbols of the principal directions.

## How to Determine the Sub-Directions

First find the centre of the home or the room you are looking at. Directions are measured from that centre. An ordinary magnetic compass can be used, or the special Feng Shui compass (Luopan), but the measuring must be done carefully.

Follow these steps:

1. Use a floor plan or blueprint to fix the centre point of the home or room.
2. Use a compass to find true north.
3. Mark the eight principal directions at 45-degree intervals.
4. Divide each principal direction into two segments of 22.5 degrees each.
5. NNE lies 22.5 degrees east of north.
6. ENE lies 22.5 degrees north of east.
7. ESE lies 22.5 degrees south of east.
8. SSE lies 22.5 degrees east of south.

Allow for the magnetic declination of your area, because only then is the reading accurate. Many practitioners advise adjusting for the local magnetic variation to obtain true directions.

## What Each Sub-Direction Stands For

**NNE (north-northeast).** This blends the work side of the north with the knowledge side of the northeast. Tradition holds that clear thinking, spiritual practice and decisive work suit it. A meditation room, a study corner and a home office are given as suitable uses. Arranged well, it is said to support clarity of mind and inspired thinking.

**ENE (east-northeast).** This combines the family and health side of the east with the education side of the northeast. It is regarded as excellent for children's study areas, a family study corner and health-related activities. Tradition describes it as supporting intellectual growth while keeping the family in harmony.

**ESE (east-southeast).** This joins the health side of the east with the wealth side of the southeast. It is considered suitable for financial planning, creative ways of earning and health-promoting activities that also add to prosperity.

**SSE (south-southeast).** This joins the fame and recognition side of the south with the wealth side of the southeast. It is regarded as effective for work aimed at public recognition, career advancement and prosperity. Tradition holds that, activated correctly, it supports visibility and financial success.

## Use in Daily Life

### The Work Desk and the Office

Tradition holds that placing the desk in the NNE improves concentration, clarity of mind and the power of decision, and that a person working here takes in positive energy and keeps better focus.

For people in creative work who also want financial success, a workspace in the ESE is considered good. For people in leadership roles, a desk in the SSE is advised, because the direction is linked with recognition and reputation.

### Home Planning and Room Allotment

When planning a home or choosing rooms, the qualities of the sub-directions can be used as a basis. For example:

- A meditation room or personal library in the NNE part supports spiritual growth and the gaining of knowledge.
- A children's study area in the ENE part supports education.
- A home office or creative studio in the ESE part supports creativity and financial success.
- Entertainment or social rooms in the SSE part support social standing and public image.

When the use of a room matches the energy of its sector, the room is said to support the activities carried out in it.

## Common Misconceptions

The first misconception is that sub-directions are either useless or too complex to use. In fact the eight principal directions give a firm base, and the sub-directions allow finer adjustment. Understanding them lets Vastu rules be applied more precisely.

The second is that a direction is lucky or unlucky in itself. Feng Shui practitioners point out that no direction is good or bad in itself. Calling north unlucky or south especially lucky owes a great deal to cultural associations. The same applies to the sub-directions: each has particular qualities that may suit particular purposes, but none is auspicious or inauspicious on its own.

## Examples

These examples are plain illustrations. They do not promise any result.

**Example 1: A home office.** A consultant found it hard to make decisions and had few new clients. A review showed that her desk faced a direction that did not suit her work. The desk was set so that she faced the NNE, with a solid wall behind her and a clear view of the door. This is the commanding position principle.

**Example 2: A family's dining and study spaces.** A family with school-age children found that their dining area and the children's study space were in directions not considered suitable for health and education. The dining area was moved to the east part, and a separate study corner was made in the ENE. Good lighting was provided in this part to help concentration.

## Conclusion

Knowing the NNE, ENE, ESE and SSE directions gives a finer way of looking at the energy of a home and a workplace. Whether a new home is being built, an office desk is being moved or an existing home is being improved, knowledge of the sub-directions is useful. These transition zones between the eight principal directions give a chance to attend to particular aspects of life with more precision.

As with every Vastu rule, the principles work best when combined with practical needs and your own judgment.

## FAQs

### How do sub-directions differ from the eight principal directions?

The eight principal directions divide a space into sectors of 45 degrees. Sub-directions refine this into sectors of 22.5 degrees. In sub-directions such as NNE, ENE, ESE and SSE, the qualities of the neighbouring principal directions are held to be mixed.

### How do I measure these sub-directions?

Fix the centre of the home or room, find true north with a compass, then mark the eight principal directions at 45 degrees and the sub-directions at 22.5 degrees. Do allow for the magnetic declination of your area.

### Which room suits which sub-direction?

Tradition gives a meditation room or library for the NNE, a children's study area for the ENE, a home office or creative studio for the ESE, and a social or entertainment room for the SSE.

### Is any direction good or bad in itself?

No. Calling a direction good or bad is largely a cultural idea. Each sub-direction has particular qualities that may suit particular purposes.

### How can a beginner try this?

Find the centre of the home or workplace and mark the principal directions with a compass. Then divide them into sub-directions. Start with one room, such as a workspace or bedroom, and place the main furniture as the sub-direction advises. Watch your energy, work and sense of ease over several weeks before moving to other rooms.
