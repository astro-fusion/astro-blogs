# Deva Vithi and Manushya Vithi: Vastu Energy Fields

## Introduction

The idea of Deva Vithi and Manushya Vithi is central to Vastu Shastra. Vastu tradition holds that it explains how cosmic energies bear on a built space and on the well-being of those who live in it. These energy fields are not only theory. Tradition holds that at each stage of construction they carry divine and earthly energies that shape the character of the building and the lives of its occupants. They are of interest to anyone who wants to create a harmonious home with the help of ancient knowledge.

## What Are Deva Vithi and Manushya Vithi?

Both are special directional zones of the Vastu Purusha Mandala, the sacred grid of Vastu Shastra. The Mandala divides a built space into 45 distinct energy fields, and each field is presided over by a deity or cosmic principle.

- **Deva Vithi** represents the four cardinal directions and is linked with the four heads of Brahma. It is held that these fields emerge when construction reaches a certain height, and that they carry creative and manifesting energies.
- **Manushya Vithi** corresponds to the corners: northeast (Ishanya), southeast (Agneya), southwest (Nairutya) and northwest (Vayavya). It has eight energy fields, each of which fosters particular qualities needed for growth, healing and transformation.

Both Vithis are said to expand during construction. They are linked with different deities whose influence is held to shape the energy and fortune of the building.

## Key Aspects

### How the Energy Fields Expand During Construction

As a building rises:

- The **central Brahma Vithi** emerges at the heart of the plot. It stands for pure creative potential.
- When the walls reach about 5 to 8 feet, the **Deva Vithi** energy fields spread outward in the directions of the four heads of Brahma and carry the process of creation forward.
- As construction continues, the **Manushya Vithi** is activated. Energy spreads along the diagonals, like a tortoise stretching its limbs, a symbol of expansion and support.

### The Four Deities of Deva Vithi

The four deities of the inner ring around Brahma each carry a set of qualities:

- **Bhudhar**: the power to begin a task and bring it to form.
- **Aryama**: energy for protection and for building relationships.
- **Vivasvan**: brings success and fame.
- **Mitra**: inspires action and connection.

These deities are, in symbol, the four heads of Brahma. Together they nourish the creative force within the Mandala.

### The Diagonal Directions of Manushya Vithi and Their Influences

The Manushya Vithi has eight energy fields along the corners, each with its deities and functions.

- **Northeast**
  - *Apaha:* healing energy.
  - *Apahavatsa:* carries the healing power to the occupants.
- **Southeast**
  - *Savitra:* energy to begin tasks, especially creation or the birth of a child.
  - *Saavitra:* sustains continuing effort and keeps actions moral.
- **Southwest**
  - *Indra:* wisdom, skill and prosperity.
  - *Indrajay:* resourcefulness and growth.
- **Northwest**
  - *Rudra:* removes obstacles and brings victory.
  - *Rudrajaya:* steadiness of mind and leadership.

Tradition holds that together these fields keep the energy of the whole building in balance and in lively flow.

## How to Identify the Fields

To identify the Deva Vithi and Manushya Vithi fields in a plot or building:

1. **Lay out the Vastu Purusha Mandala.** Divide the plot into a grid, commonly 9 by 9 or 8 by 8, with Brahma at the centre.
2. **Mark the cardinal directions.** Identify the central cells in the north, south, east and west for the Deva Vithi.
3. **Identify the corner zones.** Draw diagonal lines from corner to corner, northeast to southwest and northwest to southeast. These give the zones of the Manushya Vithi.
4. **Assign the deities.** Place each deity in its direction according to the traditional Mandala charts.

No complex mathematics is needed, but accurate orientation and adherence to the Mandala grid are essential.

## How to Read the Result

In a building, these things are kept in mind:

- **Deva Vithi zones** show where creative, inspiring and accomplishing energies are strongest. Tradition holds that using them properly supports success, connection and motivation.
- **Manushya Vithi zones** show the places of healing, resourcefulness, action and emotional support. They are taken to meet the practical and energetic needs of the occupants.
- Tradition holds that an unsuitable placement in these fields, or an obstruction of them, brings difficulties such as health problems, lack of motivation or strained relationships.

In practice, construction and the use of space are kept in line with these fields so that harmony is maintained.

## Practical Applications

### Planning Harmonious Homes and Workplaces

By setting the Deva Vithi and Manushya Vithi on a floor plan, architects and Vastu consultants can:

- Place rooms and entrances in line with the auspicious fields.
- Avoid heavy structures or obstructions in sensitive zones, especially the northeast, the southwest and the centre.
- Bring the cosmic and earthly energies that act on the space into harmony.

### Auspicious Construction and the Place of Worship

Practitioners use these energy fields to suggest auspicious ways of building and the right place for a worship space. Tradition also describes rituals to strengthen these fields or to keep them calm. They are mentioned here only as tradition, not as advice.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"It is only superstition."** In Vastu tradition the Deva Vithi and Manushya Vithi are not seen as mere symbols. They stand for deep cosmological principles that link a person with universal energies.
- **"One rule fits everyone."** The same field may act differently according to the structure, the occupant's horoscope and the deities who preside over it.
- **"These ideas are outdated."** Some people think so, but Vastu practitioners still use them for practical matters of life and building.

## Examples

These examples are traditional illustrations. They do not promise any result.

1. **A home.** A home whose main entrance falls in the field of Mitra (a Deva Vithi deity) is said in tradition to have good community bonds and supportive relationships. If the northeast field of Apaha is obstructed, tradition holds that residents may face health trouble even when other placements are favourable.
2. **A workplace.** An office whose workspace matches the field of Vivasvan (a Deva Vithi deity) is spoken of as gaining fame and recognition. If the northwest field of Rudra is left unobstructed, tradition speaks of smooth operation and victory over competitors.

## Conclusion

Deva Vithi and Manushya Vithi are basic ideas of Vastu Shastra. They describe how cosmic energy reaches a built space. By understanding the principles of these fields, and especially their deities and energetic influences, one can plan spaces that nurture creativity, well-being and prosperity. Ancient knowledge still guides modern life, and these energy fields remain the pivot of harmony between the cosmos, the built space and human life.

## FAQs

### What is the difference between Deva Vithi and Manushya Vithi?

Deva Vithi is linked with the four cardinal directions and represents the four heads of Brahma. Manushya Vithi is linked with the four corners, northeast, southeast, southwest and northwest, and has eight energy fields.

### How many energy fields does the Vastu Purusha Mandala have?

The Mandala divides a built space into 45 energy fields, and each is presided over by a deity or cosmic principle.

### How do these Vithis emerge during construction?

It is held that the Brahma Vithi emerges first at the centre of the plot. When the walls reach about 5 to 8 feet, the Deva Vithi spreads. As construction goes on, the Manushya Vithi spreads along the diagonals.

### What should I do in the plan of my home to respect these fields?

Place rooms and entrances in line with the auspicious fields, and keep heavy structures and obstructions out of sensitive parts such as the northeast, the southwest and the centre.

### Do I need my birth time for this?

Not for work on a building. The focus is the plot and the structure. For personal advice, birth details can help, but general suggestions can be made from the orientation of the space.

### How can a beginner start to understand this?

Learn the basics of the Vastu Purusha Mandala, understand the cardinal directions and the corners, and look at the traditional Mandala charts. Meeting a qualified Vastu practitioner helps in applying it properly.
