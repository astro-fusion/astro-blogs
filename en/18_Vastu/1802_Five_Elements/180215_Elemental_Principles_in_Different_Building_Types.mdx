# The Five Elements in Different Types of Building

## Introduction

Vastu Shastra is the Indian tradition of building. According to it, our surroundings affect our health, our work and our spiritual progress. These Vastu principles, which are also read alongside modern architectural thinking, give a framework for making spaces that are in tune with cosmic energies. This article looks at how the balance of the elements is arranged in different types of building and how the principles can help improve the design and energy of each.

## What are the elemental principles for different buildings?

This is the use, in different kinds of building, of the basic design ideas that balance the five elements (earth, water, fire, air and space). The understanding is that the arrangement of the elements should differ with the use of the building. Vastu Shastra literally means the science of building.

The idea goes beyond looks and reaches the energetic qualities of a space. Modern architecture also has principles such as balance, rhythm, emphasis, unity and hierarchy, which shape how we experience a built space. When these are properly applied, the space is believed to work well and also to support the physical, emotional and spiritual well-being of the people in it.

## Key aspects

### Balance and harmony

Balance is the basis of the elemental principles in a building. It covers the distribution of visual weight between size, form and scale. In the language of Vastu, balance brings stability and order, so that energies flow properly through a space. Balance can be reached in three ways.

- **Symmetrical balance:** the same arrangement on both sides of a central axis.
- **Asymmetrical balance:** equilibrium without identical arrangements.
- **Radial balance:** arrangement around a central point.

A well-balanced building gives a feeling of comfort and ease, while an unbalanced design can feel unsettling. In spiritual and healing places emotional balance is essential, but it applies in a different way to every building. For example, in residential buildings balance brings harmony in the family, while in an office it supports focused work.

### Unity and rhythm

Unity makes sure that all the elements of the design work together as one whole. According to the theory of design, unity and balance go together, so that all parts of a building are tied by one feeling.

Rhythm creates visual interest and movement through repetition and pattern. It leads people through a space and gives a sense of flow. In the Vedic understanding of building, rhythm is linked with the movement of the energy of prana through the building. Different buildings need different rhythms. In places of learning, a rhythm that encourages learning is good, while in places of healing the rhythm should be calm and soothing.

### Materials and harmony with the environment

The choice of materials strongly affects how we experience a building. Vastu holds that materials have elemental qualities of their own. For instance, wood is linked with the air and fire elements, stone with earth and glass with space. Metals are linked with different elemental energies according to their type. Modern thinking also accepts that materials touch our senses and shape our feelings about a space.

Harmony with the environment means making buildings that go with nature, not against it. It takes account of climate, the orientation of the plot, natural light and the local ecology. In Vedic terms, such harmony is believed to bring favourable energy into the building. Present-day architecture stresses the same thing through climate-responsive design and passive systems that save energy while making spaces more comfortable.

## How to decide the elements for a building

Several things have to be looked at, combining traditional Vastu with modern needs for buildings.

1. **Decide the use of the building.** First see its main purpose, such as residential, commercial, educational or health care.
2. **Look at the features of the site.** Assess the natural elements of the site, such as orientation, water bodies, vegetation, terrain and climate.
3. **Understand the energies of the directions.** Use the Vastu Purusha Mandala (a sacred diagram that maps cosmic energies onto the directional zones) to find the energetic qualities of the different parts of the site.
4. **Distribute the elements.**
   - North-east (water dominant): suitable for water features and meditation spaces.
   - South-east (fire dominant): ideal for kitchens and electrical equipment.
   - South-west (earth dominant): suitable for solid structures and the master bedroom.
   - North-west (air dominant): good for guest rooms and gathering spaces.
   - Centre (space dominant): should stay open for the flow of energy.
5. **Check the balance.** See whether the proposed design gives an equilibrium among all five elements that suits the building.

## Emphasis by type of building

- **Residential buildings:** emphasise earth and water, to create stability, nourishment and emotional security. The balance should lean towards comfort and rest, with room for family activities.
- **Commercial buildings and offices:** increase fire and air, to bring activity, motivation and communication. The balance should lean towards productivity, while comfort is kept.
- **Educational institutions:** balance all elements, with slightly more air for intellectual activity and fire for motivation. The space should arouse curiosity and also allow concentration.
- **Healthcare facilities:** emphasise water and space, for healing, calm and renewal. Tranquillity and comfort should come above all.
- **Spiritual and religious buildings:** emphasise the space element, for a link with higher consciousness, with the other elements arranged in support.
- **Industrial and manufacturing buildings:** emphasise earth for stability and fire for transformation, together with good airflow for the health and comfort of workers.

## Practical applications

### Residential buildings

In homes, the elemental principles make surroundings that suit family life and personal well-being. The main points are these.

- **Entrance and centre:** the main entrance should be well proportioned and welcoming, ideally facing east or north to receive favourable energy. The centre of the home (the space element) should stay relatively open, so that it serves as a point of distribution for energy.
- **Living areas:** place them in the north, east or north-east parts of the home to receive the most natural light and favourable energy. Balanced proportions and a harmonious mix of colours and materials work well here.
- **Bedrooms:** the master bedroom is best in the south-west (earth element), for stability. Children's rooms are best in the east or north, to support growth and learning.
- **Kitchens:** ideally in the south-east (fire element), with enough ventilation and natural light. The design should keep fire (cooking equipment) and water (sink and refrigeration) suitably separated.

A home built on the elemental principles is regarded as supporting family harmony, good sleep and spiritual growth. Modern homes can also take them up through careful space planning, choice of materials, and attention to natural light and the flow of air.

### Commercial buildings and offices

Offices need elemental principles that support work, communication and the comfort of employees.

- **Reception and shared areas:** give a balanced first impression, with a clear order and rhythm that guide visitors. In these areas the air element helps communication.
- **Work areas:** keep a careful balance of private and shared space, with attention to natural light, acoustics and seating. In an open office, use elements that divide the space without blocking communication.
- **Meeting rooms:** balanced proportions and comfortable materials help focused discussion. The fire element, carefully controlled, can encourage creative thinking.
- **Executive offices:** usually placed in the south-west (earth) part of the building, to give a sense of stability and leadership. Some readings also mention the south (fire).

Modern workplace design is also moving towards these principles through nature-friendly design, flexible layouts and attention to sensory experience.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception 1: the elemental principles need strict adherence to traditional plans.** Vastu gives directional guidelines, but in present use the stress is on balanced energy and not on a rigid plan. The essence of the principles can be applied to any style or form of building.

**Misconception 2: the principles are merely mystical and have no practical basis.** Many elemental principles are tied to practical matters such as natural light, air circulation and balanced proportions. Even so, the points of tradition are best understood as tradition.

**Misconception 3: applying the principles is prohibitively expensive.** The basic principles can be applied through careful space planning, choice of colours and arrangement of furniture, often with no added construction cost.

**Misconception 4: the elemental principles are only for residential buildings.** Vastu writing discusses homes most, but the principles apply to every type of building. In large institutions such as hospitals and schools, attention to them can be even more useful, because they affect many people.

## Examples

### Example 1: a healing health facility

The Vidar Clinic in Järna, Sweden, is cited as an example of how the design of a building can create a healing environment. The architect, Erik Asmussen, built it on seven principles, which have a good deal in common with elemental balance.

1. Unity of form and function.
2. Polarity (balancing opposing elements).
3. Metamorphosis (change through design).
4. Harmony with nature and site.
5. Living wall (breathing, dynamic surfaces).
6. Luminosity of colour.
7. Dynamic equilibrium.

The clinic is joined to gardens and nature, natural materials are used throughout, and proportions are carefully balanced. Patient rooms have natural light and a view of nature. The design shows that health facilities too can take up thinking like that of the elemental principles. It is not a building based on Vastu, only an example of similar thinking.

### Example 2: designing an educational campus

On a university campus in a tropical climate, the elemental principles might be applied as follows.

- Orient classroom buildings so that they get more natural air (air element) and less direct sun.
- Make a water feature in the middle, which cools its surroundings and also gives a place to sit and reflect.
- Use local stone and earth materials to help control temperature.
- Provide courtyards and transition spaces that give rhythm and movement to the campus.
- Build the library with balanced proportions and plenty of natural light, so that study is focused.

This is an illustration, not a claim of any measured result.

## Conclusion

The elemental principles in different buildings are a deep framework for making spaces that support human activity, well-being and spiritual progress. By understanding balance, unity, rhythm and the choice of materials, we can make buildings that are in tune with cosmic forces. Whether it is a home, an office, a school, a hospital or a place of worship, the aim of the principles is to make spaces that support our activities and do not get in their way.

The growing meeting of the old understanding of Vastu with modern architecture suggests that these principles touch basic parts of human experience. In an age of environmental challenge and the need for sustainable building, these old ways of keeping in tune with nature become more relevant still.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the arrangement of the elements the same in every building?

No. The emphasis of the elements changes with the use of the building. For example, earth and water are stressed in a home, fire and air in an office, and water and space in a hospital.

### What is said about the entrance and centre of a home?

The main entrance should ideally face east or north. The centre of the home (the space element) should stay relatively open, so that energy keeps flowing.

### Where should I put the bedroom and the kitchen?

The master bedroom in the south-west and the kitchen in the south-east. Children's rooms are advised in the east or north.

### Must I follow an old plan exactly?

No. In present use the stress is on balanced energy. The essence of the principle can be applied to any form of building.

### Are these principles only for homes?

No. They are used in offices, schools, hospitals and religious buildings alike.

### How do I begin?

First notice how different spaces make you feel and which elements seem balanced or unbalanced. Begin with simple changes such as the arrangement of furniture, colours, plants and natural materials.
