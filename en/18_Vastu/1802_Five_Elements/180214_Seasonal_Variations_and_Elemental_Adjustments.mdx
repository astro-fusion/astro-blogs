# Adjusting the Elements in Your Home by Season

## Introduction

The Vedic tradition teaches that our living spaces, our daily routine and our inner state are all affected by the qualities of the elements, which change with each season. By understanding these natural rhythms we can keep the atmosphere of the home balanced with the season. This article explains how the emphasis of the elements is said to change with the seasons and what simple adjustments can be made at home.

## What is seasonal adjustment?

This approach holds that the influence of the four main elements (fire, earth, air and water) rises and falls through the year. As the Earth moves through its yearly cycle, different elements become dominant in different seasons. The effect is believed to reach both our surroundings and our bodies and temperaments.

In tradition these elements are not only physical substances. They are the primal energies from which all matter is made and by which all life processes run. This system is closely tied to Vastu Shastra. It is said that in the Southern Hemisphere these seasonal associations are reversed, so it is sensible to go by the actual seasons of your own region.

## Key aspects

### Seasons and their dominant elements

According to this article, the emphasis of the elements through the seasons is taken to be as follows.

- **Spring** (Northern Hemisphere, March to May): the air element.
- **Summer** (June to August): the fire element.
- **Autumn** (September to November): the earth element.
- **Winter** (December to February): the water element.

This is a general framework. India also has its own tradition of six seasons, so take it as an aid to understanding and not as a hard rule.

### Qualities of the elements and human experience

Each element has its own qualities, believed to affect our mood and physical state.

- **Fire:** transformation, enthusiasm and energy.
- **Earth:** stability, practicality and groundedness.
- **Air:** intellectual clarity, communication and adaptability.
- **Water:** emotional depth, intuition and nurturing.

These qualities naturally grow and fade with the season. That gives a chance to create balance by changing our surroundings and habits with care. For example, in a fire-dominant season, balance is kept by adding the cooling water element, and in a water-heavy period stagnation is avoided by adding the warming fire element.

### Transition periods

In the time between seasons the emphasis of the elements changes considerably. Vastu thinking advises particular care at such transitions, because if balance is not kept then, restlessness or discomfort may be felt in the home.

## How to know when an adjustment is needed

1. **Identify the element of the present season.** See which season is on and what its element is. The list is given above. Reverse it in the Southern Hemisphere.
2. **Look at the atmosphere of the home.** Does it feel too hot, damp, heavy or scattered? This shows which element is in excess or lacking.
3. **Decide what is needed.** The simple principle is to increase an element that is lacking and to moderate an element that is in excess.

## Guidelines for reading the signs

- **Every home and person differs.** An imbalance of elements shows differently in each person. The same season may feel heavy to one person and comforting to another.
- **Several levels.** The effect of the elements is believed to work on the physical, emotional, mental and spiritual levels. Symptoms such as sluggishness are linked with an excess of earth or water in the season when it dominates. Emotional turbulence is linked with an excess of fire or air, or a lack of balancing elements.
- **An opportunity, not a challenge.** If you naturally lack an element, treat the season in which it dominates as a chance to develop that quality and not as something to endure.
- **Gradual change.** The elements do not change suddenly. They change gradually. That is why we often feel a season before it officially arrives.
- **Dynamic balance.** Balance of the elements is not a fixed state but a process that keeps going. The goal is not perfect balance at every moment but a harmony that flows with the cycles of nature.

## Practical applications

### Changes to the home environment

- **Fire-dominant season (summer):** add coolness with blue and green colours, an indoor fountain or pictures of water bodies. Increase the earth element with plants and wooden furniture, so that excess fire is held in check.
- **Water-dominant season (winter):** to stop emotional heaviness and stagnation, bring in fire elements such as warm lighting, lamps and red or orange accents. Keep air moving too, so that the water element does not take over.
- **Earth-dominant season (autumn):** to avoid too much heaviness, add air elements such as wind chimes, open windows and lighter fabrics. Decorations that move help balance the fixed nature of earth.
- **Air-dominant season (spring):** to hold scattered energy, bring in earth elements such as stones and heavier fabrics. Water elements such as a small fountain are believed to help moisten the dry nature of air.

### Adjusting the daily routine

The daily routine can also be fitted to the element of the season.

- **In a fire season:** balance strong outward energy with cooling activities, such as sitting in moonlight or water-based activities. Tradition also speaks of keeping foods of a cooling nature, such as mint, cucumber and coconut, in the diet.
- **In a water season:** keep emotional heaviness away with energetic activities, breathing practice (pranayama) and pleasant social activity. Warming spices such as ginger, cinnamon and black pepper are regarded as balancing the cold nature of water.
- **In an earth season:** lighten rigidity with dance, spontaneous outings or creative work with no fixed outcome. To avoid heaviness, light grains and bitter greens are advised.
- **In an air season:** hold scattered energy with rhythmic habits such as meditating at the same time every day, regular meals and contact with the earth (gardening or walking barefoot). Root vegetables and protein-rich food are regarded as giving stability to a scattered state. These are suggestions from tradition, not medical advice.

## Common misconceptions

- **Misconception: one should always oppose the dominant element.** The goal is balance, not opposition. Sometimes it is right to support the element of the season, especially when the home or the person lacks that same element.
- **Misconception: balance is needed only in the extreme seasons.** More care is needed at the transitions between seasons, because the changing energies can bring restlessness.
- **Misconception: such adjustments are only superstition.** This is the language of tradition. Even so, the effect of weather, light and surroundings on mind and body is familiar from everyday experience.
- **Misconception: balance needs elaborate rituals or costly changes.** Simple changes in colour, scent and everyday activity are enough. No big upheaval in way of living is needed.

## Examples

### Example 1: handling excess fire in summer

Suppose someone has a home where restlessness and irritability rise in the summer. Summer is regarded as the fire season, so they might make these changes:

- paint the home office a cool blue;
- put a small fountain and plants on the desk, to add the water and earth elements;
- sit in quiet meditation in the evening or in moonlight.

This example has no measured result. It only shows how cooling elements are added in the fire season. The routine can be changed too, for instance a calm evening practice in place of strenuous morning activity, and food of a cooling nature.

### Example 2: going with the air element in spring

Suppose someone finds it hard to keep their thoughts in order in spring, which is regarded as the air season. Instead of opposing the season, they can lean on it:

- make a reading corner with good ventilation near a window;
- join a weekly discussion group to practise putting ideas across;
- fly kites or take walks on breezy days;
- do breathing exercises that focus on the out-breath.

This is an example of going along with the element of the season in order to strengthen that side of one's nature, instead of holding it back.

## Conclusion

Adjusting the elements by season is a practical framework for staying in step with the cycles of nature. Knowing that the emphasis of fire, earth, air and water changes with the seasons, we can adapt the atmosphere of the home and our routine with thought.

The strength of this system is its flexibility. It can be taken up at any level, from a simple change of colour to a plan for a whole way of living. Balance is not a destination but a process of adapting to changing conditions. This thinking teaches us to be alert both to the outer seasons and to our own inner needs.

You can choose among these measures to suit your convenience and the climate of your region. It is not necessary to make every change at once. Pick one or two simple changes to begin with, watch the effect for a few weeks, and then go on. That slow and attentive habit is the heart of the whole system.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I change the colours and decoration of my home every season?

Not necessarily. This is a suggestion from tradition. Simple changes in colour, light and decoration can be made according to the dominant element of the season, but no big expense or alteration is needed.

### How do I keep the home balanced in summer?

To handle excess fire, blue and green colours, plants and water-related decoration are added.

### How do I keep the home balanced in winter?

To stop the stagnation and heaviness that come from excess water, warm lighting, lamps, red or orange colours and good ventilation are used.

### Should I always oppose the dominant element of the season?

No. The goal is balance. Sometimes it is right to lean on that same element, especially when you feel its lack.

### What should I watch for between seasons?

At a transition the change comes gradually and is often felt early. If you feel uneasy then, check the balance of the elements in the home again.

### How do I begin?

Identify the element of the present season and notice how you feel in the home. Then make simple changes in colour, light and decoration and see how they work out.
