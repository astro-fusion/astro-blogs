# Space Element in Vastu: Brahmasthan and Open Space

## Introduction

The space element, Akasha, is the central element of Vastu Shastra. It is believed to be the foundation of every creation and every experience. In Sanskrit it is called Akasha. It is a symbol of emptiness, infinite potential and pure consciousness. Understanding the space element helps in making a place that supports clarity of mind, spiritual awareness and a harmonious life. For anyone who wants both practical and spiritual benefit in a home or workplace, its principles are necessary.

## What Is the Space Element in Vastu?

Akasha is the subtlest of the five great elements (Pancha Mahabhuta) described in the Vedic tradition. Akasha stands for the boundless void and the unmanifested potential from which the other four elements, air, fire, water and earth, are held to arise. In Vastu Shastra, Akasha is linked with the energy field that pervades every space and becomes the medium for the flow of sound, energy and consciousness.

In the structure of a building, the symbol of this central element is the Brahmasthan. It is the central open part of every house, regarded as the centre of the cosmos and the seat of spiritual energy.

## Key Aspects of the Space Element

### Emptiness and Infinite Potential

- Akasha is known by its emptiness. It is the place where creation begins and where endless possibilities rest.
- It is formless, subtle and all-pervading, and it gives the other elements the way to work and to join with one another.
- It is the fertile void, like a canvas on which positive energy can move and every experience can take shape.

### The Central Place: Brahmasthan

- In traditional Vastu architecture the Brahmasthan, the open space at the centre, is dedicated to the Akasha element.
- This central area should be kept empty, open and free of clutter, so that the flow of prana in the building is not obstructed.
- The Brahmasthan is regarded as the spiritual and energetic heart of the house. According to tradition it is linked with clarity of mind, intuition and peace.

### Proportions, Open Spaces and Their Effect

- To benefit from the space element, balanced proportions and the thoughtful use of open space are necessary.
- Large, unobstructed areas, especially at the centre and in the north and east, help natural light and air to come in. This is believed to increase positivity and comfort.
- Keeping symmetry and geometric harmony in the design increases the good influence of the space element, and is considered helpful for spiritual growth and inner peace.

## Method of Checking

The checking and improving of the space element does not involve astrological calculation. It is based on analysis of the building and the space.

- Identify the Brahmasthan (the central part) of your building or plot and see that it is open and free of heavy construction.
- See whether the plan has symmetry, proportion and open paths through which energy can easily move.
- Check how much natural light and air is coming in, especially from the north and east.
- Keep clutter low in the main parts, and use light-enhancing materials so that the space feels open and spacious.

## How to Read the Signs

The presence and quality of the space element in a building is understood in this way.

- An open, well-lit and orderly central space is considered a sign of strong Akasha influence. According to tradition it helps with peace of mind, inspiration and spiritual growth.
- A Brahmasthan that is blocked, heavy or cluttered is considered an obstruction to the flow of consciousness. It is believed that this can bring stagnation, confusion or restlessness.
- To restore balance, tradition describes remedies such as removing clutter, putting up mirrors or using light colours.

## Practical Applications

### Designing Homes and Workplaces

- Architects, interior designers and home owners can adopt the principles of Akasha. For this, keep open space at the centre, avoid pillars or heavy furniture in the Brahmasthan and keep natural light at the maximum.
- Placing windows, glass doors and a small amount of furniture thoughtfully increases the subtle quality of Akasha, which is believed to help clarity of mind and comfort.

### Supporting Meditation and Spiritual Practice

- Keep the central or open part of the house as a place for meditation or worship. It is believed that the Akasha energy there increases spiritual awareness and peace.
- Such a place helps with reflection and the expansion of consciousness, and joins a person with universal energy.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Myth:** The space element is only about physical emptiness.
  - *Clarification:* It is also a concrete idea of the structure of a building (an open, orderly central space) and a subtle principle of energy, which is believed to be linked with consciousness and comfort.
- **Myth:** Taking furniture away from anywhere sets right the space element.
  - *Clarification:* The Brahmasthan in particular is what matters. Even in open space, a balance of usefulness and comfort should be kept.
- **Myth:** The effect of this element is only spiritual.
  - *Clarification:* A balanced space element also improves practical matters such as ventilation, light and clarity of mind, as well as helping meditation and peace.

## Examples

- **Example 1:** A traditional house built according to Vastu has an open, sunlit hall at the centre (the Brahmasthan), without pillars, with a line of sight to the other rooms. People living in such a house are said to experience clarity and peace of mind.
- **Example 2:** In an open-plan office with few partitions and a thoughtful use of glass, it is believed that creativity, working together and lower stress are supported. This is one example of the practical use of the space element.

## Conclusion

The space element in Vastu is basic to making a place that supports not only physical health but also clarity of mind and spiritual comfort. By paying attention to the qualities of emptiness, potential and consciousness, and by keeping the central part open and orderly, the benefit of this subtle element can be taken. When it is adopted through design, proportion and management of space, the surroundings change, and both everyday life and higher awareness are helped.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is the space element different from the other elements?

Earth, water, fire and air are comparatively solid elements, while space is pure place and potential, the base from which the other energies and experiences arise. In Vastu it governs the basic structure of the house and the central Brahmasthan, while the other elements are linked with particular directions and functions.

### Where is the place of the space element in a house?

The centre of the house, the Brahmasthan. It is advised to keep it open, empty and free of clutter, so that the flow of prana continues.

### What is believed if the Brahmasthan is spoiled or full?

According to tradition, a Brahmasthan that is blocked, heavy or cluttered obstructs the flow of consciousness and can bring stagnation, confusion or restlessness. Removing clutter, using light colours and increasing light are the traditional corrections.

### Is it necessary to know my birth time for the space element?

No. This element is applied through the structure of the building and its surroundings, not through astrology, so personal birth details are not needed.

### How do I start keeping the space element right?

Remove clutter from the central part of the home or workplace, let in natural light and adopt an open plan. Keep balance, symmetry and frequent fresh air in mind.

### Does open space mean removing all the furniture?

No. The Brahmasthan is what matters most, and even in open space a balance of usefulness and comfort should be kept.
