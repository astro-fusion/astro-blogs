# The Five Elements in the Vastu Purusha Mandala

## Introduction

Placing the five elements on the Vastu Purusha Mandala is a basic idea of Vastu Shastra, the ancient Indian tradition of building. According to this tradition, the five elements (earth, water, fire, air and space) each dominate different parts of a plot and a building. A home laid out in line with this division is said, in Vastu tradition, to support the health, prosperity and peace of mind of those who live in it.

## What are the elemental zones of the Mandala?

The Vastu Purusha Mandala is a sacred geometric grid. It is usually drawn as a square and divided into 81 or 64 padas (small squares). In tradition the square stands for the cosmic being, the Vastu Purusha, and each segment is linked to an energy or a deity.

The five elements are earth (Prithvi), water (Jal), fire (Agni), air (Vayu) and space (Akash). Vastu assigns them to different directional zones. This assignment is the basis for deciding which room or activity goes where in a building.

## Key aspects

### Where the five elements sit

- **Earth:** dominates the south-west zone. It gives stability and grounding.
- **Water:** governs the north-east. It is linked with flow, prosperity and mental clarity.
- **Fire:** resides in the south-east. It is linked with transformation, vitality and digestion, which is why the south-east is considered suitable for a kitchen.
- **Air:** occupies the north-west. It is the zone of movement, communication and networking.
- **Space:** the central Brahmasthan, which stands for expansion, wisdom and spiritual openness.

Each element gives its own character to its zone. That is why certain activities are considered best suited to certain areas.

### Elements and directions

The Mandala is said to divide space into 16 zones, each dominated by one element and its direction. The main corners have the following character:

- **North-east (water):** the zone of spirituality and mental clarity.
- **South-east (fire):** the zone of vigour, activity and transformation.
- **South-west (earth):** the zone of stability, strength and relationships.
- **North-west (air):** the zone of change, support and social interaction.
- **Centre (space):** the zone of openness, wisdom and growth.

The deities and energies of the Mandala are said to reinforce the character of each zone.

### Where zones meet

Where the zones of two elements meet, the energy is lively. Harmony between them supports balance, while clashing energies can produce disturbance. Two examples:

- **Water and fire:** the north-east and the south-east hold opposing elements and need careful planning. For example, do not place water features in the south-east.
- **Earth and air:** the south-west and the north-west call for a balance of stability and flexibility. For that reason, avoid very heavy objects in the north-west.

Vastu rules aim to keep this balance, so that supportive elements are strengthened and opposing influences are kept small.

## How to place the elements on a plan

Mapping the elemental zones on a plot or floor plan goes as follows.

1. **Draw the Mandala grid.** Lay a square grid (for example 9 by 9) over the plot or floor plan.
2. **Align the directions.** Turn the grid so that its sides match north, south, east and west exactly.
3. **Assign the elements.** North-east: water. South-east: fire. South-west: earth. North-west: air. Centre: space.
4. **Allocate rooms.** Give each zone the rooms and functions that suit its element.

No complex mathematics is involved. It is a straightforward assignment based on compass orientation and the division of the space.

## Reading a zone

- **Harmony:** an activity that matches the element of its zone is considered good, for example a kitchen in the south-east (fire).
- **Conflict:** a clashing activity, for example a bathroom in the north-east (water zone), is said to bring discomfort or imbalance.
- **Balancing:** colours, materials or decoration that match the element of a zone are used, for example blue tones in the north-eastern water zone.

Look at each part of the plan with both its element and its direction in mind. That is how the flow of energy and the usefulness of the room are said to come right.

## Practical applications

### Placing rooms

- Place the kitchen in the south-east (fire zone). Tradition holds that this keeps vitality up.
- Place bedrooms in the south-west (earth zone), considered the zone of stability and restful sleep.
- Keep the north-east (water zone) for prayer or meditation. It is believed to support mental clarity and spiritual strength.

### Handling a weak zone

- Vastu tradition uses colour. Red accents in a fire zone and blue in a water zone are said to strengthen that element.
- Simple aids such as mirrors or plants are also mentioned for reviving an inactive or imbalanced zone.

The aim of all this is that the layout of the home or workplace follows the order of the elements.

## Common misconceptions

- **Misconception 1: rooms can be placed anywhere regardless of element.** Vastu holds that room placement should match the elemental zone so that the energy stays harmonious.
- **Misconception 2: the centre (Brahmasthan) can be cluttered or built upon.** The centre should stay open and free of heavy structures. In tradition this preserves its energy.
- **Misconception 3: Vastu is only superstition.** Vastu is a traditional system, regarded as drawn from long experience of direction, light, air and the use of space. It is best understood as tradition.

## Examples

- **Example 1:** a home with the kitchen in the south-east and a meditation space in the north-east is regarded in Vastu tradition as a balanced home.
- **Example 2:** if a bathroom falls in the north-east (water zone), tradition says mental clarity may suffer there. The remedy described is to reorganise the space or to dress the zone with blue shades and water-related decoration.

## Conclusion

Knowing the elemental zones of the Vastu Purusha Mandala makes it easier to keep a building in harmony with the five basic elements. According to tradition, this is the basis of well-being and peace in a home. Placing the elements correctly on the plan is the central task of Vastu.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which direction does each element have in the Vastu Purusha Mandala?

Water in the north-east, fire in the south-east, earth in the south-west, air in the north-west and space in the centre, the Brahmasthan. The north is linked with Kubera and wealth.

### Which direction suits the kitchen?

The south-east, because it is the fire zone, and fire is linked with digestion and energy.

### Which room suits the north-east?

A prayer or meditation space. It is the water zone, linked with mental clarity. A toilet is not considered suitable there.

### How should the centre of the home be kept?

The centre, the Brahmasthan, should stay open. Avoid heavy construction or clutter there.

### What simple ways of balancing the elements does tradition mention?

Colours and materials that match the element of the zone, for example a light use of blue in a water zone and red in a fire zone. For detailed advice, consult a Vastu expert.

### What do I need to work out the zones?

A compass and a plan of the plot or house. No birth chart or birth time is needed, because the Mandala is laid out on the direction and position of the building.
