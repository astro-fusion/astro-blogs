# Pooja Room Vastu: Direction, Size, Idol Position, Colours

## Introduction

A pooja room is more than a room. Vastu tradition regards it as the spiritual centre of the home. According to Vastu Shastra, the direction and design of the pooja room matter for positive energy, harmony in the home and a deeper spiritual practice. This guide brings together the traditional rules with practical steps for making a calm and orderly place of worship in your home.

## What are the Vastu rules for the pooja room?

They are the set of Vastu guidelines that decide the place, direction, design and decoration of the sacred space in a home. According to the tradition, these rules bring the pooja room into line with cosmic energies, so that prayer and meditation are held to be more effective. The main points are the exact direction of the room, the correct position and height of the idols, the ideal size of the room and the choice of tranquil colours.

## Key aspects of pooja room Vastu

### Ideal direction: the north-east

- In Vastu tradition the north-east (Ishan Kona) is regarded as the most auspicious direction for a pooja room. It is said that this zone draws in positive and purifying energy and supports spiritual growth.
- If the north-east is not available, the north and the east are the alternatives. These directions are linked with prosperity, clarity and new beginnings.
- The south, the south-west, the space under a staircase and the space next to a bathroom should be avoided for the pooja room. It is believed that they disturb the spiritual atmosphere of the room.

### Recommended size

- The suggested size of a pooja room is **5×7 feet** or **6×8 feet**. This size balances usefulness with a sense of intimacy and gives a calm, orderly setting.
- Even where space is limited, keeping to these proportions allows prayer and meditation to go on without disturbance.

### Idol position and the direction they face

- Keep the idols at least **6 inches above ground level**, ideally on a marble or wooden platform, to keep respect and a good flow of energy.
- The preferred arrangement is that the idols face **west**, which means the devotee faces east while praying. This matches the direction of the rising sun.
- Leave a small gap between the idol and the wall so that air and energy can move. Never place idols facing each other or facing the entrance of the room, because tradition holds that this creates conflict.

## How to decide the direction and size

To decide the right direction and design of a pooja room, some practical measuring goes along with the Vastu rules.

1. Use a compass or the floor plan of the house to identify the north-east, north and east corners.
2. Measure a clear area of at least 5×7 or 6×8 feet for the pooja room.
3. Choose the wall for the idols, ideally the east or north-east wall, and build a sturdy platform at least 6 inches above the ground. The most suitable arrangement is the idol facing west and the devotee facing east.
4. Make sure natural light comes into the room.
5. Paint the room in light, calm shades such as yellow, pink or blue, so that calm and focus are kept.

## Why these points matter in Vastu

- **A place in the north-east**: tradition considers it the best for the spiritual purpose, bringing harmony and calm.
- **Correct idol height and position**: this keeps respect in worship and is believed to keep the energy in harmony.
- **The devotee facing east**: it is said to keep a person in line with the energy of the sun and of life.
- **Soft pastel colours**: they create a peaceful mood and add to the meditative feeling of the place.
- **A clean, uncluttered room**: it is necessary to keep the atmosphere sacred and positive.

If something is not in line with Vastu, for example a place in an unsuitable direction or dark, harsh colours, simple fixes are advised, such as moving the idols or repainting the room.

## Practical applications

### Building a new pooja room

- When building a new home, give the north-east corner priority for the pooja room and keep to the recommended size.

### Improving an existing space

- If the pooja room is already in the wrong place, small changes such as changing the direction of the idols, using suitable colours or adding a partition are held to correct the flow of energy without major construction.

## Common misconceptions

- **Any room can be a pooja room**: Vastu stresses specific auspicious directions and not just any space that is available.
- **Idols can be placed directly on the floor**: height is considered necessary for respect and for the flow of energy.
- **Colour makes no difference**: dark or harsh colours can disturb calm, so choose lighter shades.
- **Prayer can be done facing any direction**: Vastu advises that the devotee face east.

## Examples

- **Example 1**: A devotee makes a pooja room of 6×8 feet in the north-east corner of her home. She keeps marble idols on a platform 10 inches high, facing west. The room is light blue and morning sunlight comes in. This is an illustration of a calm, orderly prayer space.
- **Example 2**: A family with little space keeps a small mandir on the east wall of their living room. They use light yellow paint and follow the Vastu rules. The family faces east while praying, and the idols are raised and kept a little away from the wall.

## Conclusion

The Vastu rules for the direction and design of a pooja room are considered basic to making a sacred, positive and calm place at home. The choice of the north-east, north or east, the right position of the idols, the recommended size and calm colours together are meant to improve the spiritual and energetic quality of the living space. These choices honour tradition and are a thoughtful way of making a quiet place for daily worship.

## FAQs

### Which is the best direction for a pooja room?

The north-east (Ishan Kona) is considered the most auspicious. If it is not available, the north or the east can be chosen.

### Where should a pooja room not be built?

Avoid the south, the south-west, the space under a staircase and the space next to a bathroom.

### Which way should the idols face, and at what height should they be kept?

Keep the idols on a marble or wooden platform at least 6 inches above the ground. They should face west, so that the devotee faces east. The idols should not face each other or the entrance, and a small gap should be left between them and the wall.

### How large should a pooja room be?

The suggested size is 5×7 feet or 6×8 feet. If space is limited, keep these proportions in mind.

### Which colours are good in a pooja room?

Light, calm colours such as yellow, pink and blue are considered good. Avoid dark or harsh colours.

### What if there is little space for a pooja room?

A small mandir can be placed on the east wall of the living room. The idols should be raised and kept a little away from the wall, the colour should be light, and the devotee should face east.
