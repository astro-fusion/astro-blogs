# Bathroom and Toilet Vastu: Right Direction and Design

## Introduction

In Vastu Shastra the position of the bathroom and the toilet is considered important for the flow of energy in a home. According to Vastu tradition every part of the house affects our health, prosperity and harmony. Bathrooms and toilets are places of cleansing and disposal, so tradition asks for special care in where they are put, to keep the energy positive and to keep unwanted influences away. This article explains the link between the position of the bathroom and the energy balance of the home, and gives practical guidance for a home that follows Vastu.

## What are bathroom and toilet Vastu rules?

This means deciding the position of these facilities in a home according to Vastu Shastra, the ancient Indian science of building. Vastu rests on the influence of directions and on the five elements (earth, water, fire, air and space), which are said to govern the different corners of a house. In Vastu thinking, bathrooms and toilets are places of disposal, both physical and energetic, and their correct position is held to send waste matter and negative energy away from the living spaces.

It is not only a question of position. The rules also look at the energy of the directions, the relation of the elements, and the relation of the disposal area to other important rooms such as the kitchen, the bedroom and the prayer room. According to tradition, when the position is right, the flow of energy that supports health, wealth and happiness in the house is not disturbed.

## Key Vastu rules

### Importance of direction

In Vastu Shastra the directions considered most favourable for the bathroom and toilet are the north-west and the west. The north-west is linked with the air element, so it is regarded as good for a toilet, because it is said to help ventilation and the release of blocked emotions and negativity from the mind. The west, linked with the water element, supports cleanliness and hygiene.

Some directions should be strictly avoided. The north-east (Ishanya) corner is considered the most sacred in Vastu and should not have a bathroom or toilet, because it is said to disturb the spiritual energy of the house. The south-east, linked with the fire element, clashes with the purpose of cleanliness and purity of a bathroom, and is also avoided.

### Relation with other rooms

The relation of the bathroom and toilet with other rooms is just as important. For bedrooms with an attached bathroom, the advice is to put the bathroom on the west or north side of the room, so that the flow of energy stays harmonious. A toilet should never be built next to, above or below a pooja room (prayer space), because tradition calls this a serious conflict of energy.

The door of the toilet should not open facing the main entrance or the kitchen. It is believed that this can lead to health trouble and an imbalance of energy. Keeping a distance between living spaces and disposal areas is considered necessary for the purity of each zone.

### Rules about construction

Keep the slope of the bathroom floor towards the east or north, so that water flows in these directions. According to Vastu belief, water flowing in these directions carries away impurities and negativity.

## How to decide the right place

Vastu has no complicated mathematics, but choosing the right place means looking carefully at the layout and direction of the house. Take these steps to find the ideal place.

1. **Find the direction of your home**: use a compass to see which direction your house faces.
2. **Draw a map of directions**: divide the house in your mind into nine equal parts (a 3×3 grid), so that the eight directions and the centre can be identified.
3. **Mark the zones**: treat the north-west and the west as the possible favourable places for a bathroom.
4. **Check distances**: the bathroom should not be exactly at the corner but slightly away from the pure point of the direction.
5. **Check relations**: see that the proposed place does not clash with important spaces such as the kitchen, the pooja room or the main entrance.
6. **Check the direction of the seat**: the toilet seat should be on a north-south axis, so that the person using it faces north or south.

## What is believed from the Vastu point of view

- **A good position in the north-west or west**: it is regarded as helping a good flow of energy and the removal of physical waste and negative energy from the house.
- **A bathroom in the north-east**: tradition regards this as very inauspicious. It is said that it can bring financial loss, health trouble and family disagreement. Remedies are advised to be considered soon.
- **A toilet facing east or west**: in place of north or south, this is regarded as an imbalance of energy for the health and well-being of the family.
- **A bathroom directly above or below a sacred place such as a pooja room**: this is regarded as a serious Vastu fault.
- **Correct drainage**: these are considered in line with the natural flow of energy, while a wrong slope is said to bring stagnation.

These are beliefs of the tradition and not a promise of any result. Remember that Vastu is holistic: the position of the bathroom and toilet should be seen together with the flow of energy of the whole house.

## Practical applications

### For a bedroom in the south-west

For bedrooms in the south-west corner of the house, the ideal bathroom is regarded as being in the north-west corner of the room. In this arrangement the south-west (the earth element and stability) is balanced with the north-west (air and movement).

The steps to follow are these.

- Keep the door of the bathroom in the north or east.
- Keep the toilet seat facing north or south.
- Use light-coloured tiles or marble for the floor.
- For ventilation, the window should ideally be in the east or north.

### For a south-facing bedroom

The general Vastu rule is to avoid a bathroom in the south-east. In a south-facing room, keep the bathroom towards the north-west or west, and keep these points in mind.

- Fix the toilet seat in the west, south or north-west part of the bathroom.
- Keep the slope of the floor towards the east or north.
- Keep a floor of light-coloured marble or tile.
- Keep taps and water storage in the corners that are east, north or north-east.
- Keep the bathroom well ventilated, with the window ideally in the east.

### For general design

Whatever the position of the bedroom, these rules apply to all.

- Use wooden doors for the bathroom instead of metal.
- Choose light colours for the walls, such as light blue, pink, grey, white, beige or cream.
- Avoid dark colours such as black, dark blue or red, because they are believed to attract negativity.
- Keep good ventilation for the movement of air and to prevent damp.
- Keep the bathroom clean and orderly to keep the energy positive.

## Common misconceptions

**Misconception 1: a bathing area alone in the north-east does no harm.** The north-east corner is not considered right for a bathroom or a toilet, even if it is used only for bathing.

**Misconception 2: Vastu faults cannot be corrected.** The right position is ideal, but in older buildings measures to reduce the fault can be thought of, such as correcting the direction of the seat, improving ventilation and keeping things clean. A change in the structure may be needed to correct the flow of energy.

**Misconception 3: a toilet under a staircase is always inauspicious.** According to some Vastu experts, a toilet can be under a staircase if it is in the right zone, that is the north-west or the west. The zone matters more than the position under the staircase.

**Misconception 4: all Vastu rules are equally important.** Some rules matter more. The direction of the toilet, its relation to sacred places and the direction of drainage are the basic points, while colours and accessories are secondary.

## Examples

### Example 1: Renovating the bathroom of a west-facing house

A family renovated the main bathroom of their west-facing home. According to the Vastu rules, they did the following.

- Put the toilet in the north-west corner of the bathroom.
- Put the seat on a north-south axis, so that the user faces north.
- Laid a floor of light beige marble with a gentle slope towards the north.
- Put a window in the north wall for ventilation.
- Placed the entrance on the east side of the bathroom with a wooden door.
- Placed water fittings in the north-east corner.

This is an illustration of an arrangement and not a promise of any result.

### Example 2: Correcting a bathroom fault in a south-facing flat

A family found that in their south-facing flat the bathroom was in the north-east corner, which Vastu regards as a major fault. They could not move the bathroom because of structural limits, so they took these measures.

- Changed the direction of the toilet seat so that it faces north.
- Chose light blue for the walls and white marble for the floor.
- Improved ventilation with an exhaust fan in the east wall.
- Kept the bathroom very clean and orderly.

This place is not considered as good as the ideal position, and these changes are described as ways of reducing the effect.

## Conclusion

Bathroom and toilet Vastu is regarded as an important part of making a home that suits health, prosperity and happiness. Vastu Shastra gives clear rules about the ideal position, that is the north-west or the west, and warns against putting these facilities in the north-east or directly next to sacred spaces.

The rules do not stop at position. They also cover direction of drainage, arrangement inside and relation with other rooms. They are easiest to follow at the time of construction, but in existing buildings too measures to reduce a fault can be considered.

The final aim of Vastu is harmony between people and their surroundings. A bathroom and toilet in the right place are regarded as helping that harmony.

## FAQs

### In which direction is it good to build a bathroom and toilet?

In Vastu the north-west and the west are considered favourable. The north-east and the south-east should be avoided.

### Which way should the toilet seat face?

Keep the seat on a north-south axis, so that the user faces north or south. Facing east or west is not considered right.

### Which way should the bathroom floor slope?

Towards the east or north, so that water flows in these directions.

### Can a toilet be built near a pooja room?

No. A toilet should not be built next to, above or below a pooja room. Its door should also not open facing the main entrance or the kitchen.

### What should the door and colours of a bathroom be like?

The door should be wooden and not metal. Light colours such as light blue, pink, grey, white, beige or cream are fine for the walls. Avoid black, dark blue and red.

### What can I do if a bathroom in the north-east cannot be removed?

Correct the direction of the seat, improve ventilation, keep light colours, and keep the bathroom clean and orderly. For a major fault you can take advice from a qualified Vastu expert.
