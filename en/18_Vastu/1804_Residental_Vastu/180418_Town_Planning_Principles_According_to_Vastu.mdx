# Town Planning Principles According to Vastu

## Introduction

Vastu town planning brings old Indian building tradition together with present-day design for urban living. It aims to fit man-made surroundings to the forces of nature, and tradition links it with prosperity, health and a sense of community. This article sets out the principles of Vastu for the direction of a town, its roads and streets, and the arrangement of its buildings and open spaces.

## What Town Planning According to Vastu Means

These are guidelines drawn from Vastu Shastra, the traditional Indian science of architecture, and applied to urban design. They bear on the choice of site, orientation, street layout, zoning and the use of natural elements, with the aim of towns that support well-being, sustainability and positive energy. Vastu town planning is tied to Vedic ideas of the cosmos, which it turns into practical rules for the formation and growth of a city.

## Key Aspects of Town Planning Under Vastu

### Direction of the Town

- A town should ideally face the north or the east.
- The north and east are considered auspicious because they allow positive energy, sunlight and prosperity to come in. These directions are linked with the deities of wealth and growth, so that the whole community is thought to gain from auspicious influences.
- According to Vastu tradition, for a town on a river the water should flow from west to east or from south to north, which is considered a better match with the natural elements.

### Layout of Main Roads and Streets

- Main roads, often called Raja Marg, are best laid out east to west so that the sun's rays fall on them as much as possible. Shorter roads generally run north to south and cross the main roads at right angles, dividing the settlement into blocks by function.
- Streets should be straight and wide and cross at 90 degrees, for easy movement, better air circulation and sunlight throughout the town.
- Central squares and radial layouts, such as the Nandyavarta and Sarvatobhadra forms, are described as giving connection, access and flow of energy to all residents.

### Arranging Buildings for Sunlight and Air

- The design puts strong emphasis on open spaces, with buildings arranged so that they do not block natural light and air.
- Homes and other structures are ideally set so that the north-east, north and east stay open and uncluttered, which is said to help the movement of positive energy.
- Green spaces and water features, and avoiding tall construction in the auspicious corners, are held to improve ventilation and light while respecting the Vastu guidelines for balance of the elements.

## How to Decide the Plan of a Town

Working out the ideal town layout by Vastu involves the following.

- A study of the land, the environmental features (water bodies, wind direction) and the geomagnetic position.
- Using a compass or digital tools to find the main directions and mark the main axes of roads and entrances.
- Allotting zones for homes, temples, markets and administrative centres according to the auspiciousness of each direction. For example, community spaces are placed in the centre, water features in the north-east and heavier buildings in the south-west.
- Keeping a balance of the five elements (earth, water, fire, air, space) in every zone.

## How a Vastu-Based Town Is Viewed

Practitioners regard a layout that follows Vastu as favouring:

- shared prosperity and lasting well-being for all residents;
- social harmony, security and preservation of culture, owing to balanced open spaces and well-placed public amenities;
- a better flow of energy, which is believed to ease the strain of city life.

Non-compliance or imbalance, such as blocking the north-east or irregular street patterns, is taken in tradition as a cause of stagnation, illness or social friction.

## Putting It into Practice

### Sustainable Urban Neighbourhoods

Planners use these principles in designing new urban neighbourhoods by:

- aligning master plans to the main directions;
- providing central green parks, wide streets and zones for temples and water bodies;
- promoting walking, social contact and energy efficiency.

### Improving Existing Towns

In existing towns, partial use of Vastu is described as a way to ease blocked energy by:

- opening up the north-east through demolition, landscaping or water features;
- reorienting entrances, roads or community spaces where possible so that they follow the Vastu directions.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Vastu is relevant only to individual homes.** In fact it is deeply rooted in town and urban planning, as the ancient Indian cities show.
- **Vastu ignores modern sustainability or safety rules.** Modern planners combine Vastu with present-day standards to make a livable city.
- **The principles are rigid.** Vastu allows adaptation to the limits of the site and to climate, and puts harmony before dogma.

## Examples

- **Ancient Indian cities:** The city of Jaipur is often cited as a city planned on Vastu principles. Its grid layout, central temples, broad avenues and orientation to the main directions are seen as a living example of harmonious town planning.
- **Modern Vastu-inspired communities:** Recent master plans include central parks (Brahmasthana), pedestrian-friendly streets and green corridors aligned to the main axes, with public amenities placed for access and balance.

## Conclusion

The town planning principles of Vastu are a long-standing blueprint for communities meant to be prosperous, healthy and harmonious. By giving priority to an auspicious orientation, sensible street layouts, ample open space and the use of natural elements, Vastu Shastra offers a path for urban growth that is sustainable. These principles remain of interest to architects and planners today who look for a whole approach to urban development.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which direction should a town face according to Vastu?

Ideally the north or the east. These directions are considered auspicious because they allow positive energy, sunlight and prosperity to come in.

### In which direction should the main roads run?

The main roads, often called Raja Marg, are best laid out east to west so that the sun's rays fall on them. Shorter roads generally run north to south and cross the main roads at right angles.

### How should buildings be placed?

Buildings should be arranged so that they do not block light and air, and the north-east, north and east should stay open and uncluttered. Green spaces and water features are encouraged.

### Can Vastu measures be used in towns that already exist?

Yes, in part. The north-east can be opened up through demolition, landscaping or water features, and entrances, roads or community spaces can be reoriented where possible.

### Are Vastu rules rigid?

No. Vastu allows adaptation to the limits of the site and to climate, and puts harmony before dogma.

### Where can I start to learn about it?

Begin with directions and basic street layouts in Vastu. Look at examples such as Jaipur, and take advice from Vastu architects to understand how the ideas are applied in practice.
