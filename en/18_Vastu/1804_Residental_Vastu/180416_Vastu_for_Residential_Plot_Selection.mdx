# Choosing a Residential Plot by Vastu: Shape, Slope and Soil

## Introduction

Choosing a plot is the first step in building a home, and Vastu Shastra treats it as the base on which every later decision rests. The tradition holds that a plot that follows Vastu sets the stage for a peaceful and settled home. This article sets out the guidelines for judging a plot: its shape, slope, soil, surroundings and the direction it faces.

## What Is Vastu for Residential Plot Selection

It is the set of guidelines in Vastu Shastra for judging, choosing and preparing land for building a home. The principles are concerned with the distribution of energy, directions and natural elements on a site. Vastu tradition treats the plot as the foundation of every Vastu decision that follows, even more basic than the design of the building.

## Key Aspects of Plot Vastu

### Ideal Shapes: Square and Rectangular

- **Square plots:** They stand for stability and balance. In Vastu, square plots are the most auspicious, because energy is spread equally and there is little stagnation or disturbance.
- **Rectangular plots:** These are also considered highly favourable, especially when the length is not more than twice the width, that is, within a ratio of 2 to 1. Such plots allow a smooth flow of energy and flexibility in design.
- **Irregular or odd-shaped plots:** Triangular, L-shaped, circular plots or plots with cut corners are generally considered inauspicious, and the tradition links them with imbalance of energy and friction in the family. Changes in the building or landscaping are suggested as remedies.

### Slope and Height

- **Favourable slope:** A plot that slopes towards the north or east is considered good, and the north-east is the most favoured. The tradition links such a slope with the flow of positive energy and prosperity.
- **Unfavourable slope:** A slope towards the south or west should be avoided. It is believed to bring instability and negative energy.
- **Height:** Land that is high in the south or south-west and falls gradually towards the north or east is considered better.

### Quality of Soil

- **Testing the soil:** Good soil is the basis of a strong foundation. In the traditional Vastu method a pit is dug and filled back with the same soil. If soil is left over after filling, it is taken as a sign of good quality.
- **Colour and texture:** Light-coloured, fertile soil with a pleasant smell is considered best. Avoid land with clayey or sandy soil that holds too much water or cannot carry solid construction.
- **Earlier use:** Avoid a plot that was once a burial ground, a rubbish dump or an industrial site. Tradition holds that old negative energy can remain there.

### The Surroundings

- **Good surroundings:** Choose a plot near greenery, parks and open land, where air and sunlight arrive well.
- **Negative influences:** Avoid land near cemeteries, hospitals, rubbish dumps or places with a bad history.
- **Hills and tall structures:** A hill or mound in the south-west is thought to give protection, while one in the north or east is believed to block the flow of positive energy.

### Road Direction and Entrance

- **Direction:** North-facing and east-facing plots are considered the most auspicious for a home. They are described as welcoming growth and well-being.
- **South-facing and west-facing plots:** These are less favourable, but can be improved by fixing the entrance with care or by planting trees and shrubs.
- **Avoid T-junctions:** A plot at a T-junction, where a road ends facing the plot, is generally avoided because the flow of energy there is considered disturbed.

## How to Check a Plot

Vastu uses no specific mathematical formula. The process is systematic and based on observation, guided by established Vastu rules.

1. **Study the shape.** Measure the plot for square or rectangular proportions, with symmetry and right angles.
2. **Check the slope.** Look at the natural slope of the land. The preference is towards the north or east.
3. **Test the soil.** Dig a pit, fill it back and see whether soil is left over, which is taken as a sign of good quality.
4. **Find the compass directions.** Use a compass to find the orientation of the plot against the main directions.
5. **Survey the surroundings.** Look at the neighbourhood for helpful and unhelpful influences, including natural features and neighbouring structures.

## What the Findings Mean

- **Ideal plots:** A square or rectangular plot facing north or east, with fertile soil and good surroundings, is regarded in tradition as having strong potential for a harmonious home.
- **Irregular plots:** They may need corrections. Consult a Vastu expert for remedies.
- **Unfavourable slope or soil:** These point to possible difficulties. See whether changes to the building or landscaping can reduce them.
- **Surroundings:** Negative surroundings are held to outweigh good features of the plot, so the whole picture must be considered.

## Putting It into Practice

### Choosing Land for a New Home

When buying land, a buyer can use the Vastu guidelines to judge whether a plot is suitable.

- Check the shape, slope and soil quality of the plot.
- Look at the neighbourhood for harmony, safety and access to sunlight.
- Avoid plots with obvious Vastu defects unless remedies are possible.

### Improving a Plot with Shortcomings

If you are already committed to a plot with minor Vastu problems, tradition suggests these measures.

- Use landscaping such as trees and fences, changes to the structure, or a realigned main entrance to deal with imbalance of energy.
- Strengthen the north-east with a garden or a water feature.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Any plot can be corrected.** Some defects are easy to remedy, but certain shapes or locations, for example next to a graveyard, may be difficult in themselves.
- **South-facing plots are always bad.** They are less favoured, but careful adjustments can reduce the unfavourable effects.
- **Vastu is only about the design of the building.** Choosing the plot is even more basic, because it is the foundation for every later Vastu decision.

## Examples

- **Example 1:** A family buys a rectangular, east-facing plot with brown, fertile soil and gardens to the north. By Vastu tradition such a plot is regarded as favourable.
- **Example 2:** Another buyer takes a triangular plot near a hospital. Even if changes are made inside the house, a basic flaw in the plot is hard to correct later.

## Conclusion

Choosing the plot is a key step in setting up a home by Vastu. By giving priority to a good shape, a favourable slope, sound soil, good surroundings and a favourable direction, you lay a strong base that follows Vastu Shastra. Careful choice of the plot also affects how every corner of the home is arranged later.
