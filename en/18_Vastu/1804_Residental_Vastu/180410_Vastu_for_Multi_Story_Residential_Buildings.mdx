# Vastu for Multi-Storey Buildings: Staircase and Brahmasthan

## Introduction

Vastu for multi-storey residential buildings is the use of the ancient Indian science of Vastu Shastra, adapted to the demands of living in tall buildings today. As cities grow upward, many people want to know how multi-storey homes and apartment complexes can follow Vastu. According to the tradition, this matters for the well-being and peace of the residents. This article explains the main points: the open centre on every floor, the staircase, the use of each floor, and the direction of balconies and windows.

## What is Vastu for multi-storey residential buildings?

It is the method of applying the rules of Vastu Shastra to the design, plan and arrangement of homes with several floors and of apartment complexes. Traditionally Vastu was made for a single house. Because of the modern trend of living in tall buildings, special guidelines are now followed so that the flow of positive energy can be kept across several floors. The tradition holds that the physical home should support the energy of the people living in it.

## Key points

### Vertical zoning and distribution of energy

In a multi-storey building the division from bottom to top has to be thought out, so that the energy of every floor stays balanced. The lower floors are considered more stable and solid, so they suit heavier uses such as storage or parking. The upper floors suit lighter activities and living spaces. The flow of energy should be continuous, and open spaces and light wells are said to help circulation. Keeping the Brahmasthan, the central energy core of the building, free of obstruction on all floors is regarded as important for the well-being of everyone.

### Position of the staircase

The staircase is regarded as the path of energy between floors. Ideally the staircase should be in the south, west or south-west zone and should rise clockwise. Do not build a staircase in the north-east, because it is believed to block the arrival of positive energy. Keeping the staircases in line on all floors is said to strengthen the circulation of energy and the stability of the building.

### Use of each floor

Each floor of a multi-storey home can be divided according to particular uses.

- Ground floor or lobby: common areas, reception or parking (north, east)
- Middle floors: living rooms and bedrooms (south-west, south, west)
- Upper floors: entertainment and family rooms (north, east)
- Roof: recreation, garden, water tank (do not spread things over the Brahmasthan)

Keeping rooms in the right position on every floor, for example the kitchen in the south-east and the bedroom in the south-west, is said to keep harmony and to avoid conflict or stagnation.

### Direction of balconies and windows

The direction of balconies and main windows is regarded as having a direct effect on light, air and energy. A balcony facing north or east invites good morning light and positive energy into the home. A balcony facing south or west may need shade or plants to soften harsh energy. Large, uncluttered windows in these directions are said to add to liveliness and reduce stagnation.

### Keeping the Brahmasthan open

The Brahmasthan, the central zone, should stay open and free of load on every floor. Do not place heavy furniture, a staircase, a lift or a structural column at the centre, because it is believed that this stops the upward and outward flow of prana (life energy). On every level a clear Brahmasthan is considered auspicious and vital for the well-being of all residents.

## How to plan

To plan a multi-storey building by Vastu, start with a clear, square or rectangular plot facing north, east or north-east. Divide the whole plan into nine or sixteen equal parts in a simple grid and mark the Brahmasthan at the centre for all floors. The use of space, the position of the staircase, the arrangement of rooms and the open voids should then be planned by this grid, in line with the main and in-between directions.

The main steps are these.

1. Face the main entrance of the building east or north.
2. Mark the Brahmasthan at the centre and keep it open on every floor.
3. Divide the main uses by direction, for example the bedroom in the south-west and the kitchen in the south-east.
4. Place staircases and service areas according to the Vastu rules.
5. Repeat the same division on every floor, with small changes for the purpose of the building and the needs of the residents.

## How to read the results

To judge the Vastu of a multi-storey residential building, these points are looked at.

- Whether the centre (Brahmasthan) is open on every floor
- The line and direction of the staircase
- The position of utility rooms and heavy structures
- The entry and exit routes on every floor
- The direction of balconies and main windows

It is believed that in a building that follows Vastu the common spaces stay lively, air moves well and there is goodwill among the residents. Faults such as a blocked centre or a staircase in the wrong place are linked in the tradition with health trouble, financial instability or friction between people.

## Practical use

### Planning and approvals

Architects and builders can bring Vastu principles in at the earliest stage of design, that is before getting approvals and laying the foundation. This means fewer changes to the structure later.

### Choosing a flat for yourself

Residents can judge the balance of energy in a flat by these points.

- The direction of the entrance
- Natural light and movement of air
- No heavy structure at the centre
- The right position of the bedrooms, the kitchen and the balconies

Where changes to the structure are not possible, following most of the Vastu rules is still regarded as useful.

## Common misconceptions

- **Misconception:** Vastu applies only to detached houses.
  - **Correction:** if the basic principles are respected, the Vastu rules can be adapted to every kind of building, including tall buildings.
- **Misconception:** full compliance with Vastu is compulsory.
  - **Correction:** in today's setting, partial compliance is also regarded as useful.
- **Misconception:** a staircase or a lift can be put anywhere.
  - **Correction:** according to the tradition, a wrong position for these can unsettle the energy of the whole building.

## Examples

1. **A plan for an ideal tall building:** a residential building of 10 floors has its main entrance to the east, its staircases in the south-west, its lifts away from the central axis and open recreation space in the central atrium. On no floor is there storage or a pillar in the Brahmasthan, and the balconies face north and east.
2. **A problematic plan:** in a tower the lift shaft and service core at the centre come into the Brahmasthan on every floor, and the staircases are in the north-east. In Vastu tradition such a plan is taken as an example of blocked central energy and imbalance of directions.

## Conclusion

Vastu for multi-storey residential buildings joins traditional knowledge to the needs of city life today. An open Brahmasthan, a proper division of the floors, a thoughtful position for the staircase and agreement with the directions are the principles that architects and residents keep in mind to make a harmonious place to live.

## FAQs

### Can Vastu be applied in a flat or a multi-storey building?

Yes. If the basic principles are kept in mind, such as an open Brahmasthan, rooms by direction and the right position of the staircase, the Vastu rules can be adapted to every kind of building.

### Where should the staircase be in a multi-storey building?

The staircase should be in the south, west or south-west zone and should rise clockwise. Do not build a staircase in the north-east. Keep the staircase in the same line on every floor.

### What is the Brahmasthan and why keep it open?

The Brahmasthan is the central zone of a building. On every floor there should be no heavy furniture, staircase, lift or column at the centre, because it is believed that this blocks the flow of prana.

### What should I look at when choosing a flat?

Look at the direction of the entrance, natural light and air, whether there is any heavy structure at the centre, and the right position of the bedrooms, kitchen and balconies.

### What if full compliance with Vastu is not possible?

Full compliance is not considered compulsory. Where changes to the structure are not possible, following most of the rules is still regarded as useful.

### Which direction is good for a balcony?

A balcony facing north or east is considered good for morning light and positive energy. A balcony facing south or west may need shade or plants.
