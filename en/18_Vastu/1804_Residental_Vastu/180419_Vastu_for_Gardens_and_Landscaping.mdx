# Garden and Landscaping Vastu: Direction, Plants and Water

## Introduction

Vastu for gardens and landscaping is the part of Vastu tradition that fits outdoor spaces to the forces of nature. Its principles guide how to shape an outdoor space so that it is pleasing to look at and, as the tradition holds, supports a good flow of energy and well-being. This article sets out the guidelines for where to place a garden, which plants to choose, how to use water, and how to plan paths and proportions.

## What Is Vastu for Gardens and Landscaping

It is a branch of Vastu Shastra, the traditional Indian science of architecture and design, that deals with outdoor spaces. It gives guidelines for the placement, design and arrangement of garden elements in line with the five elements (earth, water, fire, air and space) and the main directions. Unlike a casually designed garden, a landscape planned by Vastu is meant to work as a channel of energy that suits the home and gives a balanced setting for the people who live there.

In Vastu tradition a garden is regarded as an extension of the living space that can raise or lower the energy of a property. When it is designed on Vastu principles, a garden is seen as a means of inviting prosperity, health and positive energy, and of reducing negative influences.

## Key Aspects of Garden Vastu

### Choosing the Direction

The position of the garden plays a major part in Vastu Shastra. The north, east and north-east are considered the most auspicious for a garden because they attract the most positive energy. These directions receive plenty of morning sunlight, which is regarded as sattvic (pure) in nature and is linked with vitality and prosperity. The north is associated with wealth and career growth, and the east with health and spiritual growth. The north-east, regarded as the most sacred corner, is said to bring divine energy and wisdom when it is used as garden space.

The south and west, on the other hand, should have less open space and more heavy elements such as rock gardens or large trees. The south-west corner in particular should never hold a garden. It is the zone that calls for stability and groundedness, and it is usually kept for the master bedroom or heavy structures of the home.

### Choosing and Placing Plants

According to Vastu, plants are more than decoration and are regarded as carriers of energy. A particular kind of plant is described for each direction, to strengthen the natural energy of that part. In the north and east, small shrubs, flowering plants and fruit trees are believed to bring positive energy and prosperity. A tulsi (holy basil) plant is held to be especially auspicious in the north-east, north or east, because tradition says it purifies the atmosphere and attracts divine energy.

Larger trees should be planted in the south or west to give stability and protection. Thorny plants, with the exception of roses, should generally be avoided in a garden, especially indoors.

### Water Features

In Vastu, water is a symbol of wealth and prosperity. Fountains, ponds and other water features are ideally placed in the north, north-east or east. A fountain in the north is regarded as especially good for financial abundance, because this direction belongs to Kubera, the god of wealth. When installing a water feature, make sure that the water flows towards the centre of the property and not away from it. This is seen as a symbol of prosperity coming in.

Water features should never be placed in the south, south-east or west. According to tradition, this brings financial instability and upsets the balance of energy in the home.

## How to Plan the Garden

Traditional Vastu has no complex mathematics, but it needs close attention to directions and proportion. These are the steps for applying Vastu to a garden.

1. **Find true north.** Use a compass to know the main directions properly. The digital compass on a mobile phone may not be accurate enough for Vastu.
2. **Draw a grid of directions.** Divide the property into nine parts, in the way of a simple grid, to see which part belongs to which direction.
3. **Fix the proportions.** Ideally the north and east parts should have more open space, about 60 percent, than the south and west parts, about 40 percent.
4. **Look at the flow of energy.** Look at the way people move in the garden. Energy should flow clockwise, and paths should be gently curved rather than straight, so that energy moves gently.
5. **Balance the elements.** Include all five elements: earth (plants and soil), water (fountains), fire (lighting), air (open space) and space (the overall design). Emphasis on any one can be raised to suit the needs of the household.

## Signs for Judging a Garden

- **Dominant direction:** If the garden lies mostly in the north and east, tradition holds that it supports career opportunities, health and spiritual growth. A garden in the south and west is thought to bring more challenges unless it is balanced with suitable elements.
- **Energy of plants:** Flowering plants are linked with joy and creativity, fruit trees with abundance, and evergreens with stability and continuity.
- **Movement of water:** The sound, movement and volume of a water feature are linked with the kind of energy it carries. Gentle, steady flowing water is said to bring steady prosperity, while a forceful fountain may bring sudden gains but its position must be decided with care.
- **Colour:** Green is linked with growth and renewal, while the red, yellow and pink of flowers are held to carry the energy of passion, wisdom and love respectively.
- **Signs of obstruction:** Dead plants, stagnant water or broken items are taken as a sign of blocked energy, which may show up as an obstacle in the area of life linked with that direction.

## Putting It into Practice

### Making a Garden for Prosperity

Tradition offers these suggestions for a garden meant for wealth and abundance.

1. Make the north and north-east parts of the property lush with greenery.
2. Install a fountain in the north with the water flowing towards the house.
3. Plant money plants or jade plants in green or blue pots in the north.
4. Make curved paths of natural stone or gravel that run clockwise through the garden.
5. Plant flowering plants in the east to add creative energy.
6. Keep the garden well maintained, because neglect is believed to block the energy of prosperity.

### A Garden for Health and Well-Being

Tradition offers these suggestions for a garden meant to be lively and restful.

1. Plant medicinal herbs such as tulsi, mint and aloe vera in the east part.
2. Keep the north-east corner clean and empty. If you wish, make a small space for meditation there.
3. Put gentle lighting in the north-east and east.
4. Make a comfortable seating place in the north or east for rest.
5. Avoid thorny plants throughout the garden so that negative energy does not collect.
6. Add things that attract birds and butterflies, to keep the garden lively.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **All thorny plants are inauspicious.** Most Vastu experts discourage thorny plants, but roses are an exception and can be placed in the north or east.
2. **Water features are always beneficial.** Water features are strong Vastu tools but they must be placed correctly. In the wrong place (south, south-west or west) they are said to cause money difficulty instead of attracting wealth.
3. **Large trees should be avoided.** Large trees are not a problem in themselves. They only need the right place. They suit the south or west, but should be avoided in the north and east, where they can block the useful morning sunlight.
4. **Garden Vastu affects only looks.** Many think Vastu is only about appearance, but tradition holds that garden Vastu influences the energy of the whole property.
5. **Vastu needs major reconstruction.** Applying garden Vastu does not need complete redevelopment. Even small adjustments, such as potted plants in particular directions, can be made.

## Examples

### Example 1: Changing a Home Garden

A family in Delhi felt money pressure and repeated illness. A Vastu check found that their garden lay mostly in the south with a large water feature, while the north was crowded with heavy stones and large trees.

These changes were made in the garden:

- The large trees in the north were moved to the south.
- The water feature in the south was removed and a new fountain installed in the north.
- The north and east parts were opened up and planted with flowering plants and small fruit trees.
- A curved path was made for a smooth flow of energy.

This example shows how a garden is changed according to direction in the tradition.

### Example 2: Changing an Office Compound

In a technology company, employees did not stay and work was stuck. A Vastu consultant looked at the office garden and found that the entrance garden was in the south-west, with thorny plants and a dry, stony design.

These changes were made in the compound:

- A welcoming space with flowering plants was made in the north and east.
- A small water feature visible from the entrance was installed in the north-east.
- Fruit trees were planted in the east as a symbol of growth.
- Gently curved natural stone paths were made to lead energy into the building.

## Conclusion

Vastu for gardens and landscaping gives a framework for outdoor spaces that please the eye and, in the tradition, support a good flow of energy. By fitting the garden to the directions, with the north, east and north-east kept for open space and water features and the south and west for larger plants and steadying elements, you make an outdoor setting that goes with the inner energy of the home. Careful choice and placement of plants, water features, paths and decorative items according to Vastu principles is how tradition shapes a garden.

Whether you apply these principles in full or bring in elements one by one, Vastu Shastra offers a long-held method for landscapes that work with natural forces.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### In which direction should a garden be made?

The north, east and north-east are considered the most auspicious, because they attract the most positive energy and receive morning sunlight. The south-west corner should never hold a garden.

### Where should a tulsi plant be planted?

In the north-east, north or east, which are held to be especially auspicious for it.

### Where should a fountain or water feature be placed?

In the north, north-east or east, with the water flowing towards the centre of the property. Never place water features in the south, south-east or west.

### What are the rules for large trees and thorny plants?

Plant large trees in the south or west for stability and protection, and avoid them in the north and east. Thorny plants, except roses, are generally avoided, especially indoors.

### How should garden paths be made?

Gently curved rather than straight, and running clockwise. Natural stone or gravel is suggested.

### How do I get started?

Clear clutter from the north and east parts of the garden, add a small water feature in the north, plant flowering plants in the east and north-east, avoid thorny plants indoors and near entrances, let paths curve gently, and keep the garden clean by removing dead plants promptly.
