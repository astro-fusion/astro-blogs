# Children's Room Vastu: Direction, Bed and Study Desk

## Introduction

Children's room Vastu is the application of the old Vastu principles to the space of the youngest members of the family. Vastu tradition holds that a child's surroundings influence development, studies, behaviour and general well-being. The guidelines give parents a systematic way to plan a room in which a child can grow, study and rest. This article sets out the traditional rules for the direction of the room, its size, the position of the bed and the study desk, colours and furniture.

## What is children's room Vastu?

It is a specialised branch of Vastu Shastra, the ancient Indian science of building and spatial arrangement, which looks at how to make a balanced living space for children. According to Vastu tradition each direction carries its own energy, and the directions, the elements and the planets are all said to touch human development and well-being.

The basic idea is that particular directions and arrangements of space draw in and carry different kinds of energy. In a child's room these rules aim at a setting that supports a good flow of energy, helps concentration, allows restful sleep and encourages all-round growth. Unlike the general rules, the children's room guidelines take into account the particular needs of growing minds and bodies, which are considered more sensitive. The guidelines also bring in the five elements (Pancha Bhutas): earth, water, fire, air and space, so that each is represented in balance in the child's room.

## Key aspects of children's room Vastu

### Direction and location of the room

The direction of the child's room within the house is the most basic point. According to Vastu, the west or the north-west is considered the most auspicious direction for a children's room. These directions are linked with creativity, communication and clarity of mind, qualities that support a child's growth and studies.

The west is said to bring discipline and focus, so it suits students and school-going children in particular. For boys the north-west is specially recommended, because it is said to foster independence and the ability to decide. For girls both the south-west and the north-west are considered beneficial for emotional steadiness and happiness.

The north-east corner (Ishanya) should generally be avoided for a children's bedroom, because tradition reserves it for spiritual activity and meditation. The south-east is also usually not recommended, because it carries the energy of the fire element, which is said to lead to aggression or restlessness.

### Size and layout of the room

Vastu gives about 10×12 feet as the ideal size for a children's room, said to give a balanced flow of energy. The room should be rectangular or square. Irregular or triangular layouts should be avoided, because tradition says they upset the balance of energy.

The room should balance fun, play and discipline. According to Vastu a child's room should be lively and positive, never dull or heavy. The overall arrangement is meant to follow a clockwise movement, which is believed to foster wisdom, wit and brightness in a child's life.

The entrance to the room should ideally be in the east or north-east, with the door opening clockwise. This is thought to welcome positive energy and give a harmonious flow through the room. Vastu also advises against hanging name boards on the doors of children's rooms, because this is said to attract negative energy and encourage self-centred behaviour.

### Bed position and sleeping direction

The position of the bed is a critical point. The ideal place for a child's bed is the south-west corner of the room. This place is linked with stability and grounding energy, which is held to support restful sleep.

The direction in which the child sleeps is equally important. According to Vastu, the child should sleep with the head to the south or the east. This direction is said to match the earth's magnetic field, and it is believed to give better sleep and pleasant dreams. Under no circumstances should the child sleep with the head to the north, because tradition says this disturbs sleep and may affect health.

A few more points about the bed:

- A wooden bed is preferred to a metal one, because a metal bed is said to attract negativity.
- The bed should not be placed directly in front of the door.
- There should be no mirror facing the bed, because it is said to disturb sleep.
- The bed should not stand under a ceiling beam, which is said to create pressure and stress.
- Keep a gap of a few inches between the bed and the walls so that energy can flow freely. In a small room, leave as much space as you can.

## How to fix the direction and check the room

Traditional Vastu does not involve complicated mathematics, but there are systematic ways of deciding the arrangement of a child's room.

1. **Find the directions correctly**: first identify the true four directions (north, east, south and west) of the house with a reliable compass. Everything else depends on this.
2. **Position of the room**: divide the floor area of the house into nine equal parts (a 3×3 grid) and see which part the child's room falls in, to understand the basic energy of that place.
3. **Proportions**: the ratio of length to width should ideally be 1:1 (square) or between 1.2:1 and 1.5:1 (rectangular). Work out the ratio for your child's room to see whether any change is needed.
4. **Assessing an existing room**: look at the direction (the main factor), the size and shape, the position of doors and windows, and the present arrangement of furniture.

When placing furniture, measure the distances properly. Keep 2 to 3 feet between pieces of furniture, and keep furniture about 3 feet away from the walls, so that energy has room to move.

## Directions, elements and colours

**Meaning of the directions**: Vastu tradition gives each direction its own energy.

- West and north-west: ideal for most children, for creativity and focus.
- South-west: considered good for the eldest son, for stability and responsibility.
- North and east: good for the study area, but not ideal for the bed.
- North-east: keep it light and free; it is not suitable for a children's bedroom.

**Balance of elements**: each direction is linked with an element.

- Earth element (south-west): gives stability and groundedness.
- Water element (north-east): said to increase knowledge, but it may also make a child more emotionally sensitive.
- Fire element (south-east): raises energy but may bring restlessness.
- Air element (north-west): encourages communication but may cause unsettledness.
- Space element (centre): keep it clear so that energy can flow.

**Colours**: the colours of a child's room are thought to matter a great deal.

- Yellow and green: for freshness, positivity and growth.
- Blue: calms and is said to help focus.
- Light colours: give a sense of space and positivity.
- Avoid too much red or black, which is said to cause agitation or heaviness.

When several Vastu points conflict, give priority to the most basic one: direction first, then the position of the bed, then the study desk, and then the decoration.

## Practical applications

### The study area

According to Vastu the study desk is best placed in the north, east or north-east of the room. These directions are linked with knowledge, wisdom and clear thinking. A well-planned study area is said to help concentration and memory.

While studying, the child should face east or north. The study area should be well lit, with natural light in the daytime and a desk lamp for the evening.

Keep the study area free of clutter, so that the mind stays focused and new ideas can come. Do not hang a cupboard or heavy items above the desk, because tradition says they create pressure and heaviness. A pendulum clock can be hung on the north wall of the room.

For modern needs, computers or laptops should ideally be placed in the north. If possible keep them out of the bedroom altogether, because they may disturb concentration.

### Furniture and storage

A proper arrangement of furniture is said to keep the energy balanced. Heavy furniture such as wardrobes, cabinets and storage units should go in the south or west part of the room, which gives stability.

Place cupboards along the southern or western walls and keep the centre of the room open, so that energy can move easily. All furniture should be about 3 feet away from the walls.

Do not have so much furniture that the room feels crowded or cramped. Multi-purpose furniture such as a bed with built-in storage can be chosen to save space.

A television, if it is really necessary, should be placed in the south-east of the room. Even so, it is sensible to keep electronic devices in a children's bedroom to a minimum, because they can disturb sleep and concentration.

## Common misconceptions

Several misunderstandings exist about children's room Vastu.

1. **"Any western position is good for a child's room"**: the west is generally favourable, but not every position is the same. The ideal place is straight west or north-west, while the south-west suits the eldest son in particular.
2. **"Vastu needs expensive renovation"**: most of the guidelines can be followed by rearranging furniture and choosing colours. Major demolition is not needed.
3. **"Modern amenities go against Vastu"**: Vastu adapts to present needs. These items only need the right direction: computers in the north and televisions in the south-east.
4. **"The effects of Vastu are immediate"**: some changes may be noticed quickly, but the full effect is said to come gradually.
5. **"Vastu is rigid and allows no compromise"**: when the ideal direction is not possible, alternative arrangements can be used to balance things.
6. **"Mirrors are always bad in a children's room"**: a mirror facing the bed should be avoided, but a mirror placed properly on the north or east wall is considered good.

## Examples

### Example 1: Arranging a west-facing room

In one family's home there is a rectangular room of 12×10 feet on the west side for their 10-year-old son. Following the Vastu guidelines, they did the following.

- Placed the bed in the south-west corner with the head to the east.
- Placed the study table in the north-east corner, facing north.
- Fitted the wardrobe along the south wall.
- Painted the room light yellow with a white ceiling.
- Added a small bookshelf on the north wall.
- Made the door open clockwise from the east wall.
- Kept the centre of the room clear.
- Placed a small desk lamp in the south-east corner.

This is only an illustration of an arrangement, not a promise of any result.

### Example 2: Adjustments for a south-east room

Another family, short of space, could give their 8-year-old daughter only a south-east room. To balance the fire energy of that direction they did the following.

- Chose cool blue and green colours to balance the fire energy of the south-east.
- Placed the bed against the west wall with the head to the south.
- Placed the study desk in the north corner, with seating that faces east.
- Fitted light curtains on the windows to soften the incoming light.
- Added a small indoor plant in the north-east corner.
- Used wooden furniture instead of metal.
- Kept electronic devices to a minimum.

Tradition holds that thoughtful adjustments like these can help balance a direction that is not ideal.

## Conclusion

The Vastu rules for a children's room give a framework for making a space that supports a child's all-round growth. They stress the right direction for the room, with the west and the north-west as ideal. They place the bed in the south-west corner with the head to the east or south, and the study desk in the north or east.

These rules rest on the understanding of energy in Vastu tradition and are still used when planning a home. Full compliance is not always possible in an old house, but partial compliance and a flexible approach can still help families make a room that suits their child.

## FAQs

### Which direction should a children's room face?

According to Vastu the west or the north-west is best. The south-west is considered suitable for the eldest son. The north-east and the south-east should generally be avoided.

### Which way should the child's head point while sleeping?

The head should point to the south or the east. Sleeping with the head to the north is not considered right in Vastu. The ideal place for the bed is the south-west corner of the room.

### Where should the study desk go, and which way should the child face?

Place the desk in the north, east or north-east of the room. The child should face east or north while studying. Keep the area tidy and well lit, and keep heavy items off the wall above the desk.

### What is the ideal size for a children's room?

A rectangular or square room of about 10×12 feet is considered ideal. Avoid irregular or triangular shapes.

### Which colours are good for the room?

Yellow and green are considered good for freshness and growth, blue for calm and focus, and light colours for a feeling of space. Do not use too much red or black.

### Can a television or computer be kept in a child's room?

Keep them to a minimum in the bedroom. If they must be kept, the advice is to place the computer in the north and the television in the south-east of the room.
