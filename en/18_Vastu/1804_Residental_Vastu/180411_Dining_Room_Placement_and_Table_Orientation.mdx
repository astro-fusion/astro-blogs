# Dining Room Vastu: Direction, Table and Seating

## Introduction

The dining room is more than a place where meals are eaten. It is a space where family bonds are strengthened, conversation flows and food is shared. Vastu Shastra, the traditional Indian system of architecture and placement, links the position of the dining room and the orientation of the dining table with harmony, abundance and good digestion. This article explains how a dining space can be arranged to look after both body and spirit, with particular attention to west and north-west placement, table shapes, seating and décor.

## What Dining Room Placement and Table Orientation Mean

Dining room placement and table orientation refer to where the eating area sits in the home and how the dining table is arranged under Vastu principles. Behind these principles is the belief that each direction carries its own energy, which can influence areas of life such as health, wealth and family harmony. In Vastu tradition the dining room is associated with nourishment, prosperity and family connection.

The tradition holds that the position and layout of this room can raise or lower those qualities, affecting not only digestion but also general well-being and relationships. The direction of the room, the shape of the table, the direction people face while sitting, and even colour and lighting together make up the balanced flow of energy that is thought to support healthy eating habits and family unity.

## Key Aspects

### Choosing the Direction

Vastu Shastra recommends the west and the north-west for the dining room. The west is linked with the planet Saturn, which stands for discipline and order, qualities considered suitable for mindful eating and good digestion. The north-west is held to be ruled by the Moon and is linked with satisfaction and emotional fulfilment, which is why it is described as ideal for family gatherings and meals.

A dining room in these directions is said to receive a balanced flow of energy that supports physical nourishment as well as emotional and spiritual well-being. If the layout of your home does not allow a dining room in these directions, the tradition suggests making up for it with suitable colours and décor that reflect the qualities of these directions.

One important point is that the dining room should not be directly in line with the main door. The belief is that energy then rushes through the room and disturbs the calm needed for a meal. In the same way, a dining room that touches a bathroom or shares a wall with it is considered inauspicious, because a bathroom is regarded as a place where energy drains away.

### Table Shape and Material

According to Vastu, rectangular and square tables are good for a dining room, especially in the west or north-west. A rectangular table stands for stability and order and reflects the qualities of Saturn, which is linked with the west. It also gives fixed seats, so the order of seniority in the family is clear, something traditional Vastu regards as important.

A square table stands for equality, balance and steadiness and reflects the four directions. It brings a sense of fairness and unity among those who eat together and keeps the atmosphere harmonious. For larger families a rectangular table gives more freedom in seating and suits larger gatherings.

The material of the table is considered just as important. Natural wood is recommended most. Wood is said to give stability and solid energy, and it is seen as warm, natural and a symbol of growth and vitality. Glass tables are best avoided, because they are thought to lack solidity and, by tradition, to create unstable energy that is linked with digestive trouble and friction in the home.

### Seating Arrangement

Seating around the table has particular importance in Vastu Shastra. The head of the family should face east while eating. East is the direction of the rising sun, a symbol of new beginnings, vitality and prosperity. The belief is that facing east lets the head of the family take in positive solar energy while eating, which supports sound decisions and general well-being.

The other members should sit so that conversation is comfortable and the family order is still respected. Traditional Vastu prefers an even number of chairs because it is thought to create balance and harmony. An odd number can bring a feeling of imbalance or of someone being left out.

The chairs should be comfortable enough for relaxed, long conversation during meals. High-backed chairs are recommended because they give support and a feeling of security, which improves the experience of the meal.

## How to Fix the Position

Follow these steps to decide the position of the dining room and the table.

1. **Identify the directions.** Use a compass to find the directions in your home and mark north, south, east and west clearly.
2. **Locate the west and north-west sectors.** The west is directly opposite the east, and the north-west is the area between north and west.
3. **Take measurements.** Measure the table and the space available so that the table fits without crowding.
4. **Fix the position of the table.** Use the rule of thirds: place the table so that about one third of the room lies behind it and two thirds in front. This gives balance and room to move.
5. **Give a rectangular table a slight angle.** In a rectangular room, a rectangular table can be set at about 5 to 10 degrees from parallel to the walls. The tradition holds that this small change improves the flow of energy around the table.
6. **Plan the seating space.** Allow at least 24 inches of table edge for each person, and at least 36 inches between the table edge and the wall or other furniture so that people can move easily.

## Understanding the Guidelines

Keep these points in mind when applying the rules.

A dining room in the west is held to bring order and discipline to eating habits, which may help digestion and weight balance. It is considered especially suitable for families who find it hard to keep regular meal times or whose members have digestive problems.

A dining room in the north-west is held to increase the emotional satisfaction that comes from meals, strengthen family bonds and create an affectionate atmosphere. It is ideal for families who treat mealtime as a chance to connect and share.

A rectangular table with the head of the family facing east sets a traditional order that gives structure and clarity to family life. It suits families who value traditional roles and clear leadership.

If your dining area is in a direction considered inauspicious, or cannot be moved, balancing measures through colour, lighting and décor are suggested.

Remember also that these principles should be adapted to your family's needs and the layout of your home. Following them rigidly without regard for practical needs can cause unnecessary stress, which is the opposite of the harmony Vastu aims for.

## Practical Applications

### Creating a Harmonious Dining Environment

Put these suggestions into practice to make the dining space harmonious.

- Place the dining table in the centre of the room instead of against a wall so that energy can flow freely around it. If space is tight, keep at least one side of the table, preferably the head end, against a wall for support.
- In the west or north-west, set a rectangular table at a slight angle instead of exactly parallel to the walls. The tradition holds that this improves the flow of energy and avoids stagnation.
- Install suitable lighting above the table. A chandelier or pendant light centred over the table gives useful light and also acts as a focal point, which is thought to draw positive energy to the dining area. Warm light from bulbs in the range of 2,700 to 3,000 K gives a welcoming mood and shows food and faces well.
- Choose colours that match the energy of the direction. In the west, earthy tones, white and metallic shades work well. In the north-west, white, silver and pastel shades are considered suitable, matching the lunar energy linked with this direction.
- Avoid heavy beams directly above the dining table. The belief is that they create a pressing-down energy that spoils the dining experience and may affect digestion.

### For Family Meals and Digestion

These measures are described by the tradition for good digestion and meaningful family connection at meals.

- Keep living plants or fresh flowers on or near the table to add life energy to the space. Plants with rounded leaves are considered better than those with sharp, pointed leaves.
- Keep the number of place settings even and arrange them symmetrically, so that diners feel balance and equality.
- A mirror placed so that it reflects the dining table is held to symbolically double the food and prosperity. Do not place a mirror where diners see themselves eating, because it creates self-consciousness and breaks the natural flow of conversation.
- Keep the use of televisions, phones and other devices in the dining area to a minimum. They are believed to disturb the harmony needed for good digestion and real connection.
- Choose table linen and tableware of natural materials such as ceramic, wood or metal rather than synthetic ones. This is thought to add the steady energy that supports digestion.

## Common Misconceptions

Several wrong ideas are common and are worth clearing up.

- **Myth:** Round tables are always better than rectangular ones.
  - **Reality:** A round table encourages equality and conversation, but in Vastu a rectangular table has its own advantages, especially for fixing the order of the family and for larger gatherings. The best shape depends on your family and the dimensions of the room.
- **Myth:** The dining room must always be next to the kitchen.
  - **Reality:** Being near the kitchen is practical, but Vastu gives priority to the direction of the dining room, west or north-west. A well-planned home can balance practicality with directional considerations.
- **Myth:** Glass dining tables are modern and therefore better.
  - **Reality:** Despite their modern look, glass tables are said by Vastu to create unstable energy. A solid wooden table gives stability, which is held to support digestion and family steadiness.
- **Myth:** Dining room colours should always be bright and stimulating.
  - **Reality:** Colours should match the energy of the room's direction. In a west-facing dining room, earthy tones suit better than bright, stimulating colours, which may disturb digestion.
- **Myth:** Vastu cannot be applied to modern homes or apartments.
  - **Reality:** Through careful use of colour, material and furniture arrangement, Vastu can be adapted to any living space, even when structural changes are not possible.

## Examples

### Example 1: A West-Facing Dining Room

A family renovated their home and placed the dining area in the western part. They chose a rectangular solid oak table and set it in the middle of the room at a slight angle for the flow of energy. The head of the family sits facing east, and the other members sit on comfortable high-backed chairs with natural fabric cushions. They chose earthy beige walls with copper-toned accents, hung a warm-toned pendant light centred over the table and placed a small indoor plant in the north-east corner of the room.

### Example 2: A North-West Dining Area in an Apartment

In a small apartment the dining area is part of an open-plan living space in the north-west. The family placed a square wooden table with rounded corners there and made sure the head of the family faces east while eating. They laid a light blue rug to mark out the dining area and hung a mirror on the east wall to reflect the table and make the space feel larger. A dimmer switch on the pendant light lets them change the mood through the day. They chose silver and white tableware to match the north-west and keep the table clear when it is not in use.

These two examples are only illustrations of how the rules can be applied at home.

## Conclusion

Vastu tradition holds that the placement of the dining room and the direction of the dining table influence the family's health, harmony and prosperity. By placing the dining area in the west or north-west, choosing a rectangular or square wooden table, seating the head of the family facing east and adopting suitable lighting, colours and décor, you can create a setting that is thought to support good digestion and meaningful family connection. Remember that these old principles are useful guidance, but they should be applied flexibly to suit the layout of your home and the needs of your family.

The aim is a dining room that feels harmonious and warm, where family members naturally share not only food but also the experiences of the day and strengthen the bonds that hold them together. By applying these Vastu principles thoughtfully, an ordinary room can become a place that nourishes body and spirit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which direction should the dining room face?

Vastu considers the west and the north-west most suitable. The west is linked with discipline and order, and the north-west with satisfaction and emotional fulfilment. If these directions are not possible, suitable colours and décor can make up for their qualities.

### What shape and material should the dining table be?

Rectangular and square tables are considered good. A rectangular table stands for stability and the order of the family, and a square table for equality and balance. Natural wood is recommended most, and glass tables are best avoided.

### Which way should the head of the family face while eating?

The head of the family should sit facing east. East is the direction of the rising sun, which is a symbol of new beginnings, vitality and prosperity.

### Does the dining room have to be next to the kitchen?

No. Being near the kitchen is practical, but Vastu gives priority to the direction of the dining room, west or north-west.

### Where should the dining room not be?

It should not be directly in line with the main door, and it should not touch a bathroom or share a wall with one. Both situations are considered inauspicious.

### How can a beginner get started?

Begin with small changes.

1. Use a compass to find the west and north-west parts of the home.
2. If possible, set up the dining area in one of them.
3. Place a solid wood rectangular or square table in the middle of the space.
4. Make sure the head of the family sits facing east.
5. Add warm lighting and suitable colours: earthy tones for the west and white or silver tones for the north-west.

Even a few of these changes can be a start. As you become familiar with the principles, you can refine the room further.
